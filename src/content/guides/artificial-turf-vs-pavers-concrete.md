Concrete, pavers and artificial turf compete for the same spots, like the patio off the back door or the strip beside the house. On Northern Colorado clay they fail in different ways, and the ice melt that suits one harms another. Prices are left to a quote for your yard.

## Which one fits a patio, a path or a side yard?

Start with what the surface has to carry every day.

- **The route everyone walks,** back door to garage or gate to trash cans: hardscape. An installer column from Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a turf distributor, recommends "concrete, pavers or stepping stones" over turf at entrances and exits, because steady traffic makes turf fibers lose their memory ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/premature-matting/)).
- **A table, chairs and a grill:** a hard surface. See [fire pits and grills on artificial turf](/guides/fire-pit-on-artificial-turf/).
- **Somewhere to play or let a dog out:** turf or a planted area. For dogs, see [turf vs grass for dogs](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-grass-for-dogs/) and [pea gravel vs turf for a dog run](/guides/pea-gravel-vs-turf-dog-run/).
- **A narrow side yard:** often a walk with something softer beside it. [Artificial turf in a side yard](/guides/artificial-turf-side-yard/) covers the turf half.

How the three compare:

| | Poured concrete | Pavers on a rock base | Turf on a rock base |
|---|---|---|---|
| On swelling clay | Too light to hold the clay down; cracks and heaves as one slab | Shifts piece by piece; lifted, re-leveled and relaid | Humps or dips with its base; turf folded back and the base rebuilt |
| Average surface reading, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. (one 2002 Utah study) | 94°F | Not measured | 117°F, peaking at 157°F |
| Snow tools | Any shovel, blower or plow | Shovel, blower or plow, run diagonally to the joints | Plastic shovel or broom |
| Ice melt | Rock salt or calcium chloride; no magnesium or ammonium salts; none the first winter | Rock salt first; calcium chloride sparingly; no magnesium chloride | None on purpose; keep rock salt off it |

## What does expansive clay do to a slab, pavers and turf?

The Colorado Geological Survey (CGS) says expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet, and names "cracked and heaved sidewalks, patios, driveways, and garage and basement floor slabs" as very common signs of them across Colorado ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Its homeowner guide, SP-14, explains why patios suffer most: outdoor flatwork is too light to resist the clay's uplift once it gets wet ([CGS SP-14](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-14.pdf)).

**Concrete moves as one piece, then cracks.** SP-14's advice for slabs on swelling soil:

- Seal cracks as soon as they show, so water can't soak the clay under the slab.
- A slab that has tilted until water ponds on it should be replaced. If it still falls at least 1 percent, sealing the cracks is enough.
- Patios should fall 1 to 2 percent away from the house. A patio that dams water against the house is on SP-14's list of homeowner-made problems.
- Score joints about an inch deep, on the order of 15 feet apart.

Side yards add a risk: the strip along a foundation is backfill, and SP-14 warns that concrete poured over it before it is compacted can settle, break and pond water against the house.

**Pavers move in pieces and go back down.** The Concrete Masonry & Hardscapes Association (CMHA), the paver industry's trade association, says a heave in pavers is typically frost heave in the soil below, and that pavers in a shallow dip can be lifted, the settlement corrected and the same pavers relaid "with no wasted paving materials or unsightly patches" ([CMHA](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/)). Its construction guide sets a minimum compacted base of 4 inches under a patio on well-drained soil and says continually wet or weak soils in cold climates need a thicker one ([CMHA](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-002/)).

**Turf moves with its base and gets fixed much like pavers.** A hump or dip is repaired by folding the turf back and rebuilding the base under it in thin compacted layers.

Whichever you pick, ask the installer how deep the base goes on your soil and which way the finished surface falls. A good answer names a depth chosen for your clay rather than a stock figure, and a fall away from the house.

## Which one gets hottest in a Northern Colorado summer?

Turf, in the one field study we found that measured turf and concrete side by side. Brigham Young University's 2002 study in Provo, Utah averaged surface readings from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.: about 117°F on synthetic turf, which peaked at 157°F, against 110°F on asphalt, 98°F on bare soil, 94°F on concrete and 78°F on natural grass. It didn't measure pavers.

Concrete isn't cool, either. EPA's cool-pavements compendium says conventional concrete and asphalt can reach peak summer surface temperatures of 120–150°F, and that pavement stores heat and releases it at night ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2017-05/documents/reducing_urban_heat_islands_ch_5.pdf)). New concrete reflects much of the sun, but foot and vehicle traffic dirty it and it darkens over time. Conventional paving reflects only 5 to 40 percent of sunlight, and EPA says a change in reflectance alone can shift surface temperature significantly, so a light-colored paver generally stays cooler than a dark one.

The nearest NOAA stations average 29.5 days a year at or above 90°F in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) and 58.3 in [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) (1991–2020 normals). For a seat people use on July afternoons, that favors light concrete or pavers with shade. For turf, see [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## Which one handles snow, shovels and ice melt?

The hard surfaces are easier to clear. CMHA says snow comes off pavers with "shovels, snow blowers or snowplows like any other type of pavement," worked diagonally to the joints so a blade doesn't catch an edge, and wants any paver standing more than 1/8 inch proud reset before winter. Concrete takes any shovel. Turf takes a plastic shovel or a broom; SYNLawn, a turf maker, warns that metal tools damage the fibers ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/articles/how-to-maintain-artificial-grass/)). With 51.4 inches of snow a year at the Fort Collins station, make the walk you shovel a hard surface.

Ice melt is where the three disagree:

| Product | On concrete (NRMCA) | On pavers (CMHA) | On turf (turf makers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock salt (sodium chloride) | Acceptable | Least damaging to concrete; use it first | FieldTurf: dries into crystals that scuff fibers. Synthetic Grass Warehouse: can plug the backing's drain holes |
| Calcium chloride | Acceptable | Only when something faster is needed, used sparingly | FieldTurf prefers it to rock salt, though it leaves a residue |
| Magnesium chloride | Never: chemically aggressive | Not recommended: can degrade all concrete | FieldTurf says it won't harm turf, apart from corroding exposed metal |
| Ammonium sulfate or nitrate | Never | Not covered | Not covered |

*Sources: [NRMCA CIP 2](https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/02pr.pdf), [CMHA](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/), [FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/en/articles/detail/everything-you-need-snow-removal/), [Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-to-care-for-artificial-grass-in-snowy-weather/). SYNLawn goes further: rock salt and chemical de-icers in general can degrade turf fibers and backing.*

So where a slab or pavers meet turf, no single ice melt suits both sides:

- **Sand for traction first.** NRMCA suggests clean sand; CMHA sands pavers before any de-icer and sweeps the leftover sand off in spring.
- **De-icer on the hard surface only,** spread away from the turf edge. CMHA also says not to pile salty snow on pavers.
- **Nothing on a new slab.** NRMCA's rule is no de-icers the first winter and moderate amounts after that. For outdoor slabs that will see de-icers, it recommends air-entrained concrete, about 6 percent air, at 4,000 psi. Ask a concrete contractor for both.

Road salt arrives anyway: NRMCA warns that magnesium salts spread on roads get tracked onto concrete by cars. [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/)'s road brine is magnesium chloride, and [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) pre-wets its rock salt with a magnesium chloride solution ([Loveland](https://www.lovgov.org/services/public-works/snow-operations); [Windsor](https://www.windsorgov.com/503/Snow-Ice-Removal)). NRMCA's advice is to hose off the salt cars leave on driveways and garage floors when conditions allow.

Freeze and thaw do the rest. NRMCA says scaling risk climbs with the number of freeze-thaw cycles, and de-icers add both saturation and cycles. The Fort Collins station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F.

## Does a patio change my stormwater bill or my town's yard limits?

In Fort Collins and Firestone, it can.

Fort Collins Utilities bills stormwater each month by lot area times a runoff factor, set by how much of the lot doesn't absorb water ([Fort Collins Utilities](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Pay-My-Bill/Rates)):

| Share of the lot that is impervious | Runoff factor |
|---|---|
| Up to 30% | 0.25 |
| 31–50% ("typical for residential") | 0.4 |
| 51–70% | 0.6 |
| 71–90% | 0.8 |
| Above 90% | 0.95 |

A lot pushed from 50% to 51% impervious goes from 0.4 to 0.6, half again the charge. The rates page doesn't say how pavers, gravel or turf are counted, or when a lot is re-measured. It depends on how the utility has your lot on file: ask Fort Collins Utilities before a large patio or paver job.

[Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) weighs patios against turf. Its code caps turf at 75% of a single-family rear or side yard, and turf plus patios and walkways must still leave at least 25% of the yard as permeable, living landscape. A separate rule wants 50% of the combined side and rear yards in live plants, and turf can't count toward it ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). In Firestone, a bigger patio leaves less room for turf. Other towns' limits are in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/). This is general information, not legal advice.
