faq:
  - q: "How can I tell if a tree is struggling after the lawn came out?"
    a: "CSU Extension's GardenNotes #657 lists the signs of prolonged drought stress: wilting, early leaf drop, smaller leaves, early fall color, scorched leaf edges, purpling or browning, and more insect and disease trouble. USU Extension says yellowing can show in the first season and crown dieback within two to three years, so have an arborist look at the first signs."
  - q: "Do tree watering bags work in a turf yard?"
    a: "Only on young trees. GardenNotes #657 says self-watering bags suit newly planted trees, aren't practical for mature ones, and can trap heat against the trunk and keep it too wet. For a mature tree, a soaker hose around the canopy's edge puts the water where the roots are."
  - q: "Should I push a deep-root watering needle through the turf?"
    a: "It won't do much for a mature tree. GardenNotes #657 says deep-root waterers are not an effective way to irrigate because most tree roots aren't deep, and CSU Extension says never to push one more than 8 inches into the soil. Fort Collins Utilities does allow a deep-root fork or needle at any time under its Watch."
  - q: "Does a sprinkler set on the turf count against my lawn watering days?"
    a: "Under some rules it does. Fort Collins Utilities limits sprinkler watering of trees to two days a week, outside 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., and Milliken's definition of hand-watering leaves out a hose with a sprinkler on it. Both give a hand-held hose and drip more room than a sprinkler."
  - q: "Is there a gentler time of year to take out the lawn around a tree?"
    a: "USU Extension says taking a lawn out between late fall and early spring gives the trees an adjustment period. Whatever the month, the tree's water has to start the day the lawn zones stop. Timing the install itself is covered in our guide to when to install turf."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** A tree in a lawn drinks the lawn's water. When the grass becomes artificial turf and the lawn zones shut off, that water stops. Here is where the water should go once turf covers the roots, how to set up drip you can still reach, and how to carry a tree through a dry Northern Colorado winter under your town's rules.

## Why do trees in a new turf yard suddenly need their own water?

Because the sprinklers were watering them too. CSU Extension's GardenNotes #657 says a lawn sprinkler system is an easy way to water mature trees in or near a lawn, since the grass and the tree roots share one rooting area ([GardenNotes #657](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/657.pdf)). On clay, 90–95% of a tree's roots sit in the top 12 inches; what digging a turf base does to them is in [artificial turf around trees](/guides/artificial-turf-around-trees/).

Take that water away at once and the tree is in drought. Utah State University Extension says yellowing can show the first season after the lawn's irrigation stops, and crown dieback within two to three years ([USU Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/cwel/research/preserving-trees-during-and-after-lawn-removal)). A turf distributor's guidance for installers says trees inside a turf area must keep getting irrigation or they will be stressed and die ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/irrigation/)).

Fort Collins Forestry sets the priority: a lawn lost to drought comes back in months, "but it will take 20 years to replace a 20-year-old tree" ([Fort Collins Forestry](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Forestry/Community-Resources)). Plan the tree's water before the lawn comes out.

## Where should the water go once turf covers the roots?

Out at the feeder roots, not the trunk. GardenNotes #657 says the most effective place to water a mature tree is just outside the dripline, the canopy's edge, and that roots may spread two to five times the canopy's width. The Colorado State Forest Service says to soak the whole area under the branches ([CSFS](https://csfs.colostate.edu/2017/02/02/winter-tree-watering-necessary-drought-conditions/)). In a turf yard, most of that area is turf. Four ways to reach it:

| Method | Where the water goes | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Drip ring in an open mulch ring or bed | Soil you can see and reach | The ring has to grow outward with the tree |
| Drip tubing under the turf at the canopy line | Right over the roots | Out of sight; fasteners can puncture it; repairs mean lifting turf |
| Soaker hose laid on the turf | Down through the turf and base | Only where the turf drains; slow |
| Hose-end sprinkler set on the turf | Wide coverage | Counts as sprinkling under some rules |

The Synthetic Turf Council says permeable turf systems are typically designed to drain 25 inches an hour ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). Putting-green turf may not pass water at all: one distributor says non-permeable backings are common on it ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-does-backing-quality-affect-your-artificial-turfs-drainage/)). A tree beside a [putting green](/services/putting-greens/) needs its water from outside the green.

## How do you set up a drip ring and a tree zone?

Give the trees a zone of their own. The sprinklers-to-dripline guide the City of Longmont posts says tree drip lines must be on their own zone, because tree zones run much longer than beds of perennials and shrubs ([Longmont guide](https://longmontcolorado.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/4-Transition-your-Irrigation-ada.pdf)). Converting an old lawn zone is covered in [your sprinkler system after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).

Then lay the ring:

- **Tubing and spacing.** USU Extension recommends half-inch pressure-compensating in-line drip tubing in a circle or grid around the tree, emitters 12 to 18 inches apart, off the trunk and at the canopy's edge or beyond.
- **No trenching.** USU says trenching cuts tree roots, and that in-line tubing will not work properly buried under soil, though it works under mulch.
- **Room to grow.** CSU's notes say emitters must move outward, with more added, as a tree grows; the tree guide Longmont posts says to make a drip ring bigger over time ([Longmont tree guide](https://longmontcolorado.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/5-Four-Year-Tree-Watering-Guide-ADA.pdf)).

So keep tree drip out from under the turf where the yard allows: a ring in an open bed moves in an afternoon, while one under turf means lifting turf, and the installer guidance above warns that nails and staples puncture buried lines ([nails or staples](/guides/artificial-turf-nails-vs-staples/)).

Ask the installer before the base goes in:

- Where will each tree's water come from, and on which zone?
- Does any drip line run under the turf? How deep, and how would anyone reach it or widen the ring?
- How will nails and staples stay clear of the lines?

A good answer names the zone, marks every line on a sketch you keep, and either puts the ring in an open bed or explains how the turf lifts to reach it.

## How much water does a tree need, and how often?

CSU Extension's rule is 10 gallons per inch of trunk diameter each watering, measured 6 inches above the ground ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/fall-and-winter-watering-of-plants-and-trees/)). GardenNotes #657 suggests one to two inches every two weeks in the growing season, and counts drip output as emitters times their flow. A 4-inch tree needs 40 gallons; a ring of 10 emitters at 2 gallons an hour puts out 20 gallons an hour, so about 2 hours.

Clay changes the pace, not the amount. The same notes say clay absorbs water slowly and may benefit from cycle and soak, and Greeley's water department says its soil can only absorb so much at a time ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/water-and-sewer/save-water/restrictions-violations-variances/)). Split the run into cycles with a rest between.

Check first: water when the top 4 to 6 inches of soil has dried, GardenNotes #657 says; if a slender screwdriver slides 6 to 8 inches deep at several spots, skip it. Test in the mulch ring and at the turf's edge. USU Extension warns that overwatering harms trees too, and says to watch a tree for two years after the lawn comes out.

## How do you water a tree through a Northern Colorado winter?

On dry, mild days, with a hose. At the nearest NOAA stations, each month from November through February normally brings less than an inch of precipitation, snow included, while normal daily highs sit in the mid-40s:

| NOAA station, 1991–2020 normals | Nov | Dec | Jan | Feb | Normal high, Dec / Jan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json) | 0.74 in | 0.47 in | 0.41 in | 0.47 in | 44.0°F / 45.0°F |
| [Greeley UNC](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json) | 0.67 in | 0.55 in | 0.45 in | 0.45 in | 43.4°F / 44.1°F |
| [Longmont 2 ESE](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00055116&format=json) | 0.75 in | 0.46 in | 0.56 in | 0.57 in | 44.8°F / 44.5°F |

GardenNotes #657 says to water trees monthly when October-to-April precipitation runs under an inch a month: a normal winter here. When to go out:

- **Above 40°F, no snow on the ground.** CSU Extension says to water only then, at midday so it soaks in before the night freeze, about once or twice a month in dry spells. Fort Collins Forestry says 1 to 2 times a month, October through March.
- **After two dry weeks.** The Colorado State Forest Service says trees on the plains and Front Range often need winter water after more than two weeks without lasting snow cover.
- **Windy, sunny spots first.** CSU says windy sites dry faster, and plants in heat reflected off walls suffer most on south and west exposures. The chinook, which the National Weather Service calls a warm, dry winter wind off the Rockies ([NWS](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=chinook)), is that kind of wind.
- **Young trees most of all.** CSU says newly planted trees are the most susceptible to winter drought injury.

The tree zone is blown out before the first fall frost, so winter water comes from the spigot. The Longmont tree guide lays a garden hose around the drip line at a slow trickle for 15 to 30 minutes, depending on the tree's size, then drains and stores the hose. In a turf yard the hose goes on the turf at the canopy's edge.

## What do 2026 town watering rules allow for trees?

Several rules treat hand-watering and drip for trees apart from lawn sprinklers. As posted on September 25, 2026 (a summary, not legal advice):

| Where your water comes from | Trees | Winter |
|---|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) Utilities | [Voluntary Watch](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Programs-and-Rebates/Water-Programs): by hand, drip or deep-root fork any time; by sprinkler 2 days a week, not 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. | City Forestry: 1 to 2 times a month |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | No watering 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. | Trees allowed October through March; no lawn watering November 1 to April 14 without a variance |
| [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/) | [Level 3](https://www.johnstownco.gov/Faq.aspx?TID=37) to October 15: hand-water on your assigned day with a shutoff nozzle; none 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. | [After October 15](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule), hand-water trees and shrubs Saturdays and Sundays |
| [Severance](/areas/severance-co/) Town water | [Hand-watering trees](https://www.townofseverance.org/396/Watering-Guidelines-and-Restrictions) every day with a shut-off hose; no outdoor water 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. | No lawn watering December 1 to March 31; the tree rule names no season |
| [Milliken](/areas/milliken-co/) | [Hand-watering and drip](https://millikenco.gov/194/Watering-Schedule) every day; a hose with a sprinkler doesn't count | No automatic sprinklers November 1 to March 1 |
| [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) Town water | [Stage 1](https://www.firestoneco.gov/1883/Current-Water-Conditions-Stage-1-Drought) through 2026: lawn and landscape watering on assigned days | No separate tree rule |
| [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) | [Mild Drought Response](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/): cutting back encouraged, not required | City-posted tree guide: days above 40°F |

Where the rules leave a gap:

- **Hour bans against a midday soak.** Severance's 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. ban names no season, so a winter soak there goes in before 11. Greeley doesn't say whether its 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. ban covers winter tree watering, nor Johnstown whether its ban outlasts October 15. It depends on each town's reading: ask Greeley's Water Efficiency office or Johnstown's conservation staff.
- **Firestone.** Whether hand-watering a tree counts as landscape watering under Stage 1 is the Town's call; ask its water department.
- **Severance's other water.** Non-potable systems and North Weld County Water District customers follow their own rules.

Other towns' rules are in [do you have to water artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/). Ask your provider how it treats hand-watering a tree before the first dry spell, not during it.
