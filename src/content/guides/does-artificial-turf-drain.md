    a: "No. The blades, backing and infill are made to be rained on, and a shower rinses dust off the surface. Trouble comes from water that can't leave. Water that stands for days is where the moss and bug questions start; our guides to mold and moss and to bugs on turf cover both."
  - q: "Why does water sit on my backyard putting green?"
    a: "It may have no drain holes at all. Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a turf distributor, says non-permeable backings are common on putting green and bocce turf, because the dense surface and heavy infill make ordinary drainage very difficult. On that kind of green, water leaves only by running off the contours, so the slope and the edge it runs to decide whether it puddles. Our putting greens page covers how a green is built."
  - q: "Can ice melt or salt clog turf drainage?"
    a: "It can. Synthetic Grass Warehouse advises against salt on artificial grass because it can clog the holes in the backing and hold back drainage; it suggests letting snow melt on its own. Our snow removal guide covers what to use on turf instead."
  - q: "Why does my turf flood when the snow melts?"
    a: "Usually because the infill and the ground under it are still frozen. SIS Pitches says heavy rain or a fast thaw on frozen synthetic turf can flood it, since the frozen sand infill stops water draining, and that it drains properly again once the infill thaws. Water on thawed ground in May is a drainage problem; water on frozen ground in February usually isn't."

Turf doesn't mind rain. What matters is where the water goes next, and in much of Northern Colorado that depends more on the ground under the turf than on the turf.

## Where does rain go when it falls on artificial turf?

Down through four layers, then out:

1. **Blades and infill.** Rain runs down the blades and through the sand or granules between them.
2. **The backing.** Most lawn turf has a backing punched with drain holes, which the distributor Synthetic Grass Warehouse calls the industry standard; flow-through backings, found on some dog turfs, pass water across the whole sheet ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-does-backing-quality-affect-your-artificial-turfs-drainage/)).
3. **The base.** Compacted crushed rock holds the water in the gaps between stones for a while.
4. **The soil, or the edge.** Water leaves by soaking into the ground or running along the base to a lower edge, bed or drain.

Firestone's code spells out that job: a compacted, porous aggregate base at least 3 inches deep "to allow for adequate drainage both horizontally and vertically and to prevent excessive runoff or pooling." It also says a turf install may not impede or alter the lot's drainage pattern ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)).

A puddle on top means one layer isn't passing water on. Here it is usually the last one.

## How fast does turf drain compared with a Northern Colorado cloudburst?

Faster than rain falls. The Synthetic Turf Council says permeable turf systems are "typically designed to drain 25” of water per hour" ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). NOAA's storm figures:

| Rain, in inches per hour | [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2-year storm, over 1 hour | 0.879 | 0.851 | 0.859 | 0.793 |
| 100-year storm, over 1 hour | 3.05 | 2.80 | 2.73 | 2.83 |
| 100-year storm, its hardest 5 minutes | 12.5 | 11.3 | 10.9 | 11.3 |

NOAA Atlas 14 point estimates: [Fort Collins](https://hdsc.nws.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/hdsc/new/fe_text_mean.csv?lat=40.5853&lon=-105.0844&data=intensity&units=english&series=pds), [Windsor](https://hdsc.nws.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/hdsc/new/fe_text_mean.csv?lat=40.4775&lon=-104.9014&data=intensity&units=english&series=pds), [Greeley](https://hdsc.nws.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/hdsc/new/fe_text_mean.csv?lat=40.4233&lon=-104.7091&data=intensity&units=english&series=pds), [Longmont](https://hdsc.nws.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/hdsc/new/fe_text_mean.csv?lat=40.1672&lon=-105.1019&data=intensity&units=english&series=pds). A 100-year storm is one that comes about once a century on average.

What each layer passes:

| Layer | Inches per hour |
|---|---|
| A turf system as typically designed (STC) | 25 |
| One distributor's hole-punched lawn turfs (SGW) | over 30 |
| Flow-through backings (SGW) | hundreds |
| One dog-turf spec, turf and base together ([K9Grass Classic+](https://k9grass.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/K9Grass-Classic-Plus-Outdoor-CSI-M1266-0322.pdf)) | at least 800 |
| Olney fine sandy loam, slowest layer* | about 1.3 |
| Nunn clay loam, slowest layer* | about 0.13 |

\*How fast a soaked soil layer passes water, from USDA-NRCS soil survey data for Larimer and Weld counties and the Longmont area.

So the backing is rarely what makes a puddle: even the hardest five minutes of a 100-year storm fall at half the typical design rate or less. Those rates are for new, clean turf, though. FieldTurf's care guide warns that leaves left over winter can rot into "a layer of organic matter that can impede drainage" ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturflandscape.com/maintenance/artificial-turf-maintenance-guide/)).

Firestone writes backing drainage into its code: no felt backing, and drain holes in a uniform grid no more than 4 by 6 inches on center. Elsewhere, the STC suggests a contract that sets the turf's and base's permeability high enough for "the local weather and rainfall patterns."

## Why does turf drain in one yard and puddle in the next?

The soil under the base, which can change within one town:

- Soil surveys map mostly Olney fine sandy loam and Kim loam around central Windsor. Olney's slowest layer passes about 1.3 inches an hour.
- Nunn clay loam, common around the Larimer foothill towns, Longmont and Frederick–Dacono, passes about 0.13 in its slowest layer: a tenth as fast.
- Longmont tells residents its "clay soils absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)).
- Greeley's water department says hard clay sits "sometimes just inches below the surface" ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)).
- CSU Extension says water moves slowly, or not at all, in compacted soils and soils over 20% clay; in many Colorado soils it soaks in only to stop at a tighter layer below. New-home soil, it adds, is typically compacted by construction traffic ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/soil-drainage/)).

Run a 100-year hour in Fort Collins through that: 3.05 inches fall, and Nunn clay loam's slowest layer takes about 0.13 of them in that hour. The rest waits in the base or moves sideways, and a level base gives it nowhere to go. One turf maker's manual says turf over clay or hard-packed soil "will not drain on a level surface" ([PolyTurf](https://polyturf.com/uploads/files/polyturf_installation_manual_landscape_only.pdf)). The base fills and water backs up through the backing: the puddle.

How to tell what is under your lot is in [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/); a new build's dirt yard is in [new-build backyard turf](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/).

## How can I check whether my soil drains?

With a shovel, a hose and CSU Extension's hole test:

1. **Request a locate first.** [Colorado 811](https://colorado811.org/homeowners/) says to contact it even for a small dig; its free locates skip privately owned lines such as sprinkler pipe.
2. **Dig a hole 12 inches deep** where the turf will go. That reaches below the base, into the soil it will drain into.
3. **Fill it with water and time it.**

What CSU's thresholds mean for turf:

- **Drained within 30 minutes:** no drainage problem by CSU's measure. The ground can take the base's water.
- **Water left after 30 minutes:** CSU calls that a drainage problem. Plan on the base sending water sideways to an edge or drain, not down.
- **Water left after 24 hours:** CSU's mark for waterlogged soil. Expect the job to need a drain with an outlet.

Test more than one spot, on ground that isn't frozen or soaked; the K9Grass specification recommends a sub-base permeability test too. Already have turf? Dig in a bed beside it, and time how long water stands after the next storm; whether turf fixes a muddy backyard covers that check.

## Why is water puddling on my turf?

Where it stands usually says why:

| Where water stands | Likely cause | Read next |
|---|---|---|
| Across the lawn after every storm | A level base over slow soil, with no way out | The fixes below |
| In one dip | The base settled there, or was never compacted | [Sinking and low spots](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/) |
| Along one edge | A curb, edging board or patio the water in the base can't get past, or a downspout emptying there | The fixes below |
| On top, even in a light rain | The surface is clogged: rotted leaves, fine debris | [Leaves](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/), [cleaning](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/) |
| After the sprinklers run | Overspray from a zone still on, or a neighbor's; CSU says many surface drainage problems come from over-irrigation | [Sprinklers after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/) |
| Only in winter or early spring | Frozen infill or frozen ground | [Winter and turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/) |
| Against the house | Grade that falls toward the foundation: poor surface drainage, which the [Colorado Geological Survey](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) lists among landscaping problems on expansive soils | [Side yards](/guides/artificial-turf-side-yard/) |

Water that stands for days is also where [mold and moss](/guides/does-artificial-turf-get-moldy/) and [bugs](/guides/does-artificial-turf-attract-bugs/) start.

## How do you fix puddles on artificial turf, and which fix is worth it?

Match the fix to the cause, starting with the least work:

| Fix | Worth it when | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| Clean the surface | Water sits on top while the base below is dry | Blow or rake off leaves and debris, brush, rinse |
| Stop water arriving | A downspout, sprinkler zone or overspray feeds the spot | Extend the downspout past the turf; cap or turn the zone |
| Lift and rebuild a dip | One or two low spots | Peel the turf back, add and recompact base, re-lay and re-seam |
| Gravel trench at the low edge | Water collects along a curb or border | A trench about 12 inches deep and 12 wide, filled with aggregate (PolyTurf's manual) |
| French drain | Clay under a large, flat area that ponds after every storm | A rock-filled trench, usually with a pipe in the bottom, sloped at least 1 to 3% to an outlet (CSU Extension) |
| Rebuild on a sloped subgrade | The whole base was laid level on clay | Turf up, subgrade reshaped to fall to an edge or drain, base rebuilt |

Two things decide whether a drain works:

- **It needs somewhere lower to empty.** CSU Extension says drain tile works only as far as the soil lets water reach it and the water can flow downhill to an outlet.
- **The outlet has to fit the lot's drainage plan.** Frederick's 2026 code says landscaping, "including any artificial turf," may not alter the drainage pattern or the amount of stormwater runoff in the lot's approved grading plan ([Frederick Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)), and Firestone's rule above covers the pattern. It depends on your lot: ask your town's Planning department, such as Frederick Planning, before a drain empties toward the street or a neighbor.

Ask anyone building or fixing your turf where water leaves the base on your lot. A good answer points to a place lower than the turf and says how the soil was tested; "it soaks in" isn't an answer on clay. ## Is artificial turf pervious or impervious?

A permeable backing lets water through, but the codes read for this page don't settle whether turf counts as pervious:

- [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) caps turf so that, counted with "other impervious surfaces such as patios and walkways," it leaves at least 25% of the yard as permeable, living landscape. For that rule, turf sits with the hard surfaces.
- [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/)'s rule above doesn't classify turf; it regulates the runoff.

If your stormwater bill depends on your lot's hard surface, ask the town's stormwater utility how it counts turf before you install. This is general information, not legal advice.
