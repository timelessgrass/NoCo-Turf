  question: "Should our school, park or club choose a synthetic or natural grass field in Northern Colorado?"
  answer: "It depends on the hours the field must carry and the water it is on. Grass stays far cooler in summer, but here it needs rest, irrigation through drought years and time to dry out after March snow and May rain. Synthetic plays through most of that and saves irrigation water, yet runs hot on summer afternoons and needs grooming."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** This page weighs the two surfaces for a public, school or club field, using what Northern Colorado towns, utilities and field researchers have published. The only dollar figures are budgets and estimates towns have made public. How long a synthetic field lasts and what replacing one involves are in how long a synthetic turf field lasts.

## What have Northern Colorado towns and schools chosen?

Both surfaces, for reasons worth reading closely.

[Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) is the freshest case. In July 2025 the town had no town-owned or town-operated lighted fields, and its deputy town manager said town athletics would not use the Future Legends complex because the playing conditions were "unsafe and unplayable." The town manager told the board the first plan was to turn two grass fields into artificial turf for "a little north of $2 million," and said Chimney Park's existing turf field, which can be used for soccer and football as well as baseball, had staff "really happy with it" ([BizWest](https://bizwest.com/2025/07/27/with-loss-of-future-legends-windsor-mulls-millions-for-sports-fields/)). Not everyone agreed: developer Martin Lind urged the town to plant 20 or 30 acres of plain grass and let kids play. In spring 2026 Windsor converted two more Chimney Park fields from grass to turf.

| Field | Surface now | What the owner said |
|---|---|---|
| Windsor, Chimney Park baseball fields 2 and 3 | Synthetic, finished May 11, 2026, on a $2,240,000 budget | Lower annual maintenance costs and a better playing surface ([Town of Windsor](https://windsorprojectconnect.com/chimneyparkturf)) |
| Longmont, Dry Creek Community Park | Synthetic, about 252,670 sq ft, the city's only synthetic space | The grass did not thrive on high-salinity soil and high groundwater, and access to school district fields was inconsistent and limited ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/facility/dry-creek-community-park/)) |
| [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) Sports Park | Grass fields plus one synthetic championship field | Grass closes for winter; the synthetic field stays open by reservation ([City of Loveland](https://www.lovgov.org/services/parks-recreation/parks-facilities/parks-reservation-spaces/loveland-sports-park)) |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), UNC's Nottingham Field | Synthetic since 2021 | Natural grass replaced in time for the September 18 home opener ([UNC Athletics](https://uncbears.com/news/2021/6/16/football-nottingham-field-to-receive-a-change-of-surface)) |
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) city parks | Grass | "Turf Fields are grass; the City does not have any artificial/synthetic fields" (parks page, [2021](https://web.archive.org/web/20211021060747/https://www.fcgov.com/parks/park-rentals)) |

The pattern: turf went in where field time was short or grass struggled. Grass works where an owner has enough fields to rest and rotate them; Firestone's parks plan says its limited space makes that hard ([Town of Firestone](https://www.firestoneco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9085/2022-07-29-POST_Master_Plan-FINAL-Compressed)).

## How much water does a grass field use, and what happens in a drought year?

A lot, and a grass field is exposed when supplies tighten. Western Resource Advocates, a Colorado-based water nonprofit, cites a study putting a full-size 1.32-acre grass field at up to 1.5 million gallons of irrigation a year, depending on location. The Synthetic Turf Council, an industry group, estimates a synthetic field that size saves 500,000 to 1 million gallons ([WRA, 2022](https://westernresourceadvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022_WRA_Artifical_Turf_Report.pdf)). At 325,851 gallons to the acre-foot ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-science-glossary)), 1.5 million gallons is more than four acre-feet, the unit Northern Colorado water is counted in. For 2026, Northern Water set the Colorado-Big Thompson quota at 0.8 acre-feet per unit.

Fort Collins shows how a shortage plan treats fields:

- Most Poudre School District athletic fields run on treated city water.
- Lawns get a target of 1 inch a week at Action Level I and 0.75 inch at Level II. Playing fields can get a permit for more, "to protect more actively used landscapes and maintain safety for those using the areas": up to 1.25 inches at Level I and 1.0 inch at Level II.
- At Level III, a projected shortage of 26 to 35%, lawn watering drops to zero and the plan issues no field permits ([Fort Collins Utilities](https://web.archive.org/web/20251008135439/https://www.fcgov.com/utilities/img/site_specific/uploads/final-wsap-effective-may-1-2020.pdf)).
- The parks department's 2021 list of reasons to cancel play included drought-related conditions.

This year shows the spread. Fort Collins declared a voluntary Water Shortage Watch in spring 2026 ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/News-Articles/News/2026-Q2/Voluntary-Water-Shortage-Watch)) and Evans a drought emergency ([City of Evans](https://www.evanscolorado.gov/news/posts/city-of-evans-declares-drought-emergency-expanded-watering-restrictions-now-in-effect/)), while Greeley called 2026 an adequate water year ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/news/2026/20260417-greeley-water-and-sewer-board-declares-adequate-water-year-urges-wise-stewardship/)). A grass field's playing surface depends on which of those years it gets.

## How many playing days does a grass field lose to spring snow and rain?

Spring is the squeeze. At NOAA's Fort Collins station, March is the snowiest month of the year at 9.4 inches on average, and May the wettest at 2.72 inches, with 5.8 days of a tenth of an inch or more ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&dataTypes=MLY-PRCP-NORMAL,MLY-SNOW-NORMAL,MLY-PRCP-AVGNDS-GE010HI&format=json)). Greeley's May averages 2.65 inches ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&dataTypes=MLY-PRCP-NORMAL,MLY-SNOW-NORMAL,MLY-PRCP-AVGNDS-GE010HI&format=json)). That is when spring seasons open.

Grass owners manage it by closing fields:

- Fort Collins schedules organized play only from the third weekend in March to the first weekend in November, starts spring and fall games an hour later "to allow for frost conditions," and tells teams to cancel for standing water, slippery ground or turf that tears underfoot ([2025 field rules](https://web.archive.org/web/20250826110620/https://www.fcgov.com/parks/files/2025-sports-field-policies-procedures.pdf)). It also rations practice: one 1.5-hour slot a week per ballfield team, two for soccer-type teams.
- Thompson School District closes its fields to community groups January through March to protect spring conditions ([TSD](https://www.tsd.org/departments/operations/facility-services/facility-use)).
- The Sports Turf Managers Association says native-soil fields, the usual school and park kind, drain more slowly than synthetic turf, and that every grass field needs time off from play and may need aerifying once or twice a year ([STMA](https://www.stma.org/sites/stma/files/pdfs/stma_syntheticturfguidehipg1.pdf)).

Synthetic's case is the reverse. Western Resource Advocates says schools pick it because it allows continuous use while grass needs rest, and Longmont's FAQ says its turf stays pliable through winter instead of freezing hard.

## How hot does a synthetic field get on a summer afternoon here?

Hot enough to move practice times. Longmont's parks FAQ says synthetic turf on sunny, calm Front Range days typically reads 20 to 40 degrees above the air at the surface, which could change programming in the hottest part of the day from mid-July to mid-September. Mead High School's football coach said the school had recorded 130 degrees on its old turf field ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/en/articles/detail/how-mead-high-school-became-the-coolest-high-school-in-colorado/)). STMA says grass rarely tops 85 degrees, and suggests an infrared thermometer with a stop temperature; some owners set it at 125 degrees.

Location matters. NOAA's Greeley UNC station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json)); the Fort Collins station averages 29.5 ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). A field in Greeley or Evans sees about twice as many 90-degree days as one in Fort Collins or Wellington, so a heat cutoff there will stop play more often. Measured temperatures and what cools turf are in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## Should a baseball or softball infield be clay or turf?

Here the weather tilts hardest. STMA's infield guide says the clay in a skinned infield turns slick when wet and is "often the cause of rainouts," then cracks and hardens in hot, dry months into a dangerous sliding surface with bad hops. It calls water the most critical element in field management ([STMA](https://www.stma.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/65d68a00-150d-457e-8708-dcb12717eecc.pdf)). A Northern Colorado skin gets both: May rain, then low summer humidity ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)) and watering-hour limits. Fort Collins' own rules say to cancel when "use of infield would cause ruts."

A clay diamond can be made more forgiving before anyone pays for turf. STMA says a sandier mix sheds rain better, with 70 to 75% sand common at the recreational level, and that a grade of about 0.5 to 1% usually moves water off an infield. Windsor went further: its two converted fields are baseball diamonds, and the town's stated reasons were lower annual maintenance costs and a better playing surface.

## Are injuries more likely on synthetic turf?

The research is mixed and depends on the sport and the body part:

- **Overall.** A 2023 review of 53 studies found that most on modern turf (13 of 18) reported similar overall injury rates to grass. Foot and ankle injuries were the pattern most often higher on turf. Soccer players showed similar knee and hip rates, but high-level football players had more knee injuries on turf. The few studies finding more injuries on grass were all funded by the turf industry ([Gould et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35593739/)).
- **Female soccer players.** A 2022 meta-analysis found a higher ACL injury risk for female players in games on turf, and no significant difference for male players ([Xiao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35990873/)).
- **Concussion.** A 2020 meta-analysis found fewer concussions on turf in contact sports such as football and rugby, and no association in soccer ([O'Leary et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32518672/)).

Questions about what is in rubber infill are covered in [is crumb rubber safe](/guides/is-crumb-rubber-safe/).

## What should a board ask before it votes?

- **How many hours, in which months?** Count the spring weeks a grass field sits closed and the rest days it needs. If the schedule already rotates several fields, grass may carry it.
- **What water is the field on, and what does the provider's shortage plan allow a field?** In Fort Collins, a Level III shortage ends field watering permits.
- **What is the heat plan?** An infrared thermometer, a stop temperature, and morning or evening slots from mid-July through mid-September, more so east toward Greeley.
- **Is it a diamond?** Weigh rainouts and daily infield work, after a sandier mix and regrading, against the cost of turf.
- **What comes with the carpet?** Colorado has barred installing artificial turf with intentionally added PFAS since January 1, 2026 ([SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-081)), so ask for that documentation, and ask which infill goes between the blades.
- **Who grooms, tops up infill, clears snow and tests hardness?** STMA lists sweeping, dragging and watering, infill top-ups as often as yearly, seam repair and snow removal, and measures shock absorption in Gmax.
