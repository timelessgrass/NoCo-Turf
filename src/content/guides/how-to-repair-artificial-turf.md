  - q: "Can I just trim a small melted spot instead of patching it?"
    a: "Sometimes. If only the blade tips have fused into hard beads and the backing underneath is sound, snipping off the melted tips with sharp scissors and brushing the area up may be enough. Once the backing has melted through or the spot is bald, it needs a patch."

  - q: "Can I patch turf in the winter?"
    a: "It depends on the glue and the forecast. Turf adhesive needs dry surfaces and its label sets a minimum temperature, and SRW Products says to keep a fresh seam away from rain, water and freezing conditions for 12 hours. A small patch can usually wait for a dry, mild spell."
  - q: "My dog keeps chewing the same spot. Will a patch hold?"
    a: "A patch fixes the hole, not the habit, and a patch adds edges a dog can pick at. Keep the dog off the spot while the glue cures and find out why it started. Our guides on pet turf versus regular turf and on dogs that dig cover the turf and the behavior."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** Most burns, holes and tears can be patched: the damaged piece comes out and a new one goes in on seam tape and glue. How well it hides depends on the grain, the color and the join, and the cause may need its own fix.

## What usually burns, melts or tears turf in a Northern Colorado yard?

Turf blades are plastic, so most damage comes from heat or from something pulling on the fibers.

- **Embers and sparks.** A fire pit, a grill, a dropped cigarette, a sparkler or firework around the Fourth of July. What is allowed where is in [fire pits and grills on artificial turf](/guides/fire-pit-on-artificial-turf/).
- **Hot tools.** Integriturf's installation guide warns that gas trimmers and hedgers have carburetors that get extremely hot, and that setting one down on turf while hot melts the fibers ([Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf)).
- **Reflected sun.** The same guide lists reflection from windows, vinyl fencing and other shiny surfaces. A melted strip along the house comes back after a patch unless the reflection is dealt with first; see [turf melting from windows](/guides/artificial-turf-melting-from-windows/).
- **Dogs.** K9Grass, a pet-turf maker, explains that most turf is tufted, each blade a loop through the backing, so a dog that grabs one end can pull it out, and that is how chewing starts ([K9Grass](https://k9grass.com/faq/do-dogs-chew-on-dog-friendly-turf/)). For diggers, see [can dogs dig through artificial turf](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/).
- **Chemicals and sharp things.** PolyTurf's manual says strong acids or other chemicals can discolor turf, and sharp objects, vehicles or vandals may damage it ([PolyTurf](https://polyturf.com/uploads/files/polyturf_installation_manual_landscape_only.pdf)). Safe cleaners are in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/), shovels and ice melt in [snow removal on turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

First check that the problem is the turf, not what is under it. A dip, a lump or a lifted edge starts in the base or the border, and a patch on top won't hold: see [sinking and low spots](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/) and [edges lifting or curling](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/).

## How do you patch a burn or a hole?

PolyTurf's manual sets out the cut-out-and-insert repair; Shaw's and SYNLawn's guides add detail on the joins.

1. **Gather the materials.** A fresh utility-knife blade, seam tape, turf adhesive, a putty knife, sandbags, a stiff broom and matching infill.
2. **Cut out the damage.** Cut between tuft rows, not through them, with a margin of sound turf around the burn. Shaw trims a roll's edge starting "two tuft rows in from edge in order to achieve proper seam strength" ([Shaw](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf)); a patch edge needs the same. PolyTurf says straight edges are easier and curved, random ones less noticeable.
3. **Trace the hole.** For an odd shape, PolyTurf traces the cut-out on newsprint or other paper and transfers the outline to the back of the new turf. Flip it the right way round, or the piece is a mirror image.
4. **Match the grain.** The new blades must lean the same way as the lawn's, with the rows lined up and evenly spaced, as SYNLawn's guide asks of any join ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)).
5. **Tape and glue.** PolyTurf cuts seam tape into short sections, glues one side, and slips each under the edge of the existing turf all the way around. Adhesive goes on the exposed tape and the new piece is pressed onto it. Where the existing turf is glued down to the surface beneath, no tape is needed.
6. **Keep blades out of the glue.** Shaw lays edges onto the adhesive without burying fibers, and butts cuts as close as possible without touching, no more than 1/8 inch apart. Some adhesives need time to "flash" off vapors first, which Shaw says can depend on temperature and humidity. Follow the label.
7. **Weight it and wait.** Sandbags along the join, or a heavy roller once the adhesive has tacked up. SRW Products, one turf-adhesive maker, says to keep light foot traffic off for 12 hours and heavy traffic for 24 ([SRW Products](https://srwproducts.com/product/seams-right-turf-adhesive-srw-products/)).
8. **Refill and brush.** PolyTurf fills the patch with infill to the same density as the turf around it, and SYNLawn cross-brushes joins in both directions to make them less visible. See [artificial turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/) and [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).

## Can a tear be fixed without cutting in a new piece?

Often, when the backing has split but no turf is missing.

- **Tape and glue from below.** Fold both sides back, slide seam tape under the full length of the split, glue, press the edges together and weight them.
- **Sewing.** PolyTurf says small, noticeable tears can be sewn with UV-resistant, rot-proof thread. In [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), though, the town's turf standard says seams "shall be taped or glued and not sewn" ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). Whether that reaches a small repair isn't spelled out; it depends on when and how your turf went in, so ask Firestone Planning. Not legal advice.
- **A split along an existing seam** is a seam coming apart. Why seams open and how they are re-glued is in [visible seams in artificial turf](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).

## Why can a patch look different from the lawn around it?

Three reasons, and you control two.

**Grain.** Integriturf warns that pieces joined without the same grain direction show a color difference and visible seams. A patch turned sideways catches the light differently even when the turf is identical.

**Sun.** Northern Colorado turf lives at high elevation. The [Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html) describes Colorado's "intense high-elevation sunlight," and the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/learn-about-uv-index) says UV intensity rises about 6% per kilometer above sea level. A lawn with years of that sun may no longer match a piece from a new roll; how fast turf changes here is in [does artificial turf fade](/guides/does-artificial-turf-fade/).

**Lot.** Smart Turf's published terms say slight color variation from lot to lot, and color change over a product's life, are common and to be expected ([Smart Turf](https://smartturf.com/warranty)). The same product bought years later may be a slightly different green.

Two ways to get closer:

- **Leftover turf.** PolyTurf advises keeping the scraps from the original install for exactly this. They share the lawn's lot, though a roll end kept in the garage has missed the sun the lawn has had.
- **A donor piece.** Cut the patch from somewhere nobody looks, such as under a planter or behind a shed, so it has the lawn's sun history, and put the new turf in the hidden spot instead.

In Firestone, matching is written into the code: damaged or worn turf "shall be repaired or removed and replaced in manner that results in consistent appearance with the existing artificial turf," and holes, tears and heat degradation are listed among the flaws a turf area may not have. Elsewhere, check your HOA's covenants for a maintenance clause.

## Does summer heat change how a patch goes in?

Yes. Warm backing grows and cool backing shrinks. SRW Products tells users to peel a seam back just before gluing, because "the turf backing can heat up and expand, leaving unwanted gaps at seams when the turf cools." A patch cut to fit on a hot afternoon can gap by morning.

Hot afternoons are common here. The Greeley UNC station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F and the Fort Collins station 29.5 (NOAA 1991–2020 normals), and the Colorado Climate Center notes large day-to-night temperature swings. So cut and glue in the cool of the morning, with the new piece lying in the same sun or shade as the lawn beside it, on dry backing over a dry base. Re-weight any corner that lifts in the first hot spell. Cold-weather limits on turf glue are in the seams guide linked above.

## Repair kit or a pro: which damage needs more than a patch?

A single burn or hole, with leftover turf on hand, is a reasonable DIY job. The glue is where kits fall short: the Synthetic Turf Council tells buyers to confirm that seams are bonded with an industrial adhesive made for synthetic grass with proven weather resistance, "a product usually not found in home supply stores" ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). SYNLawn's DIY guide adds that professional installers are trained to hide seams, and non-professional work can leave them more visible.

| Damage | Usual fix | DIY? |
|---|---|---|
| Melted tips, backing intact | Snip the tips and brush | Yes |
| One burn or hole through the backing | Cut-out patch on seam tape | Yes, with leftover turf of the same product |
| Split backing, nothing missing | Tape and glue from below | Yes |
| Seam coming apart | Re-glue the seam | Often; see the seams guide |
| Melted strip along the house | Fix the reflection, then patch | Only after the cause is fixed |
| Chewed or dug-up edge | Rebuild the base and edge, then patch | Depends on the edge |
| Brittle, bald or damaged across much of the lawn | Repair or replace | See [when to replace artificial turf](/guides/when-to-replace-artificial-turf/) |

If you hire it out, ask:

1. **"Where will the patch come from?"** A good answer is the same product and color, your leftover turf or a donor piece, not "something close."
2. **"Which tape and adhesive?"** Look for products made for turf, named, not general construction glue.
3. **"Will you fix the cause?"** A patch over a soft base, a loose edge or a reflecting window fails again.
4. **"Will you re-infill and brush it?"** A patch without infill sits differently from the turf around it.
