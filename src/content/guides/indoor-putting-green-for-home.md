**Checked September 25, 2026.** At the nearest NOAA station, Fort Collins, an average year brings 51.4 inches of snow, 150 nights at or below 32°F and 43.6 days with an inch or more of snow on the ground. In half of years the first 32°F night comes by October 7, and in half the last one comes after May 2 ([NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). That is a long stretch without a putter. Colorado State's golf teams, based at Harmony Club in [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/), have an indoor putting green in their practice building ([CSU Athletics](https://csurams.com/facilities/harmony-club-golf-course/8)).

What the floor under it needs, from moisture to a slab that moves, is in our guide to artificial turf on a basement or garage floor. If you already have a green outside, its winter care is in [artificial putting green maintenance](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/).

## Which build fits: a roll-out mat, shaped foam or a framed platform?

Indoor greens come in three builds, from lightest to most permanent.

| | Roll-out mat | Turf over shaped foam | Framed platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A finished green laid on the floor | Putting turf over foam sections or molded tiles cut to contours | A wood deck built on the floor, turf on top |
| Cups | Surface cups that need no hole | Only as deep as the foam allows | Room for a hole at least 4 inches deep |
| Break | Small sandbags or shims under the mat | Shaped into the foam | Framed into the deck, or the whole deck tilted |
| Between seasons | Rolls up for storage | Built in place | Built in place, freestanding |
| Floor it needs | Flat, clean and dry | Flat, clean and dry | Dry, and level enough to shim |

**A roll-out mat** is the quick start. StarPro Greens, which sells them, says its greens can go in family rooms or basements with cups that sit on the surface, "without coring a hole," and roll up for storage ([StarPro Greens](https://starprogreens.com/instructions-for-large-putting-green-by-starpro-greens/)). The trade-off: the ball never drops below the floor, and the break is only what fits under a mat.

**Shaped foam** gives real contours without framing. Tour Greens builds in-home greens on pre-molded tiles or custom-shaped polystyrene sections to form "realistic breaks and undulations" ([Tour Greens](https://www.tourgreens.com/indoor-putting-greens.html)). Southwest Greens describes an indoor job as prepping the room, installing a base and adding shaping material before the turf goes down ([Southwest Greens](https://www.southwestgreens.com/artificial-putting-greens/indoor-putting-greens)).

**A framed platform** is the surest way to a real hole. The Rules of Golf set the hole at 4.25 inches across and at least 4 inches deep ([R&A, Rules of Golf](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/definitions)). A bare slab has no depth for that, so a full-depth cup indoors means building the surface up. Keep the platform freestanding on the floor rather than fastened to the walls; the slab guide explains why that matters when a basement floor floats.

If someone else builds it, ask:

- What sits between the floor and the turf, layer by layer, and how deep the cups go.
- How the break is made, and whether it can be changed later.
- Whether the frame is freestanding or fastened to the walls or the slab.
- For the turf's spec sheet and any flammability test report (more below).
- How it comes out if you sell, move or finish the room differently.

A good answer names each material and its thickness, and shows you a drawing of the layers.

## How do you build break into an indoor green?

It depends on the build:

- **Under a mat:** StarPro's instructions call for half to one cup of sand in a 12-inch-square freezer bag, as many bags as you want under the green. They move in a minute.
- **In foam:** the contours are cut once and stay.
- **In a platform:** tilt the whole deck with its framing, or frame a crown or a ridge into it.

How much slope? StarPro's installation steps say the ground under a green should rise no more than 2 inches over a 10-foot run "for realistic golf practice," about 1.7% by our arithmetic ([StarPro Greens](https://starprogreens.com/product/11-x-17-5-hole-pro-backyard-or-indoor-putting-green-kit/)). That is one seller's rule of thumb. Steeper slopes, and where cups can sit on them, are covered in [building a putting green on a slope](/guides/putting-green-on-a-slope/).

A garage floor comes with break already in it. The 2021 International Residential Code requires the parking area to slope so liquids move "to a drain or toward the main vehicle entry doorway" ([IRC R309.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)). Lay a 4-foot level on the floor before you plan anything: a mat laid straight on it breaks toward the door on every putt. A platform can level that out or use it.

## How much room, headroom and light does an indoor green need?

**Length first.** Southwest Greens lists 7.5 by 15 feet, 15 by 15 and 25 by 15 among popular indoor sizes. StarPro says its 11 by 17 foot kit is its most popular because it is the largest size that fits in many golfers' recreation rooms ([StarPro Greens](https://starprogreens.com/product/11-x-17-5-hole-pro-backyard-or-indoor-putting-green-kit/)). Leave room behind the ball for your stance and past the last cup for putts that run by. Sizing and cup counts for an outdoor green are in [how big a backyard putting green should be](/guides/backyard-putting-green-size/).

**Headroom.** Under the 2021 International Residential Code, basement living space needs a ceiling at least 7 feet high, and beams and ducts may hang to within 6 feet 4 inches of the finished floor ([IRC R305.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)). A putting stroke fits easily, but a platform takes its own height off that, and a wedge swing may not fit at all. Before you plan to chip, make a slow half swing under the lowest duct. Whether turf holds a chipped ball is covered in [chipping onto an artificial putting green](/guides/chipping-onto-artificial-putting-green/).

**The egress window.** The same code requires every basement to have at least one operable emergency escape and rescue opening ([IRC R310.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)). Keep the platform and fringe clear of it. What your project needs depends on your town's code: towns and counties adopt their own editions, some with amendments, so if the green is part of finishing the basement, ask your town's building department before the work starts. Not legal advice.

**Light.** You read break by the shadows the contours throw:

- Light the green evenly from more than one side, so your own body doesn't shade the line.
- A light low along one side shows contours that a single fixture straight overhead flattens.
- Keep bright fixtures out of your eyeline as you look from the ball to the cup.

## What surface and speed work indoors?

**Skip the sand if you can.** Sand infill tracked off a green ends up on the stairs and carpet. Southwest Greens sells nylon putting surfaces for indoor greens as a "non-infill solution" ([Southwest Greens](https://www.southwestgreens.com/artificial-putting-greens/indoor-putting-greens)), and SYNLawn Golf says nylon greens are mostly fiber with very little sand, while polypropylene greens use a lot of sand that has to be topped up ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/the-nylon-advantage/)). The trade-offs are in [sand-filled vs non-infilled putting greens](/guides/sand-filled-vs-non-infilled-putting-green/) and [nylon vs polyethylene turf](/guides/nylon-vs-polyethylene-turf/).

**Pick a speed for the course you'll play in April.** The USGA says Stimpmeter readings on American golf courses generally range from 7 to 12 feet ([USGA](https://web.archive.org/web/20230411100204/https://www.usga.org/content/dam/usga/pdf/imported/StimpmeterBookletFINAL.pdf)). Makers quote a speed for their surface; StarPro says its nylon turf plays at 10 to 11. The City of Fort Collins says its Collindale course in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) is known for some of the fastest greens in the state ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Venues/Collindale-Golf-Course)), so a Collindale regular will want the indoor green on the quick side.

**Leave a level strip if you want to measure it.** The USGA's Stimpmeter method rolls three balls each way along a level area 10 to 12 feet long, or as short as 6 feet with the half-length notch, and averages the two distances. A short green with break everywhere can't be measured that way. The full method is in [putting green stimp speed](/guides/putting-green-stimp-speed/).

**Ask about fire.** A finished basement is living space. The federal Standard for the Surface Flammability of Carpets and Rugs, 16 CFR 1630, covers synthetic-fiber floor coverings used in homes that are more than 6 feet in one dimension and larger than 24 square feet, mats included ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-1630)). It doesn't name artificial turf. Ask the maker whether the product you are putting indoors was tested to it, and for the report. Not legal advice.

## What changes in a garage, and what changes in a basement?

**In a garage:**

- The floor falls toward the door or a drain, and so does the snowmelt that drips off a car. Put the green at the high end of the bay, away from where the cars sit.
- If a car parks there all winter, a mat that rolls up at night is the practical build. A platform takes the bay for the season.

**In a basement:**

- The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil "Colorado's most significant geologic hazard" ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). A slab on swelling clay can crack or move, so check yours before building over it; artificial turf on a basement or garage floor covers the tests and the prep.
- Leave access to floor drains, a sump pit, cleanouts and any radon fan.
