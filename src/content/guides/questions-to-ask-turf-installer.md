faq:
  - q: "Should I get more than one quote?"
    a: "Yes. Colorado's Attorney General suggests bids from at least three businesses before you sign anything, and the FTC says not to take the lowest bid automatically: ask why one sits far from the others. Our [guide to artificial turf cost](/guides/artificial-turf-cost/) covers what moves a Northern Colorado quote."
  - q: "What should the written estimate spell out?"
    a: "The FTC's list is a description of the work, the materials, a completion date and the price. For turf, name the exact product and attach its spec sheet, and get every agreed extra in writing, as the Synthetic Turf Council advises."
  - q: "A crew knocked on my door after a hailstorm offering turf work. Should I hire them?"
    a: "Slow down. Colorado's Attorney General lists door-to-door selling after a hailstorm among the signs of contractor fraud, and the FTC adds offers of materials left over from another job. Run the same checks you would on anyone else, and don't sign on the doorstep."
  - q: "Can an installer swap in a different turf after I sign?"
    a: "Get it in writing that they won't. The Synthetic Turf Council tells buyers to keep the sample they chose, get a written commitment that it is the product delivered, and check the labels on the rolls, country of origin included, before they go down."
  - q: "What if the installer won't come back to fix something?"
    a: "Put the request in writing. The FTC suggests following calls with a letter sent with a return receipt, and Colorado's Attorney General says to document your attempts to work it out. If that fails and you believe it's fraud, you can file a complaint with the Attorney General's office."

A quote tells you what a turf job costs; these questions tell you who will build it and who answers for it later. They matter most on design-led work: a putting green behind a golf-community home, a custom build with an approved landscape plan, a street where a committee reviews every change. For comparing line items, see our [guide to artificial turf cost](/guides/artificial-turf-cost/).

## Is artificial turf the main thing you build?

Some landscape companies lay turf alongside sod, rock and planting. What decides how a lawn looks in its fifth summer is turf-specific work: a compacted base on Northern Colorado clay, seams that disappear, infill matched to dogs or putting, and on a green, contours that roll true. Ask:

- How many turf jobs did you build last season, and how many were like mine?
- Who on the crew cuts and glues the seams?
- Who draws the turf layout, and how does it fit the rest of the plan: patio and pool edges, lighting, drainage swales, trees?
- If a landscape architect, pool builder or hardscape crew is on the project, where does turf fall in the order of trades, and who protects it once it's down?

A good answer is specific: a count of jobs, a named crew lead, photos of their own seams and base. For how the trades line up, see planning a backyard around turf and turf on a custom home; for greens, designing a backyard putting green.

## Whose crew will be in my yard, and who files the permit and the 811 ticket?

**Crews and subcontractors.** Ask whether the people cutting your seams work for the company or a subcontractor, and who runs the site each day. Subcontracting isn't a warning sign by itself; not knowing who answers for the work is. Colorado's Attorney General says general contractors have a legal responsibility to pay the subcontractors they hire, and counts failing to pay them as contractor fraud ([Stop Fraud Colorado](https://stopfraudcolorado.gov/fraud-center/contractor-fraud/)). In [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), the Town's license classes name subcontractors alongside contractors, so each company on your job should hold one.

**Permits.** Firestone requires a town permit before any turf goes in, with no fee for a single-family home ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). The FTC lists a contractor asking you to get the required building permits among the signs of a home improvement scam ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam)), and the Attorney General lists refusing to pull required local permits. Barefoot Lakes' guidelines put any Town permit on the owner ([St. Vrain Lakes Metropolitan Districts](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)); there, ask the installer to prepare it with you.

**Colorado 811.** Colorado 811 says whoever does the digging submits the locate request in their own or their company's name, and that 811 doesn't mark private lines; locating those is the excavator's job ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging/residential-requests)). Private lines include sprinkler systems, invisible fences and a gas line to the grill ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/find-a-private-locating-company)). A good answer names the company on the ticket and who arranges the private locate. More in [preparing for install day](/guides/preparing-for-turf-installation-day/).

## Which licenses does this job need, and how do I check them?

Not legal advice. Colorado's Attorney General tells homeowners to check local licensing with their city or county, and state plumbing and electrical licenses through the Department of Regulatory Agencies. On a Northern Colorado turf job, these can come up:

| Part of the job | License to ask for | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Any contractor work in Firestone, landscaping, sod and sprinklers included | Town of Firestone contractor license, renewed each calendar year | [Firestone contractor licenses](https://www.firestoneco.gov/98/Contractor-Licenses) |
| Work under a Greeley building permit, from January 1, 2027 | City of Greeley contractor license | Greeley Building Inspection |
| Spraying weed killer for hire | Colorado Department of Agriculture commercial pesticide applicator license | [CDA applicator search](https://ag.colorado.gov/plants/pesticides/pesticide-applicator-search) |
| Installing or removing a backflow preventer | State plumbing license | [State Plumbing Board](https://dpo.colorado.gov/Plumbing) |
| Capping heads and zones past the backflow device | No state plumbing license; the town may still license or permit it | Your town's building or water office |

- **Firestone** won't issue a contractor license without a liability insurance certificate that lists the Town; ask for this year's.
- **Greeley's** new rule is tied to Building Inspection permits, and the city's announcement doesn't mention turf or landscaping ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/news/2026/20260810-contractor-license-required-for-permits-in-greeley-starting-january-1-2027/)). It depends on whether part of your job needs a building permit: ask Greeley Building Inspection. The front-yard change Greeley is drafting would require an installer who is licensed or certified, but as of September 24, 2026 no ordinance had been adopted ([council status report](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9580,plainText=false))).
- **Weed spraying:** CDA says pesticide applications made for hire require a commercial applicator license ([CDA](https://ag.colorado.gov/press-release/hiring-pest-control-for-mountain-pine-beetles-or-other-forest-pests-beware-of)). See [weeds growing through turf](/guides/weeds-growing-through-artificial-turf/).
- **Backflow:** Colorado's plumbing law exempts testing and repairing a backflow device, and work on lawn sprinkler systems past it, but not installing or removing one ([C.R.S. 12-155-118](https://drive.google.com/file/d/0B-K5DhxXxJZbdy1qVnlSa1p4MDA/view?usp=drive_link&resourcekey=0-viNI7fd50n4OeOn-lFkiFA)). A hired plumbing contractor is responsible for the plumbing permit ([Division of Professions and Occupations](https://dpo.colorado.gov/ElectricalPlumbingPermits)). In [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), modifying a sprinkler system with an unapproved backflow device means upgrading the device ([Town of Windsor](https://www.windsorco.gov/m/faq?cat=34)). See [sprinklers after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).

## Can I see a job of yours that has been through a few winters here?

The Synthetic Turf Council tells buyers to visit an installer's jobs, "particularly those that are several years old," and to ask for several recent projects to inspect in person ([STC buying guide](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). Here, age means winters. Fort Collins averages 150 days a year with a low at or below 32°F ([NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)), and NOAA logged hail on 156 separate days in Weld County from 2011 through 2025 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)). Three winters show what a base and its seams really did.

Ask for a job like yours, visited with the owner's permission: a putting green, a wide lawn on an acreage lot, a dog run. On the visit:

- Walk the seams in low sun and the edges at beds, rock and concrete, looking for dips by downspouts and ripples in the open.
- Ask the owner how it drained through spring melt, and whether the installer came back when called.
- On a green, roll a few putts from different sides.

Our [guide to checking what went under the turf](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/) covers what a sound job shows; [visible seams](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/), [lifting edges](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/) and [low spots](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/) explain the failures.

## What documents come with the turf itself?

- **The spec sheet** for the exact product, before you sign. Our [guide to turf spec sheets](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/) lists what it should state. In Firestone the code sets minimums, among them a 1.75 to 3 inch pile and at least 70 ounces of face weight.
- **A PFAS statement.** Since January 1, 2026, Colorado has barred installing artificial turf with intentionally added PFAS ([SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-081)). The statute names no certificate or test ([C.R.S. 25-15-605](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/46185/download)), so ask for the maker's written PFAS statement for that product and the testing behind it. California's toxics agency notes that makers' lab reports often detect only down to tens of parts per million, so lower levels can go undetected ([DTSC](https://web.archive.org/web/20260326043412/https://dtsc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2026/02/TechnicalDocument_PFASs-in-Artificial-Turf_accessible.pdf)). More in [is artificial turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/).
- **The written terms** from the turf's maker and the installer's own terms for workmanship, before you pay. See [what those terms cover](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/).

## Who prepares the HOA or metro district submittal?

Not legal advice. Colorado law keeps an HOA from banning artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home, while leaving it free to set design guidelines ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). Some neighborhoods here send that review to a metro district's committee, and committees ask for real drawings:

- **Heron Lakes**, in [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/), wants written committee approval of a professionally designed landscape plan drawn at 1 inch to 10 feet within 30 days of occupancy, and bars landscape contractors from starting without the builder's certificate of drainage and topographical accuracy ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)).
- **The Reserve at Mariana Butte**, in [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/), keeps turf to back yards and reviews any customized plan before work starts ([landscape guidelines](https://thereserveatmarianabutte.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/HOA-Landscape-Design-Guidelines-2-7-2024.pdf)).
- **New Windsor Metropolitan District** considers back-yard turf only if it is professionally installed, with a product sample submitted alongside the application ([design guidelines](https://www.newwindsormetro.org/files/72102649d/New+Windsor_Brunner+Farm+Design+Guidelines+-+5-16-24+-+Final.pdf)).
- **Thompson Crossing**, in [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/), wants the submittal to show the turf areas and the type of edging ([design guidelines](https://www.johnstownco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1139/Thompson-Crossing-Design-Guidelines-0706--PDF-STAMPED)).

Ask who draws the plan to the committee's scale, who submits it, and who goes back for approval if the layout has to move on site around a root or a swale. Kitchel Lake's rules in [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/), for one, allow no change to an approved plan without the committee's say ([Kitchel Lake rules](https://www.serratogafallsmd1.org/files/6b86004a3/Rules+and+Regs_SFMD1+-+Approved.pdf)). Our [HOA approval guide](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) lists what a packet holds.

## How do I check the company itself?

| Check | Where | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | [Colorado Secretary of State business search](https://www.coloradosos.gov/biz/BusinessEntityCriteriaExt.do) | The name on your contract, registered, with its filings in "Good Standing" |
| Complaints | [BBB Serving Northern Colorado and Wyoming](https://www.bbb.org/local-bbb/bbb-serving-northern-colorado-and-wyoming) | Complaints filed against the company |
| Insurance | A certificate of insurance from the company | Workers' compensation and general liability, including completed operations |
| Licenses | The table above | A current license for each part of the job that needs one |

Good Standing means less than it sounds: the Secretary of State says it only shows statutory filings are met, and that its records can't tell you whether a business is reputable ([Secretary of State FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/business/FAQs/certGoodStanding.html)). The insurance row is the Synthetic Turf Council's; Colorado's permit office warns that homeowners insurance may not cover damage caused by someone without liability insurance.

Last, ask how subcontractors and material suppliers get paid, and whether the company will hand you lien waivers from each with the final invoice. Not legal advice: for what a waiver covers in Colorado, ask a Colorado attorney.
