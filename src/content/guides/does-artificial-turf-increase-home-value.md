  - q: "Should I put in turf right before I list the house?"
    a: "Only if there is time to do it properly. In an HOA, architectural approval comes before the work starts, and in Firestone so does a town permit; neither can be skipped to meet a listing date. New turf also has no history to show a buyer, so file the invoice and the product sheet with the house papers the day it goes in."
  - q: "Does front-yard turf hurt curb appeal?"
    a: "Nobody has measured that either. Realtors clearly think curb appeal matters: 97% of those in the National Association of Realtors' 2023 outdoor survey called it important in attracting a buyer. In Firestone, Frederick and Wellington's new subdivisions, the front yard is also where the town code asks for live plants, so front-yard turf is the part a buyer is most likely to question."
  - q: "Will the buyer's home inspection check the turf?"
    a: "Not in any depth. Under InterNACHI's standards, an inspection won't find concealed defects, such as a thin base under the turf, and the inspector isn't required to date an installation, judge its service life, check code compliance or inspect sprinkler systems. A buyer who wants to know what is underneath has to ask for the records or pay for a separate look."
  - q: "Is turf a selling point for a buyer with dogs?"
    a: "It can be, if the dog area doesn't smell on a warm afternoon and drains after a rinse. Clear any odor before the first showing, and be ready to say what infill is down and how the area has been cleaned."
  - q: "What if my buyer wants a real lawn instead?"
    a: "Then the buyer is pricing removal, soil preparation, sod or seed, and sprinklers that work. Tell them whether the zones under the turf were capped, rerouted or pulled out, and leave any sprinkler map you have; that decides how much of the old system can come back."

No one has published a Northern Colorado figure for what artificial turf adds to a sale price or takes off it. What a buyer reacts to is the particular yard: whether the turf was allowed where it sits, what shape it is in, whether the records exist, and what water costs at that address. This page goes through those checks, so a seller can have answers ready and a buyer knows what to ask.

## Does artificial turf raise or lower the sale price here?

Nobody can give you an honest percentage. We found no study of Northern Colorado sales that separates turf from everything else about a house, and the national estimates leave it out. The National Association of Realtors' [2023 report on outdoor projects](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/documents/2023-03-remodeling-impact-outdoor-features-03-17-2023.pdf) estimates how much of the cost Realtors think a seller gets back on 11 projects, from standard lawn care to an in-ground pool. Artificial turf is not one of them.

That leaves judgment, one yard at a time. To a buyer, turf reads one of two ways:

- **A finished, low-water yard,** when it is allowed where it sits, looks and drains well, has years left in it, and comes with records.
- **A project,** when it breaks a town or HOA rule, is worn or smells, or the buyer wants a lawn and starts pricing the swap back to grass.

The rest of this page is how a buyer tells which one they are looking at.

## Is the turf allowed where it sits?

Settle this first. Turf that breaks a rule does not go away at closing; it becomes the new owner's to fix.

- **An HOA, behind a detached house:** Colorado law bars an HOA's landscaping rules from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard, though the association can still apply its design guidelines ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). Front yards get no such protection, and attached homes get less. Not legal advice; our [guide to HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) has the details.
- **Greeley:** the development code accepts turf only in areas not visible from public rights-of-way and streets ([Sec. 24-802](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT24DECO_CH8LAST_S24-802LADE)), and city staff sum up today's rule as no turf in the front yards of houses ([Planning Commission packet](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9205,plainText=false))). A draft change would allow front-yard turf with a permit, but as of September 24, 2026 the city had not adopted it ([council status report](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9580,plainText=false))). If a Greeley house has turf you can see from the street, it depends on when and under which rule it went in: ask Greeley Planning before closing.
- **Wellington:** new single-family subdivision lots need at least 75% live materials between the front of the house and the curb ([Sec. 15-5-40](https://library.municode.com/co/wellington/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH15LAUSCO_ART5DEST_S15-5-40LASC)).
- **Firestone:** a town permit comes before any turf goes in, and live plants must cover 75% of the front yard and 50% of the combined side and rear yards, with turf counting toward neither ([Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). A Firestone buyer should ask whether the permit was pulled.
- **Frederick:** the 2026 Land Use Code requires at least 50% living plant coverage in every landscaped area, names the front yards of houses among the areas to landscape, and permits functional turf only where its definition allows ([2026 Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)). How that reaches an existing backyard is not spelled out: ask Frederick Planning.

Every other town's rule is in our [guide to artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## What records should go with the house?

The base is buried and the install date is not printed on the turf, so records are how a buyer learns what is underneath and how old it is. A seller who kept these has an easier conversation:

| Record | What it tells a buyer | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice with the install date and the installer's name | How old the turf is, and who put it in | The seller's files |
| Product specification sheet | The product, pile height, face weight, backing and infill | The installer or the manufacturer |
| PFAS documentation, for turf installed since January 1, 2026 | That the product meets Colorado's ban on intentionally added PFAS ([SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-081)) | The manufacturer |
| Written HOA approval | That the association signed off on the location and the design | The HOA's architectural committee |
| Town permit, where one applies | That the town signed off; Firestone requires one | The town |
| Photos of the dig and the base | How deep the excavation went and what base went in | The seller or the installer |
| Written repair terms | What is still covered, and whether it passes to a new owner | The installer or the manufacturer |

What a spec sheet should say is in our [guide to turf spec sheets for Colorado](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/). A buyer with no base photos can use our [guide to checking that the base went in](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/), and our [guide to what installer and manufacturer coverage includes](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/) explains the repair terms. Without records, the buyer judges the turf by eye and prices in the doubt.

## How old is the turf, and does it show?

Walk it the way a buyer will, on a warm afternoon after a few dry days:

- **Seams and edges:** seam lines you can see from the patio, or edges lifting where turf meets rock or concrete. See [visible seams](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/) and [lifting edges](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/).
- **The surface:** ripples, low spots, and flattened lanes where people and dogs walk. See [wrinkles and ripples](/guides/artificial-turf-wrinkles-ripples/), [sinking and low spots](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/) and [matted turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/).
- **Sun and heat:** melted streaks near windows and a faded side of the yard. See [melting from window reflection](/guides/artificial-turf-melting-from-windows/) and [fading in the Colorado sun](/guides/does-artificial-turf-fade/).
- **The dog area:** any smell once the turf warms up. See [getting dog urine smell out of turf](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/).

Then set the turf's age against a realistic life under Northern Colorado's altitude sun, hail and freeze-thaw, which our [guide to how long artificial turf lasts](/guides/how-long-does-artificial-turf-last/) covers. Turf near the end of its life is a replacement a buyer may subtract from an offer. If that is your yard, our [guide to repairing or replacing worn turf](/guides/when-to-replace-artificial-turf/) helps decide which to do before listing.

## How do water bills figure in?

A yard that needs no summer watering shows up on the bill, and Greeley and Windsor buyers have particular reasons to look at it.

- **Greeley** bills single-family homes against a water budget. The indoor part comes from household size, at 45 gallons a day per person; the outdoor part is sized to the yard's irrigable square footage, and the city asks customers to report changes to it ([Greeley Water](https://greeleyco.gov/water-and-sewer/save-water/budget-your-water/)). In 2026, water within budget costs $6.18 per 1,000 gallons, and above 151% of budget it costs $13.01 ([2026 rate manual](https://cogy-p-001.sitecorecontenthub.cloud/api/public/content/131d971ab9564764b86d4ab2b50679d7?v=2d52460e)). The city's page does not say whether turf counts as irrigable area, so a buyer should ask Greeley Water what area is on file for the address: it sets the outdoor budget the new owner starts with.
- **Windsor's** 2026 water, sewer and stormwater rate change raised the total monthly bill for an average single-family home by about 26% ([Town of Windsor](https://www.windsorco.gov/1440/2026-Water-Sewer-and-Stormwater-Rate-Inc)). Outdoor watering is the part of that bill a yard controls.

What a bluegrass lawn uses, in gallons and in dollars at each Northern Colorado provider's 2026 rates, is in our [guide to water savings from artificial turf](/guides/water-savings/).

## What does Colorado's seller disclosure form say about turf?

Nothing by name. Colorado's standard Seller's Property Disclosure (SPD19, in mandatory use since January 1, 2026) has no turf line, but it asks about violations of covenants or association rules, improvements built in the past year without association approval, building code, city or county violations, and landscaping requirements, and it says a seller who knows of an adverse material fact must disclose it whether or not the form has a line for it ([Colorado Real Estate Commission](https://dre.colorado.gov/sites/dre/files/documents/Seller%27s%20Property%20Disclosure%20%28Residential%29%20%28fillable%29_for%20use%20on%20or%20after%20January%201%2C%202026_0.pdf)). Not legal advice: ask your agent or a Colorado attorney how it applies to your yard.

In Northern Colorado, those lines are where a turf problem would surface: front-yard turf on a Greeley house, a Firestone install with no permit, or turf an HOA never approved.
