Every green is priced on its own yard. This is where the money goes in a backyard green, so two quotes can be read line by line; lawn turf has its own [guide to artificial turf cost](/guides/artificial-turf-cost/). Motz, an infill maker, says a professional-level green generally costs 20 to 50% more than one made for leisure, mostly because of the type and amount of material, and that extra holes, bunkers, sand traps, lighting and contours add more ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/the-best-artificial-grass-for-putting-greens/)).

## Does a bigger green cost more, or less per square foot?

More in total, less for each square foot. Southwest Greens, a putting green builder, says its residential greens are usually around 500 square feet ([FAQs](https://www.southwestgreens.com/about-us/faqs)), and its published size bands charge more per square foot below 400 square feet than at 2,000 and up ([Southwest Greens](https://www.southwestgreens.com/artificial-putting-greens/backyard-putting-greens/cost-of-a-backyard-putting-green)).

Each cup is its own small job. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer columnist says cups set in compacted dirt shift as rain, foot traffic and pulling the flag loosen the soil, so he sets them in concrete, and in performance concrete where frost heave is high ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-to-make-your-backyard-putting-green-pga-level-quality/)). Frost is a given here: in an average year Fort Collins gets 150 nights with a low of 32°F or colder (NOAA, 1991–2020 normals). How many cups your practice needs, and how long the green has to be, is in our [guide to putting green size](/guides/backyard-putting-green-size/).

## Why does the base under a green take more work than a lawn's?

Because the roll of the ball depends on it. The Colorado Geological Survey says expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). A lawn can hide a small rise in the ground. A putt finds it.

SGW's columnist recommends at least 6 inches of compacted 3/4-inch rock under a backyard green, because runoff from around the green seeps underneath and settles it, then a layer of 3/8-inch minus on top to shape the undulations to the golfer's design. SYNLawn's do-it-yourself guide for lawn turf has you dig out 3 to 4 inches ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)). Shaping break by hand into that top layer is work a lawn never gets.

What that difference means for 500 square feet, worked from published rules of thumb rather than a quote:

| For 500 square feet | Lawn turf, 3-inch base | Putting green, 6-inch base |
|---|---|---|
| Base rock | about 5 tons | about 10 tons |
| Wheelbarrow loads, if no machine fits | 65 to 80 | 130 to 160 |
| Soil and sod dug out, measured in the ground | about 4.6 cubic yards | about 9.3 cubic yards |
| That soil at Larimer County's rubble rate | about $185, plus the state surcharge | about $370, plus the state surcharge |
| Infill | 500 to 1,000 pounds | 1,000 to 2,000 pounds recreational; 2,500 to 3,500 pounds professional-style |

Inputs: SYNLawn's 1 ton of rock per 100 square feet at 3 inches, 13 to 16 barrow loads a ton and its lawn infill rate; Motz's 2 to 4 pounds of infill per square foot for a recreational green and 5 to 7 for a professional-style one.

What goes under turf on clay is in our guides to [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/) and [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/). How much tilt a green can carry is in [putting green on a slope](/guides/putting-green-on-a-slope/).

## Which surface, fringe and extras add the most?

- **The putting surface.** Motz says the type of turf is often the biggest cost difference after the level of detail, and that nylon, which resists abrasion better than polypropylene, costs more. A professional-style, sand-filled green also takes more infill (see the table) and more install work: SGW's columnist says its all-infill True Putt, which holds a shot from 100 yards, is more complex to install. Our [comparison of sand-filled and non-infilled greens](/guides/sand-filled-vs-non-infilled-putting-green/) weighs the two.
- **Fringe and rough.** Tour Greens, a putting green maker, says many owners add several cuts of fringe and fairway turf so they can practice every kind of shot ([Tour Greens](https://www.tourgreens.com/backyard-putting-green-cost.html)).
- **A chipping area.** A green that has to hold chips asks more of the surface; see [chipping onto an artificial putting green](/guides/chipping-onto-artificial-putting-green/).
- **Bunkers, creeks and lights.** Tour Greens says features such as bunkers, tee boxes, dry creeks and water hazards may not change what the green costs but do change what the project costs. Lighting and layout ideas are in our guides to [putting green lighting](/guides/putting-green-lighting/) and [putting green design](/guides/putting-green-design-ideas/).

## How do access and haul-off change the bill?

Tour Greens says costs rise when machines can't reach the space and the crew works with wheelbarrows and small equipment, and that significant grading, retaining walls or other landscaping to prepare and finish the ground have a major effect on price. In the table, a green with no machine access means 130 to 160 barrow loads of rock going in, with the dug soil going out the same way.

Then the soil has to go somewhere. Larimer County's 2026 fee schedule, which covers its landfills and transfer station, prices dirt and sod as rubble at $40 a cubic yard plus the state surcharge ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/solidwaste/fees)). Other routes for an old lawn are in our guide to [turf over grass or dirt](/guides/can-you-put-artificial-turf-over-grass-or-dirt/).

Golf communities add their own site rules. Heron Lakes in Berthoud makes landscape contractors reach a lot from the front only, bars dumping excavated soil or materials in the street or on a neighbor's lot, and allows no outside construction during the week of the golf tournament, normally held in July ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)). Those rules shape where rock is staged and when the dig can happen.

## What do Firestone's permit and HOA review add?

Mostly time, plus a few fees:

- **Firestone.** A town permit comes first, with no fee for a single-family home. Turf may cover up to 75% of a rear or side yard, but 50% of the combined side and rear yards must be live plants, and turf can't count toward that ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). The code sets one pile height for all turf, 1.75 to 3 inches, and says nothing about putting greens, while Motz puts a backyard putting surface at 3/8 to 5/8 inch. It depends how the town treats a green; before the design is final, ask Firestone Planning, including whether the code's alternative-compliance route, an equivalent design the Director approves in writing, covers one ([16.6.1.C](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.1GEPR)).
- **Barefoot Lakes, Firestone.** The metro district's pre-approved turf list names 15 lawn products and no putting surface, so a green rests on the review committee granting a variance, and the committee has 60 days to rule on a complete request ([guidelines](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)).
- **Heron Lakes, Berthoud.** The design rules strongly encourage putting greens on the Peninsula lots facing McNeil Reservoir. The district charges $250 for a landscaping review and $150 for a second one, and the committee answers in writing within 20 working days ([fee schedule](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/BHMD-Heron-Lakes-Fee-and-Fine-Schedule-04-20-2026-tjlm.pdf)).
- **Windsor.** New Windsor's design review committee charges a $50 submittal fee and answers within 14 days ([guidelines](https://www.newwindsormetro.org/files/72102649d/New+Windsor_Brunner+Farm+Design+Guidelines+-+5-16-24+-+Final.pdf)). Ptarmigan West allows turf in rear yards only, up to 25% of the rear yard, which caps a green and its fringe together ([design guidelines](https://pwmd1.specialdistrict.org/files/3deb93af5/03+PWMD+Rules+and+Regulations+-+Design+Guidelines.pdf)).

**Not legal advice:** Colorado law bars an HOA's rules from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard of a detached home, and makes a special district rule that does so unenforceable, though design rules still apply ([SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). What an approval packet holds is in our [guide to HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/), and each town's turf rules are on its page: [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/).

## Is a kit a cheaper way in, and what does a green take to keep up?

A kit skips the base, which is both its saving and its limit. Tour Greens sells DIY kits on interlocking base panels, from about 200 to 500 square feet, and SYNLawn Golf's GreenMaker kits sit on foam panels on any flat surface ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/products/)). In a Northern Colorado backyard, a flat surface means a patio, a slab or a base someone builds first. See [DIY or professional installation](/guides/diy-artificial-turf-vs-professional/) and the [indoor putting green guide](/guides/indoor-putting-green-for-home/).

Upkeep follows the surface you chose. Motz says a recreational green's crimped fibers need no routine rolling, while a professional-level green needs more grooming, brushing and leveling of its infill over time. The season-by-season routine is in our [putting green maintenance guide](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/).

## What should a putting green quote spell out?

- The dig depth, the rock sizes, how the base is compacted, and the layer where break is shaped.
- How each cup is set.
- The putting surface, the fringe and any rough, each named, with its spec sheet.
- The infill, by type and pounds per square foot.
- Access: machine or wheelbarrow, and where rock is staged.
- Soil and sod haul-off: how much, where it goes, and whether the dump fee is in the price.
- Who prepares the HOA or metro district submittal and the Firestone permit.
- What the first spring's brushing and sand top-up involve.
