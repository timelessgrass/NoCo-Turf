answer:
  question: "How do you put artificial turf strips between pavers or flagstone?"
  answer: "Set the stones first on a compacted, sloped base. Fill each joint with compacted turf base to within half an inch of the stone's top, lay large pieces of turf over the stones and cut them out rather than piecing strips, and fasten every edge. On Northern Colorado clay, drain the joints, or frost lifts stone and turf unevenly."
faq:
  - q: "Can I pull the sand out of my paver patio and put turf in the joints?"
    a: "Not in the joints it has now. CMHA lays pavers with joints of 1/16 to 3/16 inch, packed with sand so the whole surface locks together, which is far too narrow for turf. A turf-joint design means lifting the pavers and relaying them with wide gaps on a base built for it, so plan it as a new patio."
  - q: "Can a driveway have turf strips between concrete slabs?"
    a: "Carefully. CMHA rates slabs on a gravel base and bedding sand for pedestrians or light automobile traffic, wants a concrete or asphalt base under heavier traffic, and, in permeable paving with wide joints, keeps slabs 16 by 16 inches and larger to foot traffic because under vehicles they risk tipping and cracking. Ask what the slabs will sit on and where the tires will run."
  - q: "My flagstone is mortared onto a concrete slab. Can turf go in the joints?"
    a: "Only as turf over concrete. SGW's article glues turf to hardscape such as concrete instead of nailing it, and has a concrete area graded or drilled so water can get out. CMHA adds that mortar under slabs in a freezing climate needs a latex or epoxy additive, because de-icers damage plain mortar. [Artificial turf over concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-over-concrete/) covers the slab side."
  - q: "What should I check after the first winter?"
    a: "Walk it in spring. Look for a stone standing proud of its neighbors or of the turf, a turf edge that has ridden up onto a stone, low spots in the joints, and nail heads backing out. PolyTurf's manual says nails should be checked routinely for working out from traffic or earth movement and driven back in. Lifted edges are covered in [why turf edges lift](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/)."
  - q: "How wide should the turf joints be?"
    a: "No turf maker or trade group we read sets a width. The limits are practical: the base in the joint has to be compacted and both turf edges fastened, and a very wide joint stops reading as a joint. Ask the installer for the narrowest joint they will build, and to show you one of theirs after a winter."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** Turf joints between pavers or slabs, stepping stones set in turf, and a paver walk with turf beside it all put two materials that answer winter differently side by side on the same clay. Whether to pave or turf a space at all is in [turf, pavers or concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-pavers-concrete/).

## Which layouts combine turf with pavers or flagstone?

| Layout | What holds each stone | Where the turf is fastened | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turf joints between slabs or pavers | Its own bedding; no interlock with its neighbors | Both sides of every joint | Flat patios that hold water |
| Stepping stones in turf | Its own bedding | Around each stone | A stone that rocks or rises after winter |
| Flagstone with turf joints | Bedding under each flag, or mortar on a slab | Along each outline | Mortar on a slab puts turf on concrete |
| Paver walk beside turf | An edge restraint | Along the walk | The restraint; see [turf edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/) |

SYNLawn, a turf maker, recommends stepping stones or pavers on high-traffic paths so the turf doesn't mat down ([SYNLawn DIY guidelines](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)). For the whole-yard plan, see [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/) and [outdoor living with artificial turf](/guides/outdoor-living-with-artificial-turf/).

## Why do the stones and the turf need one base at one grade?

Because a turf joint takes the interlock out of the paving, and turf settles wherever its base is thinner or looser than the stone's.

The Concrete Masonry & Hardscapes Association (CMHA), the paver industry's trade group, sets pavers on a nominal inch of bedding sand, with sand-packed joints of 1/16 to 3/16 inch, over a base sloped at least 1.5% ([CMHA PAV-TEC-002](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-002/)). Open those joints for turf and each stone stands alone, so CMHA's guide for slabs, which don't interlock either, asks for a flatter, denser base: within 1/4 inch over a 10-foot straightedge and at least 98% of standard Proctor density ([CMHA PAV-TEC-025](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-025/)).

Turf asks something different of the same ground: a base that drains. The Synthetic Turf Council says a base that must stay both stable and permeable may not reach 95% Proctor. In practice:

- **One base across the whole area,** sloped so water leaves. CMHA's patio minimum is 4 inches compacted on well-drained soil, thicker on wet or weak soils in cold climates.
- **Stones first, then joints.** Synthetic Grass Warehouse (SGW), a turf distributor, says in its installer column that the fill in each joint must finish no more than 1/2 inch below the hardscape and be compacted to at least 80%, or the ribbons settle ([SGW Ask JW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/installing-artificial-grass-between-pavers/)). Its article says narrow gaps are harder to compact, suggests a stamp compactor or a block of wood and a hammer, and starts with a hose test for puddles ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/critical-steps-for-installing-artificial-turf-between-pavers/)).
- **Nothing hidden under the stones.** SGW's column notes that stone setters often bed hardscape on concrete or pour a concrete edge, leaving no depth for base and nothing to nail into.

In [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), the code requires a compacted, porous aggregate base at least 3 inches deep under turf, never compacted dirt ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)); an inch of fill over native clay in each joint doesn't meet it. More in [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).

## What does a Northern Colorado winter do to stones and turf side by side?

It works on the joints, where the water goes. Stone sheds rain and snowmelt; turf passes it down, and SGW says its hole-punched landscape turfs drain at over 30 inches per hour per square yard. Frost heave needs fine-grained soil, freezing that reaches into it, and water, all at once ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)). A joint that funnels water down to clay supplies all three.

- The Fort Collins NOAA station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F, and on about 135 of those days the air climbs back above freezing (1991–2020 normals).
- Chinook winds can lift plains temperatures 25 to 35°F in a short time ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)), thawing joints in midwinter.
- Larimer County designs footings for frost 30 inches down, and Weld County uses the same 30-inch frost line. Those are design values, not a measured freeze, but a patio base sits far above them.
- The Colorado Geological Survey says expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)).

A slab rises as one rigid piece and tips; turf flexes with its base. When it goes wrong, a stone stands proud of the turf, or a turf edge rides up over a stone. CMHA says heaves in paving are typically frost heave, and that stones can be lifted, the base corrected and the same stones relaid ([CMHA PAV-TEC-006](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/)).

Drainage keeps it flat. Ground thaws from the top down, and the frozen layer traps meltwater so it can only move sideways, so the whole area needs fall, the joint base needs a way out at a lower edge, and no spray heads or drip emitters should wet the joints. SGW's column warns that ribbons in flat or level hardscape with little or no positive drainage can flood. See [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).

## How do you shovel a walk with turf joints?

With a plastic shovel, and without rock salt. CMHA says solid pavers take shovels, blowers or plows, pushed on a diagonal across the joints. SYNLawn says turf takes a plastic shovel or a broom and warns that metal tools damage the fibers ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/articles/how-to-maintain-artificial-grass/)). A turf-joint walk gets the gentler tool. Level stones and flat, well-fastened turf edges give a blade nothing to catch; a heaved stone or a lifted edge does, and CMHA wants any paver standing more than 1/8 inch above its neighbor fixed before winter.

Ice melt is harder, because whatever goes on the stones ends up in the turf:

- SYNLawn says rock salt and chemical de-icers can degrade turf fibers and backing.
- SGW advises against salt on turf because it can clog the backing's holes and slow drainage ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-to-care-for-artificial-grass-in-snowy-weather/)).
- CMHA's order for pavers is sand for traction first, de-icer only as needed, and no salty snow stored on them.

So make the route you clear after every storm solid stone, and keep turf joints to patios and garden paths you can leave to the sun. Clearing turf is in [snow removal on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

## How are narrow strips cut and fastened?

Usually not as strips. SGW's column says turf between concrete, pavers, travertine or flagstone brings fewer callbacks when large sections are laid over the stones and cut out, though that wastes more turf. Its sequence:

1. **Plan the joints with the stone setter** to fit the roll; the column suggests ribbon layouts in 15-foot increments, and ShawGrass notes turf comes in 12- or 15-foot widths ([ShawGrass guidelines](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf)).
2. **Keep one grain direction,** fibers facing the main view. Shaw says opposing grain can leave noticeable seams.
3. **Let the turf relax** for at least an hour, then nail the middle of each joint about a foot apart.
4. **Cut along each stone's edge,** flagstone outlines included, and lift out the turf over it.
5. **Fasten every edge.** Ribbon edges take many nails, with small heads that hide in the fibers; types and spacing are in [nails vs staples](/guides/artificial-turf-nails-vs-staples/).
6. **Infill slowly, by hand,** with a smaller infill, and brush with a stiff hand brush. Heavy brushing lifts ribbon edges and pulls out edge nails.

Firestone requires turf anchored at all edges and seams, with seams taped or glued, and rules out seam separations and uplifted surfaces; a joint layout is almost all edges and seams. See [visible seams in artificial turf](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).

## Who has to approve a stone-and-turf design?

Often an HOA or metro district, and in Firestone, the town. **Not legal advice.** Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard of a detached home but lets it adopt design and aesthetic rules ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5, from SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)), and paving gets its own review:

- **Heron Lakes, [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/):** the architectural committee must approve any paving, flagstones and stepping stones included ([rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)).
- **Oakridge Village II, [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/):** a front-yard flagstone or hardscape courtyard is limited to 10 by 10 feet, or 100 square feet, in earth tones ([landscape policy](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/dc06bd71-eac3-4b0f-81df-e42efbfcd762/downloads/f1ab5b9c-f88f-4b7b-8052-d1e17109c87b/Oakridge%20Village%202%20HOA%20Landscape%20Policy%20revise.pdf?ver=1788372083923)).
- **Firestone:** turf is capped at 75% of a single-family rear or side yard, and the cap must leave at least 25% of the yard permeable, living landscape after patios and walkways. Stone with turf joints is patio and turf at once, and neither is living. It depends how the town measures such a patio, so ask Firestone Planning.

Put the joint layout, base section and drainage in the packet; see [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) and [turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## What should the stone setter and the turf installer agree on first?

- **Who builds the base under the joints,** and whether it is the stones' base.
- **What the stones sit on:** bedding sand, or concrete with nothing to nail into.
- **Finished heights:** joint base no more than half an inch below level stones.
- **Which edge the joint water leaves by.**
- **How each turf edge is held.**

Done well: straight joints, stones that don't rock, no nail heads showing, no puddles after a hose test. More in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/); the whole job is on our [artificial turf installation page](/services/artificial-turf-installation/).
