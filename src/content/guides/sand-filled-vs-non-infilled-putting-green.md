Two quotes for the same backyard green can describe different surfaces: a sand-filled green, with taller fibers packed with sand, or one sold as non-infilled, which usually means a short, crimped (texturized) green carrying a light layer of sand. They play differently, and they age differently.

## What is the difference between a sand-filled and a non-infilled green?

How much of the surface is fiber and how much is sand. Motz, which makes a coated-sand infill, draws the line this way: a texturized green is about half an inch tall and takes about 1.5 to 2.5 pounds of infill per square foot, while a sand-filled green is 1 to 1 1/4 inches tall and takes as much as 4 to 6 pounds of raw sand per square foot, topped with coated sand ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/how-many-pounds-of-infill-do-you-need-per-square-foot-of-turf/)).

"Non-infilled" is rarely sand-free. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer column says nylon and textured polyethylene greens still need infill placed "from the bottom to the top of the finished surface" ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/perfecting-putting-green-surfaces-part-2-infill-placement/)). SYNLawn Golf, which sells nylon greens, describes its own as "mostly fiber with very little use of sand" ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/the-nylon-advantage/)). Go by the pounds-per-square-foot line on the spec sheet, not the label on the quote.

| Pile height | About 1/2 inch: [Synthetic Grass Warehouse Nylon Putt 2-Tone](https://sgw-media.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets/spec-sheets/nylon-putt-2tone-ss.pdf), about 0.50 inches; [SYNLawn Golf Classic Putt](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf-products/classic-putt/), 1/2 inch | 1 to 1 1/4 inches (Motz); [Synthetic Grass Warehouse True Putt](https://sgw-media.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets/spec-sheets/true-putt-ss.pdf), about 1.00 inch |

## Which one rolls more consistently, and which lets you set the speed?

Texturized is steadier; sand-filled is adjustable. Rival sellers roughly agree on that.

- **Texturized:** the speed lives in the fiber. Motz says a low-pile or texturized nylon product "will provide consistency but, not as much speed or flexibility" ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/small-infill-big-impact-for-putting-greens/)), and that on a recreational green "you can't adjust the turf fiber" ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/the-best-artificial-grass-for-putting-greens/)).
- **Sand-filled:** the speed lives in the sand and how it is rolled. Motz says a longer-pile polypropylene green lets you "adjust the speed of the surface." Synthetic Grass Warehouse's column recommends rolling once the sand is in, because "this compression will create equal Stimpmeter speeds."

The flip side of adjustable is drift. When the sand moves, the speed moves with it, and SYNLawn Golf, selling the alternative, calls a mostly sand green "very difficult to maintain consistent performance." What speed to aim for, and how to measure it, is in [putting green stimp speed](/guides/putting-green-stimp-speed/).

## Which surface holds a chip shot?

Both can, if something under the surface soaks up the landing. Each seller says its own system does it better:

- Motz says a sand-filled green can take chips from "up to 50 or even 100 yards," while a texturized green has "less sand and less cushion layer for the ball to grab," so a ball chipped from too far "bounces off of the green."
- SYNLawn Golf says approach shots "will hold" on a polypropylene sand green but "the ball dies on impact," and that chips to its nylon greens "check and roll much closer" to a natural green. Its Classic Putt, it says, holds shots from inside 150 yards over its TrueStop underlayment, a layer laid under the turf.

On a sand-filled green the depth of sand catches the ball; on those nylon greens, the underlayment does. If chipping matters, ask what sits under the surface and hit a few shots onto a finished green first. Where to chip from, the fringe and the landing area are in [chipping onto an artificial putting green](/guides/chipping-onto-artificial-putting-green/).

## How much upkeep does each one need?

More for sand-filled, says an infill maker that sells for both.

- **Sand-filled:** Motz says the turf is "rolled extensively" at installation and then needs "more surface grooming, brushing, and leveling out of the infill layers over time." SYNLawn Golf adds that polypropylene sand greens must be replenished and that "the longer a polypropylene green is in use the harder the sand will become." FieldTurf Landscape says a home green's infill amount and condition "directly impact how the ball rolls," so it gets brushed and topped up to hold its speed ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/maintenance/artificial-turf-maintenance-guide/)).
- **Texturized:** Motz says there is "no routine maintenance to roll the turf." It still carries sand, and Synthetic Grass Warehouse's column says those infill levels "must be maintained" or the green can move and wrinkle ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/infill-maintenance/)).

Both need debris kept off. The season-by-season routine is in [backyard putting green care](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/).

## What do Northern Colorado storms, wind and snow do to the sand?

They move it, and a sand-filled green has more to lose. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's column says "rain, snow, sleet, and hail can deplete infill levels on all types of synthetic turf," and recommends replenishing and rolling greens in spring after the snow. Here that means:

- **Downpours.** The Colorado Climate Center says localized rains of more than 4 inches sometimes fall on the plains "in just a few hours" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)), and FieldTurf Landscape says infill can "wash out from heavy rains." Most putting green turf has no drainage holes, so storm water runs off the surface, and a completely flat green can trap it against the fringe ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/why-dont-putting-greens-have-drainage-holes/)).
- **Hail.** NOAA logged 253 hail reports in Larimer County and 536 in Weld County from 2011 through 2025 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)). What hail does to the turf itself is in [hail and artificial turf](/guides/does-hail-damage-artificial-turf/).
- **Wind.** The Climate Center describes "periodic, severe turbulent winds" near the mountains, and NOAA logged 48 High Wind events from 2011 through 2025 in the forecast zone for Larimer County below 6,000 feet and northwest Weld County, 44 of them between October and April. Turf collects "wind-blown dirt and debris that must somehow be removed," CSU Extension notes ([CSU Extension](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1537-artificial-turf-lawns/)), and FieldTurf Landscape warns that a blower's "high air pressure can displace or remove the infill." On a sand-filled green, the cleanup is where sand gets lost.
- **Snow.** NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year (1991–2020 normals, [NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). Clearing it is covered in [snow removal on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

A texturized green loses sand to the same weather, but it holds roughly 2 to 3 pounds per square foot rather than 4 to 7, so less goes back in each spring.

## Can you put either kind of green in Firestone?

Not legal advice. Firestone requires a town permit before any artificial turf goes in (no fee for single-family homes), and says turf areas "shall comply with all requirements" of its development code, including:

- blades only of lead-free polyethylene, polypropylene, a vegetable-based product or a blend: "Nylon or other plastic shall be prohibited";
- a permeable backing with holes no more than 4 by 6 inches apart;
- a tan thatch layer, a pile height of 1.75 to 3 inches and a face weight of at least 70 ounces;
- infill of "a non-toxic anti-microbial material such as silica sand or acrylic coated sand," and no crumb rubber ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)).

The code never mentions putting greens. It defines artificial turf as "a manufactured substitute for organic turf, lawn, or sod" that simulates "a well-maintained lawn" ([Firestone Development Code 16.11.3](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH11DE_16.11.3TEDE)). Read literally, the standard rules out far more than nylon: every green in the table above is 1/2 to about 1 inch tall with a 34- to 36-ounce face weight and no thatch listed, and most green turf has no drainage holes. The infill sentence names silica sand and acrylic coated sand as examples; it doesn't say how much sand a turf must carry.

So in [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) the real question is whether the turf standard reaches a backyard green at all. It depends on how the town reads its code: ask Firestone Planning before you order a surface, and keep its answer with your permit. How much of a Firestone yard can be turf is in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## What should you ask an installer before you choose?

- **Which system is it, by the numbers?** A good answer reads pile height, fiber, face weight and pounds of sand per square foot off the product's spec sheet.
- **What speed will it run, and can that change?** Expect a stimpmeter figure and, for sand-filled, how sand and rolling set it.
- **Will it hold a chip from where I want to hit?** A good answer names what sits under the surface, sand or an underlayment, and offers a green you can hit onto.
- **What does it need after a winter here?** How much sand goes back each spring, whether it is re-rolled, and who does it.
- **Where does storm water go?** The sheet's backing line says whether it drains through; if not, the base has to shed water off the surface.
- **In Firestone, what did Planning say?** About greens under the turf standard, and the permit.
