A spec sheet turns a turf product's sales claims into numbers you can compare. Below are the lines a sheet should carry, the benchmarks from the Synthetic Turf Council's buying guidelines and Firestone's town code, and where each line is explained. Products named are published examples, not recommendations.

## Which lines should a turf spec sheet carry?

Each measured line should carry its test method beside the value, as SYNLawn's published sheet for one lawn product does: pile height under ASTM D-5823, weights under D-5848, tuft bind under D-1335, flammability under D-2859 ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SYNLawn-SYNAugustine-847-S847B-USA.pdf)). A value with no method can't be compared with another maker's.

| Line | What a good sheet shows | Benchmark | Explained in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | Maker, product name, SKU and a revision date | The exact product, not a product family | — |
| Pile height | Inches, with the method | STC: most lawn turf runs 1½ to 2 in. Firestone: 1.75 to 3 in | [pile height](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/) |
| Face weight | Ounces per square yard above the backing, apart from total weight | STC: 40 to 100 oz is typical. Firestone: at least 70 | [face weight](/guides/artificial-turf-face-weight/) |
| Yarn | The polymer for blades and thatch, and the denier | STC: 8,000 total denier per bundle (ASTM D1577) | Firestone section |
| Primary backing | Layers and weight | STC: dual primary, at least 7 oz per square yard | — |
| Coating | Polyurethane or polyolefin, and its weight | STC: heavy enough to meet its tuft bind minimum | — |
| Tuft bind | Pounds, under ASTM D1335 | STC: 6.8 lb, and higher is better | — |
| Drainage | Hole spacing or flow-through, plus a tested rate | STC: typically 25 in of water an hour. Firestone: holes at most 4 by 6 in on center | Firestone section |
| UV weathering | An independent lab report on this yarn | STC: after 3,000 hours of UVA, color better than Gray Scale 3 and at most 50% of strength lost | sun and hail section |
| Heavy metals | Lead and other metals, with the test | STC: compliant with state and ASTM standards. Firestone: lead-free | [is turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/) |
| Flammability | The test named, and the result | Firestone: non-flammable | [is turf flammable](/guides/is-artificial-turf-flammable/) |
| Infill | Required, optional or none, and which kind | Firestone: silica or acrylic-coated sand, no crumb rubber | [infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/) |
| Origin | Country of manufacture | Matches the roll labels on delivery | last section |
| PFAS | A written statement for this product | Colorado law | PFAS section |
| Coverage terms | Years, pro-rating, exclusions, who answers a claim | Firestone: at least 8 years | sun and hail section |

Two cautions. The STC's figures date from 2013; the council says a product can miss them and still suit its use, and that they are neither manufacturing minimums nor safety standards ([Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). And don't skip tuft bind, the force needed to pull a tuft out: ASTM notes that weak tuft bind in cut pile "may result in complete loss of pile in areas exposed to severe wear" ([ASTM D1335](https://www.astm.org/d1335.html)).

## What does Firestone's code require of the product itself?

Firestone writes a product spec into Development Code 16.6.4.F.2, and a town permit comes first, with no fee for single-family homes ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). Hold the sheet against each line. Not legal advice.

| Firestone's code says | Where to look on the sheet |
|---|---|
| Blades and backing of lead-free polyethylene, polypropylene, a vegetable-based product or a blend; "Nylon or other plastic shall be prohibited" | The yarn line for every zone, thatch included, and the backing line |
| No felt backing; a permeable backing with holes in a uniform grid no more than 4 by 6 inches on center | Backing and perforation lines |
| Green blades and "a tan thatch layer" | Yarn colors |
| Pile height of 1.75 to 3 inches; face weight of at least 70 ounces | Pile height, and face weight rather than total weight |
| Materials "protected with a minimum of an eight (8) year warranty" | The maker's written terms |
| "All materials shall be non-flammable" | The flammability line |
| Turf and infill not toxic to people or pets, nor harmful to the environment | Heavy-metal results and the PFAS statement |
| A non-toxic, anti-microbial infill such as silica sand or acrylic-coated sand; crumb rubber prohibited | The infill line |
| Installed to the manufacturer's specifications, over a porous aggregate base at least 3 inches deep | The maker's installation instructions |

Two lines a sheet may not settle. A sheet like SYNLawn's cites ASTM D2859 for flammability, a test ASTM describes as identifying floor coverings that "can be rated as flame-resistant under specific controlled laboratory conditions" ([ASTM D2859](https://www.astm.org/d2859.html)); Firestone asks for non-flammable and names no test. And a knitted or woven flow-through backing has no punched holes, which the code doesn't address. For either, it depends on the product: ask Firestone Planning before you order.

In Barefoot Lakes, the metro district's guidelines repeat the 1.75 to 3 inch blade and the 70-ounce face weight ([St. Vrain Lakes Metropolitan Districts](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). The town's other turf rules are on the [Firestone area page](/areas/firestone-co/).

Two other codes touch on specs without setting numbers:

- **Greeley** allows turf only where it isn't visible from streets and public rights-of-way, and says it "may be subject to other engineering specifications" ([Greeley Sec. 24-802](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT24DECO_CH8LAST_S24-802LADE)). Front-yard turf on houses is prohibited today ([Planning Commission packet](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9205,plainText=false))), and a permit path is still being drafted ([council status report](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9580,plainText=false))). It depends on what the city adopts: ask Greeley Planning.
- **Platteville** makes turf not installed "in compliance with specifications of the manufacturer" a public nuisance ([Platteville Sec. 7-1-30](https://library.municode.com/co/platteville/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CH7HESAAN_ARTINU_S7-1-30ENNU)), so keep the maker's installation instructions with the sheet.

Every other town is in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## What should the paperwork say about PFAS?

Since January 1, 2026, no one may install artificial turf containing intentionally added PFAS on any property in Colorado ([C.R.S. 25-15-605, from SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/46185/download); [bill summary](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-081)). The section runs two sentences and names no document or test. Not legal advice.

So the paperwork is yours to ask for:

- A written statement from the manufacturer that this product, by name and SKU, contains no intentionally added PFAS. A statement about a brand in general says less.
- A date on that statement, so it covers what is being made now.
- The same statement from the infill supplier.

What PFAS are, and what a lab test can and can't show, is in [is artificial turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/).

## How do altitude sun and hail change what to ask about coverage?

Two local facts make the written terms worth reading:

- **Sun.** The EPA says UV intensity rises about 6% per kilometer of elevation above sea level ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/learn-about-uv-index)), which comes to roughly 9% more UV at Northern Colorado's elevation than at sea level. The UV lab line is where a product answers for that.
- **Hail.** NOAA's storm database logged 253 hail reports in Larimer County and 536 in Weld from 2011 through 2025 ([NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)). What hail does to turf, and whether a homeowners policy pays, is in [does hail damage artificial turf](/guides/does-hail-damage-artificial-turf/).

What the STC says to understand in those terms:

- Coverage for defects in material and workmanship, including fading and early breakdown from UV.
- Limits and remedies, and any conditions that void or limit coverage. Ask outright whether storm damage is one.
- Who must answer a claim, and how to file one, particularly against a manufacturer abroad.
- Whether it is pro-rated. The STC notes that some coverage pays less as the turf ages.

How many years turf actually gets here is in [how long artificial turf lasts](/guides/how-long-does-artificial-turf-last/).

## Which lines matter most for dogs, kids or a front yard?

Read the sheet against the use the maker states for the product, then these lines:

- **Dogs:** backing and drainage, infill and yarn. A pet product with nylon yarn fails Firestone's yarn line. See [pet turf vs regular turf](/guides/pet-turf-vs-regular-turf/), [dog turf infill](/guides/dog-turf-infill/) and our [pet turf page](/services/pet-turf/).
- **Kids:** heavy metals and infill; Firestone's code bars crumb rubber. Under a swing set or climber, the line that matters is a critical-height rating: the CPSC's playground handbook says a surface's ASTM F1292 rating should be at least the fall height of the highest equipment, and that carpeting and mats aren't appropriate unless tested to F1292 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf)). Ask for the rating of the whole surface at your equipment's height; see [playground turf vs mulch](/guides/playground-turf-vs-mulch/) and our [playground turf page](/services/playground-turf/). Summer surface heat is in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).
- **Front yard:** the appearance lines, meaning blade colors, thatch, pile and face weight, read beside a sample in daylight. The town rule comes before the sheet, as in Greeley; see [front yard turf ideas](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/) and [what makes turf look real](/guides/most-realistic-artificial-turf/).

## How do I check that the rolls delivered match the sheet?

The STC's advice for delivery ([Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)):

1. Keep the sample you chose, and compare it with the product delivered before it goes down.
2. Have the sample identify where the turf was made, then check the shipping label on the rolls and the bill of lading against it.
3. Get a written commitment that the sample you chose is the product delivered.

Add one check of your own: the product name and SKU on the roll label should match the sheet and the PFAS statement.
