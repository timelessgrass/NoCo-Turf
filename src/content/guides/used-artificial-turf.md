**Checked September 25, 2026. Not legal advice:** the PFAS section explains a Colorado statute in plain English.

"Cheap turf" usually means one of three things, each with its own problems. Used field turf is the carpet pulled off a replaced sports field. Remnants are offcuts and roll ends of new turf. Liquidator and overstock rolls are new turf sold off as surplus or discontinued stock.

| | Used field turf | Remnant | Liquidator or overstock roll |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condition | Used, usually to the end of a field's life | New | New |
| Built for | Sport, over sand and rubber infill | Whatever its parent roll was | Whatever its label says |
| Who can document PFAS | The maker, if anyone knows who it was | The maker, if the offcut kept its label | The maker on the roll label |
| What usually goes wrong | Leftover rubber, a field spec in a yard, life already spent | Extra seams, mixed dye lots | A product you can't trace to a maker |

## What comes with a used sports-field roll?

Field turf is built to be filled: a 2025 field specification from Moose Jaw, Saskatchewan calls for at least 9.2 pounds of infill per square foot, at least 3 pounds of it rubber, 1.75 inches deep ([City of Moose Jaw](https://moosejaw.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Infilled-Synthetic-Turf-Specification.pdf)). The Synthetic Turf Council's figures put an average field at roughly 40,000 pounds of turf and 400,000 pounds of infill ([California DTSC](https://web.archive.org/web/20260326043412/https://dtsc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2026/02/TechnicalDocument_PFASs-in-Artificial-Turf_accessible.pdf)).

A 2019 FairWarning investigation found a niche trade selling reclaimed field turf to homeowners for landscaping, batting cages and dog kennels, and described one reseller hanging rolls from a forklift and beating them with a broom to shake the rubber and sand out ([FairWarning](https://investigatemidwest.org/2019/12/19/fields-of-waste-artificial-turf-touted-as-recycling-fix-for-millions-of-scrap-tires-becomes-mounting-disposal-mess/)). What a broom misses stays in the backing.

- **Rubber.** FairWarning reported that most fields installed in the mid-2000s used crumb rubber. Federal research summarized by EPA detected metals, volatile and semivolatile organic compounds, and bacteria in tire crumb infill ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/chemical-research/federal-research-recycled-tire-crumb-used-playing-fields-and-playgrounds)); the health question is in our [crumb rubber guide](/guides/is-crumb-rubber-safe/).
- **Lines.** Moose Jaw's spec says lines "shall be tufted into the turf panels" wherever possible, so a sideline roll can carry a stripe that is yarn, not paint.
- **Lead, on older nylon fields.** CDC's 2008 advisory reported lead of potential concern in nylon and nylon-blend turf, very low lead in polyethylene-only fields, and lead released in dust as turf ages ([CDC](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/25186/cdc_25186_DS1.pdf)). Ask what the yarn is, and see [whether turf is toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/).

## Can used field turf meet Colorado's PFAS law?

Often nobody can show it does. Since January 1, 2026, no person may install artificial turf that contains intentionally added PFAS chemicals anywhere in Colorado ([C.R.S. 25-15-605, from SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/46185/download)). "Intentionally added" means PFAS a manufacturer added on purpose that have a functional or technical effect on the product ([C.R.S. 25-15-603(12)](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/101769/download)). The section's one exception lets owners keep maintaining turf installed before 2026; it says nothing about pulling old turf up and installing it somewhere new. Not legal advice.

Why field rolls are the hard case:

- California's toxics regulator says fluoropolymer PFAS "have been commonly used as extrusion processing aids" in making turf fibers ([DTSC](https://web.archive.org/web/20260326043412/https://dtsc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2026/02/TechnicalDocument_PFASs-in-Artificial-Turf_accessible.pdf)).
- In 2024 the Synthetic Turf Council told that regulator its member manufacturers were working toward no PFAS-based ingredients, processing aids included, by 2025. A field pulled up now after a 5- to 15-year life was made well before that date.
- The Toxics Use Reduction Institute reported nonprofit tests that found PFOS in the backing of an older, discarded turf sample, and warns that a clean result for a few PFAS doesn't prove a material is PFAS-free; a total fluorine test or a TOP assay says more ([TURI](https://www.turi.org/publications/per-and-poly-fluoroalkyl-substances-pfas-in-artificial-turf-carpet/)).

The paper that settles it is the maker's statement for that product and production run, and an unlabeled roll can't be matched to one. A lab test on a sample is the fallback; ask what it tested for. Around here the paperwork matters twice: our [HOA approval guide](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) puts the product sheet and its PFAS documentation in the architectural review packet.

## Will a field roll pass a yard spec in Northern Colorado?

Look at the yarn weight first. Two public field specs we read:

| | Wyoming school district, 2021 | Moose Jaw, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Pile height | 2.0 in | 2.5 in |
| Pile (face) weight | 30, 41 or 50 oz per sq yd | 47 oz per sq yd or more |
| Total weight | 58, 69 or 78 oz per sq yd | 68 oz per sq yd or more |
| Roll width | 15 ft | 15 ft |
| Infill | Black SBR rubber and sand | At least 9.2 lb per sq ft, 3 lb of it rubber |

Sources: [Teton County School District RFP](https://www.boarddocs.com/wy/teton1/Board.nsf/files/C2R5TV756ABB/$file/TURF%20RFP%20032521%20Final.pdf) and [City of Moose Jaw](https://moosejaw.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Infilled-Synthetic-Turf-Specification.pdf), both measured to ASTM D5848.

Firestone's code, for one, requires a pile face weight of at least 70 ounces and prohibits crumb rubber filler ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). None of the field specs above asks for 70; Firestone's full list is in our [spec-sheet checklist](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/). Watch which number a seller quotes: in the Wyoming grid, each total weight is 28 ounces more than its face weight, because it counts the backing and coating. Our [face weight guide](/guides/artificial-turf-face-weight/) explains the difference.

## How much life is left in a used field roll?

Nobody can say exactly. DTSC puts field life at 5 to 15 years, depending on use, and FairWarning found fields from the mid-2000s at or past an estimated 8- to 10-year life. The Wyoming district was replacing turf installed in 2006 and 2009 in the summer of 2021. A field comes out when its owner judges it worn out for play, and the roll for sale is that same carpet.

- **Sun.** A University of Johannesburg study of polyethylene sports turf found absorbed UV breaks bonds in the yarn and leads to cracking. Northern Colorado's weather stations sit 1.44 to 1.55 km up, where EPA's rule of about 6% more UV per kilometer works out to roughly 9% more than at sea level ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/learn-about-uv-index)). What life a roll has left gets spent under that sun; see [does artificial turf fade](/guides/does-artificial-turf-fade/).
- **Hail.** NOAA logged 536 hail reports on 156 days in Weld County and 253 on 68 days in Larimer County from 2011 through 2025 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)). A roll's storm history doesn't come with it; what hail can do to turf is in our [hail guide](/guides/does-hail-damage-artificial-turf/).
- **The maker's promise.** The two makers' terms we read follow the first buyer. SYNLawn's residential terms apply only to the original purchaser, and Smart Turf's are "provided to the original purchaser only" and ask for the original sales receipt ([SYNLawn](https://lowes.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/SY24247_Lowes_ProductWarranty_v1.pdf), [Smart Turf](https://smartturf.com/warranty)). See [what a turf maker's terms cover](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/).

## What's the catch with remnants and liquidator rolls?

They're new, so age and rubber drop away. The questions are whether pieces match and who made them.

- **Dye lots.** Turf is made in batches, and color can shift between them. Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a turf distributor, says every section seamed together "MUST BE OF THE SAME DYE LOT," and that the same product name and specs don't promise a match across batches ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/understanding-dye-lot-in-synthetic-turf/), [SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/why-dye-lot-matters/)). Smart Turf's terms call slight color variation from lot to lot "common and to be expected." Remnants from several rolls can span several lots.
- **Seams.** Every extra piece adds a seam. SGW says sections laid in the wrong direction make seams visible and advises against rotating a section more than 30 degrees ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/why-visible-seams-occur/)); cutting odd remnants to fit invites exactly that. More in [visible seams in artificial turf](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).
- **The label.** A liquidator roll should still carry its maker's label. The Synthetic Turf Council tells buyers to check the shipping label on the rolls and the bill of lading against what they were told, and to keep the sample they chose and compare it with what arrives ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). It wrote those guidelines because the landscape market includes products "of uncertain quality and durability." The label is also how you reach the maker for PFAS documentation.

A remnant makes the most sense where one labeled piece covers the whole area with no seam, such as a [narrow side yard](/guides/artificial-turf-side-yard/) or a small [dog run](/guides/pea-gravel-vs-turf-dog-run/). For new rolls off a store shelf, see [store turf vs installer turf](/guides/home-depot-vs-installer-artificial-turf/).

## What should I ask before buying, or before asking an installer to lay it?

From the seller:

- The maker, product name, production date and dye lot from each roll's label.
- The spec sheet: face weight, total weight, pile height and yarn type.
- The maker's PFAS documentation for that product, or a lab report on a sample and what it tested for.
- For a field roll: which field, the year it went in, how the infill came out, and whether rubber remains in the backing. A school or city that replaced a field may have its bid documents on file.

From an installer:

- Will you lay turf I supply, and what do you need to see first? A good answer comes before the quote and names the documents: label, spec sheet, PFAS paperwork, a sample.
- What does your workmanship cover when the turf came from me? A good answer puts in writing what the installer stands behind (base, seams, edges, fastening) and what it doesn't.
