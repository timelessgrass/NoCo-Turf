faq:
  - q: "Does a synthetic field need weed or pest treatment?"
    a: "Sometimes. The Synthetic Turf Council says weeds come out easily by hand before an infestation gets excessive, with treatments available, and that moss, mold or algae turn up in little-used, shaded, damp areas. FieldTurf says weeds and moss usually start where the turf meets the perimeter curb, and suggests a biodegradable weed killer or pulling them."
  - q: "Can a synthetic field host a graduation or a concert?"
    a: "With protection. The STC says to put plywood, interlocking plastic panels or similar weight-spreading material under every chair and table, never drive spikes or posts into the turf, and groom and magnet-sweep the area afterward. FieldTurf says the bases under its fields are typically designed for 70 pounds per square inch, and that vehicles should stay off a saturated field."
  - q: "How often do painted lines need stripping?"
    a: "The STC says to strip temporary paint after at most four applications; FieldTurf says about every five. Paint that builds up in the infill hardens the surface, so FieldTurf calls flushing with water, preferably hot, imperative. The STC adds that permanent lines and logos can age differently from the field and change its hardness readings."
  - q: "How long does a synthetic field last?"
    a: "California's Department of Toxic Substances Control puts sports fields at 5 to 15 years, depending on how intensively they are used. Windsor High School's stadium turf and shock pad were replaced in 2023 as a Weld RE-4 bond repair project. Our guide to how long a turf field lasts covers the signs of a worn-out field and what replacement involves."
  - q: "Is a backyard lawn looked after the same way?"
    a: "The ideas carry over at a far smaller scale: a yard sees a fraction of a field's hours of play. Brushing, infill depth and keeping debris off matter in both. Our guides to matted turf and to how much upkeep artificial turf needs cover the yard version."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** This page is for whoever keeps a synthetic sports field playable: a school district, a parks department, a club, or an HOA or metro district. The routine is much the same everywhere; Northern Colorado changes the calendar around it. Whether a field should be synthetic at all is in our [guide to synthetic and natural grass fields](/guides/turf-vs-grass-sports-fields/).

## What does routine care on a synthetic field involve?

The Synthetic Turf Council's maintenance guidelines for infilled sports fields start from a rule of thumb: budget one hour of inspection and maintenance for every 10 hours of play, walk the field daily, and log every job ([STC maintenance guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf)).

| Task | How often | Who says |
|---|---|---|
| Walk the field for debris, open seams and low infill | Daily | STC (2013) |
| Brush to stand fibers up and level infill | Every 4 to 6 weeks | FieldTurf (2025) |
| Rake the infill | Every 4 to 6 weeks from the second year | FieldTurf (2025) |
| Loosen the infill with rotating tines | At most 3 times a year, ideally after each sport season and after snow clearing | FieldTurf (2025) |
| Decompact, deep-clean and level the infill | Semi-annual to annual | STC (2013) |

FieldTurf's intervals are from its [owner's manual](https://fieldturf.com/workspace/uploads/files/brochure-maintenance-guidelines-fieldturf-may2025.pdf) for its own fields; other makers set their own. Two points hold for any field:

- **Brush dry and slowly.** The STC says brushing a standard multi-purpose field from a utility vehicle takes about an hour, at low speed with no sharp turns. Midfield, goal mouths and corners need the most attention.
- **Measure infill on a grid,** with a depth gauge or a nail and a tape measure, as the STC prefers. Topping up is in our [guide to adding infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).

A 2008 guide from the Sports Turf Managers Association, citing one school district, put an average field at about 375 labor hours a year ([STMA guide](https://www.stma.org/sites/stma/files/STMA_Synthetic_Guide_2nd_Edition.pdf)).

## What does a Northern Colorado year ask of a field?

| Season | What the weather does here | What the field needs |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | The Fort Collins station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F and 51.4 inches of snow | Clear snow only for scheduled play; no play on ice |
| Spring | March is that station's snowiest month, at 9.4 inches | Loosen and level infill; top up; GMAX-test once dry |
| Summer | Greeley UNC, nearest [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) and [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F | Heat checks; rinses planned around water rules |
| October to April | 44 of the 48 high-wind events NOAA logged from 2011 through 2025 in the zone for Larimer County below 6,000 feet and northwest Weld | Blown debris cleared fast; infill recovered from fences |

Sources: [NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json), 1991–2020, and [NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/).

## How should snow and freeze-thaw be handled on a field?

The STC's default is to let snow and ice melt and drain. When a scheduled game needs a clear field:

- **Clear it close to game time,** the STC says, before cold wind can glaze a damp, freshly cleared surface. Use pneumatic tires and a guarded blade, and leave ¼ to ½ inch of snow to melt; brushing it off pulls infill.
- **Plow cold, not slushy.** FieldTurf says frozen moisture holds the infill in place under a plow, and calls below freezing, about 25°F, the ideal plowing temperature.
- **Keep play off ice and mid-thaw turf.** Synthetic Turf Management, a UK pitch builder, says a rubber-infilled pitch plays harder than usual below freezing and shouldn't be used on ice or in a thaw, when water can stand on a still-frozen base ([Synthetic Turf Management](https://stmworld.co.uk/blog/post/synthetic-turf-winter-maintenance-england)). A chinook, which can lift temperatures 25 to 35°F in a short time ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)), sets that up in an afternoon.
- **Decide who may clear it.** Thompson School District bans unauthorized snow removal on its turf field ([TSD rules](https://www.tsd.org/fs/resource-manager/view/b289d026-8bb5-4ea9-843a-74c0f995c236)). Loveland Sports Park keeps its synthetic Championship Field maintained through winter, by reservation, while its grass fields close ([City of Loveland](https://www.lovgov.org/services/parks-recreation/parks-facilities/parks-reservation-spaces/loveland-sports-park)).

The backyard version is in our guides to [snow removal](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/) and [freezing](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

## What does wind do to a field here?

The National Weather Service office for northeast Colorado names chinook winds as one of the two main causes of Colorado's cold-season high winds, with gusts of 60 to near 100 mph in foothill areas such as [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/), and lists flying debris and blowing dust among the dangers ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/bou/highwind)). Besides the Larimer zone's 48 events in the table above, NOAA counted 30 in the central and southern Weld County zone from 2011 through 2025.

- **Debris works into the infill.** The STC says leaves and debris left lying migrate in, slowing drainage and compacting infill. FieldTurf holds a blower at least 2 feet off the surface at a 45-degree angle. See our [guide to leaves on turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/).
- **Dust hardens the surface.** The STC counts dust storms among events that bring surface hardening and slower drainage.
- **Loose infill leaves at the edges.** Keystone Sports Construction, a field builder, says infill piled along edges is likelier to be scattered by wind, so it sweeps the perimeter back into play and fits fine mesh to fencing ([Keystone](https://keystonesportsconstruction.com/3-ways-to-prevent-turf-pellets-from-leaving-the-field/)).

Our reading, not a maker's: after a high wind warning, walk the fence lines and downwind edge first, then gauge the goal mouths.

## How do summer heat and 2026 water limits change field care?

Mead High School's head football coach said in 2021 that they had recorded 130 degrees on the school's old turf field ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/en/articles/detail/how-mead-high-school-became-the-coolest-high-school-in-colorado/)). Penn State cites a Brigham Young University guideline of no use above 120°F, and reports that water cools a field only briefly: 30 minutes of watering took BYU's surface from 174°F to 85°F, but it read 164°F after 20 minutes ([Penn State](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature.pdf/@@download/file/temperature.pdf)). The STMA suggests an infrared thermometer to time practice.

Water limits the rinsing:

- **The supply.** The 2026 Colorado-Big Thompson quota is 0.8 acre-feet per unit ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/news/2026/20260417-greeley-water-and-sewer-board-declares-adequate-water-year-urges-wise-stewardship/)). Fort Collins's voluntary Water Shortage Watch asks for lawn watering two days a week at most, never 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/News-Articles/News/2026-Q2/Voluntary-Water-Shortage-Watch)); [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/)'s Level 3 schedule bars the same hours ([Town of Johnstown](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)).
- **Whether a rinse counts.** Those rules are written for lawn and landscape watering. Whether a cooling rinse on a synthetic field falls under them depends on the provider; ask the utility before relying on one. Our [guide to watering artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/) covers yards.
- **The water and the infill.** The STC says only potable water should go on a synthetic field. A civil engineer writing for facility managers says some organic infills must hold a set moisture content and may need irrigation ([Gale Associates](https://galeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Alternative-Infills-for-Synthetic-Turf-Fields-JMP.pdf)), a standing commitment in a short water year. [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/)'s Dry Creek fields use coated sand, with quick couplers on an irrigation system for cleaning ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/facility/dry-creek-community-park/)).

More in our [guide to how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## GMAX testing: what does it measure, and how often?

GMAX comes from a drop test: how fast a weighted missile stops on the turf shows how hard it is. It matters because, Penn State's Center for Sports Surface Research says, about 15% of concussions in football and soccer come from the head hitting the surface ([Penn State guide](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/resources/surface-hardness-synthetic-turf-guide.pdf)).

| Benchmark | Device | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [ASTM F1936](https://store.astm.org/f1936-19.html), current edition 2019 | F355 missile | Below 200 at every test point |
| [Synthetic Turf Council, 2011 guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Synthetic.pdf) | F1936 test | Below 165 |
| NFL, before every game | Clegg impact tester | Below 100 everywhere in play |

The 200 and NFL figures are as the STMA's [field hardness paper](https://www.sportsfieldmanagement.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Field_Hardness-FINAL-2.pdf) and Penn State give them; Clegg and F355 readings aren't interchangeable. The STC's [guidelines page](https://www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/page/guidelines) now lists a newer performance document, so ask the lab which benchmark it reports against.

**How often.** The STMA says at installation, then once a year or more, and ideally through the season. Penn State says yearly, more on heavily used fields, checking inlays, painted areas, seams and high-use areas. The STC's 2011 guidelines set follow-up tests every four years on a community field, yearly on a stadium field.

**A public program.** Washington, D.C.'s Department of General Services tests every synthetic field yearly at 10 high-use spots. One score over 200 closes the field briefly; fixes run from an infill top-off in 2 to 3 days to sectional replacement in 2 to 3 weeks ([DC DGS](https://dgs.dc.gov/page/2025-gmax-testing-results)). In June 2019, 15 of its 56 fields needed more maintenance ([DC DGS, 2019](https://dgs.dc.gov/node/1414941)).

**What pushes GMAX up:**

- **Low infill.** The STMA says higher hardness almost always goes with low infill, and Penn State finds depth and GMAX strongly correlated, so a low depth reading is the cue to test.
- **Layering, dirt and age.** Sand settling out of a sand-and-rubber infill can form a compacted layer, and GMAX tends to rise as a field ages (STMA); blown dust adds to it.
- **Cold.** The UK builder says fields play harder below freezing, and Penn State took its research readings on a surface free of dew and rain. Our reading: with 150 freezing nights a year at the Fort Collins station, test on a thawed, dry day in the same month each year, so the numbers compare.

Readings that keep climbing despite grooming and top-dressing point to our guide on [how long a turf field lasts](/guides/how-long-does-a-turf-field-last/); parents' infill questions are in our [crumb rubber guide](/guides/is-crumb-rubber-safe/).

## What should a field owner ask whoever maintains it?

- **What is the written plan?** Tasks tied to the field's hours of use, plus the builder's written OK of the program, provider and equipment, which the STC says should come first.
- **What infill, at what depth?** The infill recipe, a target depth range, and grid readings in the log.
- **Who clears snow, and how?** Named people, pneumatic tires, a guarded blade, a thin layer left behind and a grooming pass afterward.
- **When is GMAX tested, and against what?** A baseline after installation, then at least yearly by a third-party lab, with the test points and the benchmark in the report.
- **What's on the shelf?** The STC says to buy maintenance equipment, extra infill and repair materials with the field.
