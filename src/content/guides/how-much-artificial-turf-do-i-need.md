This page is the arithmetic behind a turf project: measuring the yard, turning it into a turf order, the rock and infill it takes, and how much of a yard turf may cover. Prices are in our [cost guide](/guides/artificial-turf-cost/).

## How do I measure a yard for artificial turf?

Draw it before you measure it. SYNLawn's DIY guide suggests a sketchpad, a measuring wheel, a tape and marking paint, and says to measure each area to its longest point ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)).

1. **Mark the edges** where turf meets beds, rock, the patio and the fence. Shaw's guidelines put new curbing or edging in before the turf is cut, because that gives a more accurate measurement ([ShawGrass](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf)). If a patio, fire feature or wall goes in first, measure after it is set.
2. **Break the area into shapes:** rectangles for most of it, triangles and circles for corners and curves. A landscape-supply calculator uses the standard formulas: a triangle is half the base times the height, a circle about 3.14 times the radius squared ([Pioneer](https://www.pioneerco.com/materials-calculator)).
3. **Add them up.** That total is the yard's square footage, the number base rock, infill and town limits work from.
4. **Mark the grain.** Turf blades lean one way, and every piece in an area must lean the same way. Shaw faces the grain toward the primary viewpoint to cut sheen; Integriturf's front-yard example leans it toward the street ([Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf)). Behind the house, that viewpoint is usually the patio or the back windows.

## Why is the turf order bigger than the yard?

Because turf comes off a roll of fixed width. Integriturf says most manufacturers make rolls 15 feet wide and 100 feet long, 1,500 square feet, and some make 12- or 13-foot widths. Width comes in whole rolls; length is cut to order.

| Example | Yard | Turf ordered | Extra turf |
|---|---|---|---|
| SYNLawn: a 15 by 16 ft area plus a 7 by 9 ft area | 303 sq ft | 360 sq ft; the 7-ft area still takes a 15-ft-wide piece | about 19% |
| Integriturf: a 20 by 25 ft front yard, rolls running the 25-ft length | 500 sq ft | two 15 by 26 ft pieces, 780 sq ft | about 56% |
| The same yard, rolls turned to run across the 20-ft side | 500 sq ft | two 15 by 21 ft pieces, 630 sq ft | about 26% |

The percentages and the turned layout are our arithmetic. What they show:

- **Rules of thumb assume the layout suits the roll.** Shaw suggests about 10% extra where the design has curves, SYNLawn 10 to 20% for many curves and irregular shapes. A 20-foot-wide yard in 15-foot rolls wastes far more.
- **Turning the rolls can save turf.** Turned, the 20 by 25 yard needs 150 square feet less, but its grain leans across the yard instead of toward the street. SYNLawn says a less-than-ideal direction sometimes makes sense to cut waste; where the yard is seen from decides.
- **Length needs slack too.** Integriturf adds 1 to 2 feet to each piece, and Shaw plans on about 18 inches of waste at the roll's core, where turf can be too wrinkled to use.
- **Every extra strip is a seam.** Where seams should go is in our guide to [visible seams](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).

## How many tons of base rock does a turf area take?

Rock is ordered on the yard's square footage, not the turf order. The published rules, worked for 1,000 square feet (our arithmetic):

| Rule | 3 inches deep | 4 inches deep |
|---|---|---|
| SYNLawn: square feet ÷ 100 for tons at 3 in, ÷ 80 at 4 in | 10 tons | 12.5 tons |
| Shaw: 1 cubic yard covers 80 sq ft at 4 in, counted as 1 ton | Not given | 12.5 tons |
| Pioneer's calculator: roadbase at 1.30 tons per cubic yard | 9.3 cu yd, about 12 tons | 12.3 cu yd, about 16 tons |

Three things move the order:

- **Loose rock shrinks.** The Federal Highway Administration's gravel roads guide warns that quantities are too often figured loose, as the rock sits in the truck, and says ordinary compaction cuts volume by 30 percent or more in many parts of the country ([FHWA](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/construction/pubs/ots15002.pdf)). A turf base isn't a road, but at 30 percent a 3-inch compacted layer starts a little over 4 inches loose: nearer 16 tons than 10 on 1,000 square feet. Pioneer's calculator asks how deep you'll spread the rock, which is the loose depth.
- **A fines cap is its own layer.** At Pioneer's 1.35 tons per cubic yard, a 1-inch cap of crusher fines over 1,000 square feet is about 3.1 cubic yards, roughly 4.2 tons. Either rock comes to about 4 tons per inch of depth per 1,000 square feet.
- **Firestone sets a floor:** a compacted, porous aggregate base at least 3 inches deep ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)).

SYNLawn counts 13 to 16 wheelbarrow loads per ton: 130 to 160 trips from the driveway for 10 tons, so whether a machine fits through the side gate matters as much as the tonnage. Which rock suits your soil is in [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).

## How much infill goes into the turf?

Infill is figured on the yard's square footage and the turf's pile height:

- Shaw estimates 3 to 5 pounds of sand per square foot on 50- to 80-ounce turf: 3,000 to 5,000 pounds for 1,000 square feet.
- SYNLawn's guide says 1 to 2 pounds per square foot, depending on the product.
- Motz's coated-sand chart gives a 1½ to 1¾ inch landscape pile 2.5 to 3.5 pounds per square foot: 2,500 to 3,500 pounds, or 50 to 70 of its 50-pound bags, for 1,000 square feet (our arithmetic; [Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Envirofill-Installation-Guidelines-for-Landscape-Applications.pdf)).

Firestone's minimum pile of 1.75 inches puts its lawns on or above that Motz row. The turf's own spec sheet overrides all of these. Which infill to choose is in our [infill guide](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/); topping up is in [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).

## How much of a Firestone yard can be turf?

Less than the headline 75%. Firestone applies its turf rules to lots that existed before its Development Code, not only new ones ([16.6.1](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.1GEPR)), and requires a town permit first. Three rules set the size, and the smallest answer counts. Take side and rear yards totaling 2,400 square feet with a 400-square-foot patio (our arithmetic, not a Town ruling):

| Rule in 16.6.4 | What it says | Turf allowed |
|---|---|---|
| The cap | Turf up to 75% of the rear or side yard | 1,800 sq ft |
| The permeable share | At least 25% of the yard stays permeable living or organic landscape, with patios and walks counted against the turf | 1,400 sq ft |
| The live-plant rule | 50% of the combined side and rear yards in live plants, which turf can't count toward | 800 sq ft |

The live-plant rule decides: 800 square feet, a third of the yard, or 1,200 with no patio. Two more rules shape where it goes:

- **A 3-foot buffer.** Turf must stop at least 3 feet from any property line, with permeable, living landscape between. On a backyard 60 feet wide and 40 feet deep, with property lines on three sides, that strip is about 400 square feet. A narrow side yard may have no room left for turf; see [turf in a side yard](/guides/artificial-turf-side-yard/).
- **Slopes.** No turf on a slope steeper than 5:1; see [turf on a slope](/guides/artificial-turf-on-a-slope/).

Whether a planted buffer counts toward the live-plant half, and how the Town measures your yards, depends on Firestone's reading: ask Firestone Planning. The code's rear yard runs across the lot between the side lot lines, from the rear lot line to the back of the house ([16.11.3](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH11DE_16.11.3TEDE)). Front yards need 75% live plants, leaving little room for turf. Not legal advice: this is our reading of the code on September 25, 2026.

## Do other Northern Colorado towns limit how much of a yard is turf?

Mostly in front yards, and it depends on the town:

- **Wellington** requires new single-family subdivision lots to keep at least 75% live materials between the front of the house and the curb ([Sec. 15-5-40](https://library.municode.com/co/wellington/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH15LAUSCO_ART5DEST_S15-5-40LASC)).
- **Windsor** exempts detached houses from most of its landscape article but requires at least 25% of every front yard to be landscaped ([Sec. 15-3-10](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH15DEST_ARTIIISTLADEAR_S15-3-10INAP)). Whether turf counts toward that share is Windsor Planning's call; ask before the design is drawn.
- **Frederick's** 2026 code wants at least 50% living plant coverage in every distinct landscaped area and says single-family landscaping must meet the section's applicable requirements ([Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)). How that reaches an existing backyard is unsettled: ask Frederick Planning.

Our [front-yard design guide](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/) sets the front-yard minimums side by side, and [turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/) covers every town. For splitting a backyard, see [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/).

## What should an installer's takeoff show?

On a custom build, a golf-course lot or any yard under architectural review, the measurements travel: an architectural review packet should carry a layout with dimensions and square footage ([HOA approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/)), and the hardscape and irrigation trades work from the same drawing. Ask for:

- The yard's square footage and the turf order, as two separate numbers.
- The roll width, the strips, where the seams fall and which way the grain leans.
- The base: compacted depth, material and tons.
- The infill: product and pounds per square foot.
- The town and HOA limits the layout was checked against, with the numbers.

A good answer is a drawing with dimensions on it; a square-foot figure with no drawing leaves all of that open. The [installation page](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) shows the layers in order.
