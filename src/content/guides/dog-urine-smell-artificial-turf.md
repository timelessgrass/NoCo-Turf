faq:
  - q: "Will vinegar take the smell out?"
    a: "It's a poor choice for a dog area. Humane World for Animals advises against cleaning pet urine with ammonia or vinegar, because strong chemical smells may encourage a pet to mark the same spot again. It recommends an enzymatic cleaner instead."
  - q: "Can I use bleach or hydrogen peroxide on the turf?"
    a: "Check the turf maker's care instructions first. Consumer Reports, testing cleaners on carpet stained with real dog urine, found that oxidizing cleaners with hydrogen peroxide or bleach work faster than enzyme cleaners but aren't safe on every surface. Don't mix the two approaches either: one enzyme maker's directions say to keep bleach away from its product."
  - q: "Why does the smell fade in winter and come back in spring?"
    a: "Cold slows the reaction that makes ammonia without stopping it; Montana State University Extension says ammonia can keep escaping slowly, over a longer time, when it's cool. NOAA's Fort Collins and Longmont stations average 150 and 177.7 days a year with a low at or below 32°F, so urine from the cold months sits in the turf until the first warm week. Give the dog area a deep soak and an enzyme round then, before summer."
  - q: "If the base has to be rebuilt, do I need new turf too?"
    a: "Not always. Lifting turf to rebuild the base is a different job from replacing it. If the backing, seams and blades are sound, ask whether the same turf can be relaid. Worn or brittle turf is a separate decision, covered in our guide to [repairing or replacing turf](/guides/when-to-replace-artificial-turf/)."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** Turf that smells of dog urine is holding it somewhere: on the blades, in the infill, in the backing, or in the base below. The fixes start at the top and get bigger as they go down, so try them in order. How a new dog run is built to drain is on the [pet turf page](/services/pet-turf/).

## Why does turf smell worse on hot days and after a light rain?

Because the smell is mostly ammonia, and warmth and a little moisture are what make it. Dog urine's major components include water, urea and uric acid ([NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/dogs-and-turfgrass-interactions)). The urease enzyme turns urea and water into ammonium, which then becomes ammonia gas at the surface. Montana State University Extension, which studies the same reaction in urea fertilizer, says warmth speeds both steps: a rise from 45°F to 60°F can double the loss to the air ([MSU Extension](https://www.montana.edu/extension/Full_HTML_Pubs/EB0208-FactorsAffectingNitrogenFertilizerVolatilization/EB0208-FactorsAffectingNitrogenFertilizerVolatilization.html)). NOAA's Greeley UNC station averages a June-to-August high of 88.9°F.

Rain matters too. In the same research, it generally took 0.5 inch or more of precipitation in one event to carry urea deep enough to stop the loss. The largest losses came from a moist surface followed by slow drying, and light scattered rain "may increase rather than prevent" them ([MSU urea research](https://landresources.montana.edu/ureavolatilization/learned.html)). A field is not a dog run, but it is the same urea: an evening sprinkle, a warm morning, and the run smells worse than before it rained.

Northern Colorado has plenty of heat and few soaking rains. NOAA's 1991–2020 normals along the corridor:

| NOAA station (nearest towns) | Days at or above 90°F | Days with any precipitation | Days with 0.5 inch or more | Of those, June–August |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greeley UNC ([Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), Windsor, [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/), [Milliken](/areas/milliken-co/), [Severance](/areas/severance-co/), Evans, Eaton) | 58.3 | 73.5 | 7.8 | 2.6 |
| Longmont 2 ESE ([Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/), Mead, Firestone, Frederick, Dacono) | 48.8 | 72.3 | 7.8 | 3.1 |
| Fort Collins (Fort Collins, Wellington) | 29.5 | 85.9 | 8.7 | 2.6 |

"Any precipitation" means 0.01 inch or more. Most wet days here are the light kind that restarts the smell, and a summer brings two or three soaking ones. The hose has to do the rest.

## What fixes the smell, and in what order?

Work from the top layer down. Each step either ends the smell or shows that it is deeper.

| What you notice | Where the urine is likely sitting | Try next |
|---|---|---|
| Smell on hot afternoons that fades after a good soak | On the blades and the top of the infill | Soak more often in hot, dry spells |
| Back within a day or two of a soak | In the infill and the backing | Enzyme cleaner, saturated and left on |
| Back within a week of an enzyme treatment | In infill with no odor control | Clean it and top up with an odor-control infill |
| Strongest along one edge, where the infill stays damp | In the base, or on the clay under it | Lift the turf (next section) |

### First, soak it instead of sprinkling it

Rinse the spots the dogs use until water runs down through the turf, not just across the blades. Half an inch, the soaking-rain figure above, is about 3 gallons on a 10-square-foot spot; put it on slowly so it soaks in. A quick spray is the light-rain case and can leave the run smelling worse by afternoon. Rinse in the evening, within your town's watering hours (last section).

### Then an enzyme cleaner, left to work

If the smell is back a day or two after a good soak, residue is holding in the infill and backing. Enzyme cleaners are made to break down what water leaves behind, and two things matter more than the brand:

- **Volume.** The cleaner has to reach as deep as the urine did. One turf product's directions say to "thoroughly saturate the entire turf area" through a hose-end sprayer ([CareFree Enzymes](https://carefreeenzymes.com/products/synthetic-turf-odor-destroyer)).
- **Time.** Another maker's directions call for at least 10 minutes of contact, 20 to 30 minutes on old deposits, and no rinsing afterward ([Nature's Freedom](https://naturesfreedom.com/blogs/news/enzyme-pet-odor-remover)).

Treat the whole area the dogs use, not only the strongest spot. Cleaning turf in general, and which cleaners are safe to rinse into a Northern Colorado yard, is in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/).

### Then an odor-control infill

If an enzyme round helps for a few days and then the smell creeps back, look at the infill. Odor-control infills include zeolite, a mineral whose second-largest use in the U.S. is odor control, 15% of 2025 domestic sales ([U.S. Geological Survey](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-zeolites.pdf)). One maker describes it as neutralizing the ammonia from urine rather than masking it ([ZeoFill](https://zeofill.com/pages/what-is-zeolite)). Brushed in over a cleaned surface, it is the last step before anything comes up, and it doesn't replace rinsing. Which infill, and how much, is in [turf infill for dogs](/guides/dog-turf-infill/).

## When does the turf have to come up?

When the smell survives deep soaks, two or three enzyme rounds and an infill change, the urine is below the turf: in the backing, the base or the ground under it. Nothing poured from above reaches it well. The turf has to be peeled back so the base can be flushed, or dug out, replaced with clean crushed rock and graded so liquid leaves.

On clay, urine can end up in one spot. It moves down through the base until something stops it, and across much of Northern Colorado that something is clay. Longmont tells residents its "clay soils absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)); Greeley's water department says hard clay can sit "just inches below the surface" ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)). When liquid reaches clay faster than the clay takes it in, it runs along the top of the clay to the low side. A curb, a patio slab or a paver edge on that side holds it there, so the smell gathers along one edge while the middle of the yard is fine. What clay means for a new base is in [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).

Other signs it's the base: rinse water seeps out at an edge or stays under the turf, and the smell is back within days of every enzyme treatment.

If the low side is the house, stop flushing toward it. The Colorado Geological Survey lists inadequate management of surface drainage among the landscaping problems on expansive soils ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)), and a dog area that drains toward a foundation needs regrading, not more water.

What to ask whoever lifts it, and what a good answer sounds like:

- **"What's under this turf, and what will you do with it?"** A good answer comes after looking: replace what is saturated or packed fine, not only flush it.
- **"Where will liquid go once it's through the base?"** A good answer names a low point away from the house and how liquid gets there over clay.
- **"Does this backing drain across the whole sheet, or only through punched holes?"** They should know, and say what it means for dogs. More in [pet turf vs regular turf](/guides/pet-turf-vs-regular-turf/).

If the turf is only a year or two old, see [how to check what's under it](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/).

## What if it's the neighbors' dogs on my front-yard turf?

Passing dogs come back to the same places: the corner by the walk, the mailbox post, the end of the driveway. Put those strips on the same soak and enzyme schedule as a backyard run. A band of rock or a planted bed between the sidewalk and the turf gives dogs somewhere else to stop; [front-yard turf ideas](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/) covers what your town's planting rules allow there.

Greeley's code, for one, says little about urine. In [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), an owner must remove feces an animal leaves on someone else's property right away (Sec. 10-65; cleanup is in [dog poop on artificial turf](/guides/dog-poop-on-artificial-turf/)), and a separate section prohibits letting an animal damage another's property ([Sec. 10-64](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT10AN_CH3VI_S10-64DAPR)). Whether a dog marking your turf counts as damage, the code doesn't say. It depends on how the city applies it; ask the City of Greeley before counting on it. Not legal advice.

## Do watering restrictions cover hosing off turf?

None of the three towns below names artificial turf in its watering rules, so the wording decides. Milliken's is explicit: hand-watering, meaning a hose held in the hand with a positive shutoff nozzle, or a bucket, is allowed every day and isn't bound by the sprinkler hours ([Town of Milliken](https://millikenco.gov/194/Watering-Schedule)). Severance makes it unlawful to use any outside water source between 11 a.m. and 5 p.m. ([Town of Severance](https://www.townofseverance.org/396/Watering-Guidelines-and-Restrictions)), so rinse before or after. Johnstown's Level 3 rules bar watering from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. and ask for hand watering on your assigned day with an automatic shutoff nozzle ([Johnstown water FAQs](https://www.johnstownco.gov/m/faq?cat=37)); after the season ends October 15, 2026, the schedule lists only trees and shrubs for weekend hand watering ([2026 schedule](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)). Neither page mentions turf, so it depends on how Johnstown reads its rules; ask the town's water conservation staff before rinsing a dog run on an off day. Every town's 2026 rules are in [do you have to water artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/).
