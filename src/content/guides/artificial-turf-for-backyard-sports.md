answer:
  question: "What does a backyard sports area on artificial turf need: a kids' soccer field, bocce court, pickleball or a batting cage?"
  answer: "Match the surface to the game, build a level base that holds on clay, and set posts for frost and wind. Soccer suits a short, resilient pile with goals anchored or weighted. Bocce needs a flat, short-pile or sand-filled court. Pickleball needs a hard court, with turf around it. A batting cage needs cage turf, a plate mat and sleeved posts."
faq:
  - q: "Is artificial turf too hot for summer soccer in Northern Colorado?"
    a: "At midday it can be. Penn State's sports surface researchers say synthetic surfaces get hottest from noon to 3 p.m. in sunny, clear, low-humidity weather, and the Colorado Climate Center describes Colorado's humidity as generally quite low. Penn State recommends practice in the mornings and evenings and cites a Brigham Young University rule of no use above 120°F. More in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/)."
  - q: "Will a sports area need infill topped up more often than a lawn?"
    a: "Plan to check it more often. Motz, an infill maker, says infill settles and gets pushed aside in high-traffic areas, that children and pets move it around, and that levels shift more where there are four seasons and plenty of precipitation. Look at the goal mouths, the batter's box and the ends of a bocce court first; see [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/)."
  - q: "Can we paint soccer lines or a bocce foul line on the turf?"
    a: "Use paint made for synthetic turf. The Synthetic Turf Council says temporary turf paint should come off and go back on after at most four applications to avoid build-up, and that permanent lines can age differently from the turf and harden or shrink at different rates. The US Bocce Federation marks its foul line on the side boards as well as the court, so a mark on the border can do the job."
  - q: "Where do a swing set or trampoline go in a sports yard?"
    a: "Out of the line of play, with room around them. The CPSC's home playground handbook wants protective surfacing at least 6 feet out from play equipment on every side. Anchoring through turf is in [anchoring a swing set or trampoline on turf](/guides/swing-set-on-artificial-turf/), and padded play turf is on the [playground turf page](/services/playground-turf/)."
  - q: "Can a putting green share the yard with a sports area?"
    a: "Yes, as its own zone with its own surface and its own shaping. Keep it clear of a cage's open end and a goal's line of fire. Sizing is in [how big a backyard putting green should be](/guides/backyard-putting-green-size/), and the build is on the [putting greens page](/services/putting-greens/)."

**Checked September 27, 2026.** Each game in a backyard sports area wants something different from the turf; all of them need flat ground and posts that stay put through a Northern Colorado winter. Putting greens have [their own guides](/guides/putting-greens/); swing sets and trampolines are in [anchoring play equipment on turf](/guides/swing-set-on-artificial-turf/).

## How much room does each game need?

| Game | What the governing body or maker sets | In feet |
|---|---|---|
| [Soccer, 2v2 (U5–U6)](https://www.usyouthsoccer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/160/2026/01/GFP-1-pager-Game-Facilitator-Cheat-Sheet.pdf) | 22 × 17 yards | 66 × 51 |
| Soccer, 3v3 (U5–U6) | 30 × 18 yards | 90 × 54 |
| [Soccer, 4v4 (U6–U8)](https://www.usyouthsoccer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/160/2023/09/Player-Development-Initiatives-2017.pdf) | 25–35 × 15–25 yards; goals up to 4 ft high and 6 ft wide | 75–105 × 45–75 |
| [Bocce](https://usbf.us/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/open-rules.pdf) | Official court 86.92 × 13.12 ft; smaller courts allowed | [30 × 8 for casual play](https://www.ecograssus.com/blog/bocce-ball-court-with-artificial-turf) |
| [Pickleball](https://usapickleball.org/docs/rules/USAP-Official-Rulebook.pdf) | Court 20 × 44 ft; playing surface at least 30 × 60 ft | 34 × 64 for new construction |
| [Batting cage](https://coversports.com/resources/field-guides/batting-cage-dimensions-setup-guide) | 55 × 14 × 12 ft net for Little League distances | about 70 × 24 × 16 of open space |

- **Soccer:** most yards fit 2v2 or 3v3 before 4v4. US Youth Soccer's Game Format Project moves U5 and U6 recreation play at its member clubs to those smaller games because players get more touches on the ball ([US Youth Soccer](https://www.usyouthsoccer.org/grassroots/)).
- **Batting cage:** size it for the oldest hitter. Little League pitches from 46 feet in the Major Division and below ([Little League](https://www.littleleague.org/league-officials/field-specifications/)); CoverSports, a cage maker, says a 55-foot cage covers that and a 35-foot cage suits tee work and soft toss only.
- **Pickleball:** USA Pickleball wants outdoor courts running north–south, so nobody plays into a low sun ([USA Pickleball](https://usapickleball.org/construction/)).

## Which turf suits soccer, bocce, pickleball and a batting cage?

- **Soccer: a shorter pile.** Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a turf distributor, says shorter piles resist matting and suit sports fields and other high-traffic areas ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/resources/terminology/pile-height-face-weight/)). Keep metal cleats off it: Thompson School District allows only rubber or plastic cleats on its synthetic field ([district rules](https://www.tsd.org/fs/resource-manager/view/b289d026-8bb5-4ea9-843a-74c0f995c236)). Heights are compared in [artificial turf pile height](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/).
- **Bocce: a surface the ball rolls true on.** XGrass, a sports turf maker, builds courts two ways: a 1-inch pile about 75% filled with round-grain sand, or a dense 0.5- to 0.75-inch pile with no infill over a cushion layer, edged with wood or concrete borders ([XGrass](https://www.xgrass.com/bocce-ball-courts.html)). The US Bocce Federation counts synthetic carpets among the court surfaces used in the U.S.
- **Pickleball: a hard court, with turf around it.** USA Pickleball's ball standard is set on stone: an approved ball must bounce 30 to 34 inches when dropped 78 inches onto a granite plate ([Equipment Standards Manual](https://usapickleball.org/docs/rules/USAP-Equipment-Standards-Manual.pdf)). Pickleheads, a pickleball site, says the game can be played on turf but won't bounce as well or as consistently as on a hard surface ([Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/guides/can-you-play-pickleball-on-grass)).
- **Batting cage: cage turf and a plate mat.** CoverSports says grass wears out fast in a cage because the footwork concentrates around home plate, and pairs its cage turf with a home plate mat that fixes the batter's spot.

## How do you keep a court level on clay or a sloping lot?

A lawn hides a small dip; a bocce lane or a goal mouth doesn't. The CPSC says to use movable soccer goals only on level fields ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/5118.pdf)), and EcoGrass says to grade a bocce site that isn't level before building. Northern Colorado ground works against a flat court in two ways:

- **Clay that swells.** The [Colorado Geological Survey](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) says some clays swell up to 20% by volume when wet, and federal soil data flag the Nunn and Weld soils, common around the Larimer foothill towns, Longmont and Frederick–Dacono, for shrink-swell. Find your soil in [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).
- **Frost that lifts.** Frost heave needs fine-grained soil, freezing that reaches into it, and water ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)).

The base answers both: its depth, its compaction and where water leaves it ([what goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/)). XGrass sets outdoor bocce courts on a concrete base with drainage tile. A court still has to shed water; EcoGrass slopes its base about 1 inch per 10 feet, so ask which way yours falls, along the lane or across it.

On a sloping lot, a flat court means cutting into the high side, building up the low side, or both, often with a wall. Grades, walls and wall permits are in [artificial turf on a slope](/guides/artificial-turf-on-a-slope/).

## How deep do goal, net and cage posts go in Northern Colorado?

Below the frost line. Larimer County sets a minimum frost depth of 30 inches below grade for footings ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2022/structural_design_information.pdf)), Weld County designs to 30 inches ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/7/departments/building/documents/design-criteria.pdf)), and Fort Collins and Loveland use 30 inches in their building design criteria. Those are building values, but a net post sits in the same ground.

- **Pickleball posts:** the rulebook sets their spacing, 22 feet apart, and a 3-inch maximum diameter, but not their depth; ask the post maker how deep they go against 30 inches.
- **Cage poles:** CoverSports sets outdoor cage poles in ground sleeves; ask whether they go in before the base, so the turf is cut around them once.
- **Soccer goals:** the CPSC, which has reports of at least 36 deaths since 1979 from goals falling over, says to anchor or counterweight movable goals at all times: at least two auger anchors, one per side, more for some soils and heavier goals, or sandbags and other counterweights on artificial turf ([CPSC guidelines](https://www.cpsc.gov/safety-education/safety-guides/sports-fitness-and-recreation/guidelines-movable-soccer-goals)).
- **Before anyone digs:** Colorado 811 says state law requires contacting it at least two full business days ahead, and that it doesn't mark private lines such as sprinkler lines ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging)). Not legal advice.

## What does Front Range wind do to nets, goals and cages?

The National Weather Service office for northeast Colorado says winds of 60 to near 100 mph reach foothill areas such as Fort Collins, and chinook and bora winds can top 100 mph ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/bou/highwind)). NOAA's storm records include 84 mph in Loveland in the event that began November 29, 2019, and 73 mph at Severance on January 16, 2026 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)).

- **Netting catches more wind than it looks.** Netex, a netting maker, measured drag of 12 to 16% on some common polyester and nylon barrier nets, 50 to 100% more than the 8% often assumed when the poles are designed ([Netex](https://www.netexnetting.com/engineering/)). CoverSports gives its outdoor cage an 18-foot-wide footprint because wind pushes the net.
- **Nets that come down.** Sportsfield Specialties sells netting that raises and lowers on a pulley and breaks away under high wind stress instead of overloading the poles ([Sportsfield Specialties](https://www.sportsfield.com/products/netting-systems/stormguard-multi-sport-netting-systems/)). Ask how a home cage's net comes down, and drop it when high winds are forecast; the NWS says to bring lightweight objects in or tie them down.
- **Goals tip.** The CPSC records a 9-year-old killed when a gust of wind tipped a goal over, and says to take nets off goals not in use and take goals apart for seasonal storage.

## Do town codes and HOAs limit fences, nets and courts?

Yes, and a 12-foot cage is taller than most residential fence limits. Colorado law protects the turf itself: an HOA's landscaping rules may not prohibit artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home, though the HOA can still adopt design and aesthetic guidelines ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5, from SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). Courts, cages and tall nets are another matter: towns and neighborhoods treat them as fences, structures or play equipment. Not legal advice.

## What should I ask an installer about a sports area?

- **Which surface goes where, and why?** A good answer puts a short-pile or sand-filled system on the bocce lane, cage turf and a plate mat in the cage, and a hard court under pickleball.
- **How flat will the court be, and how will you check it?** A good answer gives a tolerance, says which way the water runs, and checks the base with a level or string line before the turf goes down.
- **Where do posts, sleeves and anchors go, and when?** A good answer marks each one on the layout, sets them before the base, and says how deep they go against the 30-inch frost depth.
- **Where do the seams fall?** Seams under constant foot traffic can part, says an installer column from [Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/seam-integration-part-3-perfecting-seams/), so a good layout keeps them out of goal mouths, the batter's box and the bocce lane.
- **Who builds the hard court or the cage, who prepares the HOA packet and any permit, and in what order do the trades work?** The usual sequence is in [outdoor living with artificial turf](/guides/outdoor-living-with-artificial-turf/).
