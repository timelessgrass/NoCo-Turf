  - q: "Can a backyard green have two tiers?"
    a: "Yes, but the step between them eats green. Golf architect John LaFoy writes that the transition on older multi-level greens can leave as much as a third to half of a green uncuppable, and advises exaggerating the step in the first rough grading because the layers laid over it soften it. On a small green, one level with contour run in from the edges usually leaves more places for cups."

A custom green is designed from the ground up: the break lives in the base, the cups sit where you practice, and the edges decide how it meets the yard.

## How is break built into a green, and what does Northern Colorado ground do to it?

Break is shaped into the ground under the turf, never into the turf. The USGA's 2018 construction recommendations have a green's subgrade follow the slope of the finished surface ([USGA recommendations](https://archive.lib.msu.edu/tic/usgamisc/monos/2018recommendationsmethodputtinggreen.pdf)), and synthetic greens work the same way: [Tour Greens](https://www.tourgreens.com/putting-green-installation.html), a putting green builder, shapes contours as the soil is compacted and again as the stone base goes down.

John LaFoy, a past president of the American Society of Golf Course Architects, writes that every successful green must be playable, and describes running mounds from the edge onto the putting surface to add contour without using up the ground where cups go ([ASGCA](https://asgca.org/design/existing-courses/course-renovation-articles/green-design/)).

Then Northern Colorado ground works on it:

- **Clay swells.** The Colorado Geological Survey says expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). On expanding clays the USGA recommends consulting geotechnical engineers and placing fill in 6-inch lifts compacted to at least 90 percent of standard Proctor density.
- **Frost goes deep.** Larimer and Weld County building departments design footings for a 30-inch frost depth, far below a green's base. NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F, and on about 135 of those days it thaws again (1991–2020 normals).
- **That is how greens fail.** Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer columnist lists settlement and frost heave among the ways putting surfaces fail, and recommends shaping contours in 3/8-inch minus over at least 6 inches of compacted rock ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-to-make-your-backyard-putting-green-pga-level-quality/)).

A ridge mounded from loose soil can settle into a different putt after a winter or two, so ask what each contour is built from and how it is compacted.

## Where should the cups go?

Where each one gives a different putt. A superintendent quoted by the USGA spreads hole locations left, right, middle, front and back, and another keeps holes at least five paces, about 15 feet, from the edges, though no rule requires it ([USGA](https://www.usga.org/course-care/forethegolfer/2018/hole-location--location--location.html)). A backyard green rarely has that room, so the spread matters more.

- **Give each cup its own break.** Aim for one uphill putt, one downhill and one breaking each way. LaFoy's ideal course green leaves a ball in the right cupping area an easy putt within 15 to 20 feet and one in the wrong area a double break; a low ridge between two cups does that at home.
- **Keep them in from the edge.** SGW's columnist keeps cups at least 3 feet from the outside fringe, on flat, true ground ([Ask JW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/installing-putting-green-cups/)).
- **Keep them out of the wet.** Course setters pick hole locations where puddling is least likely; a backyard cup is permanent, so never set one where contours gather water.

## How should the collar and fringe meet the rest of the yard?

On a course, the collar is the intermediate surface between green and rough, so a shot that barely misses isn't severely punished. A 2002 USGA Green Section Record article says to build it as part of the green, with no standard width ([Nelson, USGA](https://gsrpdf.lib.msu.edu/?file=%2F2000s%2F2002%2F020709.pdf)); a 2022 article says it earns its place where the green would otherwise meet tall rough ([Bevard, USGA](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/course-care/green-section-record/60/06/strategies-to-prevent-putting-green-collar-decline.html)).

[SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf/fringe/) sells collar turf slightly taller than its putting surface, and [Greenline Artificial Grass's installation guide](https://greenlineartificialgrass.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Synthetic-PuttingGreen-Installation-Guide.pdf) allows 1 to 2 feet of fringe around a green, with every piece's grain run one direction. Past the fringe:

| The green meets | Plan between them |
|---|---|
| Pavers, flagstone or concrete | Fringe wide enough to stop a putt before the stone, and an edge that holds turf against the slab. |
| Rock or mulch beds | A firm edge and a band of fringe, so rock kicked or blown out lands off the putting line. |
| Boulders | Fringe between rock and green, so a long putt doesn't carom. Heron Lakes in [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/) has its McNeil Reservoir Peninsula lots edged in sandstone boulders and strongly encourages putting greens there. |
| A fence or property line | Room to stand and swing, plus any neighborhood setback (below). |

## Should a backyard green have a sand bunker, a turf bunker or neither?

[Tour Greens](https://www.tourgreens.com/residential-design.html) offers both sand and grass bunkers. In a Front Range yard, real sand brings three problems:

- **Wind.** A 2020 USGA Green Section Record article says wind erosion can cost a bunker significant sand ([Whitlark, USGA](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/course-care/green-section-record/58/11/a-guide-to-selecting-the-right-bunker-sand-for-your-course-.html)). NOAA logged 48 High Wind events from 2011 through 2025 in the zone covering Larimer County below 6,000 feet, 44 between October and April.
- **Water.** On clay, a bunker is a hole that collects water. The same article says 4 to 6 inches of sand on the floor has historically worked, and its author has seen drainage fall from 22.8 to 7.5 inches an hour within two to three years as dust, clippings and leaves clog it.
- **Splash.** Every shot out of real sand throws some onto the green and collar.

SYNLawn Golf's SYNSport White is a sand-colored turf it says replicates sand traps without the mess or maintenance ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/products/)). LaFoy also counts grass hollows as part of a green complex: a turf-lined dip gives you something to chip out of with nothing to blow away. Try a shot from a sample of either first.


## Where does the water go when it runs off the contours?

Turf drains through its backing, but a hard storm still runs across it, and contours steer the water. A 1982 article by a USGA Green Section agronomist gives the rule: let surface water leave in at least two, preferably three or four, directions, with no more than 60% going one way, and away from the line of play ([Silva, Weeds Trees & Turf](https://archive.lib.msu.edu/tic/wetrt/article/1982mar48.pdf)).

It also flags a cold-climate problem: on a green that drains back to front, snow melting at the back on a warm afternoon runs across the green and refreezes as ice by evening. With the freeze-thaw rhythm above, that happens here.

- **Shed away from the house and patio.** The residential code wants the ground to fall at least 6 inches in the first 10 feet from a foundation; keep cups out of that band.
- **Split the runoff.** Two or three low edges beat one, and none should sit where you stand to putt.
- **Expect sand to follow the water.** The Synthetic Turf Council advises checking infill after heavy rain in case it migrated with the slope.
- **Give soaked-in water a way out.** Course greens run drains along the lines of maximum fall; ask where water under a green on clay goes.

## How should the green look from the house, the patio and next door?

LaFoy notes that architects often view a green site from where the approach shot lands. At home that spot is the kitchen window, patio or deck: lay a hose along the outline and look from each before the contours are set.

- **Window reflection.** The Synthetic Turf Council reports random cases of polyethylene fibers melting from heat reflected off Low-E glass; check where south-facing Low-E windows reflect on a hot, sunny day, and put plantings or hardscape there if screening isn't practical ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)).
- **Evening.** Heron Lakes requires Dark Sky landscape lighting; see our [guide to lighting a putting green](/guides/putting-green-lighting/).

On a golf-course lot the view runs both ways: Heron Lakes asks course-side lots to consider views to and from the course, the Masters at Mariana Butte in [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) wants them to blend into it, and Windsor Highlands, around Highland Meadows, says views of the course are not protected.

## What will an HOA or metro district ask to see?

**Not legal advice.** Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting artificial turf in a detached single-family home's backyard, though design guidelines still apply, and makes special-district rules that ban backyard turf unenforceable except for attached homes and timeshares ([SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). For a green, review usually covers:

- **A scaled plan.** Heron Lakes wants a professionally designed landscape plan at 1 inch to 10 feet, showing grading, swales, berms and play areas ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)). Barefoot Lakes in [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) wants a plot plan at a measurable scale labeling every material, edging included.
- **Grading and drainage.** Highland Meadows in [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) wants a drainage and grading plan matching the builder's, and its declaration bars changes to the Town-approved grading; Kitchel Lake in [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/) treats a lot's drainage as its Town-approved grading plan, and Heron Lakes requires approval for any drainage change.
- **Setbacks.** RainDance in Windsor reviews putting greens as play equipment: back yard only, at least 6 feet from any property line, with more screening possible ([RainDance guidelines](https://irp-cdn.multiscreensite.com/3d7c42c9/files/uploaded/rain_dance_community_association_-_guidelines_and_standards_for_architectural_design_review-march_27_2019_update.pdf)). Waters Edge in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) waives review for a green no more than 4 feet high, in the back yard and 6 feet from any property line.

Shaping a mound or swale can count as a grading change, depending on the neighborhood's documents; ask the committee before the base is shaped. The green itself is on our [putting greens page](/services/putting-greens/).
