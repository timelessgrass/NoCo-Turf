faq:
  - q: "Can a real lawn go in the first 5 feet when turf can't?"
    a: "Sometimes, under the state version. Its exception covers plants on an approved ignition-resistant list, and the list from CSU Extension and the Colorado State Forest Service rates mowed Kentucky bluegrass 10 and buffalograss 9, where 10 is least ignitable. Larimer County struck that exception for unincorporated land, and Fort Collins uses a list its Director keeps, so ask which list applies to you."
  - q: "Does the code limit turf between 5 and 30 feet from the house?"
    a: "It doesn't name turf there either. On lots mapped at moderate or high intensity, the Intermediate Zone from 5 to 30 feet asks for less fuel: hazardous dead plant material removed, and no large piles of logs, branches, slash or combustible mulch. On lots mapped low, the Intermediate Zone rules don't apply."
  - q: "What if the map puts my lot in the wrong class?"
    a: "The code has a ground-truthing process. An owner can ask for a review of the parcel's mapped fire intensity, based on the fuels on the parcel and within 300 feet of its boundary, the terrain, local weather and fire modeling, documented by a qualified wildfire professional. Ask your building office how it handles a request."
  - q: "Who adopts the code where I live?"
    a: "Under SB23-166, a city or town council adopts it inside town limits, a fire protection district's board can adopt it for the district, and county commissioners adopt it only for land outside both. Weld County's version reaches parcels outside towns and fire protection districts that the state map shows, from July 1, 2026. Not legal advice."
  - q: "Was the April 2026 deadline pushed back?"
    a: "No. HB26-1334 would have moved the local adoption deadline to April 1, 2027, but a House committee postponed it indefinitely on April 7, 2026. The April 1, 2026 date, nine months after the board's adoption under SB25-142, was not changed."
  - q: "Who keeps the 5-foot band clear after the house is built?"
    a: "The owner. The code says required landscape materials, vegetation and defensible space must be maintained to the code edition they were installed under. Windsor's version adds that the duty runs with the land and can fall on an HOA or metro district that controls the ground."
  - q: "Does a fire-rated turf count as noncombustible?"
    a: "The code counts a material as noncombustible only if no part of it will ignite and burn when subjected to fire, or if it conforms to ASTM E136, and IBHS lists artificial turf among combustible groundcovers. A turf product would need the code official's approval as an alternative. What a spec sheet's fire test shows is in our guide to whether artificial turf is flammable."

**Not legal advice:** the state code and the local versions below are summarized in plain English as read in October 2026. Your building office has the final word.

Colorado's Wildfire Resiliency Code decides what may sit in the first 5 feet around a new or rebuilt home in mapped wildfire areas. It says nothing about artificial turf, which leaves the question with your local code official.

## Does Colorado's wildfire code say anything about artificial turf?

No. The words turf, artificial, synthetic and lawn appear nowhere in the 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code, Version 1.0, which the state established on July 1, 2025 ([code text](https://dfpc.colorado.gov/colorado-wildfire-resiliency-code); [Division of Fire Prevention and Control](https://dfpc.colorado.gov/sections/wildfire-resiliency-code-board)). What Section 502.1 does say about the Immediate Zone, the first 5 feet around a structure:

- **Its job:** reduce or eliminate ember ignition and direct flame contact with the structure, decks, stairs and attachments.
- **What goes there:** noncombustible, hard surface materials, such as rock, gravel, sand, concrete, bare earth or stone and concrete pavers.
- **The one exception:** ignition-resistant plantings on an approved list that is not less than the Colorado State Forest Service's.
- **What comes out:** shrubs, slash, combustible mulch and other woody debris.

The only plastic product the code's site chapter names is vinyl fencing, which may be allowed within 8 feet of a structure. Turf gets no line of its own.

That silence isn't permission. The code lets its official approve a material it doesn't prescribe only on a written request showing the alternative is at least equivalent for its purpose, fire safety included, and the official must put the reasons for a refusal in writing. If turf is planned inside 5 feet of a house that is under a permit, that request is the conversation to have before anything is ordered.

The guidance around the code points the same way:

- **CSFS:** the code tells officials to refer to the Colorado State Forest Service's Home Ignition Zone guide, whose Zone 1 asks for rock, gravel, sand, cement, bare earth or pavers and doesn't mention turf ([HIZ guide](https://csfs.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/2021_CSFS_HIZGuide_Web.pdf)).
- **NFPA:** Firewise calls the area 0 to 5 feet from the furthest attached point of the home a non-combustible area, the most important zone to act on ([NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/wildfire/preparing-homes-for-wildfire)).
- **IBHS:** the Wildfire Prepared Home designation standard is the one that names artificial turf, and it is stricter than the state code: it clears all vegetation, grass included, combustible groundcover such as turf, and combustible fencing, vinyl included, from the first 5 feet ([IBHS standard](https://wildfireprepared.org/wp-content/uploads/WFPH-Technical-Standard-12-25.pdf)).

## Why can living grass sometimes go where turf can't?

Because the state exception is written for plants, and the state's plant list includes lawn grasses.

CSU Extension and the Colorado State Forest Service's Fact Sheet 6.305, updated July 2025, scores plants from 0, the most ignitable, to 10, the least, and recommends only 8 to 10 within 30 feet of a building. Its turf grass table, mowed to 4 inches, rates Kentucky bluegrass 10 and buffalograss 9 ([fact sheet 6.305](https://csfs.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CSFS_CSU-Ext_Fact-Sheets_IRLP_2025_081825-2_web_accessible.pdf)). The state's own summary of the site rules ties the Immediate Zone exception to that fact sheet ([DFPC key requirements](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IEBV2iICJnv6mvtBrEwov2peQSuYyjtQ/view)). Artificial turf isn't a plant, and it isn't on the list.

Three cautions before you plan a strip of real grass at the wall:

1. **No plant is fireproof.** The fact sheet says so, names moisture content as the biggest factor in how readily a plant burns, and says plants must be maintained to stay ignition resistant.
2. **Larimer County struck the exception.** In its version for unincorporated land, the first 5 feet take hard surfaces only and all plantings come out ([Larimer County code](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/2025-wildfire-resiliency-code-w-lc-amendments.pdf)).
3. **Fort Collins keeps its own list.** Its Land Use Code allows hard surfaces and ignition-resistant plantings on a list kept by its Director in the 5-foot zone ([Ordinance No. 222, 2025](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/fortcollco-meet-9b4e8e099e3f4e9cbeaa4a206b542f9a/ITEM-Attachment-004-241d5ceeae6f47f78fb732207afe77eb.pdf)).

Whether a low-water grass suits the rest of the yard better than turf is in [artificial turf vs low-water grass](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-low-water-grass/).

## When does the code reach a yard?

Mostly when a building permit does. The Division of Fire Prevention and Control calls it a state minimum code for new construction and significant exterior alterations and additions, and says it "is not a retrofit code" ([CWRC What and Why](https://dfpc.colorado.gov/cwrc-what-and-why)).

| Situation | What the state code says |
|---|---|
| A new home in a mapped area | The 5-foot Immediate Zone applies at every fire intensity |
| An addition of more than 500 square feet of footprint | Covered; smaller additions are exempt |
| Replacing 25 percent or more of the exterior walls | The whole wall surface goes to new-construction materials, and the zone within 5 feet is brought into compliance |
| Exterior repairs under 25 percent, painting, interior work | Exempt |
| A 35-acre parcel with one home that doesn't abut a residential or commercial area | Exempt |
| Turf laid at an existing home with no permit | Not addressed. Conditions legal at adoption may continue, but the code also applies to conditions that arise after it. Ask first. |

Once a house is built under the code, the owner must keep its required landscape materials, vegetation and defensible space to the code edition they went in under. So plan turf to stop where the approved band ends rather than fill it later.

## Is my Northern Colorado address on the wildfire map?

Look the address up on the [2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code Map](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/34c113129c044004bc672ca5493378de/), then ask which map your town or county uses.

The Division of Fire Prevention and Control and the Colorado State Forest Service built the map to cover current and potential wildland-urban interface, including every place in Colorado with combustible vegetative fuels, so it reaches well past the foothills ([map description](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=34c113129c044004bc672ca5493378de)). It shows three classes as hexagons:

- **Low, in yellow:** light to medium fuels such as grasses, shrubs and scattered vegetation. These lots follow Class 1, which includes the 5-foot Immediate Zone.
- **Moderate and high, in orange and red:** dense shrubs and small trees, up to dense forest and heavy dead material. These lots follow Class 2, which adds an Intermediate Zone from 5 to 30 feet and an Expanded Zone from 30 to 100 feet.

A local government may adopt the state map or make its own. As read in October 2026:

| Where | What its code says |
|---|---|
| Unincorporated Larimer County | All of it is deemed wildland-urban interface, and the 5-foot zone takes hard surfaces only |
| Unincorporated Weld County | The state map, on parcels outside towns and fire protection districts, enforced from July 1, 2026 |
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | Interface areas follow the state map; the 5-foot landscaping rule is in the Land Use Code ([City Code Ch. 5](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH5BUBURE_ARTIXWIREST)) |
| [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | The state code and its map, with a compliance date of July 1, 2026 ([Windsor code](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH18BURE_ARTXIFIZO_S18-11-40ADCOWIRECO)) |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | Adopted March 17, 2026; the city will develop and adopt its own map ([Greeley code](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT22BUCO_CH17WIRECO)) |
| [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) | Adopted March 24, 2026; the Chief Fire Marshal maintains the city's map ([Longmont code](https://library.municode.com/co/longmont/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT16BUCO_CH16.54COWIRECO)) |
| [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/) | Adopted, effective January 1, 2026 ([Wellington code](https://library.municode.com/co/wellington/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH18BURE_ART7.52025COWIRECO)) |
| Loveland, Berthoud, Estes Park, Timnath and other towns | It depends on the town: ask its building office which code and which map apply |

Weld County's terms are in its [Sec. 7-1-10](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH7ADMISTCO_ARTICOWIRECO_S7-1-10ADCOWIRECOEFDAAP).

## When was the code due, and did the deadline move?

- **July 1, 2025:** the Wildfire Resiliency Code Board adopted the code, established through the state rule 8 CCR 1507-39.
- **Nine months to adopt:** [SB25-142](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25-142), signed June 3, 2025, gave cities and towns 9 months instead of 3 to adopt a code that meets or exceeds the board's. The Division of Fire Prevention and Control put the deadline at April 1, 2026, with enforcement no later than 3 months after each adoption ([Fall 2025 journal](https://dfpc.colorado.gov/sites/dfpc/files/documents/FLSNewsletterFall2025.pdf)).
- **No extension:** [HB26-1334](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1334) would have moved the deadline to April 1, 2027. A House committee postponed it indefinitely on April 7, 2026.

Who must adopt comes from [SB23-166](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/94092/download): city and town councils, fire protection district boards, and counties for land outside both.

## What does this mean for turf near a house in the Larimer County foothills?

On unincorporated land west of [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/), [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) or [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/), or around [Estes Park](/areas/estes-park-co/), plan as if the first 5 feet are hard surface, because for new work the county's code says they are.

1. **Draw the band first.** Rock, gravel, concrete or pavers for 5 feet. NFPA measures from the furthest attached point of the home, so a deck or stair moves the line out.
2. **Start the turf past it,** with an edge that holds the line: [edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/).
3. **Keep 5 to 30 feet lean.** Under the state code, lots mapped moderate or high must clear hazardous dead plant material there and avoid big piles of mulch or slash; ask the county how its version applies. Turf isn't named in that zone.
4. **Before a permit, ask in writing.** For a new home, an addition of more than 500 square feet or re-siding a quarter of the walls, ask the county building office how it treats turf near the band.
5. **With no permit, still ask.** Whether turf laid now at an existing house counts as a new condition is the code official's call.

Lot-wide planning for foothill and plains acreage is in [artificial turf on acreage lots](/guides/artificial-turf-on-acreage-lots/), how turf itself behaves near heat and flame is in [is artificial turf flammable](/guides/is-artificial-turf-flammable/), and an HOA's room to protect fire buffers is in [HOA approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/). Ask an installer where the turf will stop and what fills the band; a good answer gives a distance from the walls and decks and names the hard surface. For the turf itself, see [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/).
