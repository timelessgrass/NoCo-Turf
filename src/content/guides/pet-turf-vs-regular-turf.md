  - q: "Does pet turf need infill?"
    a: "It depends on the product. ForeverLawn's K9Grass specification says no infill is to be used with it, while other makers pair their pet turf with one.   - q: "Will pet turf keep my yard from smelling?"
    a: "Not by itself. Urine that stays in the base smells whatever the label says, and in Firestone the code lists odors among the kinds of deterioration a turf area may not have. Rinsing and drainage do most of the work."
  - q: "Can the dog's corner get pet turf and the rest of the yard regular turf?"
    a: "Yes, and Barefoot Lakes' guidelines in Firestone allow for it: a pet-only area may use a product with less secondary thatch, though the same blade-height and face-weight minimums still apply."

"Pet turf" is a label each maker applies its own way. Sometimes it names a different product: a shorter pile, nylon yarn, a backing built to pass liquid anywhere. Sometimes it names the same product under a second name. The spec sheet tells you which, and the ground under the turf decides how much any of it matters. Products below are named as published examples, not recommendations.

## What does a "pet turf" label actually change?

Depending on the maker, one or more of these:

- **The backing.** Most turf has a coated backing punched with drain holes; the distributor Synthetic Grass Warehouse calls hole-punch "the industry standard for most synthetic grass products" ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-does-backing-quality-affect-your-artificial-turfs-drainage/)). Some dog-specific products, the K9Grass line among them, use a knitted backing that lets liquid through across the whole sheet instead.
- **The yarn.** Some pet products use nylon: K9Grass Elite is all nylon, and K9Grass Classic+ mixes nylon with polyethylene.
- **The pile.** Both K9Grass products are 7/8 inch, well under the 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches on the SYNLawn sheets below. Our [pile height guide](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/) covers what length does in a yard.
- **An antimicrobial in the yarn.** What that does, and doesn't do, is further down.
- **Infill.** Some dog products are made to go down with none; others expect one.

## Is a flow-through backing worth it for one dog?

Often not, by one distributor's own account. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installation column sorts its line three ways: landscape turf with drainage holes "may be used for Pets" at 1 to 3 pets; its pet turf for multiple pets; and its 100% permeable pet turf for high pet use, small condensed areas, kennels and dog parks ([SGW, Ask JW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/100-permeable-pet-turf/)).

The reason it gives is the gap between holes: when liquid can't flow, "contaminants may remain between drainage holes" and linger as odor. That matters most where urine keeps landing on the same few square feet: a side-yard potty strip, a small run, several dogs. One to three dogs is the case the distributor sells its ordinary landscape turf for.

How a dog run is built around these backings is on our [pet turf and dog runs page](/services/pet-turf/).

## What does an antimicrobial in the yarn actually do?

It protects the turf. Under EPA's treated-articles exemption, a product with a built-in antimicrobial may claim protection for the article itself, and EPA lists "a claim to inhibit offensive odors in the treated article" among the allowed non-public-health claims. Claims against germs that make people sick fall outside the exemption ([EPA PR Notice 2000-1](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-04/documents/pr2000-1.pdf)).

Under that exemption, "antimicrobial" on a turf sheet is a claim about the turf itself, not a promise that the turf protects your family from germs. Two more things to know:

- **It isn't a pet-only feature.** SYNLawn builds Sanitized antimicrobial into SYNAugustine 847, which it also sells for lawns ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/product/synaugustine-847/)).
- **Ask where the treatment is.** On the K9Grass sheets it is "manufactured into yarn." Urine that has drained through the backing is below the yarn, in the base, where rinsing and drainage do the work.

## Will a short pet turf pass Firestone's rules, or an HOA's?

Firestone writes turf product specs into its town code. Its turf standard sets a pile height of 1.75 to 3 inches and a face weight of at least 70 ounces, allows blades of polyethylene, polypropylene or a vegetable-based product and prohibits nylon, bans felt backing, and requires a permeable backing "with holes spaced in a uniform grid" no more than 4 by 6 inches on center. It also requires a town permit first, with no fee for single-family homes ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)).

Read the table against that:

- **Both K9Grass products** fall short on pile height and face weight, and both carry nylon.
- **The SYNLawn sheets** fall short on pile height; SYNCanine also falls short on face weight.
- **A flow-through or woven backing** has no punched holes at all, and the code doesn't say how that is treated.

Firestone's code also allows "alternative equivalent compliance" with its landscaping section, approved in writing by the Director for by-right projects ([Firestone 16.6.1](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.1GEPR)). Whether a backyard dog run qualifies isn't settled in the text. It depends on the product and the yard: ask Firestone Planning before you order. This is general information, not legal advice.

In Barefoot Lakes, the metro district's guidelines repeat the 1.75 to 3 inch blade and 70-ounce face weight and call for infill such as silica sand or Envirofill in kid and pet areas ([St. Vrain Lakes Metropolitan Districts](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). Its review committee keeps a [pre-approved turf list](https://www.stvrainmd.live/design-review), and a product not on it goes in with a sample and specifications. Elsewhere, Colorado law keeps an HOA from banning turf in a detached home's backyard but still lets it adopt design and aesthetic guidelines, so check yours for product minimums before ordering a short pet product; see [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) and the [town-by-town turf rules](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## Does a pet backing help if my yard is clay?

Only as far as the ground under it lets liquid go. The K9Grass Classic+ specification says so itself: if the sub-base doesn't let liquids "freely percolate," it requires auxiliary drainage. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's column goes further: soil that doesn't drain has to come out and be replaced with a drainage material that holds compaction.

That is a common Northern Colorado yard:

- [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) tells residents its "clay soils absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)).
- [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/)'s water department says local soils are shallow, with hard clay "sometimes just inches below the surface" ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)).
- Federal soil surveys flag the Nunn and Weld soils, common around the Larimer foothill towns, Longmont and Frederick–Dacono, for shrink-swell (USDA-NRCS soil survey data).

A backing that passes urine anywhere still hands it to the base. On slow clay, that moves where the urine sits, not whether it leaves. If you are still deciding between turf and grass for the dogs at all, start with [artificial turf vs grass for dogs](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-grass-for-dogs/).

## What should I ask before paying more for pet turf?

- **Can I see the pet product's spec sheet next to the lawn product you'd otherwise use?** Compare pile height, face weight, yarn and backing. If they match, you are paying for the name.
- **What kind of backing is it?** A good answer names it from the sheet: hole-punched, with the spacing, or knitted or woven flow-through.
- **Where is the antimicrobial, and what does the maker claim for it?** A good answer says it treats the yarn and doesn't promise a germ-free yard.
- **Does the maker require infill, forbid it, or leave it open?** See [dog turf infill](/guides/dog-turf-infill/).
- **Does the choice fit my dogs?** One dog across a backyard and three dogs in a side-yard run are different jobs, and a good answer asks which you have.
- **What happens under it on my soil?** A good answer describes the base and what happens if the clay below doesn't percolate, not only the turf.
- **Does it meet my town's and HOA's minimums?** In [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), check pile height, face weight, nylon and backing before ordering. A spec sheet to hold any product against is in [a turf spec sheet for Colorado](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/).
