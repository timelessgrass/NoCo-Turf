For most dogs and cats the turf is an easy surface; the trouble comes from sun, from what a pet swallows, from cleaners and from cats in the infill. Lead and PFAS concern pets as much as children, and that evidence is in [is artificial turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/). Turf or grass for a dog at all: [artificial turf vs grass for dogs](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-grass-for-dogs/).

## How hot is too hot for paws on artificial turf?

Penn State's Center for Sports Surface Research says synthetic turf generally runs 35 to 55°F hotter than natural grass, and gets hottest on sunny, clear, dry days between noon and 3 p.m. ([Penn State](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature.pdf/@@download/file/temperature.pdf)).

Paw pads are tough, but VCA Animal Hospitals says they can still burn on a scorching summer surface ([VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/first-aid-for-torn-or-injured-foot-pads-in-dogs)). Cornell's veterinary college gives a test written for pavement that works on turf too: if you can't hold your hand on it, or stand on it barefoot, for about 10 seconds, it is likely too hot for your dog ([Cornell](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/summer-heat-safety-tips-dogs)). Test the spot where the dog lies and plays, in the afternoon sun.

If a dog comes in licking its feet or limping, VCA says to soak the feet in room-temperature water, and to call your veterinarian if the pads look discolored or the tissue under a pad shows.

The hot-paw season runs longer on the Weld plains. By NOAA's 1991–2020 normals, the Greeley UNC station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F and 6.5 at or above 100°F, while the Fort Collins station averages 29.5 days at or above 90°F ([NOAA, Greeley UNC](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json); [NOAA, Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). A dog yard in [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) has about twice as many hot afternoons to plan around as one in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/). Every town's figures and what cools turf down are in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

Cornell says the day is hottest around 3 to 5 p.m. and suggests walks in the early morning or evening; turf time fits the same hours.

## Which dogs overheat first, and what are the warning signs?

Paws are only part of it. Cornell calls heatstroke a life-threatening emergency, and notes that dogs sweat only through their paws and cool themselves mainly by panting. Some run out of margin sooner:

- Short-muzzled breeds such as pugs, bulldogs and boxers, which can't pant as efficiently.
- Overweight and elderly dogs, and dogs with heart or breathing conditions.
- Dogs with thick or dark coats.
- Any dog in early summer, before it is used to the heat.

CSU's James L. Voss Veterinary Teaching Hospital in Fort Collins lists the early signs of heat exhaustion: heavy panting and fast breathing, bright red gums and tongue, weakness, and a body temperature of 104°F or more ([CSU](https://cvmbs.source.colostate.edu/pet-health-protect-your-dog-from-heat-exhaustion-and-heat-stroke-during-summer-months/)). Take the dog out of the heat, bathe it in cool water, put cloth-covered ice packs on the groin and recheck its temperature every 10 minutes. White or blue gums, labored breathing, a dog that won't move or one that loses consciousness are signs of heat stroke, and need a veterinarian at once.

CSU's everyday rule fits a turf yard: fresh water and shade, especially in the heat of the day. A dog run in full afternoon sun breaks it by design; laying out a dog area is on the [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/) page.

## What if my dog chews the turf or eats the infill?

Start with the edges. K9Grass, a pet-turf maker, says turf held down only by spikes or infill weight leaves edges a dog can lift, after which the blades and spikes become things it can swallow; it adds that tufted blades can be pulled out, a pitch for its own knitted turf ([K9Grass](https://k9grass.com/faq/do-dogs-chew-on-dog-friendly-turf/)). Block off a lifted edge or seam until it is fixed ([how to repair artificial turf](/guides/how-to-repair-artificial-turf/)), and see [can dogs dig through artificial turf](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/) for a dog that works at it.

Plastic is on VCA's list of things dogs with pica eat, and the most common result is a gastrointestinal obstruction, which can cause pain, reduced appetite and vomiting ([VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/pica-in-dogs)). A dog that vomits or goes off its food after chewing turf needs a vet.

What the paperwork says about swallowing infill:

| Infill | What the paper says | Read it with |
|---|---|---|
| Zeolite ([ZeoFill safety data sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0590/5448/9763/files/ZeoFill_Products_SDS.pdf)) | Not classified as hazardous; no known toxicity to plants or animals; if swallowed, rinse the mouth and drink plenty of water | That first aid, in Section 4, is written for people |
| Acrylic-coated sand ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill-faq/), maker of Envirofill) | Won't cause serious harm if kids or pets swallow it by accident; passes ASTM F3188 | A maker's FAQ, not a safety data sheet |
| Plain silica sand ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/silica-crystalline)) | Silica's known hazard is breathing fine dust, particles at least 100 times smaller than playground sand | Ask for the bag's safety data sheet and read Sections 4 and 11 |

ASTM F3188 is the test that speaks to swallowing: it measures the metals that could be extracted from infill in a fluid that approximates the stomach, against the limits for children's toys ([ASTM](https://store.astm.org/f3188-16.html)). It was written for sports fields, but it's a fair result to ask for. Choosing one is covered in [dog turf infill](/guides/dog-turf-infill/).

A dog that keeps eating infill needs a vet before a new infill: VCA lists medical causes of pica, among them intestinal parasites and an imbalanced diet.

## Which turf cleaners are safe around dogs and cats?

Read "EPA-registered" for what it is. Disinfectants are pesticides; EPA verifies that a registered one works according to its label directions, says disinfectants "can be harmful," and states that its product lists are not an endorsement ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/selected-epa-registered-disinfectants)). The federal labeling rule goes further: a pesticide label that calls the product safe, harmless or "nontoxic to humans and pets" is misbranded ([40 CFR 156.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-156/subpart-A/section-156.10)). On a registered turf disinfectant, the pet advice lives in the precautions and directions, such as how long to keep animals off, not in a "pet safe" badge.

Enzyme cleaners and deodorizers that only clean are a different product. EPA regulates cleaning products only if they sanitize or disinfect ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/coronavirus/whats-difference-between-products-disinfect-sanitize-and-clean-surfaces)), so "pet safe" on one of those is the maker's own word. The ASPCA's poison control staff do say the enzymes in cleaners generally have a wide margin of safety ([ASPCApro](https://www.aspcapro.org/resource/ingredients-toxicities-cleaning-products)).

Cats need the closer read:

- **Quats.** Cationic surfactants such as benzalkonium chloride turn up in sanitizers and germicides, and the ASPCA's toxicologists say cats appear sensitive to them.
- **Essential oils.** Cats are especially sensitive, and the ASPCA advises against oils where pets have access unless they are supervised or a vet approves ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/pet-poisons-be-mindful-these-household-products-and-cleaning-agents)). An oil-scented deodorizer isn't automatically gentler.

Whatever the product, the National Pesticide Information Center's routine fits: move pets, toys and bowls out first, and keep pets off until the treated area is completely dry ([NPIC](https://npic.orst.edu/faq/pets.html)).

Then mind the rinse water. In Fort Collins, storm sewers carry runoff to the creeks and the Poudre River without treatment, and driveways drain to creeks ([Fort Collins Utilities](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Other-Utilities-Pages/Water/Stormwater)), so rinse toward the middle of the turf. Each town's stormwater rules are in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/); odor is in [dog urine smell in artificial turf](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/).

## Will cats use the infill as a litter box?

Some will. Cornell's Feline Health Center says most cats prefer unscented, fine-textured litter ([Cornell](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/choosing-and-caring-your-new-cat)), and sand infill is exactly that. Humane World for Animals even suggests a mound of sand as an outdoor litter box for neighborhood cats ([Humane World for Animals](https://www.humaneworld.org/en/resources/how-keep-stray-cats-away)).

Cornell says cat feces carrying mature Toxoplasma oocysts are a source of infection whether they sit in a litter box, a garden or a sandbox outdoor cats have used, and that the oocysts can survive well over a year ([Cornell](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/toxoplasmosis-cats)). Cornell and the CDC both say to keep sandboxes covered ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/prevention/index.html)). A lawn can't be covered, so:

- **Pick droppings up daily.** The CDC says Toxoplasma doesn't become infectious until 1 to 5 days after a cat sheds it.
- **Wear gloves** and wash your hands afterward, as the CDC advises for soil or sand cats may have used.
- **Rinse and treat the spot** as you would for a dog: see [dog poop on artificial turf](/guides/dog-poop-on-artificial-turf/). Where small children play, see [playground turf](/services/playground-turf/).

For the neighbors' cats, Humane World for Animals says motion-activated sprinklers work, often within a few months, but can't be used in cold weather because the hose freezes. The Fort Collins station averages 150 days a year with a low at or below 32°F, so here it's a spring-to-fall tool. Pair it with a sand spot of their own in a corner you don't mind.

## What should I ask an installer before the pets move in?

- **How are the edges and seams fastened?** A good answer: fixed to a solid edge all the way round, not held by infill weight or a few spikes. K9Grass calls for a plastic perimeter board with stainless staples every inch.
- **Which infill, by name, and may I see its safety data sheet and ASTM F3188 result?** A good answer hands you both.
- **Where will the shade fall at 3 p.m.?** A good answer walks the dog's part of the yard with you.
- **What cleaner do you suggest?** A good answer names it. If it disinfects, it has an EPA registration number; an enzyme cleaner that only cleans won't.
- **Where does rinse water go?** Down through the turf and base, not across the driveway.

What else changes between pet turf and ordinary turf is in [pet turf vs regular turf](/guides/pet-turf-vs-regular-turf/).
