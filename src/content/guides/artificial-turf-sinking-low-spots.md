faq:
  - q: "Will a low spot in artificial turf level itself out?"
    a: "No. Turf lies on whatever the base does, and a hollow in the base doesn't refill on its own. A dip that is still getting deeper needs its cause found first; one that has stopped moving still needs the base under it rebuilt to come back level."
  - q: "Can a hot tub, shed or parked trailer make turf sink?"
    a: "It can press a dip into a base that wasn't built for that load. Whether turf can carry a vehicle or a heavy fixture, and what goes under it if so, is in our guide to parking on artificial turf."
  - q: "Does a dip next to the house mean a foundation problem?"
    a: "Not by itself. But the Colorado Geological Survey lists poor surface drainage near foundations among the landscaping problems on expansive soils, so a dip that holds water against the house should be fixed soon. Cracks inside the house are a question for a structural or geotechnical engineer, not a turf crew."
  - q: "Does a putting green need to be flatter than a lawn?"
    a: "Yes, because a rolling ball finds any dip a foot would miss. FusionTurf aims for about 1/4 inch over 10 feet on performance areas, against about 1/2 inch for general landscapes."
  - q: "Can a dip be fixed without cutting the turf?"
    a: "Often, when it sits near an edge or an existing seam, because the turf can fold back from there. A dip in the middle of a piece may need a cut and a new seam. Ask where the turf will be opened and how the seam will be closed."

A low spot in artificial turf is the ground under it moving. If your turf is bunched into ripples rather than sunk, that is a turf problem: see [wrinkles and ripples in artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-wrinkles-ripples/). This page is about the ground under the turf, and how a dip gets rebuilt.

## What makes artificial turf sink in one spot?

Turf is a thin sheet on compacted rock, and it copies whatever the rock does. From the Synthetic Turf Council's field guidelines: "Irregularities in the surface of the base materials are typically reflected in the finished field surface" ([STC, 2011](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Suggested_Guidelines_for.pdf)). The usual causes:

- **Ground that was never compacted.** A plate compactor can't reach edges, corners or the ground around posts and valve boxes, which need a hand tamper. Ground left short of adequate density "will continue to compress, deform or erode after construction," says Pavement Interactive, an engineering reference ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/construction/site-preparation/subgrade-preparation-for-new-pavements/)).
- **A settled trench.** Sprinkler mains, drains and utility lines sit in refilled trenches, and the same STC guidelines ask for "special attention" to compacting trench backfill under turf. On a newer lot, soil backfilled against the foundation settles too; a Colorado Geological Survey homeowner guide warns that "not correcting settlement of the soil near foundations, will create ponding of rain, snowmelt and irrigation water" ([CGS SP-11, 1978](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-11.pdf)). See [turf on a new build](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/).
- **Water where it shouldn't be.** A lawn zone capped at the heads can leak under the turf. A turf distributor's installer guidance says threaded caps "may leak over time, eroding the soil, and causing depressions in the turf surface" ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/irrigation/)). The Colorado Geological Survey says collapsible soils can lose 10 to 15 percent of their volume when wetted, and names unrepaired leaking water lines among the causes ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/collapsible-soils/)). A downspout emptying onto the turf does the same from above; SP-11 says downspouts should carry roof runoff at least 10 feet from the house. Shutting lawn zones off is in [your sprinkler system after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).
- **Clay that swells and shrinks.** CGS calls expansive soil "Colorado's most significant geologic hazard," with clays that can swell up to 20% by volume when wet, and says that when "a previously wet expansive clay is dried" it shrinks, as many times as the cycle repeats ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). A lawn watered for years, then left dry under turf, can be that clay. SP-11 traces uneven movement to uneven moisture: drainage, local watering, and trees drawing water from the soil. Federal soil surveys flag the Nunn and Weld soils, common around the Larimer foothill towns, [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) and [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/)–[Dacono](/areas/dacono-co/), for shrink-swell (USDA-NRCS soil survey data). See [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).
- **Something buried that rots.** Sod, roots or a stump left under the base break down and leave a hollow. The Synthetic Turf Council's base guidelines strip out "stumps and roots" before rock goes in ([STC, 2017](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Base_Syst.pdf)); see [turf over grass or dirt](/guides/can-you-put-artificial-turf-over-grass-or-dirt/).
- **Burrowing animals.** CSU Extension lists "spongy soil from burrowing activity" among vole signs, and says most vole damage happens in winter, under snow ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/managing-voles-in-colorado/)). Keeping them out is in [gopher and vole wire under turf](/guides/gopher-wire-under-artificial-turf/).

## How can I tell what's under the dip from its shape?

A dip's shape and place usually point to its cause before anyone digs.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to check first |
|---|---|---|
| A straight, narrow dip across the yard | A settled trench: sprinkler main, drain or utility line | A valve box or meter at one end |
| A band along the house | Foundation backfill settling | Whether the ground still falls away from the house |
| A round soft spot near a head, valve or line, sometimes damp | A leaking or capped sprinkler line | Run each zone and watch the spot |
| A hollow under a downspout | Roof water softening the base and soil | Where the downspout empties |
| A broad, shallow bowl that changes with the seasons | Clay shrinking and swelling, or a base compacted unevenly | Whether it grows after a wet spring or a dry summer |
| Soft, winding lines, spongy underfoot, often after snowmelt | Voles or other burrowing animals | Open holes along the turf's edges |
| Low edges, corners, or a ring around a post | Ground the compactor couldn't reach | Whether the dip follows the edge |

Then measure it: a straight board or taut string across the dip, a tape at the lowest point. FusionTurf calls about 1/2 inch over 10 feet acceptable for general landscapes; the Synthetic Turf Council holds a sports-field base to 1/4 inch under a 10-foot straightedge. Photograph the tape, date it, and measure again in a few weeks. A dip that keeps deepening means its cause is still at work; rebuilding before it is found only buys time.

Winter can fool you: wet, fine-grained soil can frost-heave, and a sudden thaw traps meltwater over the still-frozen layer ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)). Judge a winter bump or dip after the thaw; see [what winter does to turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

## How is a low spot in artificial turf fixed?

By rebuilding the base under it. The sequence is [FusionTurf's](https://find.fusionturf.com/answers/can-i-use-a-plate-compactor-when-repairing-my-turf-base/), with ForeverLawn where it adds something:

1. **Open the turf.** Fold it back from the nearest edge or seam, protect the backing, and mark the soft or ponding area.
2. **Dig out what failed.** Loose or contaminated base comes out "until you reach firm support." Digging below the base is excavation: Colorado 811 says any digging, however shallow, needs a locate first, and it doesn't mark your sprinkler lines ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging); [private lines](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/find-a-private-locating-company)).
3. **Fix the cause.** Repair the leak or move the downspout's water, whichever made the dip. FusionTurf also dries or stabilizes a subgrade that pumps water, digs out organic soil or clay pockets, and gives trapped water "a path with slope or a drain line before rebuilding."
4. **Refill in thin, damp lifts.** Clean, angular base rock goes back in 1- to 2-inch lifts, misted "damp, not muddy," each compacted with 2 to 4 overlapping passes. FusionTurf rules out "round rock, pea gravel, topsoil, or clay." ForeverLawn spreads rock a little high because it settles on compaction, and adds more if it settles too much ([ForeverLawn](https://www.landscapesbyforeverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/DIY-Landscape-Installation-Guidelines-.pdf)).
5. **Check it flat.** Run the straightedge or string across the patch and onto the old base on every side.
6. **Relay and close it up.** Reset the seams, re-fasten the edges, brush the fibers up and put the infill back. Seam repairs are in [visible seams in artificial turf](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).

What doesn't fix a dip: brushing extra infill or sand into it, or stuffing soil under the backing. That hides the hollow for a while; the ground keeps moving. The rock itself is in [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).

## When in a Northern Colorado year can a dip be repaired?

When the ground has thawed and the seams can cure:

- **Not over frozen ground.** Colorado's highway specifications bar frozen material from fill and compact base within 2 percent of its optimum moisture ([CDOT](https://www.codot.gov/business/designsupport/construction-specifications/2025-construction-specifications/specs-book/2025-cdot-specs-book.pdf)). A patch compacted over frost can drop when it thaws.
- **Warm enough for seam glue.** One seam adhesive's data sheet bars use below 5°C (41°F); it hardens in 24 hours ([MAPEI](https://cdnmedia.mapei.com/docs/librariesprovider2/products-documents/1_00260_ultrabond-turf-pu-2k_en_093f204613974a27aeab019c84085115.pdf?sfvrsn=1831d6bc_0)).

Snowmelt-soaked clay also has to dry before it will compact. Seasonal timing is in [when to install artificial turf](/guides/when-to-install-artificial-turf/).

## Is a sinking spot the installer's problem?

It depends on the cause and on your written terms. General information, not legal advice:

- **Often the installer's:** a dip in the first seasons over uncompacted ground or a trench the installer dug, a lawn zone capped at the heads during the install, or no aggregate base at all.
- **Often not:** a utility trench dug after the install, a downspout moved onto the turf, animals that arrived later, or a leak on a zone that stayed in service.
- **The turf maker usually won't pay for labor.** One maker's published terms say it "shall not be obligated to pay for installation, labor, or other incidental costs" ([Smart Turf](https://smartturf.com/warranty)), so a base repair falls to the installer's workmanship terms or to you. See [what turf coverage includes and leaves out](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/).
- **Some towns set the base in code.** [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) requires turf over a compacted, porous aggregate base at least 3 inches deep ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). In Platteville, covered on our [Greeley page](/areas/greeley-co/), turf "not laid over an aggregate base" is on the town's public-nuisance list ([Platteville Code 7-1-30](https://library.municode.com/co/platteville/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CH7HESAAN_ARTINU_S7-1-30ENNU)).
- **On a new build, ask about the lot.** If the dip is settled backfill along the house, the builder's grading may be the question, not the turf. Colorado requires builders to give buyers a soils report summary at least 14 days before closing ([C.R.S. 6-6.5-101](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_6-6.5-101)); it says what the lot sits on.

Send the installer dated photos with a straightedge and tape in the dip, when you first noticed it, any zone, downspout or line nearby, and the base depth the contract promised. A good answer: they lift the turf, show you the base depth and what's under the dip before refilling, name the cause, rebuild in thin compacted lifts, and re-seam. A weak answer is more infill brushed on top. Unsure the base went in? See [checking the base was installed](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/). Installer won't return? See when a turf installer won't come back. For a new install, see [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/).
