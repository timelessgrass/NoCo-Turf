At the nearest NOAA station to [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), Greeley UNC, an average year has 19.1 days when the high stays at or below 32°F and 152.7 nights at 32°F or colder. In half of years the first freezing night comes by October 8, and in half the last one comes after May 2 ([NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json&includeStationName=true)). That is the stretch a basement or garage simulator is for.

This page covers the room and what goes on its floor: the hitting mat, the turf around it and a place to putt. The slab underneath, from moisture tests to subfloors, is in our guide to [artificial turf on a garage or basement floor](/guides/artificial-turf-on-a-garage-or-basement-floor/). A putting green with real cups and built-in break is in [an indoor putting green for your home](/guides/indoor-putting-green-for-home/).

## How big does a golf simulator room need to be?

Launch monitor makers publish their own numbers, and they land close together:

| Launch monitor or maker | Ceiling | Width | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trackman iO, in the ceiling | 10 feet or more | 15 feet or more | 10 feet or more from screen to ball, plus room behind you to swing |
| Trackman 4, behind the ball | 10 feet or more | 15 feet or more | 18 feet or more from the screen to the unit |
| Foresight Sports, general room guide | 10 feet; 9 works for many golfers, 8.5 for irons only | 12 to 15 feet | 15 to 18 feet, depending on the launch monitor |
| Foresight Falcon, in the ceiling | 9.5 to 10.5 feet | Room to swing left- or right-handed | Center of the mat 10 to 12 feet from the screen, 4 feet from the unit |
| Uneekor overhead units | At least 9 feet, mounted 9 to 10 feet above the hitting area | 13 feet wall to wall; 15 for golfers 6 feet or taller | 12 feet from screen to tee, 10 at the least |

Sources: [Trackman](https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/how-much-room-for-a-golf-simulator), [Foresight Sports](https://www.foresightsports.com/blogs/golf-tips/complete-golf-simulator-room-setup), the [Falcon quick start guide](https://support.foresightsports.com/sites/default/files/downloads/2024/Falcon%20QS%20Guide.pdf) and [Uneekor](https://uneekor.com/en-us/help-center/pre-purchase-and-setup/how-much-space-is-needed-for-setup). Trackman 4 needs the extra depth because it sits behind you; Trackman says that space is not optional if accuracy is the priority.

**The ceiling decides most rooms.** Trackman calls 10 feet the practical minimum for most golfers and says an 8-foot ceiling is not recommended for full swings and long-term use. A finished basement can meet the 2021 International Residential Code at 7 feet, with beams and ducts allowed down to 6 feet 4 inches above the floor ([IRC R305.1](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IRC2021P2/chapter-3-building-planning)). A room that is legal to live in can be 3 feet short of the 10 feet Trackman calls the practical minimum.

**The floor comes off the ceiling.** Every layer under your feet takes height: a subfloor, a pad, the mat itself. SYNLawn Golf's Tee Strike Golf Mat, for one, is 1 5/8 inches thick ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf-products/tee-strike-golf-mat-5x5/)). Uneekor measures the 9 to 10 feet its overhead units need from the top of the hitting area to the bottom of the mounting bracket, and says it cannot vouch for the data outside that range. Settle the finished floor height before anything is mounted overhead.

**Swing before you buy.** Trackman's test is to take your longest club and make full swings in the room, slowly at first and then at speed, before committing to equipment. Do it under the lowest beam or duct, not in the open middle of the room.

**Width is for the swing and the second golfer.** Foresight says more width supports both right- and left-handed players, and Uneekor wants 15 feet wall to wall for golfers 6 feet or taller. Measure between posts and ductwork, not only between walls.

## What goes on the floor: a hitting mat, turf, or both?

Both, in different places. A simulator floor has separate jobs, and turf makers sell a separate surface for each. SYNLawn Golf, for example, offers Tee Strike for hitting areas, Precision Putt and Classic Pitch for receiving areas and SYNSport for fringe areas, and says its simulator turf mimics a tee line, rough or fringe ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-simulator-turf/)).

- **The hitting spot.** A mat or strip built for full swings. Trackman's garage guide calls a hitting mat required and says to make sure it is level and stable so it won't disrupt your swing ([Trackman](https://www.trackman.com/blog/garage-golf-simulator)). Fiberbuilt Golf, a hitting mat maker, builds the hitting areas of its combo mats generally 4 feet deep ([Fiberbuilt Golf](https://fiberbuiltgolf.com/pages/combo-mats)).
- **The stance and the rest of the room.** Turf to stand and walk on, picked to look like a tee line, rough or fringe. Swing only from the mat; our floor guide explains why the turf around it shouldn't take full shots.
- **In front of the screen.** Where balls drop and where you may putt. More on that below.

**Mind the step.** A mat sits on top of the floor. SYNLawn's Tee Strike mat is 1 5/8 inches thick, 5/8 of that a foam pad. Put it on a bare slab with thin turf around it and the edge of the mat becomes a ledge at your feet. Either build the surrounding turf up to the same height or set the mat down into a subfloor; the floor guide covers framing it flush.

**Keep the sensors' view clear.** Trackman says to keep the ceiling, walls and floor clear of obstructions, and to keep reflective surfaces such as glass, big fans or polished metal away, because they can interfere with radar readings. Fiberbuilt suggests laying a mat's base pieces loose first, checking the layout with your launch monitor maker, and only then pinning them together ([Fiberbuilt Golf](https://fiberbuiltgolf.com/pages/installation-recommendations)). The hitting spot has to line up with the sensor, and turf is hard to move once it has been cut around the mat.

**In a garage,** Foresight says many simulators use removable turf and mats so the space can still be used for parking or storage ([Foresight Sports](https://www.foresightsports.com/blogs/golf-tips/complete-golf-simulator-room-setup)). What cold, snowmelt and a sloped garage floor do to turf is in the garage and basement floor guide.

## Why does the hitting strip wear out first?

Every full swing comes down on the same few inches. On a course you move to fresh grass; on a mat you don't, so the makers' care advice is about spreading that wear and keeping grit out of it:

- **Move the ball.** Fiberbuilt says regularly changing where you hit on its panels spreads the wear, and that a panel can be turned 180 degrees as wear shows on one side. On a double-hitting mat, the left and right panels can trade places, or only the worn one gets replaced ([Fiberbuilt Golf](https://fiberbuiltgolf.com/pages/product-care)).
- **Turn the mat.** SYNLawn Golf says rotating a hitting mat after extended use or noticeable wear extends its life, and that its mats have replaceable hitting inserts ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf/hitting-mats/)).
- **Keep grit out.** Fiberbuilt says sand and dirt that collect in nylon turf fibers deteriorate and damage the nylon. It says to pressure-wash its hitting panels once a year and to lift its stance turf off the rubber base every few months to wash it, which indoors means carrying the pieces outside. Wipe range balls and shoes before they come in.
- **Less drag, less wear.** Both makers design around it: Fiberbuilt says its Fiberbuilt Grass is built to let the clubhead slide through to minimize wear ([Fiberbuilt Golf](https://fiberbuiltgolf.com/pages/fiberbuilt-grass-series)), and SYNLawn says Tee Strike reduces club head drag ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf-products/tee-strike/)).

How do you know it's done? SYNLawn says heavier use means replacing a hitting surface sooner to keep a smooth swing-through and accurate results and feedback. Watch for a matted or bald patch where you hit and a club that grabs at impact, and compare a few shots from the worn spot with a few from fresh turf beside it.

Before you buy a mat, ask:

- Can the strike surface be turned or replaced on its own, without a new mat?
- Is the replacement insert sold separately, and how is it swapped?
- How thick is the mat, and what is under the turf?
- How does the maker want it cleaned indoors?

Golf clubs deal with the same wear on their range tee lines; how they specify swappable strips is in [synthetic turf at golf clubs](/guides/golf-club-practice-turf/).

## Can you putt in a simulator room?

Yes, in three ways. Foresight Sports lists them: putt a real ball and let the sensor read it, use auto putt, or putt on a separate green and log the result ([Foresight Sports](https://www.foresightsports.com/blogs/golf-tips/how-does-putting-work-golf-simulator)).

- **Into the screen.** Foresight says most simulators read the initial launch of the ball, so in most setups a putt breaks on the screen's green, not the floor's. What the floor needs is a smooth stretch in front of the screen that rolls true.
- **Learn the scale.** Foresight's check: with the screen 15 feet away, roll a putt that stops right at the screen and see what distance the simulator gives it. If it reads 15 feet, you are working at 1:1.
- **A separate green.** Fiberbuilt says the putting turf on its greens runs at a stimpmeter speed of 9, with metal cups, and its combo mats put a hitting surface and a putting green on one base ([Fiberbuilt Golf](https://fiberbuiltgolf.com/pages/putting-greens)). How fast a home green should roll is in [putting green stimp speed](/guides/putting-green-stimp-speed/), and a green with built-in break and full-depth cups is in the indoor putting green guide.

## What goes between the slab and the mat?

Something firm. Fiberbuilt says its mats can go on indoor concrete or carpet, but on carpet a mat may move slightly with the grain while you hit, unless two-way carpet tape anchors the frame. A hitting spot that shifts under a full swing is what Trackman's advice to keep the mat level and stable guards against.

The rest belongs to the slab, and it has its own guide: how to test a basement or garage slab for moisture, what to do about a floor built to move on Northern Colorado clay, and foam tiles versus a framed wood subfloor are all in our guide to artificial turf on a garage or basement floor.

## What does the building code say about a basement simulator room?

A few lines of the 2021 International Residential Code bear on a simulator room ([ICC, IRC Chapter 3](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IRC2021P2/chapter-3-building-planning)):

- **Ceiling height.** 7 feet for basement living space, with beams and ducts allowed down to 6 feet 4 inches (R305.1).
- **The escape window.** A basement needs at least one operable emergency escape and rescue opening that works from inside without keys, tools or special knowledge, with the bottom of the opening no more than 44 inches above the floor (R310.1, R310.1.1, R310.2.3). Don't build the screen, frame or netting across it. Trackman also suggests limiting windows in a simulator room because the balls will be flying, so put the screen wall away from that window and keep a clear path to it.
- **An existing basement.** Finishing or altering one doesn't by itself require a new escape opening; adding a bedroom does (R310.7).
- **Smoke alarms.** One on each story, basements included, and when work that needs a permit is done, alarms wherever a new home would need them (R314.3, R314.2.2).
- **Opening the ceiling for swing room.** Taking a basement ceiling down to the joists can run into R302.13, which calls for a 1/2-inch gypsum or 5/8-inch wood structural panel layer under floor framing. Its exceptions include a floor over a sprinklered space, open areas up to 80 square feet per story ringed with fireblocking, and floors framed with dimension lumber 2 by 10 inches or larger. Which one fits depends on how your floor is framed.
- **The turf itself.** Whether synthetic turf is a fire risk, and which test reports a maker can show you, is in [is artificial turf flammable?](/guides/is-artificial-turf-flammable/)

Those are the model code's words. Your town or county enforces its own adopted edition and amendments, so check with the building department for your address before you finish the room or open the ceiling. Not legal advice.
