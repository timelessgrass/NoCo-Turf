Master Spas doesn't recommend draining a hot tub for the season, so a tub here may run straight through January. That makes four things matter before any turf goes down: what the tub sits on, where its water goes, how wet feet get to it on a snowy night, and where its power runs under the yard. The photo on this page shows the layout this guide argues for: the tub and its steps on their own concrete slab, pavers at the steps, and turf running up to the slab's edge. Pools, and the drain rules in Fort Collins, Greeley, Loveland and Windsor, are in our guide to [turf around a hot tub or pool](/guides/artificial-turf-around-pool/).

## Can a hot tub sit on artificial turf?

No. Take the turf out of the tub's footprint, build the base the tub's maker asks for, and run the turf to its edge.

| Maker | What it wants under the tub | What it says about grass |
|---|---|---|
| Master Spas | A uniformly firm, continuous, level surface; its recommended foundation is a concrete pad at least four inches thick with steel reinforcement bars crossed throughout ([pre-installation guide](https://www.masterspas.com/documents/pre-installation-guides/hot-tub-pre-installation-guide.pdf)) | Grass and bare soil "simply can't support" a filled tub, and a spa pad goes down only after the grass or sod comes out and gravel or sand is compacted ([Master Spas](https://www.masterspas.com/blog/hot-tub-installation-questions/)) |
| Jacuzzi | A poured concrete slab, a deck that has been inspected and properly supported, or a prefabricated spa pad ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/3-FOUNDATION-TYPES-FOR-HOT-TUBS.html)) | The grass and topsoil still have to be cleared for a spa pad |
| Caldera | A reinforced concrete pad at least four inches thick, or a deck able to carry the spa's dead weight ([Caldera](https://www.calderaspas.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/caldera-spas-vacanza-pre-delivery-instructions.pdf)) | Grass or dirt around a spa adds debris carried into the water on users' feet |

Weight is the reason. Jacuzzi says a fully loaded tub can weigh more than 4,000 pounds, and Master Spas' model chart runs from 2,575 pounds filled for its smallest tub to 7,825 for its largest.

Turf isn't on any of these lists. It is a surface, not a foundation, so whatever goes under the tub is the tub maker's call: get the dealer's answer in writing before anything else is drawn. Where the turf meets the slab, finish it like any patio edge ([edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/)).

## What does hot tub water do to the turf beside it?

It lands there in small amounts every time someone gets in or out, and the chemicals that keep it clean get handled a step away from the turf.

- **What's in the water.** The CDC's numbers for a hot tub are at least 3 ppm of chlorine or 4 to 8 ppm of bromine ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/safety/what-you-can-do-to-stay-healthy-in-hot-tubs.html)). Drips and splash carry some of that onto whatever is beside the steps.
- **What a turf maker says.** ForeverLawn's care sheet tells owners to keep harsh chemicals off its turf, suggests an occasional rinse with water, and says to clean a spill quickly with water, mild detergent and a stiff nylon brush ([ForeverLawn](https://www.foreverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/M0945-Landscape-Maintenance-Sheet-1.pdf.pdf)). We found no turf maker that publishes a test of hot tub sanitizer on its fibers. What makers' written terms say about chlorine and bromine is in the [pool guide](/guides/artificial-turf-around-pool/).
- **Dosing.** Master Spas says to add products one at a time, never mixed or premixed, and to store them somewhere cool, dry and ventilated, out of reach of children and pets ([Master Spas](https://www.masterspas.com/blog/hot-tub-safety/)). Add them standing on the slab, where a spilled scoop can be swept up, not leaning over the turf.
- **Grit.** Caldera warns that grass or dirt around a spa adds debris carried in on wet feet. We'd treat infill the same way, since sand and rubber granules are loose too, and give the steps a hard landing.
- **Water on the ground.** Master Spas wants the ground around an outdoor tub to drain well and slope away, with the equipment where it can't flood. Grade the turf down and away from the slab, with no flat pocket at the edge for splash to sit in.
- **The service side.** Master Spas asks for at least three feet of clearance around a tub for service and cover removal. Turf can run into that strip; planters, posts and anything bolted down shouldn't.

## Where can hot tub water be drained in Northern Colorado?

Not across the turf, and into a street or storm drain only on the town's terms.

A drain moves a lot of water at once: Master Spas' model chart lists tubs holding 170 to 615 gallons. The Colorado Geological Survey says water added after construction makes expansive soils swell, and counts water ponding near a foundation among the causes of damage ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Run the hose where the water can spread out, well away from the house, the slab and the turf.

What four more towns' storm drain codes say (not legal advice):

| Town | What its code allows into the storm drain |
|---|---|
| [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) | Bans non-stormwater discharges, but exempts water from pools, spas and hot tubs with "no detectable residual chlorine or other chemicals that could endanger aquatic life," unless it causes a public nuisance or safety concern ([Sec. 14.26.050](https://library.municode.com/co/longmont/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT14PUSE_CH14.26STQU_S14.26.050ILDIPREX)). |
| [Evans](/areas/evans-co/) | Its stormwater chapter exempts swimming pool water dechlorinated to less than 0.05 ppm chlorine, and discharges that fully follow the state's low risk guidance ([13.17.050](https://library.municode.com/co/evans/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=MUCO_TIT13PUSE_CH13.17STQU_13.17.050ILDIPREX)); an older chapter says "typically less than one PPM" ([13.32.060](https://library.municode.com/co/evans/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=MUCO_TIT13PUSE_CH13.32ILDIINEVSTDRSY_13.32.060DIPR)). Neither names hot tubs, so ask the city which applies. |
| [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/) | Bans materials and polluted water other than storm water, but exempts listed flows that include swimming pool water "if de-chlorinated typically less than one PPM chlorine." Hot tubs aren't named ([8.3-7](https://library.municode.com/co/berthoud/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CH8HESA_8.3NORWASTDRSY)). |
| [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) | Allows only naturally occurring stormwater runoff. Its exclusions don't name pools or hot tubs but cover discharges that follow the state's low risk guidance, and whoever drains stays liable for any damage ([13.13.050](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT13PUSE_CH13.13STQU_13.13.050ILDIPR)). |

Put the CDC's hot tub numbers beside those: at least 3 ppm of chlorine in use, against Evans' 0.05 ppm at the storm drain. Tub water in use is far over every chlorine figure in that table, so test it before the hose goes anywhere near the curb, and wait or treat it until the strip reads clear. None of the four codes names bromine; ask the town how it wants a bromine tub handled. The state's method for letting sanitizer dissipate is in the pool guide.

Winter adds one more thing. Longmont's code names ice formation in gutters and on sidewalks as a nuisance condition for sump-pump and irrigation water. It doesn't say so for spa water, but a hose run to a frozen gutter is worth a call to the city first. In every town, keep the hose off the walk to the tub, where spilled water can freeze under bare feet. For a town not covered here or in the pool guide, ask its stormwater or public works office before the first drain.

## Is turf a safe surface for wet bare feet?

It can be part of the route, but the spot where wet feet land should be a hard, clear landing.

Master Spas calls getting in and out one of the biggest winter hot tub safety concerns, because snow, ice and freezing temperatures make surfaces slippery. It suggests keeping the area clear of snow and ice, a non-slip mat or outdoor rug near the entrance, and slip-resistant footwear to and from the tub ([Master Spas](https://www.masterspas.com/blog/should-you-use-a-hot-tub-in-the-winter/)). It also recommends non-slip spa steps, and a handrail for anyone worried about falling.

We found no published slip test of wet turf underfoot, barefoot or otherwise. What we'd plan:

- Set the steps on the slab, not on turf, so they stay level.
- Give the steps a landing of pavers or concrete for the mat and the shovel.
- Keep sandals at both ends of the path for frosty nights. What frost and frozen infill do to turf is in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

## How should the path from the house work in snow?

Hard and shovelable from the door to the steps, with turf on either side.

- **How much snow.** The Fort Collins NOAA station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year and 43.6 days with at least an inch on the ground; the Greeley UNC station averages 34.9 inches and 39.3 days ([Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json), [Greeley](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json)).
- **The route.** Master Spas says to plan how people will walk to the tub in winter and where they step out, and to use pavers, decking or gravel where the route crosses a lawn ([Master Spas](https://www.masterspas.com/blog/hot-tub-landscaping/)). Turf is a lawn for this purpose: a shovel and ice melt belong on the hard path beside it.
- **The turf beside it.** How to clear snow off turf without damage, and what ice melt does to it, is in [snow and ice melt on turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

## What does the electrical code ask for, and where does the wire run?

Not legal advice. Colorado's State Electrical Board made the 2026 National Electrical Code the state's minimum standard on August 1, 2026 ([3 CCR 710-1, Rule 1.5](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=12606&fileName=3%20CCR%20710-1)). The NEC's pool and spa rules are Article 680, and the International Residential Code restates them for houses. The figures below are from the residential code's 2021 edition, which tracks an earlier NEC, so the electrician and the town's inspector have the last word on the current text.

| What | Residential code | What tub makers add |
|---|---|---|
| The disconnect | Readily accessible, within sight of the equipment, and at least 5 feet horizontally from the tub's inside walls unless a permanent barrier makes the reach path 5 feet or more ([E4203.3](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/42/swimming-pools)) | Caldera: its GFCI subpanel no closer than five feet to the water's edge, within sight and no more than 50 feet away. Master Spas recommends the GFCI breaker within 25 feet of the tub, outside for service ([Master Spas](https://www.masterspas.com/documents/pre-installation-guides/hot-tub-pre-installation-guide.pdf)) |
| GFCI protection | On the outlet that supplies the tub, unless the unit is listed with its own; Class A on 15- and 20-amp, 125-volt receptacles within 20 feet | Caldera's plug-in models carry the GFCI at the end of the cord, with the outlet 6 to 10 feet from the tub |
| Other outlets | At least one general-purpose receptacle 6 to 20 feet from the tub; any other receptacle at least 6 feet away | Caldera: never plug a spa into an extension cord |
| The buried feed | Allowed within 5 feet of the tub, in rigid metal, intermediate metal, rigid PVC or reinforced thermosetting resin conduit, or Type MC cable, at the code's minimum depth ([E4203.8](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/42/swimming-pools)) | Master Spas: no overhead power lines within 15 feet of the tub |

How deep is the feed? The code's minimum cover, measured from the top of the cable or conduit to finished grade, is 24 inches for direct-burial cable, 18 inches for nonmetallic conduit, 12 inches for a 120-volt GFCI circuit of 20 amps or less, and 6 inches for rigid metal conduit ([Table E3803.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/38/wiring-methods)). Colorado 811 doesn't mark private lines, the ones on your property that no public utility owns ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging)), and a feed run from your own panel to your own tub is one of them. So:

- Draw the feed's route and depth on the plan, and photograph the open trench before it's backfilled.
- Keep turf nails, edging stakes and any later digging off that line.
- Put the disconnect on a post or wall at least 5 feet from the water and in sight of the tub, and run the turf around its base.
- Ask about the permit before the trench: Caldera says most cities and counties require one for the circuit.

## What should the plan show before the tub and the turf go in?

1. **The base** the tub's maker asks for, with a landing at the steps, sloped so water runs away from the tub and the house.
2. **Three feet clear** on the sides that need service, with nothing fixed in that strip.
3. **A hard path** from the door to the steps that you can shovel all winter.
4. **The drain route:** where the hose goes, which town code applies, and a test strip.
5. **The wiring:** panel, disconnect, outlets, and the feed's route and depth.
6. **Overhead lines:** none within 15 feet of the tub, per Master Spas.

The pad and the wiring go in before the turf; the order of trades is in [planning a backyard around turf](/guides/outdoor-living-with-artificial-turf/). The open lawn around the tub is ordinary [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/).
