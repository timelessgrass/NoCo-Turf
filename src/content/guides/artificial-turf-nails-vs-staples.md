**Checked September 25, 2026.** Artificial turf is held down in one of two ways: long nails (installers also call them spikes) driven through it into the compacted base, or short staples shot into a board along the edge. Many yards use both. Here is what turf makers' installation guides specify, what a Northern Colorado winter does to each, and what to ask before you sign. The border itself is in our guide to [artificial turf edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/).

## What's the difference between nailing turf and stapling it?

What does the holding.

- **Nails into the base.** A long nail goes through the backing into the crushed rock, and the base and the soil below grip it. SYNLawn's DIY guide uses 6-inch (60d) galvanized nails ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)); ForeverLawn uses 6-inch 60D galvanized spikes with a twisted shaft ([ForeverLawn](https://www.landscapesbyforeverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/DIY-Landscape-Installation-Guidelines-.pdf)). Integriturf calls nailing the most common method ([Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf)).
- **Staples into a board.** A pneumatic stapler fixes the turf to a board set along the perimeter. ForeverLawn stands pressure-treated 2x4s on edge and staples into them every 2 to 3 inches with galvanized staples 1 inch long. A staple that short holds only in wood or plastic, so the board carries the load: ForeverLawn pins a free-standing board with 1/2-inch rebar 18 to 24 inches long about every 2 feet, and screws or anchors a board beside a sidewalk or foundation into the concrete.
- **Screws into a board.** PolyTurf screws edges to a perimeter edging board with 1-inch screws, because a screw comes out more easily than a nail when turf has to be re-stretched to take out ripples ([PolyTurf](https://polyturf.com/uploads/files/polyturf_installation_manual_landscape_only.pdf)). More on that in [why turf wrinkles or ripples](/guides/artificial-turf-wrinkles-ripples/).

ForeverLawn recommends boards around commercial installations and allows boards or spikes at a house. Integriturf switches to a nailer board with 3/8-by-1-inch galvanized staples for playground areas and wherever nails aren't advised, the kind of area our [playground turf](/services/playground-turf/) page covers.

## How long should turf nails be, and how far apart?

Most guides call for 5 or 6 inches. Spacing varies more:

| Installation guide | Nail or spike | Along edges | Along seams | Through the middle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SYNLawn | 6 in (60d), galvanized | 6–8 in on its materials page, 8–12 in in its steps | Both sides, staggered 8–12 in | Not stated |
| ForeverLawn | 6 in 60D, twisted shaft, galvanized | 8–10 in, set 3/8–1/2 in from the edge | Spikes down each roll to hold it for seaming | Wherever it is still loose |
| [ShawGrass](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf) | About 3.5–10 in; soil and use decide | About 6 in; 4–8 in where there's no edging | About 6 in | Not stated |
| Integriturf | 5 in 40D common or 6 in electro-galvanized | 4–6 in | Not stated | Every 2–3 ft |
| [Watersavers Turf](https://www.watersaversturf.com/how-to-install-artificial-grass-guide/) | 5 or 6 in | 3–4 in | Every 4 in | Optional, 12–24 in |

Where they agree: heads sit flush with the backing, because overdriving leaves a dimple or dip (Shaw, ForeverLawn, Watersavers), and no blades get pinned under a head (SYNLawn, ForeverLawn). Shaw leaves the choice of nail to local soils and the application, so ask for the installation guide from the maker of the turf you are buying. It belongs with the [turf spec sheet](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/).

Length only means something next to base depth. SYNLawn digs 3 to 4 inches for the base; ForeverLawn lays about 3 inches of crushed stone under an inch of fines. A 6-inch nail through 4 inches of base leaves its last 2 inches in the native soil, and in a clay yard that is the layer winter moves. Base depth is covered in [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).

## Can frost push turf nails back up?

It can. A National Research Council Canada building digest explains the mechanism for posts and foundations: wet soil freezes onto the post (adfreezing) and passes it the upward push of ice lenses growing below. Soil then falls into the gap underneath, so the post can't settle back at the thaw, and the problem can grow year after year ([NRC Canada, CBD-128](https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/object/?id=8896d31f-b220-4a29-af64-a673b9d4f2da)). The digest dates from 1970 and is about foundations; applying it to a 6-inch nail is our reading, not the digest's. Turf makers see the result: PolyTurf tells owners to check nails routinely for "working out" from traffic, earth movement or other causes, and to drive back any that stick up, because a raised head can hurt people and animals.

Northern Colorado runs that cycle often:

- **Freeze and thaw.** NOAA's 1991–2020 normals count 150 nights a year at or below 32°F at the [Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json) station and 177.7 at [Longmont 2 ESE](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00055116&format=json). At Fort Collins about 135 of those days climb back above freezing.
- **Chinooks.** The Colorado Climate Center says rises of 25 to 35°F in a short time are not uncommon when these winds reach the plains ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). Turf expands and contracts with heat and cold, which is why Watersavers Turf wants a tight perimeter.
- **Frost depth.** Larimer County designs footings for a 30-inch frost depth ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2022/structural_design_information.pdf)). A footing reaches below that; a nail sits wholly inside it.
- **Clay.** Frost heave takes fine-grained soil, freezing and water; clean sand and gravel don't heave ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)). The Colorado Geological Survey says expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Soil changes by town: federal soil surveys map central [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) mostly as Nunn clay loam, a high shrink-swell soil, and central Firestone mostly as Vona loamy sand.

The digest's first remedy is a drained zone of gravel or crushed stone around the post to take up the uplift, with a catch: if it stops draining, the stone freezes into one solid mass. The turf version is a clean, compacted rock base with somewhere for water to go. Where water sits in winter is in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/); clay yards have their own guide, [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).

## Do boards and staples hold up better in winter?

Neither is frost-proof, and none of the guides we read compares them through a winter. What differs is what the ground has to move:

- **A board fastened to concrete** moves only if the slab does.
- **A free-standing board** in ForeverLawn's detail is pinned by rebar 18 to 24 inches long, far deeper than a nail, though still inside Larimer County's 30-inch design depth.
- **Nails** each depend on their own few inches of base and soil, so one can rise while its neighbors stay put.

The trade-off: a staple is only as good as its board. ForeverLawn specifies pressure-treated lumber, Integriturf pressure-treated or composite, K9Grass plastic. Ask what the board is and how it is anchored. For edges already lifting or curling, see [turf edges lifting](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/).

## Does a yard with dogs need more fastening?

At the edges, yes, going by the makers. Shaw tells installers to secure the entire perimeter so pets can't pull the turf up. K9Grass, ForeverLawn's pet line, says spikes or infill weight leave edges dogs can pull up, and a pulled-up spike becomes something a dog can swallow. Its pet installation uses a plastic perimeter board anchored to the ground or an adjacent surface, with stainless steel staples every inch around every edge and cutout ([K9Grass](https://k9grass.com/faq/do-dogs-chew-on-dog-friendly-turf/)). The same company allows spikes around an ordinary lawn, so the stricter spec is about dogs.

Where a dog runs the fence line or turns hard at a corner, ask for a board with staples, or nails at the tight end of the ranges above. Diggers are their own problem: [can dogs dig through artificial turf](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/). How a dog area drains is on our [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/) page.

## What should I check each spring?

Once the ground has thawed and dried, walk every edge and seam:

- **Nail heads standing proud of the backing:** tap them back flush, as PolyTurf advises. Paws find them before feet do.
- **The same nails rising every year:** that points to water sitting around them. Fix where it goes, or ask about a board on that edge.
- **Turf pulling off a board,** or staples that have let go.
- **Seams opening:** see [visible seams in artificial turf](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).

## What should a turf quote say about fasteners?

Ask these, and expect numbers back:

1. **What holds each edge?** Nails, or a board with staples or screws, edge by edge: beds, rock, fence, patio. A good answer walks your actual edges.
2. **What nail, and what finish?** Makers disagree: PolyTurf says always galvanized because it doesn't rust, while Synthetic Grass Warehouse recommends non-galvanized 5- or 6-inch nails because they expand over time and hold tighter as the installation ages ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/resources/installing-artificial-grass/)). A good answer names the nail and says why.
3. **How far apart?** On edges, seams and through the middle, in inches, within the turf maker's guide.
4. **How far past the base does the nail reach?** That is how much of it sits in your soil.
5. **Who resets nails that rise after the first winter?** Get it in writing, and compare it with [what turf coverage includes](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/).

**In Firestone.** Not legal advice. Firestone's code says artificial turf "shall be anchored at all edges and seams," without naming a fastener ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). Whether an edge detail meets that line depends on how the town reads it, so ask Firestone Planning. The rest of the standard is in our [turf spec sheet guide](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/), with local notes on the [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) page.
