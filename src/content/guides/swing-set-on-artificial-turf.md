**Checked September 25, 2026.** A swing set or trampoline can stand on artificial turf, but the turf can't hold it down: it is a surface over a compacted base, and every anchor has to reach the ground under both. What a child lands on is on our [playground turf page](/services/playground-turf/); padded turf against mulch and gravel is in [playground surfaces compared](/guides/playground-turf-vs-mulch/).

## Can a swing set or trampoline sit on artificial turf?

Yes. Springfree Trampoline, a trampoline maker, says a trampoline can go on artificial grass: it isn't as soft as natural grass, but anchors can still secure it to the ground ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/does-a-trampoline-need-to-be-on-grass)). Three things change on turf:

- **The ground stays level.** The CPSC says a level spot lowers the chance of a play set tipping over ([CPSC home playground handbook](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/324.pdf)), and a turf base is compacted flat before the turf goes down.
- **Anchors go through two layers.** The turf backing, then the base, before they reach soil. The base is covered in [what goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).
- **The use zone still applies.** The CPSC wants protective surfacing at least 6 feet out on every side, and in front of and behind a swing to twice the height of the top bar. Turf alone isn't that surfacing; the pad under it is.

## How do you anchor a swing set through artificial turf?

The way the set's maker says to. The CPSC's home handbook says play equipment may need anchors to keep it from tipping over, to follow the manufacturer's anchoring instructions, and to bury or cover anchors so they don't trip anyone. Its public handbook puts concrete footings below ground level, where no child can land on them ([CPSC public playground handbook](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325_PublicPlaygroundSafetyHandbook2025_7-30-25_1.pdf)).

Most home sets use one of two methods ([Gorilla Playsets](https://gorillaplaysets.com/blogs/the-playground/how-to-anchor-a-swing-set-safely-and-securely)):

| | Twist-in (auger) anchors | Concrete footings |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A steel corkscrew turned into the soil beside each leg, then fastened to it | A hole at each leg filled with concrete around the anchor hardware |
| Gorilla Playsets suggests it for | Hard, compact soil | Looser soil, or the most stability |
| On a turf yard | Through a small cut in the backing, before or after the turf goes down | Poured before the base and turf; the turf is cut to fit around each leg |

For augers through finished turf, EcoGrass, a turf company, spreads the blades, cuts a small X in the backing, drives or drills the anchor through at a 45-degree angle until its eyelet reaches the backing, and caps or covers any head that sticks up ([EcoGrass](https://www.ecograssus.com/blog/2020/1/30/inserting-anchor-or-stake-into-turf)). Where the set's manual says otherwise, the manual wins. Gorilla Playsets adds that anchors under a loose surfacing must reach the solid earth beneath it; under turf, that earth is below the base.

## Does frost depth matter for swing set anchors in Northern Colorado?

Yes. Larimer County sets a minimum frost depth of 30 inches below grade for footings ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2022/structural_design_information.pdf)), and Weld County designs to the same 30 inches ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/7/departments/building/documents/design-criteria.pdf)). Those are building values, not a measured freeze in your yard, but the ground treats a swing set footing the same way:

- **Frost lifts wet, fine soil.** Frost heave needs soil with plenty of fine particles, freezing that reaches into it, and water ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)). Clay qualifies; see [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).
- **Clay moves on its own.** The [Colorado Geological Survey](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) says some clays swell up to 20% by volume when wet.
- **Freezing nights are common.** NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 150 days a year with a low at or below 32°F (1991–2020 normals).

Any anchor or footing shallower than 30 inches sits in soil the counties design for frost. If the manual calls for a shallower one, follow it and check the set after its first winter.

## Will Northern Colorado wind tip over a playset or trampoline?

It can. The National Weather Service office for northeast Colorado says winds of 60 to near 100 mph reach foothill areas such as Fort Collins, and that chinook and bora winds can exceed 100 mph ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/bou/highwind)). NOAA's storm records include 84 mph in Loveland in the event that began November 29, 2019, 73 mph at Severance on January 16, 2026, and gusts of 85 to 95 mph across northern Larimer County on March 12, 2026 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)).

Gorilla Playsets says an unanchored playset can topple over during vigorous play or shift in high winds, and the CPSC counts children struck by equipment that moves or tips over among the deaths on home playgrounds. Trampolines catch wind too: Springfree says anchors keep one from blowing away, and before severe weather suggests lowering the enclosure, moving the trampoline to shelter or taking it apart ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/how-to-anchor-a-trampoline)). The NWS says to bring lightweight objects inside or tie them down when high winds are forecast.

Some neighborhoods put it in writing. [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/)'s Heron Lakes requires a trampoline to be buried at ground level and firmly anchored for wind protection ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)), and it and [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/)'s Timnath Ranch ask owners to secure lightweight play equipment that high winds can move ([Timnath Ranch guidelines](https://www.timnathranchmd.live/files/8c91d1751/Timnath-Ranch-ACC-Guidelines-Amended-2020-06-25-Final.pdf)).

## Will a trampoline ruin artificial turf?

Not the way it ruins a lawn. Springfree says grass under a trampoline suffers mostly from lost sunlight and from the legs, which concentrate hundreds of pounds on a few small contact points; synthetic grass needs no sunlight ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/will-a-trampoline-kill-your-grass)). On turf, the legs are what the surface feels:

- **Spread the load.** Springfree suggests flat pads under each leg, plus rubber mats or padding around the base on surfaces harsher than grass. It counts turf among those, and says hard surfaces can raise the chance of damage to a trampoline's legs.
- **Expect flat spots.** Pile pressed down all season needs brushing to stand again; see [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/).
- **Anchor it anyway.** Springfree ranks turf above concrete for a trampoline because anchors can hold it down.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that children of all ages avoid trampolines and that children under 6 never jump. For families who get one anyway, it says to set the bouncing surface as close to the ground as possible, far from trees, walls, buildings and other play areas ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Trampolines-What-You-Need-to-Know.aspx)).

## How do you keep turf from wearing out under the swings and slide?

Plan it before the turf goes in. The CPSC says activity under swings and at slide exits displaces loose surfacing quickly, and that any wear mat there should sit flush so it isn't a tripping hazard.

- **Seams.** An installer column from [Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/seam-integration-part-3-perfecting-seams/), a turf distributor, says seams under constant foot traffic can part, and that under swing sets it may be worth wasting material so no seam lands there.
- **Fiber.** The same distributor says high-traffic areas may need a textured or grooved fiber ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/premature-matting/)).
- **Infill.** [Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/how-to-get-turf-fibers-in-artificial-grass-to-stand-up-tall-when-theyre-flat/), a turf maker, says children's running feet move infill around. Check under the swings first when [topping up infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).
- **Nothing loose on top.** A rubber mat laid over turf is a lip to trip on. Reinforcement belongs under the turf; ask the installer.
- **Static at the slide.** An antistatic-turf patent says charge a child picks up on a plastic slide isn't drained across the turf until they touch something grounded, such as a metal pole ([patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10323361B1/en)). Fixes are in [static shocks from artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-static-shock/).

Keep an offcut from the same roll so a worn patch can be [replaced to match](/guides/how-to-repair-artificial-turf/).

## What has to happen before anyone digs or drills?

- **Call 811.** Colorado 811 says state law requires property owners to contact it at least two full business days before digging, not counting the day of notice, and lists digging holes for swing sets and driving landscaping stakes among its examples ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging)). It doesn't mark private lines such as sprinkler lines, which a turf yard may still have; see [your sprinkler system after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/). Rainbow Play Systems says utilities in the play zone must be located before an anchored set is assembled ([Rainbow Play Systems](https://www.rainbowplay.com/create-a-safe-play-set-area-2/)). Not legal advice.
- **Check the permit rule.** [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) exempts swings, playhouses and other play equipment from a residential permit when the total area is 120 square feet or less, with no more than one elevated playhouse, of at most 64 square feet and 6 feet high ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Business/Permits-and-Licensing/Building-Permits)). Elsewhere it depends on the town's adopted building code: ask the town's Planning or Building office.
- **Check the HOA.** Barefoot Lakes in [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) keeps play structures to rear or side yards, at least 5 feet from the property line and no taller than 12 feet ([Barefoot Lakes guidelines](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). Buffalo Creek in [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/) doesn't allow metal A-frame swing sets ([Buffalo Creek guidelines](https://www.buffalocreekhoa.com/_files/ugd/393016_c765f78371fa41c08df663199318a09e.pdf)). Turf approval is its own step: [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/).

Then ask the installer where the anchors or footings go, whether they go in before the base, how deep they reach against that 30-inch frost depth, and where the seams fall. A good answer names the set's manual and shows the seams on the layout.
