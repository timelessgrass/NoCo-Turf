**Not legal advice:** this page explains Colorado well permits and Larimer and Weld County rules in plain English. The Division of Water Resources, your county and your HOA have the final word.

## Why is a green lawn so hard to keep on an acreage lot?

Water comes first. Outside town water lines many homes run on a well, and in Colorado the well permit, not the size of the lot, decides whether that water may reach a lawn. Under section 37-92-602 of the Colorado Revised Statutes, the Division of Water Resources (DWR) says wells permitted under the provision most exempt wells use, on parcels under 35 acres outside cluster developments, are limited to "ordinary household purposes inside one single-family dwelling, with no allowed outdoor uses" ([DWR Guideline 2023-1](https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/dwr/ElectronicFile.aspx?docid=4187690&dbid=0)). DWR's [Beginner's Guide to Well Permits](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1axZVUaG9QGdBLebzM6F0YOut2MrL4pI5/view) sorts residential wells roughly like this:

| Well permit | Where it usually applies | Water outside the house |
|---|---|---|
| Household use only | Parcels created before June 1, 1972 or by a county exemption; new wells on parcels under 35 acres | None: no lawn, garden or livestock |
| Domestic | 35 acres or more, outside augmentation plans and subdivisions created after June 1, 1972 | One acre or less of home lawn and garden, plus animals |
| Under an augmentation plan | Subdivision lots created after June 1, 1972, in most of the state | Whatever the plan allows; adding a use means water court |

DWR's [Well Permit Search](https://dwr.colorado.gov/services/well-permitting) pulls up your permit file, which lists the well's allowable uses. Read it before planning a dog area you expect to hose down: on a household-use-only permit, that is outdoor use too (our reading; ask DWR).

CSU Extension puts bluegrass at about 24 inches of supplemental irrigation in a normal Front Range year ([GardenNotes #564](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/564.pdf)); over one acre that is 2 acre-feet, and the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-science-glossary) counts 325,851 gallons in each. A rural water district tap brings a price instead of a permit: every Little Thompson Water District tap has an annual allotment, and use above it costs an extra $10 per 1,000 gallons on a standard 5/8-inch tap and $20 on other residential taps ([LTWD Schedule C](https://www.littlethompsonwd.gov/files/d6e8362ff/2026+Section+1502.2+Schedule+C-04162026.pdf)). Other providers: [water savings](/guides/water-savings/).

That is the case for turf on acreage: a green yard where the household lives, with no irrigation ([do you have to water artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/)). It is not a case for covering the lot.

## Where on the property does turf belong?

Near the house, sized to what people and dogs do there. The Colorado State Forest Service's wildfire zones make a useful map ([CSFS Home Ignition Zone guide](https://csfs.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/2021_CSFS_HIZGuide_Web.pdf)):

| Part of the property | What fits there | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First 5 feet around the house and decks | Rock, gravel, pavers or concrete | Wildfire guidance, and Larimer County's code for new work, want it noncombustible |
| 5 to 30 feet | The turf: play lawn, dog area, putting green | Close enough to use daily; CSFS wants any grass here mowed to 4 inches or less |
| Septic drain field | Planted soil, never turf | Larimer and Weld want at least 10 inches of soil that grows plants over it |
| Around the well | Open ground | State rules keep the casing at least a foot above finished ground |
| 30 to 100 feet and beyond | Native grass | Larimer's code recommends native grasses for low-traffic areas |

Draw each use at its real size and join them into one or two shapes: turf that follows the house reads as design, a patch out in the pasture as a leftover. Our guides to [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/), [putting green size](/guides/backyard-putting-green-size/) and [a dog run](/guides/pea-gravel-vs-turf-dog-run/) size the pieces. Where turf meets native grass, give it an edge that holds ([edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/)) and keep that grass mowed short. For a living low-water lawn instead, see [buffalograss and blue grama](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-low-water-grass/).

## What do wildfire rules say about the ground next to the house?

CSFS counts a home in or near grasslands, shrublands, foothills or mountains as wildland-urban interface, plains acreage included. Under [SB23-166](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb23-166), a state board adopts a minimum wildfire code, and a county or town with interface land and building- or fire-code authority must adopt one that meets or exceeds it within 3 months. The two counties differ:

- **Larimer County** adopted its amended code on September 22, 2025, effective January 1, 2026 ([building codes](https://www.larimer.gov/building/building/codes)), and deems all of unincorporated Larimer County wildland-urban interface. The first 5 feet around a structure and its decks take "noncombustible, hard surface materials" such as rock, gravel, sand, concrete, bare earth or pavers; the county struck the state exception for ignition-resistant plantings ([amended code](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/2025-wildfire-resiliency-code-w-lc-amendments.pdf)). Conditions that were legal at adoption may continue, so the code matters most when you build a house or an addition. Whether it reaches turf added beside an existing house is unsettled: ask Larimer County's building office first.
- **Weld County** enforces the state code from July 1, 2026, but only on parcels the state map designates outside town limits and outside a fire protection district, and exempts a parcel of 35 acres or more with one house that doesn't abut a residential or commercial area ([Sec. 7-1-10](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH7ADMISTCO_ARTICOWIRECO_S7-1-10ADCOWIRECOEFDAAP)). It depends on your parcel: ask Weld County's Building Department.

Turf is not on Larimer's list for that band, and the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety's [Wildfire Prepared Home standard](https://wildfireprepared.org/wp-content/uploads/WFPH-Technical-Standard-12-25.pdf) names artificial turf among the combustible groundcovers to clear from the first 5 feet. Colorado's backyard-turf protection also leaves an HOA free to restrict changes that interfere with fire buffers or defensible space ([SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). Turf near a flame: [fire pits on artificial turf](/guides/fire-pit-on-artificial-turf/).

## What about the septic field and the well?

- **Septic.** Larimer and Weld septic rules want at least 10 inches of soil suitable for vegetation over the drain field ([Larimer OWTS Regulations](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2024/2024_larimer_county_owts_0.pdf); [Weld Chapter 30](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH30WECODEPUHEENTEWATRSYRE_ARTXDECROITRAR)). A turf base digs that soil out and compacts rock in its place, so the field stays planted. Lids, ports and machine routes: [turf over a septic field](/guides/artificial-turf-over-septic-field/).
- **The well.** Colorado's well rules want the steel casing at a pitless adapter at least one foot above the finished ground level ([2 CCR 402-2, Rule 11.2.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/colorado/2-CCR-402-2-11)). A turf base built up around the wellhead raises that ground, so stop the turf and base short of the casing. That is our reading of a pump-installer rule, not a DWR ruling on turf.

## How do wind and wildlife change turf on open ground?

**Wind.** NOAA's [Storm Events database](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/) logged 48 High Wind events, sustained winds of 40 mph for an hour or gusts of 58 mph by the [National Weather Service's definition](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=high+wind), from 2011 through 2025 in the forecast zone for Larimer County below 6,000 feet and northwest Weld, and 30 in central and southern Weld, most between October and April. On an open lot, set the turf in the lee of the house, a barn or a windbreak, and fasten every edge and seam ([turf edges lifting](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/)).

**Wildlife.** CSU Extension says the black-tailed prairie dog is the most common prairie dog along the Front Range ([Fact Sheet 6.521](https://www.extension.colostate.edu/docs/pubs/natres/06521.pdf)), and that prairie dog burrows run 3 to 14 feet deep and 10 to more than 100 feet long ([Fact Sheet 6.506](https://www.extension.colostate.edu/docs/pubs/natres/06506.pdf)), long enough to reach a yard from the next field. If mounds or runways show up nearby, plan wire before the dig.

## Which county rules apply to turf in unincorporated Larimer and Weld?

- **Larimer County.** Where its landscaping standards apply, nonfunctional artificial turf is prohibited, functional turf is limited to recreation and community spaces, and native grasses are recommended for low-traffic areas. But properties with only single-unit detached, cabin or duplex dwellings are exempt from that section except street landscaping, buffering and screening, and fences and walls ([Land Use Code §4.7](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/article-4-development-standards-clean_3.pdf)). A house-only acreage lot falls under the exemption; an HOA common area or a business does not. It depends on what else is on the property: ask Larimer County Planning.
- **Weld County.** No artificial-turf provision turned up in the [Weld County code](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code).
- **Coming by 2028.** [HB25-1113](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/40673/download) requires every local government with land-use power, counties included, to regulate live turf grass in new residential development by January 1, 2028, each with its own standard.
- **HOAs and metro districts.** A rural subdivision with covenants is still an HOA. Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting artificial turf in a detached home's backyard, though it can adopt design guidelines, and makes special-district rules that prohibit backyard turf unenforceable ([SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). See [HOA approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) and [turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

The countryside around [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/) is unincorporated Larimer; around [Severance](/areas/severance-co/), [Eaton](/areas/eaton-co/) and [Milliken](/areas/milliken-co/), unincorporated Weld; [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/) sits in both counties.

## What should I ask before anyone digs on an acreage lot?

- **What does my water source allow?** A good answer comes from your well permit or tap terms.
- **Where are the well, tank, field and ports, and how will machines reach the yard?** A good answer marks each on the plan, with the machine route.
- **Where does the turf start, and how is its edge held down?** A good answer puts it outside the first 5 feet and names the edge material and fastening.
- **In what order do the trades work?** On a new build, a written sequence; see [outdoor living around turf](/guides/outdoor-living-with-artificial-turf/).
