**Checked September 25, 2026.** Turf doesn't mind what month it goes in. The ground under it and the glue in its seams do. This page sets the Northern Colorado year against both.

## What does a Northern Colorado year look like for a turf install?

An average year at NOAA's Fort Collins station ([1991–2020 normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). Longmont and Loveland run colder at night; Greeley runs hotter in summer.

| Months | Precipitation | Nights at or below 32°F | Average high | For a turf install |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dec–Feb | 1.35 in over the three months | 85.2 | 44.0–46.8°F | Workable in mild spells; frozen ground and seam curing set the pace |
| March | 1.31 in | 21.5 | 56.4°F | Thawing and often muddy |
| April | 2.10 in | 9.3 | 62.5°F | Search interest peaks; wet clay is common |
| May | 2.72 in, the wettest month | 1.2 | 70.9°F | Rain delays on clay |
| Jun–Aug | 1.45–1.90 in a month | 0 | 81.8–87.4°F | No frost; heat and thunderstorms |
| September | 1.43 in | 0.5 | 77.4°F | Dry and mild |
| October | 1.25 in | 8.5 | 64.3°F | First freezes arrive |
| November | 0.74 in | 23.9 | 52.5°F | Short, cold afternoons for seams |

Two things the table doesn't show:

- **Spring is when most people start looking.** Colorado search interest in "artificial turf" runs highest from April through June and lowest from November through January; the single highest week of the past five years was April 12–18, 2026 ([Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=US-CO&q=artificial%20turf&date=today%205-y)). If you want turf by Memorial Day, the conversation starts in winter.
- **The wettest month shifts a little by town.** May tops the year at the Greeley and Loveland stations too, at 2.65 and 2.68 inches. At Longmont 2 ESE, April edges it, 2.04 inches to 1.97.

Approvals come before any of this: an HOA or metro district review, Firestone's town permit, the utility locate. That calendar, from first call to finished yard, is in [how long turf installation takes](/guides/how-long-does-turf-installation-take/).

## Can artificial turf go in over winter?

On many winter days, yes. Two things decide it: whether the ground is frozen, and whether the seams can cure.

**Frozen ground.** A turf base is compacted rock laid over the soil. The street standards Larimer County, Loveland and Fort Collins share say work "shall not occur on the subgrade in freezing conditions, or when the subgrade is frozen," and no base course goes on "a soft, spongy, or frozen subgrade" ([Larimer County Urban Area Street Standards, Chapter 22](https://larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2023/ch22_-_construction_specifications_0.pdf)). Those rules are written for streets, not backyards, but the reason carries over: when ice in the soil melts, the water can't drain out fast enough and the soil loses bearing capacity ([Pavement Interactive](https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/)). A base compacted over frozen clay sits on ground that will soften when it thaws. What winter does to a finished yard is covered in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

**Seam glue.** Where two rolls meet, they are glued to seam tape. Adhesives have temperature limits, and they differ:

- SRW Products says its Seams Right adhesive needs surfaces "flat, completely dry, and above 40°F," free of moisture and frost, with the turf protected from rain and freezing for 12 hours afterward ([SRW Products](https://srwproducts.com/product/seams-right-turf-adhesive-srw-products/)).
- TEC's 149 two-part turf adhesive wants the adhesive, air, base and turf between 59°F and 84°F, and says lower temperatures prolong curing ([TEC data sheet](https://www.tecspecialty.com/hbfuller-media/7742/pds_tec_149_2-cturfadhesive_r0326.pdf)).
- Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a turf supplier, says cold seam edges go stiff and make a good seam harder, a heater may be needed to get cold turf to lie flat without hard stretching, and many hardware-store adhesives are water-based and can freeze ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/cold-weather-artificial-grass-installation/)).

Set those against a Northern Colorado winter:

| Nearest NOAA station | Dec–Feb days reaching 40°F | Dec–Feb nights at or below 32°F | First fall freeze, median | First 28°F night, median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fort Collins, for [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) and Wellington | 64.3 | 85.2 | Oct 7 | Oct 17 |
| Greeley UNC, for [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), Windsor and [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/) | 61.1 | 86.0 | Oct 8 | Oct 18 |
| Longmont 2 ESE, for [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/), Mead, Firestone, Frederick and Dacono | 64.5 | 88.7 | Oct 5 | Oct 12 |
| Loveland 2N, for [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) and Berthoud | 61.6 | 88.0 | Oct 1 | Oct 10 |

Station data: [Greeley UNC](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json), [Longmont 2 ESE](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00055116&format=json), [Loveland 2N](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00055236&format=json), [Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json).

About two winter afternoons in three reach 40°F, and nearly every winter night freezes. So a seam can be glued at midday in January, but SRW's 12 hours of protection will almost always cross a freezing night. Chinook winds help: the Colorado Climate Center says they "greatly moderate average winter temperatures" in places near the mountains ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). A warm week in February can be a good install week; a cold snap stops the job until the ground thaws.

## Can turf be installed in the rain, or right after it?

Not well. Three parts of the job need dry conditions.

- **The clay.** CSU Extension says soils compact more when wet because water "acts as a lubricant," and it warns never to work clayey soil wet. Its test: squeeze a handful. If it crumbles, it's dry enough; if the ball only reshapes, it's too wet. On some clays only a few days, or hours, separate too wet from too hard ([CSU Extension GardenNotes #215](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/215.pdf)). That was written for gardens, but a turf base is compacted clay too. Greeley's water department describes hard clay sometimes inches below the surface ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)), and Longmont says its clay soils absorb water slowly ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)). More in [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).
- **The seams.** SRW wants surfaces completely dry and 12 hours without rain after gluing; TEC says not to apply its adhesive in wet, raining or misty conditions.
- **The infill.** Synthetic Grass Warehouse says the turf must be dry when infill goes down, or the infill sticks to the blades instead of settling to the base of the fibers.

A morning shower can cost a day or more, because the wait is for the clay to dry, not for the rain to stop. The Colorado Climate Center says the eastern side of the state gets its heaviest, most widespread rain from April through early September, and summer rain on the plains comes largely from thunderstorms, sometimes very heavy ones. Seams glued before a storm need their full cure time.

## Is summer too hot to install turf?

No, but heat changes how the seams are handled. SRW advises peeling a seam back just before gluing, because turf backing heats up, expands and can leave gaps at the seam once it cools. TEC says heat shortens its adhesive's working time, and its range tops out at 84°F. The Greeley UNC station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F, so ask how the crew handles seams on a 90-degree afternoon. How hot finished turf gets, and what cools it, is in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## Why does fall work for turf when it's late for sod?

Because turf doesn't have to root. CSU Extension's PlantTalk Colorado says new sod should be well rooted within two to three weeks, kept moist until then, and "well established before freezing temperatures in the fall" ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1517-sodding-lawn/)). With the first fall freeze by early October in half of years, a late-September sod job is close to the line.

Some towns close the door for sod outright. Johnstown's 2026 New Lawn Permit says that regardless of the permit, "no turf establishment shall occur after October 15th," meaning a live lawn, and applications close October 12, 2026 ([Town of Johnstown](https://www.johnstownco.gov/689/New-Lawn-Permit)). Johnstown's outdoor watering season, on its Level 3 drought schedule, also ends October 15 ([2026 Watering Schedule](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)). Not legal advice: the rules are Johnstown's for 2026, and next year's may differ.

Artificial turf needs none of that. October is dry at Fort Collins, 1.25 inches in an average year, and fewer people are searching for turf. Seams still get workable afternoons until November shortens them. If you're weighing the two, see [artificial turf vs sod](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-sod/).

## What should I ask an installer about timing?

- **"What happens if the ground is frozen or soaked on install day?"** A good answer: they check it (a test hole, the squeeze test) and move the date rather than compact over frozen or wet clay.
- **"Which seam adhesive do you use, and what does its data sheet say about temperature?"** A good answer names the product, its minimum temperature, and how long seams are kept from rain, frost and foot traffic. Seam trouble after the fact is covered in [visible seams](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/).
- **"If it's a winter job, how do you keep seams warm and flat?"** Listen for midday gluing, weights on the seams, a heater or cover when needed, and rolls and adhesive stored out of the cold.
- **"Do you spread infill the same day if the turf is wet?"** The answer you want is no.
- **"How far out are you booking right now?"** Search interest peaks in April, so ask early if you want a May date.

What you do before the crew arrives is in [preparing for turf installation day](/guides/preparing-for-turf-installation-day/), and the steps of an install are on our [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) page.
