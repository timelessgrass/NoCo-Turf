  - q: "Should the trampoline be sunk into the turf?"
    a: "Only a trampoline made for it. The AAP says to set the bouncing surface as close to the ground as possible, but Springfree, a trampoline maker, says not to bury a trampoline built to stand above ground, because it could compromise jumpers' safety. Sinking one means digging through the base the turf sits on, so it is a decision for before the turf goes in, not after."
  - q: "Can a trampoline go on turf laid over a concrete patio?"
    a: "Pick a spot over soil if you can. Springfree says a surface like concrete would not absorb a fall from the trampoline very well, and suggests sandbags rather than anchors on concrete.   - q: "Sandbags or anchors?"
    a: "Anchors, where they can reach soil. Springfree suggests sandbags on the legs for extra weight if you worry the anchors won't be enough, and sandbags in place of anchors on concrete.     a: "Regularly, and after every big wind. Springfree says to inspect anchors periodically because soil conditions change, and EcoGrass, a turf company, says to inspect anchors in turf after severe weather or heavy use. If a trampoline is arriving for the holidays, a turf yard changes the questions. There is no lawn to kill under it, but there is a backing to cut, a firm base below that, and a winter of wind ahead. Anchoring play equipment in general and frost depth are in [putting a swing set or trampoline on artificial turf](/guides/swing-set-on-artificial-turf/); padded surfaces under playsets are compared in [playground surfaces compared](/guides/playground-turf-vs-mulch/).

## What do pediatricians and safety agencies say first?

That most children shouldn't jump at home. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends keeping kids away from trampolines except with professional coaches and specialized equipment, and says children under 6 should never jump ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Trampolines-What-You-Need-to-Know.aspx)). It counts about 110,000 trampoline-linked emergency room visits in the U.S. a year, and two of the ways children get hurt involve the ground and the frame around the mat: falling from the jumping surface to the ground, and falling on the springs, hooks or frame.

For families who go ahead, the rules agree:

- **One jumper at a time,** with an adult watching (AAP and the [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/085%20Trampoline%20Safety.pdf)).
- **No somersaults.** The CPSC says landing on the head or neck can cause paralysis.
- **Pads and a net.** The CPSC says not to use a trampoline without shock-absorbing pads that completely cover its springs, hooks and frame, and that enclosures can help prevent injuries from falls off trampolines. The AAP keeps the net zipped shut while children jump.
- **No ladder left out.** The CPSC says not to use one; the AAP says to take it down when no one is jumping, so small children can't climb up alone.

The standard behind home trampolines is ASTM F381. It says trampolines that meet it are not recommended for children under six, and requires each one to come with an information packet that includes assembly and installation instructions and warnings ([ASTM](https://store.astm.org/f0381-24.html)). ASTM sells the standard itself, so the manual in the box is the part of it a family can actually read. Keep it.

## How much clear space does a trampoline need on a turf lawn?

More than its own footprint. The CPSC says to place a trampoline away from structures, trees and other play areas, and the AAP says far from trees, walls, buildings and other play areas; neither gives a distance. The numbers come from the makers' manuals:

| Around the trampoline | What the makers say |
|---|---|
| Sides | Clear space on all sides, away from walls, structures, fences and other play areas ([JumpSport manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/a337c920-14f2-40bc-a243-63b1aa925eb4/50272407.pdf)). Makers typically recommend 3 to 5 feet; at least 5 feet from a wall ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/how-much-space-do-you-need-for-a-trampoline)) |
| Overhead | At least 24 feet from the ground, clear of wires and tree limbs (JumpSport; Springfree cites ASTM F2225 for the same figure) |
| Underneath | Nothing under the trampoline (JumpSport) |
| The ground | Level (JumpSport); a rise of no more than three degrees across the space (Springfree) |
| A worked example | A 14-foot trampoline with 5 feet on each side needs a 24-by-24-foot area (Springfree) |

Three things to check in a turf yard:

- **Keep the clear space on turf.** Clear space that runs onto a patio, a concrete mow curb or a paver walk ends in a hard landing; Springfree says a surface like concrete would not absorb a fall from the trampoline very well ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/does-a-trampoline-need-to-be-on-grass)).
- **Count the playset.** Both the CPSC and the AAP say to keep a trampoline away from other play areas, so its clear space and a swing set's own use zone shouldn't overlap.
- **Check the slope.** A turf base is compacted to a firm, even surface, but one maker's guide also contours it for drainage and looks where wanted ([Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf)). Lay a level across the spot before the legs go down.

## Is artificial turf a soft enough landing around a trampoline?

Not on its own, and no standard says how soft the ground around a trampoline should be. For playgrounds, the CPSC wants a surface's ASTM F1292 critical height rating to be at least the fall height of the tallest equipment, doesn't count grass or dirt as protective surfacing, and doesn't accept carpeting or mats unless they are tested to F1292 ([CPSC 2010 handbook](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf)). Trampolines sit outside that system: the CPSC's 2025 public playground handbook lists them among the equipment it doesn't recommend for public playgrounds at all ([CPSC 2025 handbook](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325_PublicPlaygroundSafetyHandbook2025_7-30-25_1.pdf)). So there is no table depth for what goes around one.

What turf changes:

- **Firmer than a lawn.** Springfree says a trampoline on artificial grass won't be as soft as on natural grass. The base under turf is built to be firm: one turf maker compacts the native soil, then about 3 inches of class II road base and 1 to 2 inches of decomposed granite (Integriturf).
- **A pad is what can be tested.** If the trampoline's clear space is part of a padded play area, ask for the F1292 report for that turf, pad and infill together; what a good report looks like is in [playground surfaces compared](/guides/playground-turf-vs-mulch/).
- **The safety rules do the rest.** The CPSC and the AAP don't lean on the ground. They lean on keeping children on the mat: one jumper, the net zipped, the pads in place, and the bouncing surface as close to the ground as possible.

## How do you anchor a trampoline through artificial turf?

With the kit made for that trampoline, through small cuts in the backing, down into the soil under the base. Wind is the reason. The National Weather Service office for northeast Colorado ties Colorado's high winds to the cold season, when winds aloft are much stronger than in summer, says winds of 60 to near 100 mph reach foothill areas such as Fort Collins, and that lightweight objects should come inside or be tied down when high winds are forecast ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/bou/highwind)). JumpSport's manual says high wind can blow a trampoline about and that even anchored trampolines can become airborne; the CPSC's tip sheet says not to use one in high wind, because it can shift, move or blow away ([CPSC tip sheet](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/Trampoline%20Safety_Eng01.pdf)).

What an anchor kit meets in a turf yard:

- **The backing.** EcoGrass, a turf company, cuts a small X in the backing for each anchor and uses a drill for twist-in anchors ([EcoGrass](https://www.ecograssus.com/blog/2020/1/30/inserting-anchor-or-stake-into-turf)).
- **The base.** EcoGrass says ground anchors screw into the base material. In Integriturf's method that is about 4 to 5 inches of compacted road base and decomposed granite before the anchor reaches soil.
- **What the kit expects.** JumpKing's tie-down kit uses four 13-inch auger stakes, turned in about 6 inches outside each leg, and says the ground should be soft, even softened with water first ([JumpKing](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/67/67ae0f30-acce-4603-875e-168e1f889c7b.pdf)). The top of a turf yard is the opposite, so ask the installer how anchors go through the base, and tell them where the trampoline will stand if the turf isn't in yet.
- **Lines under the turf.** Colorado 811 says state law requires contacting it at least two full business days before digging, lists driving landscaping stakes among the examples, and doesn't mark private lines such as sprinkler lines ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging)). JumpKing lists sprinkler lines among what to clear, and Springfree warns to check for utility lines before installing anchors ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/how-to-anchor-a-trampoline)). Not legal advice.

Before a forecast wind, JumpSport says to secure the frame with an anchor kit, and before severe winds to move the trampoline to shelter or take it apart; Springfree adds lowering the enclosure. JumpSport also suggests asking a qualified contractor which anchor works where you live. Wind that moves a trampoline can find a loose turf edge too: walk the edges afterward ([turf edges lifting](/guides/turf-edges-lifting/)).

## Will a trampoline leave a dead spot on artificial turf?

Not the yellow circle it leaves on a lawn. Springfree says grass under a trampoline yellows, thins and dies mainly because the mat blocks sunlight, while the legs compact the soil, and it suggests moving the trampoline two to three feet every couple of weeks so the grass can recover ([Springfree](https://www.springfreetrampoline.com/blogs/beyond-the-bounce/will-a-trampoline-kill-your-grass)). Synthetic grass under a trampoline, the same page says, requires no sunlight. On turf there's no need to rotate it for the lawn's sake.

What turf does feel is the legs:

- **Weight on small feet.** Springfree says the legs concentrate hundreds of pounds on a few small contact points, and that flat pads under each leg spread the weight.
- **Pressed pile.** Where a leg or a pad sat all season, brush the fibers back up; see [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/).
- **Around each anchor.** EcoGrass adds a little infill around each anchor to keep the spot level and brushes the fibers there to keep them from matting.

## How do you move a trampoline on artificial turf?

The way its manual says, and with a plan for the anchor holes. JumpSport's manual uses at least two people, keeps the trampoline horizontal, lifts one side and swings that end about five feet, then does the same at the other end, and says to take it apart for any move of more than a few feet. Then:

- **Anchors out first,** by the kit's own instructions.
- **Close up the old spot.** EcoGrass says to cover unused anchor holes in turf with plugs to keep debris out and keep the lawn looking right, then brush the fibers back. Damage bigger than a small cut is a [repair job](/guides/how-to-repair-artificial-turf/).
- **Start over at the new spot:** the clear-space check above, and Colorado 811 before the new anchors go in.

## What if the trampoline arrives in December?

Then it meets the windy season first. A few things to settle before it goes up:

- **Up now, or in spring?** JumpSport recommends that where snow and ice are common, the mat, springs, frame pad and net come off and go into dry storage out of the sun; Springfree says its trampolines can stay outdoors year-round, even in snowy climates. Follow the manual for the model you have.
- **Anchored before the first jump.** If it goes up now, anchor it then. Colorado 811's 48-hour count leaves out weekends, the day of the request and legal holidays, so ask early in holiday week.
- **Snow on the mat.** Springfree clears it with a soft-bristled broom or brush, not a shovel, and JumpSport warns against a shovel, hoe or garden rake on the mat. Snow on the turf around it is covered in [snow removal on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).
