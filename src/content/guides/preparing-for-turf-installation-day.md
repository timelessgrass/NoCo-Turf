faq:
  - q: "What do the colored flags and paint in my yard mean?"
    a: "They follow a national color code. Colorado's Public Utilities Commission lists red for electric, yellow for gas, blue for drinking water, orange for communication lines such as cable or internet, purple for reclaimed water, irrigation and slurry lines, green for sewers and drains, white for proposed excavation and pink for temporary survey marks."
  - q: "Does having private lines marked cost anything?"
    a: "The 811 locate of public lines is free. Colorado 811 puts private locate services at $100 to $350 on average, depending on the company and the type and size of the property, and its website lists private locating companies with a filter for each county, Larimer and Weld included."
  - q: "Can I pull up the lawn myself before the crew comes?"
    a: "Ask the installer first, because the grade you leave is where their base starts. If you do dig, the 811 rule applies to you too: Colorado 811 says hand digging, grading and scraping all need a locate, however shallow, and that digging waits until every utility has posted an all-clear."
  - q: "What if a buried line gets hit during the dig?"
    a: "For a utility's line, Colorado 811 says to get to safety, call 911 in an emergency and report the damage to 811 right away. A cut sprinkler pipe or dog-fence wire is a repair between you and the installer, so ask before the job starts how they handle one."
  - q: "What if rain or snow pushes the install date back?"
    a: "Ask the installer whether the date moves; one turf maker's installation manual says rain-soaked soil may need a few days to dry before work begins. A long delay can also outlast the locate: Colorado law treats marks as valid only while they're clearly visible and for no more than 30 calendar days, after which the excavator has to notify 811 again."

## Which lines in my yard won't Colorado 811 mark?

Anything you own. Colorado 811 passes locate requests to the utilities, and each marks only the lines it owns. 811 lists private lines as sprinkler systems, gas lines to backyard grills, invisible fences, septic systems, buried water tanks and service lines, and says the owner pays a private locating company to find them ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/find-a-private-locating-company)).

| Line | Who marks it | What to do before the crew comes |
|---|---|---|
| Gas, electric, communications, water and sewer lines owned by a utility | The utility, after an 811 ticket | Check that every utility has responded |
| Sprinkler pipe, heads and valve wires | No one, unless you hire a private locator | Flag heads and valve boxes |
| Landscape lighting cable | No one, unless you hire a private locator | Mark fixtures and known runs |
| Dog-fence boundary wire | No one, unless you hire a private locator | Mark the loop |
| Gas line to a grill, fire pit or heater | No one, unless you hire a private locator | Mark the run from the house |
| Water service line from the meter pit to the house | You, in Little Thompson Water District | Ask where your provider's locate stops |
| Septic tank and drain field | You | Mark both; see [turf over a septic field](/guides/artificial-turf-over-septic-field/) |

Depth is why this matters. A sod cutter alone can slice up to 2.5 inches down ([Ryan's Jr. Sod Cutter spec](https://contractorssupplyllc.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOD-Cutter-Ryan-Manual.pdf)), and PetSafe tells owners to bury a dog-fence wire [1 to 3 inches deep](https://www.petsafe.net/media/manuals/PIG00-14582_Basic_In-Ground_Fence_Manual.pdf). The base then goes in below the sod line; [Firestone's code](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE), for one, requires at least 3 inches of compacted aggregate. Low-voltage lighting cable is typically buried at least 6 inches deep ([VOLT](https://www.voltlighting.com/learn/how-to-install-landscape-lighting)), so ask how deep the dig goes; at 6 inches or more, the lighting is in play too.

Two Northern Colorado specifics:

- **Your water line is partly yours.** Little Thompson Water District, which serves [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/), [Mead](/areas/mead-co/) and [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) customers, says its locate runs from the main to the meter pit, and the line from the meter pit to the house is yours to locate ([LTWD](https://www.littlethompsonwd.gov/call-before-you-dig-in-colorado-call-811)).
- **Oil and gas flowlines go through 811.** If there are wells near your subdivision: the state's Energy and Carbon Management Commission says its public flowline map is approximate and incomplete, and that Colorado 811 must be used to locate any flowline precisely ([ECMC](https://ecmc.colorado.gov/data-maps-reports/maps-gis/flowlines-gis-data)).

**Who calls 811? Not legal advice.** Colorado law says nobody may begin excavation without first notifying Colorado 811, at least two business days ahead, not counting the day of notice, and it counts grading and scraping as excavation. Its one landscape exception, routine maintenance, excludes work meant to permanently lessen the ground cover or lower the ground, which is what taking out a lawn does ([C.R.S. 9-1.5-102 and 9-1.5-103](https://ops.colorado.gov/sites/ops/files/documents/UDPSafetyCommissionStatutes010623.pdf)). The duty sits with whoever digs, but Colorado 811 tells homeowners not to rely only on the landscaper ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging)). Ask the installer for the ticket number and look it up on 811's ticket-status page.

Once the paint and flags are down, leave them alone: skip the last mow, and don't rake or rinse them off. Within 18 inches of a mark, Colorado 811 says to dig only by hand, if at all ([What to Know Before You Dig](https://www.colorado811.org/residential-digging/what-you-need-to-know)).

## Why does the crew want a sprinkler map, and when can I make one?

Because the pipe and heads sit where the dig happens. Tarkett's installation manual warns that irrigation and electrical lines may lie just under the surface and tells installers to locate every irrigation line and head, cap what isn't needed and test for leaks before starting ([Tarkett](https://tarketthome.com/TarkettResidential/media/Tarkett-Residential/Product%20Documents/Artificial%20Turf/Artificial-Turf-On-the-Green-Installation-Instructions.pdf?ext=.pdf)). Your map saves that hunt:

- Run each zone and push a flag in beside every head inside the future turf area.
- Note which zones also water trees, shrubs or beds that stay.
- Mark the valve boxes, the backflow device and the controller's zone numbers.
- Photograph the flagged yard; the flags come out when the sod does.

Your 2026 watering rules decide when you can run the zones:

- **Johnstown** is on its Level 3 schedule: 2 assigned days a week, no watering from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., and the outdoor season ends October 15. The Town says servicing that can't fit the assigned days should be raised with its conservation staff first ([Johnstown water FAQs](https://www.johnstownco.gov/m/faq?cat=37); [2026 schedule](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)).
- **Fort Collins** asked residents under its voluntary Water Shortage Watch to water lawns no more than 2 days a week and not from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/News-Articles/News/2026-Q2/Voluntary-Water-Shortage-Watch)).
- **Little Thompson Water District** is at Watch, its lowest level, with no mandatory restrictions ([LTWD](https://www.littlethompsonwd.gov/quarterly-news-from-ltwd-summer-2026)).

A fall install also meets the winter blowout. At NOAA's Fort Collins station the median first fall freeze is October 7 ([NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)), and a system that has already been blown out can't be run to find heads. Flag them before the blowout.

## Can the crew and the material get into the backyard?

Measure the narrowest point between the street and the turf: a gate, a side yard, an air-conditioner pad. For scale, Toro says its narrow-track Dingo TX 1000 compact loader is under 35 inches wide, enough for a standard 36-inch gate, and weighs 2,826 pounds ([Toro](https://www.toro.com/en/product/22587)); Ryan's Jr. Sod Cutter is 25 inches wide. A narrower opening means wheelbarrows or a fence panel coming out. Ask the installer which, and ask the neighbor first if the fence is shared.

- **The path.** CSU Extension says there should be no grading, trenching, parking or stockpiling of materials inside a tree's protected root zone, which starts at the dripline and needs more room on clay ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/tree-preservation-during-construction/)). Keep the route and the rock pile away from trees you're keeping.
- **The driveway.** Base rock, turf rolls and the old sod need somewhere to sit, usually the driveway. Move the cars out the night before. If a pile or trailer will sit in the street, ask your town and your HOA or metro district whether that needs a permit.
- **Water.** Tarkett's manual has the area lightly wetted before the base is compacted. Show the crew a working outdoor tap, and check it hasn't been shut off for winter.
- **Photos.** Photograph the driveway, fences, gate and lawn edges the day before, so any damage is easy to show.

## What should happen with dogs, kids and neighbors?

The locators come first. Colorado 811 asks that the area be completely open to locate crews during the 3 business days after the ticket is confirmed: no locked gates and no animals.

On install day the gates stand open and machines come and go, so plan for the dog to be inside, at daycare or with a friend, and the kids somewhere the loader isn't.

With an in-ground dog fence, plan for the wire before the dig. PetSafe recommends burying the wire so it isn't damaged and the system isn't disabled, and lists digging around landscaping as a common cause of breaks. Ask the installer where the loop goes back: under the turf or along its edge. For the dog itself, see our [pet turf page](/services/pet-turf/) and the guide to [dogs that dig through turf](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/).

Tell the neighbors when a truck will block the street.

## Which papers should be on hand when the crew arrives?

- **The HOA's written approval,** with any conditions on color, borders or layout. Give the crew lead a copy, and walk the marked outline against the approved plan before the first cut. Our [guide to HOA turf approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) covers the request.
- **Firestone's town permit.** Firestone requires a permit before artificial turf is installed, with no fee for single-family homes. Elsewhere, our [town-by-town turf rules](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/) list what each code says. Where a code doesn't settle your lot, it depends on the project, so ask your town's Planning department before the dig.
- **The 811 ticket number,** so you can check that every utility has responded.
- **Your sprinkler map** and the product spec sheet the HOA approved, so the rolls that arrive match it.

## What should I check before the crew leaves?

Tarkett's manual has the irrigation turned on once the turf is down, to check that heads are capped and no pipe was punctured, and the lighting checked, with repairs finished before infill goes on. Ask to see your remaining zones run at that point. Then walk it:

- The zones that stay reach their trees and beds, and no water pools on the new turf.
- The dog fence and the landscape lights work.
- Gates close and latch.
- Leftover rock and old sod are gone, and the driveway and street are swept.
- Your sprinkler map is updated to show what was capped and what now runs under the turf, so the next fence post or tree planting misses it.

What you can't see, such as the base and weed barrier, is in our guide to [checking the base was installed](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/).
