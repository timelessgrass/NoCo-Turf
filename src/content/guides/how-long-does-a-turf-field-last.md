  answer: "Most synthetic sports fields last 8 to 12 years; the Synthetic Turf Council says 8 to 10, and heavy use shortens that. Replacement swaps the carpet; the infill is reused or hauled away. **Checked September 27, 2026.** This page is for whoever answers for a synthetic sports field in Northern Colorado: a school district, a parks department, a club or a metro district. It covers how long a field lasts, what wears one out here, and what replacing it involves, drawn from industry guidelines and what local owners have published. Whether a field should be synthetic in the first place is in our [guide to synthetic and grass sports fields](/guides/turf-vs-grass-sports-fields/).

## How many years does a synthetic turf field last?

Most published estimates fall between 8 and 12 years:

| Who says | Estimate | What moves it |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic Turf Council, 2017 | 8 to 10 years | Use, intense sunlight, maintenance, overuse |
| SportsField Management, 2022 | 8 to 12 years | Worn fibers, lost infill, torn seams, hardness |
| California DTSC, 2026 | 5 to 15 years | How intensively the field is used |
| City of Longmont, Dry Creek fields | 10 or more years for the top layer | Proper maintenance |

Sources: [Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guideline_for_Recycle_Re.pdf), [SportsField Management](https://sportsfieldmanagementonline.com/2022/04/28/recycling-and-repurposing-synthetic-turf/13693/), [California DTSC](https://web.archive.org/web/20260326043412/https://dtsc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2026/02/TechnicalDocument_PFASs-in-Artificial-Turf_accessible.pdf) and [City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/facility/dry-creek-community-park/).

How hard a field is used matters most. In 2015 Poudre School District's athletic director said the turf at French Field, installed in July 2012, should last 12 to 14 years under normal use, but that at its schedule it would need replacing after about eight. French Field had hosted 43 events in a 12-week fall season, and he wanted more multi-use fields so the district could "get the life out of our fields" ([The Coloradoan](https://web.archive.org/web/20151224184223/http://usatodayhss.com/2015/psd-looks-to-add-new-sports-complex)).

## What wears a field out faster in Northern Colorado?

- **Hours of play.** A stadium carrying several schools' games, as French Field did, wears fastest. Spreading play across more fields is the plainest way to stretch each one.
- **Sun at altitude.** The Synthetic Turf Council counts exposure to intense sunlight among the factors that set a field's life. The EPA says UV rises about 6% for each kilometer above sea level ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/learn-about-uv-index)), and NOAA's stations along the corridor sit at about 4,700 to 5,100 feet. By our arithmetic, that is roughly 9% more UV than at sea level.
- **Freeze-thaw under the carpet.** The Fort Collins station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F ([NOAA](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). The council's base guidelines say water left in low spots under the stone makes soft spots that freeze and swell in cold climates ([STC base guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Base_Syst.pdf)). That damage shows up when the base is judged at replacement time.
- **Hail.** NOAA logged 536 hail reports in Weld County and 253 in Larimer County from 2011 through 2025 ([NOAA Storm Events](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/)). It is a storm risk more than a wear rate. A 2008 guide from the Sports Turf Managers Association (STMA) tells owners to check whether their insurance covers a field for Acts of God and vandalism, and says add-on coverage may be needed ([STMA guide](https://www.stma.org/sites/stma/files/STMA_Synthetic_Guide_2nd_Edition.pdf)).
- **Grooming.** The STMA says a field's hardness tends to rise as it ages, faster or slower with its construction, play, weather and how often it is maintained. The year-round routine is in our [guide to synthetic turf field maintenance](/guides/synthetic-turf-field-maintenance/).

## Which signs mean a field is due for replacement?

SportsField Management lists four: torn seams, lost infill, degraded or worn fibers, and high GMAX readings. Hardness is the one with a set limit. ASTM F1936 sets an upper limit of 200 Gmax, above which, the STMA says, life-threatening head injuries may be expected ([STMA field hardness paper](https://www.sportsfieldmanagement.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Field_Hardness-FINAL-2.pdf)).

One high reading is usually a maintenance job. Our reading, not a standard's: when hardness climbs back soon after top-ups and grooming, and seams and fibers keep failing where play is heaviest, the carpet is near its end, and the next step is pricing a replacement rather than another repair.

## What gets replaced, and what can stay?

The carpet always comes out. The rest depends on its condition:

| Layer | At replacement |
|---|---|
| Turf carpet | Replaced |
| Infill | Cleaned and reused in the new field, often topped up with new infill, or hauled away |
| Shock pad | Can stay if sound; the council's pad guidelines set durability for at least two carpet lifecycles ([SportsField Management, 2020](https://sportsfieldmanagementonline.com/2020/08/27/new-shock-pad-guidance-offers-insight-into-selection-and-maintenance-process/11895/)) |
| Stone base and drainage | Can stay if in good shape, per the American Sports Builders Association's field manual as summarized by [SportsField Management](https://sportsfieldmanagementonline.com/2024/09/26/synthetic-turf-updates-from-the-field/17226/) |

Local owners have gone both ways. [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) plans to replace the Dry Creek top layer at 10 years and the pad under it no sooner than 2049; the pad carries 25 years of written coverage. Weld RE-4 replaced both the turf and the shock pad at [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) High School's stadium between May and August 2023 ([Weld RE-4](https://bond.weldre4.org/projects/repairs-replacements)).

Before anything comes up, the council says to settle three things: whether the base is stable enough to work on without being disturbed, who is responsible if removal damages it, and how long the contractor answers for the base afterward. A field built to a FIFA or World Rugby standard may not accept reused infill, so check that before counting on it.

## How do Northern Colorado schools and parks plan and pay for it?

Through bonds, mill levies and capital plans. Here is what local owners have published:

| Field | Synthetic since | What the owner published |
|---|---|---|
| French Field, Rocky Mountain High, [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | July 2012 | Poudre's 2024 mill levy, $22.9 million a year for upkeep, lists synthetic turf among Rocky Mountain High's projects for its first five years ([PSD](https://www.psdschools.org/community/2024-mill-levy/facilities-projects)) |
| Fossil Ridge High, Fort Collins | Not stated | "In dire need of replacement" in 2015, with about $500,000 planned for 2015 or 2016 |
| Windsor High stadium | Not stated | Turf and shock pad replaced in 2023, a $1.3M line in Weld RE-4's bond |
| Mead High, [Mead](/areas/mead-co/) | Not stated | New turf in August 2021 replaced the old turf field ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/en/articles/detail/how-mead-high-school-became-the-coolest-high-school-in-colorado/)) |
| Ray Patterson Stadium, [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) | 2020 | Thompson School District's first synthetic field, $900,000 of bond money with goalposts and a fence, as a local station reported ([Retro 102.5](https://retro1025.com/take-at-look-at-lovelands-ray-patterson-stadium-upgrade-video/)) |
| Nottingham Field, UNC, [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | 2021 | Grass replaced by turf for the September 18 home opener ([UNC Athletics](https://uncbears.com/news/2021/6/16/football-nottingham-field-to-receive-a-change-of-surface)) |
| Dry Creek Community Park, Longmont | Built after January 2024 | Top layer in the five-year capital plan from 2029, with funds projected for 2034 |
| Chimney Park fields 2 and 3, Windsor | May 2026 | A $2,240,000 budget to convert grass to turf ([Town of Windsor](https://windsorprojectconnect.com/chimneyparkturf)) |

Longmont's approach is the one to copy: a replacement year set when the field went in, and the money in the capital plan five years ahead. The fields that went in from 2020 to 2022 reach the council's 8-to-10-year range around the end of this decade, so their owners' next capital plans are where those replacements will show up.

For an outside benchmark, the STMA's 2008 guide said to budget $6.50 to $7.80 a square foot for disposal and resurfacing; those are 2008 prices. Windsor High's replacement ran from May through August, in the summer break between school seasons.

## What happens to the old carpet and infill?

There is a lot of it. The council puts an average field at about 80,000 square feet, with about 400,000 pounds of infill and 40,000 pounds of turf. The infill alone is roughly 400 cubic yards, almost fourteen 30-yard dumpsters.

- **Get the infill out first.** SportsField Management says 75% or more of a field's weight is infill, so extracting it cuts hauling and disposal. A reclamation firm told the magazine that contractors often take something off the bid when the old infill goes back into the new field.
- **Cut and roll the carpet.** Specialized equipment cuts it into sections and rolls it into bundles. The council calls shipping one of the biggest challenges of reclaiming turf, and says to look at every recycling and reuse option in the region before landfill.
- **Ask what recycling costs.** Longmont's turf came with its maker's end-of-life program, which tracks each field by reference number, at $0.15 a square foot plus freight.
- **Keep a paper trail.** The council recommends a chain-of-custody record for every load, whether recycled, reused or landfilled, with a third-party signature confirming where it arrived.

Where a load of old turf can go in Larimer and Weld counties is in our [guide to getting rid of old artificial turf](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-artificial-turf/).

## What should an owner ask before replacing a field?

- **What is down there now?** The council says to know the turf system and its maker, and to ask the original maker for product information on the carpet, infill and pad if the file is missing.
- **Will the base, drainage and pad be tested before anyone decides they stay?** A good answer names the tests, and who pays if removal damages the base.
- **Can our infill go back in, and what is the credit?** A good answer puts a figure on the reused infill in the bid.
- **Where does each load go?** A good answer names the recycler, reuse buyer or landfill, with chain-of-custody paperwork.
- **Does the new carpet meet Colorado's PFAS law?** Since January 1, 2026, Colorado has barred installing artificial turf with intentionally added PFAS ([SB24-081](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-081)). Longmont had its turf fiber tested for fluorine and published the result, below the test's 1.0 ppm detection limit.
