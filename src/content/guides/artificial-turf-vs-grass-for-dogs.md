A dog tests a lawn where it pees and where it runs. Bluegrass and artificial turf fail those tests in different ways here, and a grass selected for dog yards splits the difference with trade-offs of its own.

## What does a dog do to a bluegrass lawn?

Two kinds of damage, and this climate slows the repair of both.

**Urine spots.** CSU Extension says the straw-colored spots with dark green borders come from the concentrated salts in dog urine, particularly from female dogs ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1503-dog-spots/)). It says water is the only thing that reverses the damage. That runs into this year's watering rules:

- **Fort Collins** asked residents from May 1, 2026 to water lawns no more than 2 days a week and never between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., under a voluntary Water Shortage Watch.
- **Johnstown** is on a Level 3 schedule: 2 assigned days a week, and the outdoor season ends October 15 ([Town of Johnstown](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)).
- **Evans** declared a drought emergency on April 21, 2026, and its outdoor watering restrictions are enforced.
- **Greeley** went the other way and declared 2026 an adequate water year, so residents water under normal guidelines through October 31.

Bluegrass does heal well. CSU lists "high recuperative potential" among its strengths ([GardenNotes #561](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/561.pdf)), and says homeowners found twice-weekly watering still produced healthy bluegrass lawns. The trouble is the ratio: more dogs on less lawn make new spots faster than old ones fill in.

**Worn paths and spring mud.** Dogs run the same line along the fence every day. CSU Extension says traffic is the major cause of soil compaction, and that a moist soil can reach 75% of maximum compaction the first time it is stepped on and 90% by the fourth ([GardenNotes #215](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/215.pdf)). Wet soil compacts more easily, and much of Northern Colorado is clay that the Colorado Geological Survey says can swell up to 20% by volume when wet. Fort Collins' NOAA station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year, and every melt soaks the path again. The grass thins, the path goes to bare clay, and the dogs carry it inside. If the whole yard stays wet, not only the dogs' path, read whether turf fixes a muddy backyard first: that is a drainage problem.

## What does a dog do to artificial turf?

Turf takes away both lawn problems. Nothing dies, and the fence line has no grass to wear through to mud. It adds two chores in their place.

**Urine has to leave.** It no longer soaks into living roots; it passes through the turf's backing into the base below. A turf manufacturer's care guide tells pet owners to hose down each spot where the dog urinates, which "washes it through the turf's drainage system" ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/maintenance/artificial-turf-maintenance-guide/)), and to use an enzyme deodorizer about once or twice a month where odor persists. For solids, see [dogs pooping on turf](/guides/dog-poop-on-artificial-turf/); for what goes between the blades, [infill for dogs](/guides/dog-turf-infill/).

**Heat.** Penn State's sports-surface researchers report that synthetic turf generally runs 35 to 55°F hotter than natural grass, which commonly measures 75 to 95°F on a hot day and very rarely tops 100°F ([Penn State](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature.pdf/@@download/file/temperature.pdf)). Their figures come from sports fields, but the reason carries over to a yard: grass leaves release water vapor and cool themselves, and plastic blades don't. Surfaces get hot on sunny, clear, low-humidity afternoons, and hosing turf down helps only briefly: in Penn State's tests, temperatures rebounded 20 minutes after watering. The plains towns get more hot afternoons: the NOAA station at Greeley averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F, against 29.5 at Fort Collins. More in [how hot turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/) and [whether turf is safe for dogs and cats](/guides/is-artificial-turf-safe-for-pets/).

**Digging.** A dog that digs grass will try turf too; see [stopping a digger](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/).

## How do bluegrass, turf and Dog Tuff compare for a dog yard?

| What the dogs do | Kentucky bluegrass | Artificial turf | Dog Tuff grass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urinate | Straw-colored spots with dark green borders; water is the fix | No dead spots; each spot needs hosing through | "VERY resistant to dog urine" |
| Run the fence line | Heals well, but wet clay paths compact and turn to mud | No grass to wear through | Recovers from heavy foot traffic quickly |
| Lie in afternoon sun | Commonly 75 to 95°F at the surface on a hot day | Generally 35 to 55°F hotter than grass | Loves full, hot sun |
| Use a shady corner | Poor to fair shade tolerance | Needs no sun | Not tolerant of shade |
| Color through the year | Green when watered; can go dormant in drought | Same color all year | Green from early June to about October 1 |
| Water it needs | Twice a week kept lawns healthy under past restrictions | Rinse water for the dog spots | May need it only every 10 to 14 days once established |

Sources: CSU Extension (bluegrass), Penn State sports-field research (heat), Plant Select (Dog Tuff).

## Which grass holds up to dogs in Colorado?

The grass named for dog yards is Dog Tuff, introduced by Plant Select, the nonprofit plant program Colorado State University helped create. Its profile says the grass is named for its durability in yards with dogs, calls it very resistant to dog urine and quick to recover from heavy foot traffic, and lists clay among the soils it grows in ([Plant Select](https://plantselect.org/plant/cynodon-pwin04s/)). The trade-offs are real:

- **It is tan most of the year.** It is a warm-season grass, not green until early June or after October 1 on the Front Range ([Plant Select's growing guide](https://plantselect.org/learn/dog-tuff-grass-everything-you-need-to-know/)).
- **Full sun only.** It isn't tolerant of shade.
- **The old lawn comes out first.** It doesn't blend with other grasses, so the existing lawn is killed at least 2 weeks before planting. Plugs go in from late May until July 15, and late plantings may not fill in until the second summer.
- **It spreads by runners.** Plant Select says it is sterile, so it won't reseed, but it can reach a neighbor's yard by runners; it suggests a border to keep it in bounds. Dog Tuff is a Cynodon, the bermudagrass genus, and CSU Extension says bermudagrass that gets into a lawn is considered a weed there and is hard to eradicate.
- **Less water and mowing once it is in.** Established, it may need water only every 10 to 14 days, and mowing only for a trim look. No weed-and-feed products.

Buffalo grass is the other low-water grass people ask about, but CSU Extension says it will not tolerate heavy, constant traffic and is not well adapted to small, heavily used home lawns. The water-saving grasses are compared in [turf or a low-water grass](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-low-water-grass/).

## When should a dog owner keep the grass?

Keep bluegrass, or patch it, when most of these are true:

- One dog, or a small one, on a big lawn, so the spots have room to heal.
- You can water, and your town's schedule lets you.
- The dogs' favorite spot is in full afternoon sun and they lie out there in July.
- Trees in the lawn depend on its water; see [watering trees once the lawn is turf](/guides/watering-trees-with-artificial-turf/).
- You won't hose down the dog's spots through the summer.
- The dog digs, and that isn't solved yet.

Try CSU Extension's own fix first: train the dog to a designated area off the grass, such as a graveled corner. For a dedicated run, compare [pea gravel and turf for a dog run](/guides/pea-gravel-vs-turf-dog-run/). For spots that won't come back, CSU says to reseed or resod after heavily watering the spots with a hose for a few days.

## When is turf worth it with dogs?

- Two or more dogs on a small lawn, where the spots join up and the grass never catches up.
- A fence-line path that turns to mud every thaw and comes inside on paws.
- Spots you have already reseeded or resodded, only to lose them again the next summer.
- A shady yard where bluegrass is thin anyway, given its poor to fair shade tolerance; see [turf in a shady yard](/guides/artificial-turf-shady-north-facing-yard/).

Before you hire, ask how urine will drain through the turf and the base on your soil, what infill goes in the dog area, and where you will hook up a hose to rinse it. A good answer names the drainage path on clay and a rinse routine you can keep, not only the turf product.
