  - q: "Is there a rebate for new sod or for artificial turf?"
    a: "No Northern Colorado program we checked pays for artificial turf, and the lawn-replacement rebates pay to take lawn out, not to put it in.   - q: "Will Colorado law stop me from putting in new sod?"
    a: "State law doesn't, today. By January 1, 2028, every Colorado town and county with zoning power must regulate live turf grass in new residential development and redevelopment, each choosing its own standard, and none may restrict native, arid-hybridized or low-water grass seed or sod ([HB25-1113](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/40673/download)). Not legal advice."
  - q: "Should I resod the whole yard or fix the thin spots?"
    a: "If the soil is sound and the lawn is only thin, CSU Extension describes a partial renovation instead: core aerate thoroughly, overseed, and keep the seed moist while it sprouts. It calls September a good time to overseed along the Front Range. If the same spots thin out every summer, find the cause (shade, traffic, dogs, packed clay) before you buy seed or sod."
  - q: "If I choose turf and change my mind, can I go back to grass?"
    a: "Yes, but it is a project: the turf and the base under it come out, and the soil is prepared as for any new lawn, under the same watering rules."

Sod fails early if it dries out before it roots, and slowly if the watering, mowing and feeding stop. Turf skips that work, but it runs warm in the sun, gives a tree nothing to drink, and wears out in time. Here is what each asks of a Northern Colorado yard.

## What does new sod need in its first weeks?

Water, often, until it roots. CSU Extension says to keep new sod and the soil under it moist, not soggy, and that it should be well rooted within two to three weeks; tug a corner to check ([PlantTalk Colorado 1517](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1517-sodding-lawn/)). After that, water less often and more deeply.

The ground under it matters as much:

- **Prep.** CSU says to grade drainage away from the foundation, loosen compacted soil 12 inches deep, and till in three to five cubic yards of compost or other organic matter per 1,000 square feet. On expansive clay, the Colorado Geological Survey lists irrigation water soaking in beside foundations among the landscaping problems on that soil ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)), so the grade is not a detail.
- **Compaction.** CSU says sod laid over compacted soil develops a thatch problem within a couple of years ([GardenNotes #551](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/551.pdf)). A new-build lot packed down by equipment is the usual case; see [what to do with a new-build dirt backyard](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/).
- **Timing.** CSU wants the roots established before the fall freeze, and says sod laid late in the fall must be watered through the winter, as often as every 2 to 3 weeks in a dry one ([PlantTalk Colorado 1539](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1539-fall-planting-sod/)).

That last point is where town watering rules come in.

## What do Johnstown, Severance and Fort Collins allow for new sod this year?

A schedule of assigned days can't keep sod moist for three weeks, so these towns handle new lawns separately:

| Town | 2026 watering rule | What a new lawn gets | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Johnstown | Level 3: 2 assigned days a week, no watering from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. | A Town New Lawn Permit, which the municipal code requires before newly planted grass is watered. It lifts the assigned days, not the 10-to-6 ban, and expires 45 days after installation | Applications close October 12, 2026. No lawn may be established after October 15, when watering drops to hand-watering trees and shrubs |
| Severance | Standing schedule: 3 assigned days, no watering from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. | A variance from the Town Manager for establishing a new lawn, requested at Town Hall | No lawn watering from December 1 through March 31 |
| Fort Collins | Voluntary Water Shortage Watch, still the posted level on September 25: lawns 2 days and 1 inch of water a week, not from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. | Nothing to apply for while the Watch is voluntary. Under mandatory restrictions, exception permits cover new seed and sod | The exception permits are posted for use only if mandatory restrictions begin |

What that means on the ground:

- **Johnstown.** Even with the permit, July sod gets no water in the heat of the day ([Town of Johnstown](https://www.johnstownco.gov/689/New-Lawn-Permit)), and late sod can't get the winter water CSU says it needs.
- **Severance.** Lawn watering ends November 30, so late-fall sod goes into winter on whatever water it got by then unless the Town grants a variance; ask Town Hall before you order ([Town of Severance](https://www.townofseverance.org/396/Watering-Guidelines-and-Restrictions)). The schedule doesn't apply to approved non-potable systems or North Weld County Water District systems, so check which one waters your lot.
- **Fort Collins.** The Watch covers Fort Collins Utilities customers, and grass irrigated with well or raw water is exempt ([Fort Collins Utilities](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Programs-and-Rebates/Water-Programs)). If another provider serves your lot, ask it.

Elsewhere, ask your water provider how it treats a new lawn before the sod is cut. Turf needs no establishment water, so these windows don't bind it; [when to install artificial turf](/guides/when-to-install-artificial-turf/) covers what the season does to the install.

## What does a lawn ask for every year, and what does turf ask?

Once sod roots, the work becomes weekly. From CSU Extension's [Lawn Care Basics](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/lawn-care-basics/) and [GardenNotes #564](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/564.pdf):

| | Bluegrass lawn | Artificial turf |
|---|---|---|
| Water | About 24 inches of supplemental irrigation a year on the Front Range, and up to 2.25 inches a week in hot, dry, windy weather | None for the surface; dog areas and dust need rinsing ([do you have to water turf?](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/)) |
| Mowing | At 2.5 to 3 inches: every three to four days in spring, every seven to 10 days when heat, drought or cold slow growth | None |
| Feeding | Nitrogen in up to four windows: mid-March to April, May to mid-June, mid-August to mid-September, early October to early November | None |
| Aeration | Core aeration, which CSU calls essential for all lawns | None; clearing leaves and wind-blown dirt, and [topping up infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/) instead |
| When it wears | A thin lawn can be renovated in place | It gets replaced; Firestone's code even requires turf to be replaced once its life expectancy is reached ([how long turf lasts](/guides/how-long-does-artificial-turf-last/)) |

CSU's own note on artificial turf is even-handed: it needs no mowing, fertilizing or watering and "can be a legitimate solution for high traffic sites," but it lacks the environmental benefits of living grass ([PlantTalk Colorado 1537](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1537-artificial-turf-lawns/)). The environmental trade is in [is artificial turf bad for the environment?](/guides/is-artificial-turf-bad-for-environment/), and the real turf chores here in [does artificial turf need maintenance?](/guides/does-artificial-turf-need-maintenance/)

## When is new sod the better call?

- **A big tree shares the lawn.** CSU says that in clay soil half of a tree's roots often grow in the top 4 inches, along the surface of the lawn ([PlantTalk Colorado 1507](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1507-tree-roots/)), and it says those roots share the lawn's water ([Lawn Care Basics](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/lawn-care-basics/)). Take the sprinklers away and the tree needs its own. Read [watering trees after the lawn becomes turf](/guides/watering-trees-with-artificial-turf/) first.
- **A sunny lawn where kids play barefoot.** CSU says turf lacks the cooling effect of a living lawn and becomes quite warm on a sunny day, and Greeley's NOAA station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F. Our guide to [how hot turf gets here](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/) covers what cools it down.
- **A front yard your town or HOA wants living.** Greeley prohibits artificial turf in the front yards of houses. Firestone requires live plants on 75% of a single-family front yard, and turf doesn't count toward it. Wellington requires 75% live materials in front of houses on new subdivision lots. In Timnath, the December 2025 code says no artificial turf may be installed outside an athletic field; it depends on how the Town applies that to your lot, so ask Timnath Planning. Colorado law stops an HOA from banning turf in the backyard of a detached home, not in the front ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5, SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download); not legal advice). Every town is in our [turf rules guide](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).
- **You want grass and don't mind the work.** CSU calls bluegrass the standard home lawn for its color and "its high tolerance for wear" ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/water-wise-landscape-design-selecting-turf-options/)). If you want grass on less water, compare [low-water grasses with turf](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-low-water-grass/).

Shade is not a clean win for either. CSU says grass does better in sun, that some bluegrass varieties have only fair shade tolerance, and that in heavy shade ground covers or mulch may be more practical ([PlantTalk Colorado 1518](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1518-growing-grass-shade/)). Turf in shade has its own problems; see [turf in a shady or north-facing yard](/guides/artificial-turf-shady-north-facing-yard/).

## When does artificial turf make more sense?

- **Traffic wears the grass out faster than it heals.** That is the case CSU itself calls legitimate. If the traffic is dogs, read [turf or grass for dogs](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-grass-for-dogs/).
- **The spot is hard to water or mow:** a narrow [side yard](/guides/artificial-turf-side-yard/), a [slope](/guides/artificial-turf-on-a-slope/), a strip along the fence.
- **You are done mowing, feeding and aerating.** The table above is the list of what stops.
- **The lawn is mud.** Check first whether that is a drainage problem turf won't solve.

## Is artificial turf worth it over time?

It depends on numbers only your yard can supply. Work through these in order:

1. **What does the lawn's water cost you?** Find your provider in our [water savings guide](/guides/water-savings/), which prices bluegrass irrigation at each Northern Colorado provider's 2026 rates.
2. **What does turf cost for this yard,** including the base work clay needs? Our [cost guide](/guides/artificial-turf-cost/) lists what moves a quote. Get a sod quote for the same area, with soil prep, so you compare like with like.
3. **How long will the turf last** in your sun and traffic, and what happens at the end? See [how long turf lasts](/guides/how-long-does-artificial-turf-last/) and [getting rid of old turf](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-artificial-turf/).
4. **Is anything on the sod list above true of your yard?** A big tree, a sunny play lawn or a front-yard rule outweighs the water math.
5. **Would a lawn left to brown in July do?**

If you get turf quotes, ask each installer how deep they dig on your soil, what base goes in, and where the water goes; a good answer names all three for your yard.
