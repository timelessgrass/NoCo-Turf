**Checked September 25, 2026.** Turf rarely looks fake because of one flaw. It reads as carpet when a few small things add up: a single flat green, a shine, blades lying the wrong way, a hard line where it ends. Most of those can be checked on a sample before you buy. One can't: in a Northern Colorado winter, real lawns change color and turf doesn't.

## Why does some artificial turf look like green carpet?

A lawn is uneven in ways a cheap roll isn't. Real grass varies in shade, carries a brown layer of thatch at its base, and has no plastic sheen. The usual giveaways:

- **One flat green.** Every blade the same shade, often brighter than any lawn on the street.
- **Shine.** Flat plastic blades have a sheen that grass doesn't, which is why makers reshape blades (more below).
- **Thin or flattened pile.** The Synthetic Turf Council says "longer pile and denser synthetic grass will look more natural" ([Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). The numbers behind that are in [pile height](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/) and [face weight](/guides/artificial-turf-face-weight/).
- **Hard lines.** Seams you can see from the patio and edges that end in a raw cut. Those fixes live in [visible seams](/guides/visible-seams-in-artificial-turf/) and [edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/).

## What does thatch do, and why is it tan?

Thatch yarn copies the dead layer at the bottom of a real lawn. CSU Extension describes lawn thatch as "a tight, brown, spongy, organic layer of both living and dead grass roots and stems," and says it tends to build up in Kentucky bluegrass ([CSU Extension, GardenNotes #551](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/551.pdf)). Part the blades of a bluegrass lawn and you see brown under the green.

Turf makers copy it with a second yarn that sits low in the pile, often textured, in tan, beige or a darker green; SYNLawn's sheets call that layer the thatch zone. It fills the space above the backing, so you see depth between the blades instead of backing. Spec sheets list it separately, so you can check before a sample arrives. Three examples of how makers write it, not recommendations:

| Product (maker) | Blade colors | Thatch colors | Blade shape listed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Select LX ([ForeverLawn](https://www.landscapesbyforeverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Select-LX.pdf)) | Field and olive green | Turf green and dark tan (a second yarn, same row) | Not listed |
| SYNAugustine 847 ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SYNLawn-SYNAugustine-847-S847B-USA.pdf)) | Sport green, apple, olive | Field green, beige | Biomimetic U |
| Majestic, TigerTurf ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/product/tigerturf/majestic/)) | Field green, lime green, tan | Green and tan | 3D curvy W and oval |

A sheet with one blade color and no thatch color is the carpet look on paper. Tan down in the pile is there on purpose; pale, brittle blade tips are a different problem, covered in [does artificial turf fade](/guides/does-artificial-turf-fade/).

## Which greens fit a Northern Colorado lawn?

The greens of Kentucky bluegrass, because that's what the lawns around you are. CSU calls it "the most widely used lawn grass in Colorado" ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1521-choosing-lawn-grass-colorado/)) and credits its long run as the standard lawn partly to its "rich bluish green color" ([CSU Extension, GardenNotes #561](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/561.pdf)). The Synthetic Turf Council's buying guidelines put the same idea as a rule: choose turf "that replicates the grass varieties that are most prevalent in your locality."

In practice:

- Favor a blend built on a deep field green with olive or bluish tones over a single bright lime.
- Judge the sample on your own ground, beside the nearest real lawn, not on a showroom floor or a screen.
- Look at it from where you'll see it most, whether that's the kitchen window, the patio or the sidewalk.

## What happens in winter, when the lawns around it go brown?

This is the test turf can't pass. From fall into spring, many lawns nearby change color, and turf stays the green it was made.

- **Buffalograss and blue grama**, the warm-season lawn grasses, turn straw-colored with the first fall frost and stay that way until late spring, around May, according to CSU's species guide.
- **Bluegrass** rides out dry spells by going dormant; CSU credits "its excellent dormancy mechanism" ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1540-drought-tolerance-kentucky-bluegrass/)), and its lawn advice writes of "dormant (brown) lawns" ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1535-fall-lawn-fertilization-drought/)).
- **Winters here are dry.** CSU notes that "often there is little or no snow cover" from October through March ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/fall-and-winter-watering/)), and some towns switch lawn watering off: Severance allows none from December 1 through March 31 ([Town of Severance](https://www.townofseverance.org/396/Watering-Guidelines-and-Restrictions)).

So a turf yard can sit in summer green beside straw and brown lawns for months, often with no snow to hide the difference. What you can do about it:

- **Pick the quieter blend.** Olive and tan yarns sit closer to a dormant lawn than a bright emerald does. Look at samples in November, not only in June.
- **Put turf where the contrast matters least.** A backyard seen from the house is judged against your own beds and fence, not the neighbor's lawn. Front-yard layouts are in [front yard turf ideas](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/).
- **Break the line.** A bed, rock band or mow strip between turf and a real lawn makes the color change look planned. Proportions are in [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/).

## Why does turf shine, and which way should the grain run?

Turf has a grain: its blades lean one way, like the nap of a carpet. Look into the lean and the color reads deeper; look along it and the same turf reads paler and shinier. Integriturf's installation guide says looking into the grain gives the richest color and the "least amount of sheen," so in a front yard the grain would usually lean toward the street ([Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf)). SYNLawn's guidelines say to keep one grain direction for every piece and "try to face it toward the direction it will be seen most often" ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)). Pieces seamed with the grain mismatched show, in Integriturf's words, "a color difference."

Winter light makes this matter more here. CSU points to "the low angle of winter sun" on south and west exposures, and a low sun skims across turf instead of shining down into it. Two things help:

- **Blade shape.** Makers shape blades partly to scatter light. Synthetic Grass Warehouse says blade shapes were developed "to reduce the plastic sheen" and describes its S-shaped blade as reflecting less sunlight ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/resources/terminology/about-blade-shapes/)). Those are sellers' claims; the real test is your own sample in low afternoon sun.
- **Blades that stand.** Turf walked flat loses the texture that makes it read as grass. Integriturf's guide brushes the fibers against the grain to "help the fibers stand up and look more natural." Keeping them up over the years is covered in [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/) and [turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).

Ask an installer which way the grain will run and why. A good answer names your main view, such as the patio door or the street, and says where the seams will fall. More questions are in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/).

## Do Northern Colorado towns set rules for how turf looks?

A few do. Firestone's code has an appearance section: "Blade yarn colors shall mimic natural turf including green blade color(s) and include a tan thatch layer." The same section requires a border "to conceal edges of the turf," and turf "maintained in a manner to mimic healthy living turf" ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). Not legal advice. Firestone's other turf rules are on the [Firestone area page](/areas/firestone-co/).

Greeley's development code handles looks by placement instead. In new development, artificial turf "is only acceptable as ground cover for areas not visible from public rights-of-way and streets" ([Greeley Municipal Code Sec. 24-802](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT24DECO_CH8LAST_S24-802LADE)). Not legal advice. More is on the [Greeley area page](/areas/greeley-co/).

Elsewhere, whether an appearance standard applies depends on the town and on when your neighborhood was approved, so ask your town's Planning department. Each town's code is in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/). An HOA's design guidelines are a separate layer, covered in [HOA turf approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/).

## How should I compare samples?

- Take them outside and lay them flat on the ground, grain toward you, then walk around them.
- Look in morning and late-afternoon light, and again after the first hard frost if you can wait.
- Set them beside a real lawn, yours or a neighbor's.
- Read the spec sheet's color lines: blade colors, thatch colors, blade shape.
- Keep the one you choose. The rest of the sheet is in [the turf spec sheet guide](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/), and quotes for [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) should name the same product.
