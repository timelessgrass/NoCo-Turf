**Checked September 25, 2026.** We read all 1,209 Google Maps reviews of 27 Front Range artificial turf business listings, captured September 24, 2026, and sorted every complaint by what went wrong. No business is named here, and none of the reviews is of NoCo Turf Co. How the count was made, and what it can't show, is at the end of the page.

## Do people regret artificial turf?

Some do, and they say so plainly. But the regret in these reviews is mostly about who put the turf in and what happened after something went wrong, not about artificial turf itself.

- Of the 1,209, 1,150 rate the business 4 or 5 out of 5. Only 59 rate it 3 or lower, and 55 of those are real complaints.
- Only 19 of the 55 complaints are about turf already in the ground.
- At least 12 reviewers say they wish they had done it sooner, and 3 hired for turf a second time, 2 of them at a new house.

| What the complaint is about | Complaints |
|---|---|
| Before any work: unanswered calls, missed or late appointments, quotes or samples that never came | 22 |
| Turf already in the ground | 19 |
| Buying turf or supplies (four from one contractor crew, about one short order) | 7 |
| Paid, and never got the job | 3 |
| Other: a fence job, a price, an environmental objection, a former worker | 4 |

These are reviews of businesses, not a survey of owners. Someone who tore out a DIY yard, or lives with a bad one and never posted, isn't here. Read the counts as a map of what goes wrong, not a regret rate.

Only 3 of the 19 blame the turf product itself: two dog owners whose pet backing held urine, and one whose turf stained yellow.

The 8 "not as sold" stories share a shape. The shortcut was under the turf, where an owner can't see it once the crew leaves: a weed barrier that was never dug in, a foam pad the crew skipped until the owner noticed it was missing. On one new build, a promised crushed-granite base was swapped for a powdery material, a small swale flooded for almost 3 days after the first moisture, and the owner planned to pull the turf out.

## Why do those failures show up in Northern Colorado yards?

Because local ground and weather find a weak install quickly.

- **Base and drainage.** The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil Colorado's most significant geologic hazard, with clays that can swell up to 20% by volume when wet ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Greeley's water department says hard clay can sit just inches below the surface ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)). On clay, a thin or wrong base turns into puddles and low spots. See [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).
- **Settling and new builds.** Five reviewers describe turf that settled, sank or held water after install, from small patches within months to sinking after 4 years; two of those yards were new builds. Colorado requires a builder to give a new-home buyer a summary of the soils report at least 14 days before closing ([C.R.S. 6-6.5-101](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_6-6.5-101); not legal advice). Hand that summary to whoever designs the base, and see [turf in a new-build backyard](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/).
- **Heat and dog odor.** Fort Collins averages 29.5 days a year at or above 90°F, and Greeley 58.3 (NOAA 1991–2020 normals). One dog owner's complaint says the yard smelled on any warm day above 70°F, even after the turf was replaced once. [Pet turf versus regular turf](/guides/pet-turf-vs-regular-turf/) covers the backing and infill choices behind that.
- **Freeze and thaw.** Fort Collins averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F. One reviewer noticed seam lines after the first winter, and the installer ended up replacing all of the turf.
- **Windows.** Three reviewers had turf melted by reflected sun; one found burn lines within 2 weeks and paid $800 for window tint afterward.

## Where do larger, multi-trade projects go wrong?

A custom build, a putting green or an outdoor room around a patio and fire feature has more trades and more handoffs, and the reviews show where the turf layer slips between them.

- **Turf laid by a trade that doesn't do turf.** One Northern Colorado owner's stonework contractor installed the turf with no turf experience, and a turf crew had to fix it. At least 8 reviewers who rated a business 4 or 5 describe a second company fixing or finishing a first company's turf work, including a rebuilt putting green and a failed putting course and game court.
- **Nobody owns the fix.** After turf pulled up with nails showing, one owner's landscape architect asked for a site visit for about a week, and no one came.
- **Putting surfaces show every flaw.** One complaint describes a green with grooves that make the ball wobble.

What helps on these projects:

- Put the turf layer in the landscape plan: base material and depth, drainage, edges, and who answers for each, so the architect, builder and turf installer work from one drawing. Our guide to turf in a custom-home landscape covers the timing.
- Settle the order the trades go in before anyone digs; planning a backyard around turf walks through it, and putting green design covers contours and cups.
- Plan for architectural review. Colorado law bars HOA rules, and metro or special district rules, from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard of a detached home, though an HOA can still apply design guidelines ([SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download); not legal advice). The packet is in our [HOA approval guide](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/).

## What do the owners who don't regret it have in common?

Not a problem-free yard. At least 8 reviewers who gave 5 out of 5 describe something that went wrong: a seam line, a melt spot, weeds, a settled patch. The difference is that the installer came back. One owner wrote "3 months in - we're regretting the decision" after weeds came through new front-yard turf; the installer put down commercial weed barrier, reinstalled the turf at no cost, and the review ends happy.

Other habits recur in the happy reviews:

- **Risks raised before the contract.** One installer walked an owner through window melt and drove large samples out before any turf went down.
- **Samples on site.** Of the 1,150 rated 4 or 5, 24 mention samples.
- **The HOA side handled.** Six credit the installer with knowing the HOA's rules, installing the edging the HOA asked for, or helping at the HOA meeting.

## How were the reviews counted, and what can't they show?

**Source.** Google Maps pages of 27 artificial turf business listings along the Front Range, loaded September 24, 2026, with every review expanded. Only reviews with written text count, dated from about 2012 to September 2026. General landscapers were counted only where a review mentions turf.

**Ratings.** The 1,209 break down like this:

| Rating, out of 5 | Count |
|---|---|
| 5 | 1,141 |
| 4 | 9 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 2 | 8 |
| 1 | 47 |

**Northern Colorado and the rest.** 260 of the 1,209 are of 13 Northern Colorado listings: 256 rated 5 and 4 rated 1, and none of those 4 is about installed turf (a cancelled site visit, a missed appointment, a late crew, a fence job). The other 949 are of 14 listings elsewhere on the Front Range, and 51 of those are rated 1 or 2. Most of the failure stories above come from outside Northern Colorado.

**What the count can't show.** Businesses often ask happy customers to post, so ratings lean positive. DIY yards, yards that came with the house and turf someone quietly removed don't appear. One reader sorted each complaint, and a review can name several failures. New reviews keep posting, so the counts are redone before this page is published.
