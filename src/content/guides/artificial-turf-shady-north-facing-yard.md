  question: "Is artificial turf a good fix for a shady or north-facing yard where grass won't grow?"
  answer: "Often, yes. Turf needs no sun, and shade keeps it far cooler than turf in full sun. The trade-offs: a north-side strip holds snow and ice longest and dries slowest, and trees drop leaves, needles and sticky honeydew on it. Plan the drainage, downspouts and cleanup. In light shade, fine fescue is the grass worth trying first."

    a: "Probably. A 2020 review of turf-fiber breakdown from NTNU says UV radiation combined with surface heating is believed to be the most damaging cause, and shade cuts both.   - q: "Should I trim the tree so the turf or the grass gets more sun?"
    a: "Where you can, it helps either one. FieldTurf's care guide suggests trimming overhanging branches to let more sun reach damp, shaded turf where moss shows up, and more light is exactly what a shade grass is short of. Keep the turf and its base well back from the trunk; our guide to [turf around trees](/guides/artificial-turf-around-trees/) explains why."
  - q: "Should I shovel snow off a shaded strip of turf?"
    a: "Only where you need a path, such as to the dog's spot. SIS Pitches, which builds synthetic sports surfaces, says snow and ice are not harmful to synthetic grass and should generally be left to melt and drain off. How to clear a path without damage is in [clearing snow off artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/)."
  - q: "Can I use turf in the shade and grass in the sun?"
    a: "Yes, and CSU's advice points that way: no grass species is perfect for all situations, and the first step is picking one for the lawn's use and the site. One way to split it: turf where the shade and the foot traffic are, and grass, rock or planting beds where the sun is. Our guide to [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/) covers the layout."
  - q: "Is a shady corner a good place for a putting green?"
    a: "It's a risky one. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer column says most putting green turf has no drainage holes in its backing, so moisture stays in the fibers, and that most mossy greens its author has seen were installed in shade. If the shady corner is the only spot, plan how water leaves it; see [putting green maintenance](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/) and our [putting greens](/services/putting-greens/) page."

Kentucky bluegrass is the most widely used lawn grass in Colorado, CSU Extension says ([PlantTalk Colorado 1521](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1521-choosing-lawn-grass-colorado/)), and it wants sun. Hence the bare patch so many Northern Colorado yards share: along the north wall, behind the garage, under the big tree. Turf ends the growing problem, since nothing has to grow. It doesn't change what shade does to water, snow and falling debris, and those decide how well it works.

## Why won't grass grow on the north side or under my tree?

Light first, then water.

- **Bluegrass is a sun grass.** CSU lists "poor to fair shade tolerance" among its weaknesses ([GardenNotes #561](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/561.pdf)), and PlantTalk says plainly that "Grass performs better in sunny locations" ([PlantTalk Colorado 1518](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1518-growing-grass-shade/)).
- **Shade comes in grades.** CSU calls the north or northeast side of a building, wall or fence full shade, shaded all day through the growing season. Evergreens and dense trees such as maples or lindens cast medium shade; a north-facing yard under open trees, or a spot with only morning sun, is light shade ([PlantTalk Colorado 1127](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/design/1127-kinds-shade/)).
- **Winter shadows are long.** At about 40.3 degrees north, close to every Northern Colorado town, the solstice sun stands only about 26.2 degrees above the horizon at noon ([National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/romo/winter_solstice.htm)). By our arithmetic, a noon shadow then runs about twice the height of the house or fence casting it.
- **The tree takes the water.** A lawn competing with a large tree for soil moisture may become overly dry, CSU says, and PlantTalk warns that tree growth can turn a sunny site shady over the years.
- **Snow sits longest there.** Gray snow mold follows long snow cover on Front Range lawns, and CSU says it hits north-facing and shaded lawns most ([PlantTalk Colorado 1543](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1543-gray-snow-mold-lawns/)).

## Is there a grass that will grow there instead?

In light shade, often. In full shade, rarely. How CSU Extension rates each option:

| Option | Shade | Foot traffic | Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kentucky bluegrass | Poor to fair | Wear tolerant, recovers well | About 24 inches of supplemental water in a normal Front Range year |
| Turf-type tall fescue | Moderate; "does well in shade" in GardenNotes #561 | Wear tolerant | Only about 10% less than bluegrass, and it can't go dormant |
| Fine fescues (hard, Chewings, creeping red, sheep) | Good to very good | Moderate; not for constant heavy traffic, slow to recover | 18 to 20 inches, and it thins if the root zone stays wet |
| Buffalograss | Poor to fair; needs at least half a day of full sun | Won't take heavy, constant traffic | Excellent drought resistance |
| Blue grama | Not shade tolerant | Not traffic tolerant | Excellent drought tolerance |
| Artificial turf | Needs no sun | "a legitimate solution for high traffic sites" | No watering, mowing or fertilizing |

Sources: [GardenNotes #561](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/561.pdf), [Fine Fescues for Lawns](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/fine-fescues-for-lawns/), [Selecting Turf Options](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/water-wise-landscape-design-selecting-turf-options/), [PlantTalk Colorado 1537](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1537-artificial-turf-lawns/).

Fine fescue is CSU's pick for shade, and hard fescue, the most heat tolerant of the group, is the one it calls especially well suited to the Front Range. It still needs watering, though, and it thins if the root zone stays wet, so it suits dry shade under a tree better than the soggy strip below a downspout. It won't take a dog's daily laps either; see [artificial turf or grass for dogs](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-grass-for-dogs/). In heavy shade, PlantTalk suggests ground covers, or wood-chip mulch with shade-loving perennials, instead of any lawn. The alternatives are compared in [turf vs xeriscape](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-xeriscape/), [low-water grasses](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-low-water-grass/) and [turf vs new sod](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-sod/).

**A shady front yard.** A north-facing house keeps its front yard in its own shadow, and front yards carry the tightest turf rules. [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) staff describe turf as prohibited in the front yards of houses while the city drafts a change. [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) requires live plants on 75% of a single-family front yard and doesn't count turf toward it ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/) requires 75% live materials in front of houses on new single-family subdivision lots ([Wellington Code 15-5-40](https://library.municode.com/co/wellington/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH15LAUSCO_ART5DEST_S15-5-40LASC)). Elsewhere it depends on the town and the lot; ask your town's Planning department first. This is general information, not legal advice. Each town's rule is in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/), and layouts that fit them in [front yard turf ideas](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/).

## What does shade do to artificial turf in summer?

Keeps it far cooler. In a 2022 playground study tabulated by a 2024 review, synthetic grass averaged about 136°F in the sun and about 90°F in the shade, the same average as natural grass in the sun ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272752/)). The nearest NOAA stations average 29.5 days a year at or above 90°F in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) and 48.8 at Longmont 2 ESE (1991–2020 normals), so a shaded strip can be the one stretch of turf a child or a dog can use on a July afternoon. The full numbers are in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

The trade is drying time. A north-facing exposure is typically cooler and moist, CSU says ([GardenNotes #143](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/143.pdf)). Colorado's low humidity "favors rapid evaporation" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)), but shade slows it. CSU also notes that most shade in Colorado gardens is dry until it collects runoff from rain or sprinklers. On a turf yard, that runoff is a downspout, a sprinkler that reaches the turf, or a low spot the yard drains toward. Find each one before the turf goes in.

## What happens to turf on the north side in winter?

Snow and ice stay there longest.

- **Snow.** The Fort Collins station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year and 43.6 days with an inch or more on the ground (1991–2020 normals).
- **Sun.** Colorado's thin atmosphere lets strong sun through "even during the winter," the Climate Center says. [Fort Collins](https://fortcollins.gov/Services/Transportation-and-Roads/Snow-Removal) even counts "frequent sunny conditions that help melt snow naturally" among its reasons for not routinely plowing residential streets. The house's winter shadow gets none of that help.
- **Aspect.** In a Front Range foothills catchment, USGS-led researchers found north-facing slopes hold a seasonal snowpack while south-facing slopes get snow that comes and goes through winter and spring ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70208439)). A north-side yard is the same effect, small.
- **Freezing nights.** The Longmont 2 ESE station, nearest [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/), averages 177.7 nights a year at or below 32°F, and the Fort Collins station 150. Meltwater running onto the shaded strip has plenty of nights to freeze.

Plan for the ice more than the snow. Send downspouts past the turf, and be wary of a water-holding infill in a strip that dries slowly: a turf-field engineer notes that organic infills hold moisture and can be prone to freezing ([Gale Associates](https://galeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Alternative-Infills-for-Synthetic-Turf-Fields-JMP.pdf)). More in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/) and [turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).

## What do the trees overhead drop on the turf?

The tree that makes the shade also makes the mess. CSU notes that artificial turf "collects leaves and other wind-blown dirt and debris that must somehow be removed." The Synthetic Turf Council, writing for sports fields, says leaves left lying work down into the turf, slowing drainage and compacting infill, and that leafy trees should not stand next to a field if possible ([STC guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf)). In a yard the tree stays, so plan for:

- **Leaves, seeds and twigs.** Tools and timing are in [clearing leaves off turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/).
- **Needles.** Evergreens shade all year, and CSU says Front Range pines, spruces and firs often drop needles heavily ([PlantTalk Colorado 1760](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/trees-shrubs-vines/1760-excessive-fall-needle-drop/)).
- **Honeydew.** Aphids feeding in a tree excrete a sticky waste that may coat "anything that lies beneath an infested plant," CSU says. Sooty mold grows on it, and it draws ants, yellowjackets, flies and bees; CSU's example is a walkway under an aphid-infested linden ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/aphids-on-shade-trees-and-ornamentals/)). Which cleaners are safe to rinse off is in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/).
- **Roots.** Where turf meets a tree, the dig matters more than the shade; see [turf around trees](/guides/artificial-turf-around-trees/).

## Will a shady spot grow moss or turn slick?

Where shade and water meet, it can. The Synthetic Turf Council says moss, mold or algae may appear "particularly if it is in shade and damp," and TigerTurf, a turf maker, tells homeowners to check for moss or algae especially in damp weather and in shaded areas ([TigerTurf](https://tigerturf.com/us/maintaining-your-synthetic-grass-lawn/)). In Northern Colorado's dry air, shade alone rarely does it; a shaded spot that holds old leaves and gets steady water does. The slick spot most north-side yards actually get is winter ice. What the growth is and how to get it off: [mold and moss on artificial turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-get-moldy/).

## What should a turf plan for a shady spot include?

Ask the installer, and listen for answers about this spot rather than the yard in general:

| Ask | A good answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Where does water leave this spot? | A fall away from the house they can point to, and the turf's drain rate on its spec sheet. |
| Where do the downspouts empty? | Past the turf or into a drain, never onto the shaded strip. |
| Does any sprinkler still reach it? | The lawn zone shut off at the valve, and bed heads turned away or switched to drip. |
| Which infill, and does it hold water? | A reason it suits a spot that dries slowly and freezes early. |
| How far back from the trunk does the turf stop? | A mulched ring sized to the tree, not turf to the bark. |
| How will leaves and needles come off? | Edges that hold up to a blower, and a way to reach every corner. |

For the sprinkler question, see [what happens to the sprinkler system](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/). The installation steps themselves are on our [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) page.
