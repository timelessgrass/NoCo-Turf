faq:
  - q: "Is pile height the same thing as face weight?"
    a: "No. Pile height is how long the blades are; face weight is how many ounces of yarn sit in a square yard. The Synthetic Turf Council says face weight depends on several factors, pile height and the spacing between tufts among them, so a sheet needs both numbers. Our face weight guide covers density."
  - q: "Can one yard have two pile heights?"
    a: "Yes. A putting green is built that way on purpose, with a fringe slightly higher than the green. A shorter product can also go where the dogs run and a longer one where the yard is mostly looked at. In Firestone, each artificial turf area still has to meet the town's 1.75 to 3 inch range, so check with Firestone Planning first."
  - q: "Does a longer pile feel softer?"
    a: "Usually. Synthetic Grass Warehouse says longer piles tend to have a softer, more natural texture. Softness under a fall is a different matter: that comes from a tested pad and infill system, not from blade length."
  - q: "Is 2 inches too long for a yard with a dog?"
    a: "Not under Firestone's code, where it sits inside the required range. One supplier, FusionTurf, suggests 1.0 to 1.25 inches for most dogs because longer blades fold over under paws, but allows a groomed 1.25 to 1.5 inch pile for a single dog on a lightly used lawn. More dogs or heavier daily use points shorter."
  - q: "How do I find the pile height of turf that is already down?"
    a: "Look for the product name on your paperwork and ask for its spec sheet. To check by hand, part the blades at an edge down to the backing and measure to the tip of a blade held upright, then note how high the infill comes. Flattened blades read shorter than they are; our guide to matted turf covers lifting them."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** Pile height is one short line on a turf spec sheet, and it decides much of how a yard looks and wears. Products below are named as published examples, not recommendations.

## What does pile height measure?

The length of the blade. Synthetic Grass Warehouse, a national turf distributor, measures it "from where the tufts bind to the backing to the tips of the blades" and says artificial turf generally runs from 0.4 to 3 inches ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/resources/terminology/pile-height-face-weight/)). The Synthetic Turf Council describes it as the length of the yarn above the backing ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)).

On a spec sheet the line may read "nominal" or "finished," sometimes with a test method. SYNLawn's sheet for SYNAugustine 847 gives 1 1/4 inches by ASTM D-5823 ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SYNLawn-SYNAugustine-847-S847B-USA.pdf)), the ASTM lab method for the tuft height of pile floor coverings; ASTM notes that changes in tuft height can affect both appearance and performance ([ASTM](https://store.astm.org/d5823-19.html)). Some sheets add a tolerance: K9Grass Classic+ is 7/8 inch, plus or minus 1/8 ([K9Grass](https://k9grass.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/K9Grass-Classic-Plus-Outdoor-CSI-M1266-0322.pdf)).

What the number leaves out:

- **How much blade shows.** Infill fills the bottom of the pile; more on that below.
- **How thick the turf is.** That is face weight, which the STC says depends on several factors, pile height and tuft spacing among them. See [artificial turf face weight](/guides/artificial-turf-face-weight/).
- **Which way the blades lie.** Turf has a grain. The K9Grass specification requires every roll laid with the pile "leaning the same direction."

## How tall is most lawn turf, and what do Northern Colorado rules require?

The STC says most synthetic grass has a pile of 1.5 to 2 inches. Its guidelines are written for lawns and, it says, "may not apply" to putting greens, dog runs or playgrounds.

Two Northern Colorado rule books we found set a number:

- **[Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/).** The town's turf standard requires a pile height of 1.75 to 3 inches and at least 70 ounces of face weight, and a town permit comes before installation ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). The floor sits inside the STC's usual range; the ceiling is well above it. The code sets that one range for all artificial turf and has no provision for putting greens.
- **Barefoot Lakes.** The metro district's guidelines repeat the 1.75 to 3 inch blade and 70-ounce face weight, and a submittal must state the product's color and pile height ([St. Vrain Lakes Metropolitan Districts](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). Its committee keeps a [pre-approved turf list](https://www.stvrainmd.live/design-review), and anything else goes in with a sample.

Elsewhere, look to the HOA or metro district. Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home but lets it adopt design and aesthetic guidelines ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). In [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), New Windsor's committee considers turf in back yards only and wants a product sample with the application ([New Windsor Metropolitan District](https://www.newwindsormetro.org/files/72102649d/New+Windsor_Brunner+Farm+Design+Guidelines+-+5-16-24+-+Final.pdf)). This is general information, not legal advice. Town by town: [turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/); the packet: [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/).

## Does a longer pile look better, and what does snow do to it?

It usually looks more natural. The STC says longer, denser synthetic grass "will look more natural," and Synthetic Grass Warehouse says longer piles tend to look more realistic and feel softer. The same distributor says shorter piles "are much more resilient," meaning they resist matting, and suit heavy traffic; longer piles suit lighter foot traffic.

The STC also advises choosing turf that replicates the grass varieties common where you live. For scale, CSU Extension's preferred mowing height for Colorado lawns is 2.5 to 3 inches ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/lawn-care-basics/)), so typical 1.5 to 2 inch turf stands shorter than a neighbor's mowed lawn before any infill goes in. That gap shows most in a front yard beside real grass.

Then there is winter. NOAA's stations average 51.4 inches of snow a year at Fort Collins, 47.0 at Loveland 2N and 34.9 at Greeley UNC (1991–2020 normals). Motz says snow "can lay heavily" on turf, flattening the blades and sometimes matting them ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/our-guide-to-protecting-your-artificial-turf-in-the-winter-motz/)). Synthetic Grass Warehouse says the blades should bounce back over time, faster with cross-brushing ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-to-care-for-artificial-grass-in-snowy-weather/)). Put that beside the resilience point and a long pile is the one more likely to need a brush after a snowy stretch. That is our reading of the sources, not a test result. Fixes are in [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/); clearing snow is in [artificial turf snow removal](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

## What pile height suits dogs, play areas and putting greens?

Published figures by use, with Firestone's rule and a mowed lawn for comparison:

| Use | Pile height | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mowed lawn grass, for comparison | 2.5 to 3 in | CSU Extension |
| Firestone and Barefoot Lakes required range | 1.75 to 3 in | Town code, metro district |
| Most lawn turf | 1.5 to 2 in | STC |
| Pet or playground turf in an infill maker's table | 1 to 1.25 in | Motz |
| One supplier's pick for most dogs | 1.0 to 1.25 in | FusionTurf |
| Same supplier, several dogs or heavy daily use | 0.875 to 1.0 in | FusionTurf |
| Dog-specific knitted turf (K9Grass Classic+) | 7/8 in | Spec sheet |
| Low-pile turf sold for golf fringe (SYNAugustine 847) | 1 1/4 in | SYNLawn |
| Sand-filled putting surface | 1 to 1.25 in | Motz |
| Texturized putting surface | About 1/2 in | Motz |

- **Dogs.** K9Grass lists a "shorter pile height to make solid waste removal easier" among what dog turf should have ([K9Grass](https://k9grass.com/faq/)), and FusionTurf says longer blades "can fold over under paw traffic and infill weight" ([FusionTurf](https://find.fusionturf.com/answers/shorter-or-longer-pile-better-for-dogs-on-artificial-turf/)). In Firestone and Barefoot Lakes the 1.75-inch floor still applies; the exception Barefoot makes for a pet-only area is less secondary thatch, not a shorter blade. More in [pet turf vs regular turf](/guides/pet-turf-vs-regular-turf/) and on our [pet turf page](/services/pet-turf/).
- **Play areas.** Blade length is not fall protection. The CPSC says carpeting and mats are not appropriate playground surfacing unless tested to ASTM F1292 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf)), and [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/)'s park standards accept playground turf only as a system of turf, shock pad and infill that meets Head Impact Criteria ([Town of Frederick](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33243/POST-Design-Standards)). See [playground turf vs mulch](/guides/playground-turf-vs-mulch/) and our [playground turf page](/services/playground-turf/).
- **Putting greens.** The putting surface is far shorter than lawn turf, and SYNLawn Golf's fringe sits "slightly higher than the putting green" ([SYNLawn Golf](https://www.synlawngolf.com/golf-turf/fringe/)). SYNLawn sells SYNAugustine 847 for golf fringe and chipping ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/product/synaugustine-847/)). Because Firestone's code sets one pile range for all artificial turf, a half-inch putting surface there is unsettled: it depends on how the town reads it, so ask Firestone Planning before ordering. See [sand-filled vs non-infilled putting greens](/guides/sand-filled-vs-non-infilled-putting-green/) and our [putting greens page](/services/putting-greens/).
- **Front yards.** First check that turf is allowed there at all; some HOA rules, New Windsor's among them, keep it to back yards. Where it is allowed, looks count most and traffic is usually light, which suits a longer pile. See [front yard artificial turf ideas](/guides/front-yard-artificial-turf-ideas/).

## How does infill change the height you see?

It fills the bottom of the pile. Motz measures pile height from the backing to the blade tips and infill depth from the base of the turf to the top of the infill ([Motz](https://themotzgroup.com/infill_blog/how-many-pounds-of-infill-do-you-need-per-square-foot-of-turf/)). Its Envirofill table pairs them like this:

| Pile height (Motz's turf type) | Infill depth |
|---|---|
| 1 to 1.25 in (pet or playground) | 3/8 to 1/2 in |
| 1.5 to 1.75 in (landscape) | 1/2 to 3/4 in |
| 1.875 to 2.25 in (landscape) | 3/4 to 1 in |

By that table, a 1.75-inch pile, Firestone's floor, with 1/2 to 3/4 inch of infill leaves about 1 to 1.25 inches of blade above the sand. A taller pile takes a deeper bed; Motz's rule of thumb for infill volume starts from half the pile height, and it warns that too little and too much infill can both lead to matting.

Which infill to use is in [artificial turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/) and, for dogs, [dog turf infill](/guides/dog-turf-infill/); topping it up is in [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).

## What should I ask an installer about pile height?

- **What is the pile height on the spec sheet, nominal or finished, and with what tolerance?** A good answer reads it off the sheet for the exact product. What else belongs on that sheet is in [a turf spec sheet for Colorado](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/).
- **How deep will the infill be, and how much blade will show above it?** A good answer gives both in inches and matches the maker's figures.
- **Does it meet my town's and HOA's rules?** In Firestone and Barefoot Lakes, the answer names the 1.75 to 3 inch range. Elsewhere, it names what your HOA's packet asks for.
- **Can I keep the sample?** The STC tells buyers to keep the sample, compare it with what is delivered, and get a written commitment that the sample is the product installed. Set it where it will go and look at it from the street or the back door.
- **Will any part of the yard get a shorter product?** A good answer names where (the dog run, a putting fringe, a play area) and how the two products meet.
- **What does this height need after snow and heavy use?** A good answer mentions cross-brushing and doesn't promise a long pile that never lies down. What makes turf look real beyond its length is in [the most realistic artificial turf](/guides/most-realistic-artificial-turf/).
