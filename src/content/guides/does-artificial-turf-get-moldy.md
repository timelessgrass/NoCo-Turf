  question: "Does artificial turf get moldy or grow moss, and why would it here?"
  answer: "It can, but not on the plastic itself: mold, moss and algae grow on the leaves, dirt and water the turf holds. Northern Colorado's air is dry most days, so growth that keeps returning means one spot stays wet: shade, sprinkler overspray, a leaking capped head, ponding clay, a wet pad or matted leaves. faq:
  - q: "Is mold or moss on turf a health risk for kids or dogs?"
    a: "It can be for some people. EPA says touching or breathing mold or its spores may cause allergic reactions in sensitive people, such as sneezing, a runny nose, red eyes or a skin rash, so wear gloves while you clean it. Pull any mushrooms that come up before a dog finds them: CSU Extension says lawn mushrooms may be poisonous and shouldn't be eaten by people or pets unless they have been identified. More on turf and health is in [is artificial turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/)."
  - q: "What time of year does moss show up on turf here?"
    a: "Mostly spring, then fall. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer column says moss grows most in the cool weeks of early to mid-spring and in the fall. Spring is also Northern Colorado's wet stretch: the Colorado Climate Center says widespread soaking storms come from early March through early June, and 70 to 80 percent of the plains' precipitation falls from April through September, while winter more often brings dry air and strong winds."
  - q: "Does antimicrobial infill stop mold?"
    a: "It treats the infill, not the water. Synthetic Grass Warehouse, which sells one, recommends a coated infill with an antimicrobial it says fights mold and mildew, and notes that plain sand has no such defense. Water held in a pad, a low spot or a leaking line is still there. How infills compare where dogs go is in [which infill is right for dogs](/guides/dog-turf-infill/)."
  - q: "Is the smell mold or dog urine?"
    a: "Where and when it smells usually tells you. A sharp, ammonia smell where the dog goes, worst on hot afternoons, points to urine. An earthy, musty smell in a shady or low spot, worst after rain or a sprinkler cycle, points to trapped water. Urine has its own fixes in [getting the dog urine smell out of turf](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/)."
  - q: "Does moss mean my turf was installed wrong?"
    a: "Not by itself. Deep shade and a layer of old leaves can grow moss on a sound install. Moss that comes back in the same spot after cleaning, a musty smell from under the turf or mold on the backing points to water the install should have dealt with: an old sprinkler line, a low spot or a pad that doesn't drain. If the installer won't come back to look, see what to do when a turf installer won't come back."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** Turf blades and backing are plastic. Mold, moss and algae need something organic to live on and water that stays, and turf supplies the first on its own: CSU Extension notes that artificial turf collects leaves and wind-blown dirt that has to be removed, and doesn't disinfect itself the way a living lawn does ([CSU Extension](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1537-artificial-turf-lawns/)). Water decides the rest, and in Northern Colorado water needs help to stay put.

## Why is mold or moss uncommon on turf in Northern Colorado?

Because mold needs water that lingers, and the air here keeps taking it away.

- **Mold runs on water.** "The key to mold control is moisture control," the EPA says. No type of mold grows without water or moisture, and drying wet material within 24 to 48 hours usually keeps it from starting ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home)).
- **Little rain.** The nearest NOAA stations average 15.88 inches of precipitation a year in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) and 14.65 inches at Greeley UNC (1991–2020 normals).
- **Dry air.** The Colorado Climate Center says Colorado's humidity "is generally quite low; this favors rapid evaporation" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)).
- **Yards dry out daily.** At CoAgMET's Fort Collins station on the CSU campus, relative humidity stayed at or above 60% all day on only 26 of 365 days in 2025, and never for more than 3 days running. Greeley's station logged 25 such days out of 364 recorded, no more than 4 in a row ([Fort Collins data](https://coagmet.colostate.edu/data/daily/fcl01.json?from=2025-01-01&to=2025-12-31&fields=rhMax,rhMaxTime,rhMin,rhMinTime), [Greeley data](https://coagmet.colostate.edu/data/daily/gly04.json?from=2025-01-01&to=2025-12-31&fields=rhMax,rhMaxTime,rhMin,rhMinTime)). For scale, 60 percent is the ceiling EPA suggests for humidity indoors.

Turf makers write their moss advice for other places. FieldTurf files it under "Damp Climates" and describes moss and algae in "consistently damp and shaded areas" ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/maintenance/artificial-turf-maintenance-guide/)). A Northern Colorado yard is rarely that from edge to edge. So when you find moss or smell mold, look for the one spot that is getting or holding water the rest of the yard isn't.

## What is that growing on my turf?

| What you see | Likely | What it lives on |
|---|---|---|
| A slick green or brown film on the blades or backing | Algae | Standing water in shade. On lawns, CSU finds it most in shaded, over-watered grass and compacted spots where water stands. |
| Soft green cushions along seams, edges or a shady corner | Moss | A thin layer of dirt and broken-down leaves in the fibers that stays damp |
| White, gray or black fuzz with a musty smell, often under leaves or on the backing | Mold or mildew | Wet leaves and debris on top, or a wet pad, base or backing below |
| Mushrooms pushing up through drain holes or seams | Fungi under the turf | Buried organic matter: CSU names old tree roots, stumps and construction debris, especially sawdust |
| A foamy white, yellow or orange patch, often next to fresh wood mulch | Slime mold | Organic matter after a warm, wet spell; CSU calls it more unsightly than harmful |

CSU sources: [algae](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1558-algae-turfgrass/), [mushrooms](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1506-mushrooms-fairy-rings/), [slime mold](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/insects-diseases/1435-slime-mold/). Outdoors, the EPA says, the job molds do is breaking down dead organic matter such as fallen leaves. That's why nearly every case on turf starts with a layer of leaves, dirt or organic infill that stayed wet.

## Where does it show up in a Northern Colorado yard?

Almost always in one spot, and the spot points to the cause.

**The north side, in the shade.** CSU Extension's note on gray snow mold, a fungus of Front Range lawns, says it follows long snow cover and fall leaf piles and hits "North-facing lawns, shaded lawns" and deep drifts hardest ([CSU Extension](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1543-gray-snow-mold-lawns/)). Turf can't catch a grass disease, but those are the same places snow and meltwater sit longest on turf. NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year, and a strip along a north wall or fence holds it long after the sunny side is dry. Whether turf suits a shady yard at all is covered in [artificial turf for a shady or north-facing yard](/guides/artificial-turf-shady-north-facing-yard/).

**Next to a sprinkler that still runs.** Local watering rules keep sprinklers out of the heat of the day: [Windsor](https://www.windsorco.gov/1222/Efficiency) bars lawn watering from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., and [Wellington](https://www.wellingtoncolorado.gov/211/Water-Efficiency-Program) asks residents to water between 7 p.m. and 8 a.m. So a bed zone or a neighbor's lawn that throws water onto the turf does it in the evening or before dawn, when it dries slowest. On lawns, CSU says evening watering "has been reported to increase problems with algae."

**A spot that's damp in a dry week.** Suspect a sprinkler head capped under the turf. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer guidance warns that threaded caps "may leak over time, eroding the soil," and says to cap the old lawn zone at the valve instead ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/irrigation/)). More in [what happens to the sprinkler system](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).

**A low corner on clay.** Turf backing drains; the ground under the base decides where the water goes next. [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) tells residents its clay soils "absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)), and [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/)'s water department describes hard clay "sometimes just inches below the surface" ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)). A dip that holds water for days is where algae starts. The fixes are in [why water puddles on turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/) and [turf that's sinking or uneven](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/).

**Over a pad or a slab.** FieldTurf says good foam pads are perforated so water "doesn't pool," some have channels underneath "to prevent moisture buildup," and turf laid straight on concrete can have water pool on top ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/backyard/get-the-facts-on-foam-padding-for-artificial-turf-landscaping/)). A pad or slab that can't shed water keeps the underside of the turf wet where you can't see it. See [artificial turf over concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-over-concrete/).

**On a shaded putting green.** Most putting green turf has no drainage holes in its backing, Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer column says, so moisture "resides within the fibers" and mold and moss can take over; shade and moisture are the two causes ([Synthetic Grass Warehouse](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/moss-and-putting-greens/)). More in [putting green maintenance](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/) and on the [putting greens](/services/putting-greens/) page.

**Under last fall's leaves, or in organic infill.** FieldTurf warns that leaves left over winter decompose into a layer that "can impede drainage and encourage moss growth." Organic infills such as cork, coconut or walnut shell hold moisture, and a turf-field engineer notes some degrade and need topping up every two to three years ([Gale Associates](https://galeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Alternative-Infills-for-Synthetic-Turf-Fields-JMP.pdf)). See [clearing leaves off turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/) and [infill types compared](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).

## How do I get mold or moss off artificial turf?

Pick a cool, dry day.

1. **Clear the surface.** Rake or blow off leaves and debris and bag them, so you aren't scrubbing mold's food back into the fibers.
2. **Lift the moss.** A flat scraper or a stiff plastic rake takes most of it. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's column says to hold a scraper blade as flat as possible so it doesn't gouge the fibers.
3. **Scrub and flush together.** Work a stiff broom, or a rented power broom, while someone runs a hose over the spot.
4. **Let it dry completely, then repeat** if anything is left. The same column expects more than one round.
5. **Spot-treat what remains.** FieldTurf suggests a 50/50 white vinegar and water spray, left a few minutes, scrubbed with a stiff brush and rinsed, or a turf-safe moss remover. Other cleaners, and what is safe to rinse toward a storm drain here, are in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/).
6. **Top up the infill** that scrubbing moved; see [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).

If the surface looks clean but the smell stays, look under an edge where the turf meets a border, or ask the installer to lift one. A dark, slimy backing or a soggy pad or base means the water is underneath, and scrubbing the top won't reach it. The column also says a putting green completely covered in moss may need to be taken up and replaced; for that decision, see [when to replace artificial turf](/guides/when-to-replace-artificial-turf/).

## How do I keep it from coming back, and what should I ask the installer?

Fix the water first. Cleaning without that buys you a season.

- **Overspray:** turn or swap heads that reach the turf, or move bed zones to drip.
- **Old heads under the turf:** have the lawn zone shut off at the valve.
- **Downspouts:** extend any that empty onto the turf past it or into a drain.
- **Low spots:** have them lifted and regraded, not filled with extra infill.
- **Shade:** FieldTurf's advice is to trim overhanging branches so sun reaches the spot.
- **Leaves and dirt:** clear them before the first snow and brush through the year. FieldTurf says removing them "stops a top layer from forming where weeds or moss could take root."

Whether you're planning turf for a shady spot or calling an installer back to one, these questions show whether water was planned for:

| Ask | A good answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| What happened to the sprinkler heads under this area? | The old lawn zone was shut off or capped at the valve, not capped head by head under the turf. |
| Does this backing drain, and how fast? | Perforated or fully permeable, with the drain rate on the spec sheet; for a putting green, where the water goes instead. |
| Is there a pad, and how does it drain? | Perforated or channeled, laid over a base that drains. |
| Where does water go from this low corner? | A slope they can show you, or a drain. |
| What infill is going in? | Whether it holds water, and why it suits this spot. |

For turf you already have, see [how to check the base was installed](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/).
