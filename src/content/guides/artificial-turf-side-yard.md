faq:
  - q: "Will turf in the side yard keep water out of my basement?"
    a: "No. Turf doesn't decide where water goes: rain passes through it to the ground, and the grade, gutters and downspouts do the steering. If water reaches the basement now, have the grading, gutters and downspouts checked and fixed before any turf goes in, so the turf isn't laid over the problem."
  - q: "My neighbor's downspout empties into our shared side yard. What can I do?"
    a: "Talk to the neighbor first. The Colorado Geological Survey says water carried away from one house shouldn't be sent against a neighboring structure. Ask whether the extension can run along the swale toward the street or the back of the lot. In some communities any drainage change needs committee approval; Severance Shores and Hunters Overlook in Severance require it."
  - q: "Do I need a permit for turf in a side yard?"
    a: "In Firestone, yes: the town requires a permit before artificial turf goes in, with no fee for a single-family home. Elsewhere, check the town in our guide to turf rules, and check whether your HOA or metro district wants its own approval first."
  - q: "Will a long side yard need seams?"
    a: "Often not across its width. Integriturf says most turf rolls are made 15 feet wide and 100 feet long, with some 12 or 13 feet wide, so a strip narrower than the roll can usually be cut from one piece run lengthwise. A run longer than the roll needs a seam across it; ask where any seam will fall."
  - q: "Can a narrow side yard hold a putting green?"
    a: "A short, straight one. A strip a few feet wide gives putts along its length and little else. Sizing and cups are in our guide to backyard putting green size, and the swale, clearance and buffer rules on this page apply to a green the same way."

A side yard gets the least attention on a lot and does some of the hardest work: it carries water from two roofs past two foundations, and it's often the only way into the backyard. Turf can suit it, as long as that work goes on underneath.

## Is a narrow side yard a good place for artificial turf?

Often. CSU Extension says strips of grass narrower than 8 feet are hard to water well with spray heads ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/xeriscaping-retrofit-your-yard/)), and on expansive soils it says to keep sprinkler spray at least five feet from the foundation ([CSU PlantTalk](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/soils-amendments-composting/1617-expansive-soils/)). In a narrow side yard those two rules cover nearly all the ground. Turf needs no spray.

Many side yards are that narrow: [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/)'s zoning table lets a single-family house sit as close as 5 feet to an interior side lot line ([Table 4-1](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH4DIST_16.4.1TADIST)), so two neighbors' walls can stand about 10 feet apart.

## What is the builder's swale, and how does turf keep it working?

The 2021 International Residential Code wants the ground to fall at least 6 inches in the first 10 feet from a house. Two houses 10 feet apart can't both get that, so the code adds: where "lot lines, walls, slopes or other physical barriers" prevent the fall, "drains or swales shall be constructed" ([IRC R401.3](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/4/foundations)). The side-yard swale is that shallow valley between the walls, carrying water from both roofs to the street or the back of the lot. [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/)'s drainage criteria require subdivision plans to show swales, "including side/rear yard swales," with their cross-sections ([Town of Windsor](https://windsorgov.com/DocumentCenter/View/23471/Storm-Drainage-Design-Criteria---Revised-July-2020)). Towns adopt their own code editions; ask yours which applies.

Turf sits on the swale; it doesn't replace it. Firestone's code says the lot's drainage pattern "shall not be impeded or altered by the installation of artificial turf" ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)), and [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/)'s bars landscaping, "including any artificial turf," from changing the pattern or runoff in the approved grading plan ([Frederick Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)). On the ground:

- **The dig follows the swale,** so its low line stays low and runs unbroken to where the builder sent it.
- **Nothing crosses the flow line.** The Colorado Geological Survey counts patios, fences and "other obstructions that dam and pond water" among homeowner mistakes on swelling soil ([CGS SP-14](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-14.pdf)). An edging board laid across a swale is one.
- **The neighbor stays dry.** The same guide says water carried off one house must not be "directed against a neighboring structure," which in a side yard stands a few feet away.

Reading a grading plan: [new-build backyard turf](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/).

## How close can turf come to the foundation and siding?

It can meet the wall. Two things decide whether it should.

**Height.** A base and turf can raise the finished ground at the wall, and building code keeps a gap there:

| At the wall | Gap in the 2021 IRC | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Wood siding, sheathing, wall framing | 6 inches above ground, 2 above a patio slab, unless the wood is decay-resistant or treated | [R317.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning) |
| Wood framing on the foundation wall | 8 inches above exposed ground | R317.1 |
| Stucco weep screed | 4 inches above earth, 2 above paving | [R703.7.2.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/7/wall-covering) |

Measure the gap before anyone digs, and keep it: a higher grade at the wall isn't the answer.

**Water.** The Colorado Geological Survey lists poor surface drainage, and planting by foundations so irrigation water soaks in, among landscaping problems on expansive soils ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Turf removes the sprinkler. But turf and its porous base pass water down, and CGS's advice for the band beside a house runs the other way: gravel over non-woven fabric, at least 5 feet out, the fabric lapped onto the foundation, "to prevent percolation of water into the soils next to the foundation." Turf tight to the wall works only when the ground under the base falls away from the house, as R401.3 asks, so water leaves instead of soaking in at the footing.

In a 5-foot side yard, that edging is the whole yard. On some lots the honest layout is rock along the house and turf where the yard opens up; [turf, pavers or concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-vs-pavers-concrete/) compares hard surfaces. On a newer house the backfill is still settling.

## What about window wells, downspouts and the air conditioner?

**Window wells.** An egress window well must be at least 9 square feet, reach 36 inches out, and drain into the foundation drain unless the house sits on well-drained sand or gravel ([IRC R310.4](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)). Clay isn't that, so water that gets into a well on a clay lot ends up at the footing drain. Keep the rim above the finished turf, with the ground falling away from it. A cover must open from inside without a key or tool.

**Downspouts.** CGS says downspouts should carry roof runoff at least 10 feet from the house ([CGS SP-11](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-11.pdf)), and CSU Extension warns against pulling extensions off to water beds by the house. Ten feet straight out can hit the neighbor's wall, so the extension turns and runs down the swale. If it goes under the turf, ask how it gets cleaned out.

**Condenser, meters and hose bib.** Leave them reachable, with firm footing for service. The condenser's installation manual gives its clearance.

## Do town rules and HOAs allow turf in a side yard?

Some don't. Not legal advice.

| Where | What it says about side yards |
|---|---|
| Firestone (town code) | Turf allowed in rear and side yards up to 75%, but 50% of the combined side and rear yards must be live plants, so turf is usually held to about half; a 3-foot living buffer at every property line; town permit first, no fee for a single-family home |
| Barefoot Lakes, Firestone | Turf "may be considered in fenced side or rear yards," at most 50% of the combined rear and side yard ([guidelines](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)) |
| Severance Shores and Hunters Overlook, [Severance](/areas/severance-co/) | "Artificial turf is prohibited within the front/side yard areas" ([Severance Shores](https://ssmd.specialdistrict.org/files/3cc9774d3/SSMD+-+Amended+RIGSR+09.05.24.pdf), [Hunters Overlook](https://homd.specialdistrict.org/files/60183cf34/HOMD+-+RIGSR+Amended+04.01.25.pdf)) |
| New Windsor (Brunner Farm), Windsor | Artificial turf "is not permitted in front or side yards" ([design guidelines](https://www.newwindsormetro.org/files/72102649d/New+Windsor_Brunner+Farm+Design+Guidelines+-+5-16-24+-+Final.pdf)) |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) (development code) | Turf only where not visible from public streets ([Sec. 24-802](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT24DECO_CH8LAST_S24-802LADE)); a corner lot's street side is visible |
| Frederick (land use code) | On a corner lot, both yards along a street count as front yards |

Firestone's buffer decides narrow lots. Beside a house 5 feet from the side lot line, a 3-foot planted strip at the line leaves about 2 feet, so in many Firestone side yards the space goes to plants. That's arithmetic on the code, not a Town ruling: the code measures a side yard from the house to the side lot line ([16.11.3](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH11DE_16.11.3TEDE)), and how the Town applies both rules depends on your plan, so ask Firestone Planning. In Greeley it depends on the lot and when it was developed: ask Greeley Planning.

State law helps less than it seems. Colorado's [SB23-178](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download) bars an HOA from prohibiting turf in the backyard of a detached home and makes special-district rules that do so unenforceable. The turf clause names only the backyard; the clause beside it protects vegetable gardens in the "front, back, or side yard." Whether your side yard counts depends on your community's documents: ask the HOA in writing before you build, and a Colorado attorney if it becomes a dispute. More in [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/) and [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/).

## What else does a strip between two houses ask of turf?

- **Reflected heat.** NAHB says closeness to a neighboring structure is a factor in damage from sunlight reflected off Low-E windows; a builders' survey found most siding damage within 30 feet of the window ([NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/-/media/NAHB/advocacy/docs/legal-issues/construction-liability/builder-resources/sunlight-double-paned-low-e-windows-2014.pdf)). A side yard sits well inside that.
- **Shade and snow.** A north side yard holds both longest: see [turf in a shady, north-facing yard](/guides/artificial-turf-shady-north-facing-yard/).
- **Dogs.** Some communities send dog runs here: [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/) Lakes puts a run in the rear or side yard, against the home ([Timnath Lakes rules](https://web.archive.org/web/20240527033949/https://timnathlakesmetrodistricts1-6.com/uploads/tlmd-rules-and-regs-final-version-ratified-1.28.22-.pdf)). A potty strip along the wall puts rinse water beside the foundation, where CSU says water should be carefully regulated. Rinse toward the swale; see [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/).
- **Access.** Toro sells a narrow-track loader under 35 inches wide, made for a standard 36-inch gate ([Toro](https://www.toro.com/en/product/22587)). Measure your gate; see [getting ready for install day](/guides/preparing-for-turf-installation-day/).

## What should I ask before turf goes in along the house?

- **"Where does water go after it passes through the turf?"** A good answer names the swale's low line and the outlet the builder's plan uses, with the ground under the base falling away from the house.
- **"How much will the finished turf raise the ground at the wall?"** A good answer comes after they've measured the siding or weep-screed gap, and keeps it at code.
- **"What happens at the window wells and downspouts?"** Rims stay above the turf; extensions stay on and discharge down the swale.
- **"Does my town or HOA allow turf in this side yard, and who files?"** In Firestone that includes the permit and the 3-foot buffer.
