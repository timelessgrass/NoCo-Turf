  question: "Is artificial turf a good idea around a pool or hot tub?"
  answer: "Around one, yes; under one, no. A hot tub needs a level, reinforced pad built for its weight and Northern Colorado's swelling clay, and the turf starts at the pad's edge. faq:
  - q: "Can I put artificial turf under an above-ground pool?"
    a: "Not turf you want to keep. A filled pool sits in one place all season, and its maker's setup instructions decide what goes under it, so read them before drawing the turf. If turf is part of the yard, run it around the pool's footprint on its own base, and plan the end-of-season drain the same way as a hot tub's."
  - q: "Does a swim spa need anything different?"
    a: "A heavier pad. Bullfrog Spas treats 4 inches of concrete as a general minimum and suggests 6 inches or more for larger tubs and swim spas. A longer tub also has a longer edge shedding water, so the grading and the turf edge along that side matter more."
  - q: "Will turf around the tub put grit in the water?"
    a: "It can. Hot Spring says grass or dirt around a spa increases the debris carried into the water on users' feet, and loose infill rides on wet feet the same way. A hard landing at the step, with a mat on it, keeps most of it out."
  - q: "When should I drain a hot tub here?"
    a: "In mild weather. Hot Spring doesn't recommend refilling when the air is below 50°F, and the state wants drained water to stand until its chlorine or bromine is gone and then soak slowly into the ground, which frozen ground can't do. A spring or fall drain suits both."
  - q: "Does a hot tub or pool next to turf draw mosquitoes?"
    a: "Not a covered, maintained one. The CDC says Culex mosquitoes lay their eggs on permanent standing water, including unmaintained swimming pools. Around a tub, the risk is a puddle at a flat edge of the pad or water sitting on a cover, which is one more reason to grade the pad's edge so it drains."

A hot tub here gets used in January, not just July, so the turf beside it has to handle ice as well as heat. Turf can frame a tub or a pool well, but it can't carry the tub or take its drain water. On a custom build or a lot with architectural review, the tub, its pad, its wiring and the turf belong on one plan.

## Can a hot tub sit on artificial turf?

No. The tub goes on its own pad, and the turf meets the pad's edge.

- **The weight.** Hot Spring's Grandee holds 455 gallons and weighs 5,810 pounds filled, counting the people in it. The maker requires a reinforced concrete pad at least 4 inches thick, or a deck rated for more than 125 pounds per square foot ([Hot Spring pre-delivery instructions](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/hot-spring-highlife-collection-pre-delivery-instruction-2026.pdf)). Bullfrog Spas puts a portable spa's filled weight at 6,000 to 20,000 pounds before anyone gets in, and doesn't recommend grass ([Bullfrog Spas](https://www.bullfrogspas.com/hot-tub-foundation/)).
- **Level, and staying level.** Bullfrog allows half an inch of slope over an 8-foot run, and no shims. Hot Spring says a spa set on the ground, even on stepping stones, "will inevitably settle and become unlevel." A turf base is built to keep turf flat, not to carry a spa.
- **The clay underneath.** The Colorado Geological Survey says swelling clay can push with up to 30,000 pounds per square foot, against the 115 to 130 pounds per square foot Hot Spring lists for its spas, and that water added after construction is what sets it off. It calls cracked and heaved patios "very common indicators of expansive clay" ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)). Ask whoever builds the pad what they found in the soil and how the pad answers it. More in [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/); turf over an existing slab is in [artificial turf over concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-over-concrete/).

## Is turf a good winter path to the hot tub?

It beats bare dirt, but it isn't ice-free. The Fort Collins NOAA station averages 150 nights a year at or below freezing and Longmont's about 178, and the walk back from the tub happens on those nights in wet bare feet.

| Surface | What drips do on a freezing night |
|---|---|
| Concrete or pavers | Water stays on top. Hot Spring warns that "wet surfaces can be very slippery" ([owner's manual](https://waves-console-watkins-wellness.s3.amazonaws.com/content/148147/hot-spring-spas-hot-spot-owners-manual-2025.pdf)). |
| Turf | Water runs into the infill instead of sheeting, then stays: SIS Pitches says frozen sand infill "prevents the water draining," and Synthetic Grass Warehouse says frozen blades turn brittle underfoot. |

FieldTurf Landscape calls turf "a safer, non-slip surface" for pools ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/residential/artificial-grass-and-poolside-installations/)) but cites no test, and we found none comparing frosted turf with icy concrete. What helps:

- A short route on the sunny side of the house, where frost leaves first.
- A hard landing at the tub's step that sheds water to somewhere it can drain.
- Snow cleared with a plastic shovel, and the landing's ice melt kept off the turf: [snow removal on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/) and [what winter does to turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

## Where can hot tub and pool water be drained in Northern Colorado?

Not across the turf, and not into the street without checking the town's code. Hot Spring's manual says to drain a spa 3 to 4 times a year and warns that water with a high sanitizer level "may harm plants and grass."

Colorado's rule, briefly (not legal advice): the Water Quality Control Division lets pool and hot tub water be drained without a permit only when it carries no chlorine or bromine above detection levels, after standing uncovered until none shows up and heated water has cooled ([CDPHE low risk discharge guidance](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1CRa8epLZly14Lv3jEyATMr3TncGX1Fnk)). Onto land, it should soak into a pervious surface without leaving the property, stay off foundations and swelling ground, and drain at least 100 feet from drinking water wells "or other human contact areas." Read literally, that keeps it off the turf you sit on, and on an acreage lot away from the well.

Each town's code sets terms for the storm drain:

| Town | What its stormwater code says about pool and spa water |
|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | Defines "swimming pool discharges" and heat as pollutants ([Sec. 26-491](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH26UT_ARTVIISTUT_DIV1GE_S26-491DE)). A pollutant may not go into a street or storm drain, or onto property where it could reach one, without suitable treatment ([Sec. 26-498](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH26UT_ARTVIISTUT_DIV1GE_S26-498WAQUCO)). Storm sewers run to the creeks and the Poudre River untreated. |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | Allows a discharge made in accordance with the state's low risk guidance ([Sec. 20-563](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT20PUWOUT_CH3WASASESE_ARTVIISTDRSY_DIV1INGE_S20-563ILDIALDINOTA)). |
| [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) | Allows pool discharges that follow the state's low risk guidance for swimming pools ([13.20.130](https://library.municode.com/co/loveland/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIILOCOMUCO_TIT13UT_CH13.20STQU_13.20.130ILDI)). |
| [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | Exempts pool water only if dechlorinated to less than 0.05 ppm chlorine ([Sec. 13-5-50](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH13MUUT_ARTVSTQU_S13-5-50ILDIPREX)). |

The state also asks you to notify the storm sewer's owner first, and to get the wastewater utility's go-ahead before using a sanitary sewer, because "not all facilities are able to accept such discharges." Fort Collins doesn't define suitable treatment. It depends on the water and where it goes; ask Fort Collins Utilities before the first drain, and elsewhere the town's stormwater office.

## Where should splash and rain around the tub or pool go?

Away from the tub, away from the house, and never into a flat pocket of turf.

- Hot Spring prefers a pad sloped 1/2 inch per 10 feet so rain and spillover "run off and not puddle underneath the spa."
- The Colorado Geological Survey lists poor surface drainage among the landscaping problems on expansive soils; splash soaking in beside a foundation is that problem.
- Turf drains fast: the Synthetic Turf Council says permeable systems are typically designed for 25 inches of water an hour ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)). The ground under it may not. PolyTurf's installation manual says turf over clay or hard-packed soil "will not drain on a level surface," and Greeley's water department says hard clay can sit "sometimes just inches below the surface."

So grade the turf away from the house like the rest of the yard, and let the pad's low edge hand its water to something that carries it on. More in [what happens to artificial turf when it rains](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/).

## What do pool chemicals and summer sun do to turf beside a pool?

**Chlorine and bromine.** Makers don't agree in print. FieldTurf Landscape says artificial grass is "highly resistant to chlorine and other pool chemicals," but that they "can accumulate on the turf surface" and should be rinsed off regularly. SYNLawn's residential terms exclude damage from exposure to halogen chemicals, naming chlorine and bromine ([SYNLawn terms](https://lowes.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/SY24247_Lowes_ProductWarranty_v1.pdf)), the sanitizers the state's pool guidance names. Ask for the maker's written terms on pool and spa chemicals before choosing a product ([what turf coverage leaves out](/guides/artificial-turf-warranty/)), and dose the tub away from the turf: Hot Spring says not to let chemicals "get on surrounding surfaces or landscaping."

**Salt systems.** Turf makers' salt warnings were written about de-icers. FieldTurf says regular salt "dries into hard, abrasive crystals that can scuff the fibers," and Synthetic Grass Warehouse says salt can clog the holes in the backing. Salt-pool splash dries the same way, fast in air the Colorado Climate Center calls "generally quite low" in humidity. Rinse with fresh water; cleaners are in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/).

**Heat.** Greeley's NOAA station averages 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F, and Fort Collins' 29.5. Penn State researchers say synthetic turf generally runs 35 to 55°F hotter than natural grass ([Penn State](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature.pdf/@@download/file/temperature.pdf)), and in one playground study turf averaged about 136°F in sun and 90°F in shade. Splash cools it only for minutes: in a BYU test, 30 minutes of water took turf from 174°F to 85°F, and it was back to 120°F five minutes later. Put lounging turf in afternoon shade, and give the barefoot route to the water a surface people can cross at midday. More in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## What should the plan show before the tub or turf goes in?

Pad and wiring go first, then grading, then base and turf. The plan shows:

- **The pad:** size, thickness and reinforcement from the tub maker, its slope, and the edge where turf meets it ([edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/)).
- **The spa's electrical run.** Hot Spring says not to bury a power cord unless it is direct-burial cable. Colorado 811's free locates don't mark privately owned lines ([Colorado 811](https://colorado811.org/homeowners/)), so draw the spa's feed on the plan and keep turf nails and edging stakes clear of it.
- **Where the water goes:** splash, snowmelt and the drain hose, away from the house and off the turf.
- **Shade and the winter route:** where people lounge in July and walk in January.
- **Design review.** Barefoot's guidelines in Firestone want a hot tub to be "an integral part of the patio, rear yard or side yard area," not immediately visible to neighbors, and applied for with a water usage calculation ([Barefoot guidelines](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). The turf side of an HOA packet is in our [guide to HOA approval](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/), and the order of trades in planning a backyard around turf.

Done well, the tub sits level on its own pad, water leaves the pad toward somewhere it can go, the turf starts at a clean edge, and the drain hose has a route that isn't the lawn.
