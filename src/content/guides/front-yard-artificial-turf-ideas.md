  question: "How do I design a front yard with artificial turf that looks right and meets my town's planting rules?"
  answer: "Check the town and HOA first. Greeley doesn't allow turf in house front yards today, many HOAs and metro districts keep it out, and Wellington, Firestone, Frederick and Milliken set live-plant minimums. Where turf is allowed, use one framed panel with planted beds along the house, walk and street, and leave the parkway strip to the town's rules."
faq:
  - q: "Will Greeley allow artificial turf in front yards soon?"
    a: "Maybe. City staff have proposed allowing it on houses with a permit, with trees and live plantings still required, but as of September 24, 2026 no ordinance had been adopted. It depends on what City Council passes: ask Greeley Planning before you design around it."
  - q: "Does my HOA have to offer any front-yard option?"
    a: "Colorado law requires every HOA to preapprove at least 3 water-wise garden designs for front yards. Those are planting designs, not a right to turf, but one of them may suit the planted part of your plan, so ask the HOA for its list. This is general information, not legal advice."
  - q: "Can I skip turf and do the whole front yard in rock?"
    a: "Rarely, under the rules here. Frederick's code says water conservation does not include large mulched or gravel beds or areas without plants, Oakridge Village II in Fort Collins bars boulders and cobbles from more than 25% of any yard, and the live-plant minimums above count rock as nonliving too."
  - q: "Do corner lots have two front yards?"
    a: "In Frederick, yes: its 2026 code treats both yards along a street as front yards, and both must meet the landscape minimum. Other towns measure corner lots their own way, so ask your town's planning office before the drawing is done."
  - q: "Does a townhome's front yard follow different rules?"
    a: "Often. Colorado law lets an HOA limit turf to rear yards on an attached home that shares a wall, and Mead's code asks for at least 75% live materials between the front of the house and the curb on single-family attached and multifamily development unless the Town approves otherwise. Not legal advice."
  - q: "Can the planted part of my front yard earn a rebate?"
    a: "In some places. Windsor's 2026 lawn-replacement program pays for front-yard lawn replaced with living plants and does not accept artificial turf. Our rebates guide lists each Northern Colorado provider's program and what it pays for."

This page is about design out front: where turf may sit, how much must stay alive, and layouts that meet both. Every town's full turf code is in [artificial turf rules in Northern Colorado](/guides/turf-rules-northern-colorado/). Not legal advice.

## Can a Northern Colorado front yard have artificial turf at all?

Sometimes. Check the town, then the HOA or metro district, before anything is drawn.

- **Greeley: no, for now.** City staff put it plainly: "Artificial turf is prohibited in front yards of houses" ([Planning Commission packet](https://greeleyco.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9205,plainText=false))). A change is being drafted; see the questions below.
- **Timnath and Frederick: it depends.** Since December 2025 Timnath's code says no artificial turf may be included in any landscape plan or installed, yet a detached single-family lot needs no landscape plan for a building permit ([Ord. 28](https://library.municode.com/co/timnath/ordinances/municipal_code?nodeId=1412646)). Frederick's 2026 code permits functional artificial turf only where it fits the code's definition ([Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)). Neither says outright how that reaches a redone front yard: ask Timnath Planning or Frederick Planning.
- **HOAs and metro districts.** Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting turf in a detached home's backyard and lets it keep turf to rear yards on an attached home; front yards get no protection ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)). Many guidelines here use that room. [New Windsor's](https://www.newwindsormetro.org/files/72102649d/New+Windsor_Brunner+Farm+Design+Guidelines+-+5-16-24+-+Final.pdf) in Windsor, [Waters Edge's](https://www.watersedgemetrodistricts.org/files/b7f0e00a8/061825_Final-Design-Guidelines-Waters-Edge.pdf) and [Oakridge Village II's](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/dc06bd71-eac3-4b0f-81df-e42efbfcd762/downloads/f1ab5b9c-f88f-4b7b-8052-d1e17109c87b/Oakridge%20Village%202%20HOA%20Landscape%20Policy%20revise.pdf?ver=1788372083923) in Fort Collins, and [Severance Shores'](https://ssmd.specialdistrict.org/files/3cc9774d3/SSMD+-+Amended+RIGSR+09.05.24.pdf) and [Hunters Overlook's](https://homd.specialdistrict.org/files/60183cf34/HOMD+-+RIGSR+Amended+04.01.25.pdf) in Severance keep artificial turf out of front yards. [Liberty Draw](https://www.libertydrawsmds.com/files/952790d36/Liberty+Draw+ARB+Guidelines+SF+(Final).pdf) in Evans requires sodded front yards, and [Kitchel Lake](https://www.serratogafallsmd1.org/files/6b86004a3/Rules+and+Regs_SFMD1+-+Approved.pdf) in Timnath won't accept turf in place of required front-yard sod.

CSU Extension's design notes explain the scrutiny: the front is the public area, "openly viewed by others," and HOAs and cities often regulate it ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/water-wise-landscape-design-steps/)). What an HOA may review is in [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/).

## How much of a front yard has to stay living plants?

Where turf is allowed, most rules set a floor of living plants, and turf, rock and paving share whatever is left.

| Town | Front-yard rule | Applies to | Does turf count as living? |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/) | At least 75% live materials between the house and the curb, driveway excluded | New single-family subdivision lots | No |
| [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) | 75% of the front yard, driveways excluded, in live plants; turf needs a town permit first | Single-family lots | No; the code says turf can't be used to comply |
| [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/) | At least 50% living plant coverage at maturity in every landscaped area | Front yards of single-dwelling, paired-home and multi-plex lots | No |
| Milliken | At least 50% live materials at maturity between the house and the curb | New single- and two-family development | No |
| Evans | 60%, 75% or 50% landscape by frontage type; R-1's first-preference frontage takes 50% | New or substantially improved front yards | The code doesn't say: ask Evans Planning |
| [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | At least 25% of each front yard landscaped | Detached houses and duplexes | Windsor Planning's call: ask before designing |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | At most 50% hardscape, 50% live coverage in the planting areas, one tree if space allows | Front yards of houses | Turf isn't allowed at all |

*Each town's code as read on September 25, 2026; Greeley's row is city staff's summary of its current standard.*

Neighborhood rules often go further:

- **Rinn Valley Ranch** (Frederick): live vegetative cover on 70% of the front yard; gravel and mulch count only where plants are installed, and only in brown, gray or Colorado sandstone ([guidelines](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aZCTIwI7zpyviLbepOG_CtOYIPMdRgZJ/view)).
- **Kitchel Lake** (Timnath): no more than 40% of front-yard landscaping non-living. **Ptarmigan West** (Windsor): no more than 30%, and its [guidelines](https://pwmd1.specialdistrict.org/files/3deb93af5/03+PWMD+Rules+and+Regulations+-+Design+Guidelines.pdf) don't say whether turf counts as non-living.
- **Timnath Ranch**: front yards 50% sod ([guidelines](https://www.timnathranchmd.live/files/8c91d1751/Timnath-Ranch-ACC-Guidelines-Amended-2020-06-25-Final.pdf)). **Harvest** (Berthoud): at least 30% lawn.

Where a rule says "sod" or "lawn," ask the committee in writing whether artificial turf counts before the design is drawn.

## Which front-yard layouts meet a live-plant rule and still look designed?

CSU's design notes say water-wise landscaping "is not necessarily anti-turf": it fits the design to how the site is used, not around a little-used lawn. Out front, that means a modest turf panel where people step or sit, and the living share where the eye lands.

1. **One framed panel.** A single rectangle or one bold curve of turf, bordered by beds, reads as a lawn; thin strips and leftover corners read as carpet. CSU advises one style, rectilinear, curvilinear or angular, with features of the house as size references: line the panel up with the porch, the entry walk or the front windows. CSU also asks whether a plan should match the neighborhood's style; a panel sized like the lawns next door stands out less than wall-to-wall turf.
2. **Beds along the house, the walk and the street.** Some HOAs spell this out: Harvest in Berthoud asks for foundation plantings along street frontages at 10 plants per 40 linear feet ([Harvest Declaration](https://berthoudharvest.com/s/07-01-2021-Harvest-HOA-CCRs-As-Recorded.pdf)), and Heron Lakes wants two specimen trees in the front yard on top of the Town's street trees ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)).
3. **Beds wide enough to water well.** CSU says to avoid irrigated areas less than about eight feet wide or irregularly shaped, and to put any you can't avoid on drip or hand-watering. Converting the spray zones is covered in [your sprinklers after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).
4. **A clean line between turf and plants.** Barefoot Lakes' guidelines in Firestone keep trees, shrubs and perennials out of the turf and at least half their mature spread from its edge, and conceal every edge with bender board, concrete curbing or similar ([Barefoot Lakes guidelines](https://www.stvrainmd.live/files/5457a4faf/SVLMD+-+2026+08+07+-+Amended+%26+Restated+Residential+Improvement+Guidelines+%26+Site+Restrictions+for+Barefoot.pdf)). Edge materials are in [turf edging options](/guides/artificial-turf-edging-options/), trees in [turf around trees](/guides/artificial-turf-around-trees/).

## Can the strip between the sidewalk and the street be turf?

Usually not, and it isn't yours alone to decide. In the towns below, the parkway or tree lawn is street right-of-way: the owner looks after it, and the town sets what grows there.

- **Fort Collins:** Streetscape Standards adopted in December 2025 bar artificial turf from parkways and every other streetscape for applications from January 1, 2026. A parkway on a local or collector street takes turf-type grass or mulched beds planted to 50% live cover within 3 years, with no fences. Where an approved landscape plan sets it, a change takes a Parkway Landscaping Amendment ([Streetscape Standards](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/fortcollco-meet-9b4e8e099e3f4e9cbeaa4a206b542f9a/ITEM-Attachment-001-c4cbe11e9146456885974472aba7c2ef.pdf)).
- **Loveland:** the development code calls the strip between sidewalk and curb a parkway, once a street tree lawn ([18.19.03](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/loveland-co/doc-view.aspx?pn=0&ajax=0&secid=5937)), and lists nonfunctional artificial turfgrass on parkways among plants not allowed in the city ([18.08.02.01.C](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/loveland-co/doc-view.aspx?pn=0&ajax=0&secid=4233)).
- **Windsor:** detached houses keep the tree lawn requirement; the owner looks after tree lawns and street trees unless an owners' association or metro district has taken them on, and no tree goes in or comes out without the Town's authorization ([Sec. 15-3-20](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH15DEST_ARTIIISTLADEAR_S15-3-20LAST)).
- **Firestone:** live groundcover such as grass, trees, flowers or shrubs in the right-of-way beside a property.
- **Greeley:** staff describe streetscape planters as needing 50% plants, groundcover and trees every 25 to 35 feet.
- **Neighborhood rules stack on top.** Harvest in Berthoud plants the curb strip with shrubs and ornamental grasses and allows no turf there. Waters Edge in Fort Collins wants committee approval, then the city's amendment, before a parkway is xeriscaped.

Elsewhere, it depends on the local code: ask the town's planning or public works office before changing the strip.

## What else at the front of the lot changes the plan?

- **The water meter.** A meter pit in the yard has to stay reachable. Longmont makes residents keep a clear line of access, remove landscaping that blocks an outdoor meter, sod and yard edging included, and keep the meter at finished ground level ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-meter-maintenance-program/)). Cut the turf around the lid, never over it, and ask your water provider for its own rule.
- **Sidewalk snow.** Windsor requires sidewalks cleared within 24 hours after a storm ends, asks residents to pile the snow in the yard, and says to apply de-icer to an icy walk ([Town of Windsor](https://www.windsorgov.com/Faq.aspx?QID=206)). The turf right behind the walk takes that salted snow, so a rock band or planted bed there gives it somewhere to go. Clearing and de-icers are in [snow removal on turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).
- **Winter color.** Neighbors' bluegrass goes dormant in dry spells, thanks to what CSU calls its "excellent dormancy mechanism" ([PlantTalk Colorado](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/lawns/1540-drought-tolerance-kentucky-bluegrass/)), while turf stays green. Blends, placement and grain direction for a street view are in [what makes turf look real](/guides/most-realistic-artificial-turf/).
- **Passing dogs.** The corner by the walk and the mailbox post get the neighborhood's dogs; see [dog urine smell on turf](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/).

## What should a front-yard plan show before it goes to review?

On a custom build, a golf-community lot or any street with architectural review, the committee, the town and the trades work from one drawing. Rinn Valley Ranch requires complete landscaping plans for any front- or back-yard change and allows no work before approval; Severance Shores wants a plan that shows sod, rock, the sprinkler plan and a drainage and grading plan. Ask your designer or installer for a front-yard sheet that shows:

- Turf, planted beds, rock and paving drawn separately, each with its square footage and the percentage checked against the town's and the HOA's minimums.
- The edge detail at the walk, the driveway, each bed and the parkway line.
- Tree rings and plant spacing from the turf edge.
- Which spray zones are capped and which beds go on drip.
- The meter lid, the snow storage and the grain direction.

A good answer puts the percentages on the sheet; a plan that only says "turf here" leaves the committee guessing.
