faq:
  - q: "Can I glue a lifted seam back down myself?"
    a: "For a short stretch, a temporary fix is reasonable. The Synthetic Turf Council's field guidelines allow immediate, temporary gluing of an open seam until the installer can inspect and correct it, and treat an open joint of 12 inches or less as possibly not a failed seam. The Council recommends an industrial turf adhesive over a home-store product, and if the turf is new, call the installer first."
  - q: "Do seams show more on a putting green?"
    a: "They can. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer columnist says seam tape thicker than 1/16 inch shows on putting greens, that greens are thin and move with temperature, and that a green whose seams rupture has to be removed and replaced. Ask where a green's seams will fall before it goes in, and see our [putting greens](/services/putting-greens/) page."
  - q: "Will brushing make a seam disappear?"
    a: "Sometimes, and sometimes it does the opposite. SYNLawn's guidelines cross-brush seams in both directions to make them less visible. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's columnist warns that over-brushing abrades fibers so the seam reads as a different shade, and runs a power broom at 35 to 45 degrees to a seam, never straight across it."
  - q: "Is a mohawk the same thing as a wrinkle?"
    a: "No. A mohawk is a line of fibers from both edges crossing along the center of a seam. A wrinkle or ripple is the turf itself lifting off the base, usually from temperature swings and too little infill. Wrinkles have their own guide: [why turf wrinkles or ripples](/guides/artificial-turf-wrinkles-ripples/)."
  - q: "My turf went in last week. Is a visible seam normal?"
    a: "A band of flattened fibers right after an install is common. Synthetic Grass Warehouse's columnist names installers' body weight as the main cause, fixed by infilling and brushing the whole area to match. A gap, a ridge or a two-tone stripe won't brush out; raise it with the installer now."

Any turf area wider than one roll has seams, and rolls come 12 or 15 feet wide ([ShawGrass](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf)). This page covers why seams show, why some open after a Northern Colorado winter, and what the repair involves. Burns, holes and tears are a different fix: see [how to repair artificial turf](/guides/how-to-repair-artificial-turf/).

## Why can I see the seam in my turf?

Because something about the two pieces doesn't match, and sunlight shows it. Synthetic Grass Warehouse (SGW), a turf supplier, runs an installer column whose author says "most seams are visible due to sunlight reflection" ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/seam-integration-part-3-perfecting-seams/)): fibers that lean or lie differently on each side of the join catch the light differently.

| What you see | What usually causes it | The usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| One side lighter or shinier along the whole seam | The pieces were laid with the grain running opposite ways | Re-lay or replace one piece so the grain matches |
| A groove or line of backing that fills with infill | Rows set farther apart at the seam than elsewhere, or fibers cut while trimming the edge | Re-cut and re-seam at the turf's row spacing |
| A ridge of fibers along the center | A "mohawk": dense fibers from both edges leaning into each other | Re-cut the seam; working the fibers only makes it worse |
| A darker or lighter band a few inches wide | Fibers flattened by knees, boots and bags during the install | Infill and brush the whole area up to match; see [matted turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/) |
| Stiff or shiny fibers stuck along the line | Too much adhesive, or adhesive foaming up through the seam | Cut out and re-seam |
| A light or dark stripe on a product with alternating stitch rows | Rows paired light to light or dark to dark | Re-cut so the rows keep alternating |
| A gap along the seam, or a seam edge that lifts | A failed glue bond, or staples or nails instead of glue | Re-tape and re-glue (below) |

- **Grain.** ShawGrass says sections laid in "opposing pile patterns may result in noticeable seams"; the SGW columnist says mismatched grain looks like two colors even from one dye lot ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/get-in-the-grain/)). More on grain and sheen: [what makes turf look real](/guides/most-realistic-artificial-turf/).
- **Gaps, mohawks and glue.** He lists a gap too wide or too tight, cut fibers that let infill part the seam, and adhesive seeping up, and says a mohawk never goes away on its own ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/seam-integration-part-1-the-mohawk/)). On products whose stitch rows alternate light and dark, the pattern has to keep alternating across the seam ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/a-b-stitch-rows/)).

## How close should the rows be across a seam?

As close as they are everywhere else in the roll. Three installation guides put it three ways:

| Guide | Edge preparation | The gap at the seam |
|---|---|---|
| [ShawGrass](https://qmsview.shawinc.com/QMS_View/files/e5/e52baf26-71b9-4b41-beb0-101543c846f2.pdf) | Trim the un-tufted selvedge, starting two tuft rows in | Cuts as close as possible without touching, no more than 1/8 inch apart |
| [ForeverLawn](https://www.landscapesbyforeverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/DIY-Landscape-Installation-Guidelines-.pdf) | Cut the factory edge off between rows so long seams line up | Rows about 3/8 inch apart across the seam; backings just short of touching |
| [Integriturf](https://integriturf.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Installation-Guide.pdf) | Trim 2 to 4 stitch rows off each side | Close to the turf's stitch-row gauge, commonly 3/8 or 1/2 inch, up to 5/8 |

Read together: the backing edges nearly meet, and the fiber rows land at the turf's normal spacing. The SGW columnist says rows set too far apart show, and show more once infill goes in, so seams go "fiber row to fiber row" at the maker's gauge ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/seam-integration-part-2-manipulation/)). ForeverLawn adds a test anyone watching an install can use: "Whatever the seam looks like now, is how it will look when finished."

## Why did my seams open after the first winter?

Northern Colorado works a seam hard. NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 150 nights a year at or below 32°F ([1991–2020 normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)), and the Colorado Climate Center describes the plains as having "a large daily and seasonal range in temperature," with chinook rises of 25 to 35°F in a short time ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)).

Turf moves with those swings. SRW Products warns that heated backing can expand, "leaving unwanted gaps at seams when the turf cools" ([SRW Products](https://srwproducts.com/product/seams-right-turf-adhesive-srw-products/)). The SGW columnist has seen seams move half an inch in a few hours, says seams are usually the first place sections pull apart in extreme temperatures ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/thermal-expansion-contraction-in-synthetic-turf/)), and counts the rate of temperature change among what decides whether seam adhesive holds ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/the-importance-of-adhesive-thickness/)).

What turns that movement into an open seam:

- **Glue that never took hold.** SRW's Seams Right adhesive needs surfaces "flat, completely dry, and above 40°F," then 12 hours protected from rain and freezing. TEC's two-part turf adhesive wants the adhesive, air, base and turf between 59°F and 84°F ([TEC](https://www.tecspecialty.com/hbfuller-media/7742/pds_tec_149_2-cturfadhesive_r0326.pdf)). A seam glued on a cold late-fall afternoon may miss both. Timing is in [when to install artificial turf](/guides/when-to-install-artificial-turf/).
- **Too little adhesive or pressure.** Adhesive spread too thin, or stuck to the stitch rows but not the backing, lets a seam rupture early; on a hot day it can cure before the seam is pressed in ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/adhesive-compression-vs-longevity/)).
- **Staples instead of glue.** Staples driven into hard, rocky ground can bend outward and spread a seam.
- **Low infill.** Infill is ballast; without enough, turf moves enough to rupture seams. See [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/).
- **A base that moved.** A seam over rocky or uneven base can rise, sink or shift: see [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/) and [sinking or low spots](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/).

Don't leave an open seam for next summer: the SGW columnist says ruptures get worse the longer the repair waits.

## Can a turf seam really be invisible?

From your usual viewpoint, nearly. From every angle in every light, seldom. ForeverLawn calls finished seams "virtually invisible," and SYNLawn says professionals hide them "so they are almost invisible," warning that non-professional installs could leave them more visible ([SYNLawn](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/240a8581-4267-4cdf-aa5f-d4d1e250fec6/44269346.pdf)).

A fair test is the view you use. Stand at the patio door or the street in morning and late-afternoon light. A seam line that shows from there is worth raising with the installer; one you must kneel beside in low sun to find is about what the makers describe.

Grain is a trade-off. ShawGrass faces the grain toward the main viewpoint to cut sheen; the SGW columnist prefers fewer seams even if the grain then faces away. Settle it on the layout drawing, before anything is cut.

## How is a visible or open seam fixed?

It depends on the cause. A flattened band usually recovers with infill and brushing across the area. A grain mismatch means re-laying or replacing a piece; brushing can't change direction. A gap, a mohawk, glue on the fibers or an open seam means doing the seam again:

1. **Fold the turf back** on both sides; ShawGrass folds the edges about two feet apart.
2. **Pull any staples or nails,** as the SGW columnist did to fix one separated seam before gluing it on tape.
3. **Re-cut the edges between rows,** without cutting fibers, so the rows across the seam match the roll's spacing.
4. **Center new seam tape** under the join, with adhesive over the tape at the maker's rate.
5. **Set the edges** into the adhesive without burying fibers, and check the gap end to end.
6. **Weight or roll the seam** while it cures, and keep traffic off as long as the data sheet says; SRW's says 12 hours for light foot traffic and 24 for heavy.
7. **Infill by hand along the seam** before any brushing; the SGW columnist fills it to half the infill height ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/stop-beating-up-your-seams/)).

In winter, the repair waits for a dry stretch warm enough for the adhesive.

**In Firestone.** Not legal advice. The code requires turf anchored at every seam, seams "taped or glued and not sewn," no seam separations, and damaged areas repaired or replaced to match ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). There, an open seam is a code matter, not only a looks problem. The full Firestone standard is in [a turf spec sheet for Colorado](/guides/turf-spec-sheet-for-colorado/). Whether it reaches turf installed before the standard depends on the property and its permit; ask Firestone Planning. If an installer won't return, see when your installer won't come back.

## Where should seams go in a new install?

Placement is settled on the layout drawing, before cutting:

- **Few, and out of sight.** ShawGrass plans around the roll width to keep seams few, at the back or out of the line of sight. Roll arithmetic is in [how much artificial turf you need](/guides/how-much-artificial-turf-do-i-need/).
- **Out of the paths.** The SGW columnist keeps seams out of walkways, play areas, entrances and the ground under swing sets, because steady traffic parts the fibers. For a dog, that means off the fence line and running track: see [can dogs dig through turf](/guides/can-dogs-dig-through-artificial-turf/).

Questions to ask the installer, and what a good answer sounds like:

- **Where will the seams fall, and which way will the grain run?** Lines on a drawing, with your main view named.
- **Which seam tape and adhesive, and what is its temperature floor?** A named product with a data sheet. The Synthetic Turf Council's buying guide asks for an industrial turf adhesive with proven weather resistance, usually not a home-store product ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)).
- **How is the seam held while it cures, and for how long?** Guides differ: Integriturf leaves infill sacks on overnight, while the SGW columnist has seen installs ruined by weight left overnight. A good answer follows the adhesive's instructions.
- **Who fixes a seam that opens, and how fast?** A specific answer. More are in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/); installation is on the [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) page.
