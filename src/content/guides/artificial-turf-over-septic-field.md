**Checked September 25, 2026. Not legal advice:** this page explains Colorado and county septic rules in plain English. Your county health department has the final word on your system.

## What do the septic rules say goes on top of a drain field?

Soil that grows plants. Colorado sets the minimum for every septic system in [Regulation 43](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/colorado/5-CCR-1002-43.10), amended in 2025, and county health departments permit systems under their own versions of it. The state rule calls for "a final cover of soil suitable for vegetation at least ten inches deep" over the soil treatment area, which is the drain field or leach field. That cover must be free of cobbles, boulders, debris and other non-permeable material, and the rule prefers sandy loam under 2 to 3 inches of topsoil. The ground must be graded to shed stormwater away from the field, and backfill must allow "the establishment of vegetative cover."

In Northern Colorado, two county health departments apply that rule:

- **Larimer County**, which covers Wellington, the Larimer side of Berthoud and the countryside around them. Its [OWTS Regulations](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2024/2024_larimer_county_owts_0.pdf), effective February 5, 2024, repeat the 10-inch cover rule and reach inside town limits unless a town runs its own health agency.
- **Weld County**, which covers Severance, Eaton and the countryside around them. [Chapter 30 of the county code](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH30WECODEPUHEENTEWATRSYRE_ARTXDECROITRAR) says the same in Sec. 30-10-40 and applies in incorporated and unincorporated areas alike. Municode marks the chapter as amended by Ordinance 2026-07, so ask the county for the current text.

None of these rules mentions artificial turf. The one line that sounds like a ban, "An impervious covering must not be used," is about the fabric laid directly on the gravel inside a rock-and-pipe field, under the soil, not about what sits on the lawn.

| Part of the system | What Larimer and Weld rules require | What it means for turf |
|---|---|---|
| Drain field (soil treatment area) | At least 10 inches of soil suitable for vegetation; graded to shed stormwater; no compaction from vehicles or site work | Keep turf and its base off it; leave it planted |
| Septic tank | Watertight risers to or above final grade; lids at or above the surface, bolted, locked or at least 59 pounds | Cut turf around each riser; never cover a lid |
| Inspection ports | 4-inch ports at the ends of trenches or corners of beds, reachable from the surface, or in a valve box with a removable cover at the surface | Leave each port or valve-box lid exposed |
| Distribution box and other serviced parts | Larimer: access risers up to ground level | Same as the tank lids |

## Why is the turf base, not the turf, the real conflict?

Because of what an install does to the ground under it. Writing in the trade magazine [Onsite Installer](https://www.onsiteinstaller.com/editorial/2024/10/artificial-turf-over-septic-systems), onsite wastewater educator Sara Heger describes the usual steps: remove the vegetation to a depth of 2 to 4 inches, lay a geotextile and a weed barrier that "could impact the oxygen transfer to the system," then compact a base, which "can also compact the underlying natural soil and damage shallow components." Her conclusion is that turf should not go over a soil treatment area at all. Our guide to [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/) has the layers; over a drain field, each one runs into the county rules:

- **Digging out** takes soil from a cover that may only have been built to the 10-inch minimum. Taking 2 to 4 inches off that leaves less than the rule requires.
- **Crushed rock** replaces the soil suitable for vegetation that the rule asks for, and nothing grows up through turf.
- **Plate compaction and loaded machines** are what both counties' surface-activity rules forbid: the field "must not be subject to damage or soil compaction from livestock, vehicular traffic, recreational use, or other site development activity" ([Larimer 43.8.J](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/2024/2024_larimer_county_owts_0.pdf); [Weld Sec. 30-8-90](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH30WECODEPUHEENTEWATRSYRE_ARTVIIIDECREN)). If the soil does get compacted, Larimer's rule calls for new soil evaluations, and a redesign if the soil's permeability has changed.
- **Flat turf takes in all the rain.** Heger notes the recommendation is to crown septic components so they shed water, and the rules want the field graded to divert stormwater. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/septic/how-care-your-septic-system) says excess water "slows down or stops the wastewater treatment process."

The soil needs air, too. [UF/IFAS Extension](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/SS687) says the bacteria that treat the wastewater need oxygen, so over-compaction and heavy traffic must be avoided, and [WSU Extension](https://s3.wp.wsu.edu/uploads/sites/2079/2014/02/landscaping-septic.pdf) says landscape fabric and plastic "reduce air exchange." Heger adds that turf insulates less than living cover, so freezing may be more likely through a Northern Colorado winter.

## Can turf go around the septic tank and its lids?

Yes, if the lids stay uncovered. Both counties require watertight risers over each tank access manhole reaching to or above final grade, with each lid "brought to or above the surface" and fastened or heavy enough, 59 pounds or more, to keep people out ([Weld Sec. 30-8-30](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH30WECODEPUHEENTEWATRSYRE_ARTVIIIDECREN)). Larimer's [septic page](https://www.larimer.gov/health/environmental-health/septic-systems/septic-systems-owts) puts it plainly: access risers go "up to ground level on all tank lids, distribution boxes, and other parts that need to be inspected or maintained." So:

- **Cut the turf around each riser** and finish the edge there. Heger's alternative, turf pulled up and relaid at every pumping, leaves a lid buried between visits.
- **Leave the 4-inch inspection ports** at the ends of the field, or the valve-box lids over them, at the surface.
- **Keep the pump truck off the field.** The EPA says not to park or drive on a drainfield, and Heger notes most turf is not built to carry heavy equipment. Plan the truck's path before the turf goes in.
- **Hide lids with something that moves.** WSU Extension suggests a planter box or bench that can be moved for service.

An older tank whose lids sit below ground needs risers before the lids can come to the surface. That is work on the system itself, so talk to the county health department first.

## How do I find the tank, the field and the ports before planning the yard?

Start with county records, then confirm on the ground.

- **Larimer County** posts septic documents on its property records page under a property's "Building Info" tab, and takes requests for permit records. Homes built before 1973 often have no permit record, so probing may be the only map.
- **Weld County** suggests finding where the sewer pipe leaves the house, probing with a thin metal rod 5 to 15 feet from the foundation to find the tank, and probing beyond it for the field. Draw a map once you find them, and ask the county whether a permit is on file.
- **Colorado 811 won't mark it.** 811 does not locate private lines such as septic systems; the owner pays a private locating company ([Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/find-a-private-locating-company)). Our guide to [preparing for install day](/guides/preparing-for-turf-installation-day/) covers marking every private line.

The field can be large. Larimer says a standard gravity system can be up to 12 feet wide and 100 feet long, and that clay-like soils usually need a larger treatment area than sandy soils, so a clay lot tends to carry a bigger field. On an acreage lot the field can fill much of the backyard, so draw it on the plan first and lay turf, rock and beds out around it. Our guides to [mixing turf, rock and plant beds](/guides/mixing-turf-rock-and-plant-beds/) and turf on acreage lots take it from there.

## What happens when I sell, or when the system needs work?

The lids and the field get looked at, so they need to be reachable.

- **Larimer County:** a property on septic generally must be inspected by a third-party inspector, and the report must reach the county health department before the sale closes ([Transfer of Title](https://www.larimer.gov/health/environmental-health/septic-systems/septic-systems-owts/transfer-title)). The county's Acceptance Document requires a structurally sound tank "provided with safe and secure lids" and a soil treatment area that is not failing.
- **Weld County:** there is no septic inspection requirement at sale, but the county recommends having the system inspected and the tank cleaned before new owners take over ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Health-and-Environment/Environmental-Health-Services/Septic-Systems)).
- **Between sales:** the EPA says a household system should be inspected at least every three years, and Weld recommends every year.

Any repair to the field means digging in it, and turf laid over the field would have to come up first.

## What should I ask before anyone digs near a septic system?

It depends on your system and your county: ask Larimer County's septic (OWTS) program or Weld County's Environmental Health Services before anyone digs near the field or the tank. Then ask the installer:

- **Where are the tank, the field and the ports, and how do you know?** A good answer cites the county record or a locate, with marks on the ground.
- **Where does the turf stop?** A good answer puts the turf edge outside the field and leaves the field planted.
- **Will any machine or loaded wheelbarrow cross the field?** A good answer is no, with the equipment route on the plan.
- **How will the lids and ports be finished?** A good answer is turf cut and edged around each riser, with every lid at the surface.
- **Which way does water leave the turf area?** A good answer: away from the field.
