faq:
  - q: "Do lighted cups work on an artificial green?"
    a: "Yes, as a target rather than as light for the surface: a cup light sits in the hole, directly under the cup. One model sold for greens draws 2 watts, gives 200 lumens at 2700 K, runs on 8 to 15 volts and sets into a brass pour sleeve, so a wired one needs its sleeve and wire placed when the cup is set; a rechargeable battery version skips the wire. Timnath exempts ornamental landscape fixtures only at 100 lumens or less and prohibits upward lighting except as its code allows, so ask Timnath Planning how it treats a cup light."
  - q: "Are solar stake lights enough to putt by?"
    a: "They can mark the edge of the green and the path to it. A stake light that can't be aimed won't give the low, raking light that shows a slope. Timnath exempts solar fixtures with their own rechargeable batteries only when each gives 100 lumens or less; brighter ones must meet its shielding and color rules."
  - q: "Can I hang string lights over the green instead?"
    a: "They light the green from above, and flat overhead light hides the small slopes that edge lighting reveals, so string lights suit a patio better than a practice green. Timnath exempts string lights at homes as long as they cause no excessive glare or light trespass. Fort Collins' residential code asks for every exterior fixture to be fully shielded, so ask Building Services before stringing them."
  - q: "How long can I putt before a 10 p.m. lights-out rule?"
    a: "At midsummer, not long. In Fort Collins on June 21, 2026, the sun set at 8:34 p.m. and civil twilight ended at 9:08 p.m., so a 10 p.m. cutoff like Loveland's code or the Waters Edge guidelines left under an hour of lit putting. On September 22, civil twilight ended at 7:24 p.m., which left more than two and a half hours."
  - q: "What counts as light trespass if a neighbor complains?"
    a: "Timnath puts a number on it: no more than 0.1 footcandle at a property line in or next to a residential zone, and 0.0 at a line next to public open space or a stream. Windsor asks for light sources shielded to minimize spill light and glare on neighbors, and covenants such as The Masters at Mariana Butte's want the light source hidden from nearby homes. **Not legal advice:** the lighting rules below are quoted from each town's, county's and association's own code or guidelines as they read on September 26, 2026. Your town, county and HOA have the final word.

This page covers the lights, the wire and the rules. The green's shape and cups are in [designing a custom backyard putting green](/guides/putting-green-design-ideas/); patio, kitchen and fire-feature lighting is in [planning a backyard around turf](/guides/outdoor-living-with-artificial-turf/).

## What kind of light lets you read the break at night?

Low light from the side. A landscape lighting contractor describes two ways to light a green ([Landscape Lighting Pro](https://utahlights.com/3-ways-to-light-your-putting-green-this-summer/)):

- **A down light** mounted on the house or in a large tree, which bathes the whole green in flat light from above.
- **Directional path lights around the border,** which light the green from its edge. Their low light throws shadows off small knolls, and those shadows hint at the line the ball will take.

For practice, the edge lighting is the one that shows the break; flat overhead light suits a green used mostly for a casual game. What makes either work:

- **Aim across the green, never at the golfer.** A bright lens in your eye line at address makes the ball harder to see. A shielded fixture with its lamp hidden also meets most of the town and HOA rules below.
- **No brighter than necessary.** DarkSky International and the Illuminating Engineering Society's principles for outdoor lighting say to use the lowest light level required, aim and shield light so it points down and doesn't spill, and use warmer-color light ([DarkSky](https://web.archive.org/web/20260918092702/https://darksky.org/resources/guides-and-how-tos/lighting-principles/)). Several dim fixtures spaced around the edge light a green more evenly than one strong one, which leaves a hot spot beside it and a dark far side.
- **Its own switch or timer.** The contractor advises a separate on/off switch for the green; the DarkSky principles call for timers or motion controls so the light is off when nobody is putting.

## Where do the fixtures and the wire go on a turf green?

Outside the putting surface, and under the base before the turf goes down.

- **Fixtures stand in the beds, boulders or hardscape beyond the fringe,** never in the putting surface or the fringe, where a stake means a hole in the turf and a fixture is something to trip on or putt into.
- **Wire runs in conduit or sleeves laid before the base is compacted,** with the ends capped and marked on a drawing. Once the turf is seamed and infilled, getting a wire under it means cutting the green open.
- **A wired cup light needs its sleeve and wire set with the cup,** because cups are set into the base; one cup light sold for greens sets into a brass pour sleeve ([Main Place Lighting](https://shopmpl.com/products/usa-made-golf-cup-light-1)).
- **Get a locate before trenching.** Colorado 811 says digging of any size needs a locate first and that privately owned lines aren't marked ([Colorado 811](https://colorado811.org/)), so an old sprinkler or landscape-light wire won't show up. Find those on the builder's drawings or from the sprinkler controller.
- **Leave room for snow.** Fort Collins averages 51.4 inches of snow a year (NOAA climate normals), so keep short fixtures out of the spots where snow off the patio gets piled.

On a new build or a full landscape job, the conduit belongs in the same step as the irrigation sleeves and the base under the turf, so it fits the plan the town and HOA approve.

## Does lighting a green need an electrical permit?

Colorado requires an electrical permit before wiring is installed, from the State Electrical Board or the local building department. A homeowner may wire their own residence under a homeowner permit if they do the work themselves, it is inspected, and it meets the National Electrical Code; an electrical contractor pulls its own permits ([Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations](https://dpo.colorado.gov/ElectricalPlumbingPermits/Info)).

Low-voltage landscape lighting runs from a transformer, and what counts as permit work for it is the local building department's call. Fort Collins exempts residential electrical systems under 25 volts and under 50 watts, but adding or extending a receptacle needs a permit, and so do new or relocated light fixtures and controls ([City of Fort Collins](https://fortcollins.gov/Business/Permits-and-Licensing/Building-Permits)). A transformer feeding a ring of fixtures can pass 50 watts, so ask Fort Collins Building Services, or your own town's building department, before the trench is dug. Line-voltage fixtures, or a new outdoor circuit for the transformer, are wiring work either way.

## What do Northern Colorado town and county lighting codes allow?

Every code below asks for shielded light; the details differ by town:

| Where | What the code says | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | The Land Use Code's lighting standards exempt detached houses and duplexes, but the residential building code requires every exterior fixture to be down-directional, fully shielded and no higher than 3000 K ([Sec. 5-30, R333.2](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH5BUBURE_ARTIIBU_DIV2BUCOST_S5-30AMDE2024INRECO)); the city says detached homes must use fixtures with a Dark Sky Fixture Seal of Approval ([City of Fort Collins](https://fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Code-and-Charter/Building-Code/Rules-for-Common-Projects)) | Each fixture's cut sheet; ask Building Services about the permit |
| [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) | Full cut-off fixtures with flat lenses, light falling on the ground or the structure, and no exterior lighting from 10 p.m. to sunrise unless needed for safety or security or for a use operating after 10 p.m. ([Unified Development Code](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/loveland-co/doc-view.aspx?pn=0&ajax=0&secid=4429)) | Landscape lighting is evaluated at development review or the building permit; for an existing yard, it depends, so ask Loveland Planning |
| [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | In every zone: outdoor lighting at least 3 feet from an abutting property line, no taller than 15 feet unless otherwise regulated, and sources concealed or shielded to minimize uplight, spill light and glare on neighbors ([Sec. 15-12-20](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH15DEST_ARTXIISILI_S15-12-20GERE)) | Fixture spacing from the fence line |
| [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/) | Fully shielded, full cut-off LED meeting DarkSky requirements, 3,000 K or less (2,700 K next to public open space or natural areas), dimmable; no more than 0.1 footcandle at a residential property line and 0.0 next to open space or a stream; upward lighting prohibited except as the code allows; ornamental landscape fixtures of 100 lumens or less exempt ([Land Use Code Sec. 5.7](https://library.municode.com/co/timnath/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CD_APXALAUSCO_ART5DEST_S5.7STDE)) | Lumens per fixture; ask Timnath Planning about lighted cups |
| Unincorporated Larimer County | New and replacement exterior fixtures fully shielded so the lighting element can't be seen from on or beyond the property line, with a cut-off no more than 90 degrees from vertical ([R325.9](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/1-2024-building-code-amendments-final0.pdf)) | Ask Larimer County Building |
| Unincorporated Weld County | In the A and E zones, light sources shielded so beams don't shine directly onto adjacent properties, and no colored lights that could pass for traffic signals ([Sec. 23-3-70](https://library.municode.com/co/weld_county/codes/charter_and_county_code?nodeId=CH23ZO_ARTIIIZODI_DIV1AAGZODI_S23-3-70BURE)) | Ask Weld County Planning |

For a town not in the table, it depends on that town's code; ask its Planning department before buying fixtures.

## What will an HOA or metro district ask for?

Often approval first, and sometimes a curfew, a height limit or a wattage cap. From the guidelines of seven Northern Colorado communities:

| Community | What its rules say about lighting |
|---|---|
| Waters Edge (Fort Collins) | Approval before installing; aimed down with the light kept on the lot; walkway lights no taller than 24 inches; landscape lighting off from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. |
| The Lakes at Centerra (Loveland) | No uplighting; full cut-off, fully shielded fixtures; low-level landscape lighting no more than 500 watts in total |
| The Masters at Mariana Butte (Loveland) | Written approval; subdued, with the source hidden from neighboring homes and shielded against spill; no uplighting |
| Heron Lakes (Berthoud) | Committee approval; aimed at the ground and house, the light kept inside the lot and the source hidden from outside it |
| RainDance (Windsor) | Walkway lights pointed down and no taller than 24 inches |
| Falcon Point (Firestone) | Small, low-wattage lights in the builder's style and aimed toward the home need no approval; high-wattage spotlights or floodlights do |
| Spring Valley at Ute Creek (Longmont) | Lamps no brighter than a 100-watt incandescent; low landscape lighting used sparingly; security floodlighting and LED lighting called unacceptable |

The rules differ enough that one fixture can pass in one place and fail in another: Timnath's code requires LED, while Spring Valley at Ute Creek's guidelines reject it. A lighting request usually goes in with the green's paperwork: each fixture's cut sheet (color temperature, lumens, shielding), a plan showing where each fixture stands and which way it aims, the timer setting, and the wire route.

## What should I ask whoever designs and wires the lights?

- **"Will any fixture sit in the putting surface or the fringe?"** A good answer is no: they stand outside the fringe and aim across the green.
- **"Where does the wire run, and is there a spare sleeve?"** A good answer names conduit laid under the base before compaction, capped and drawn on the plan, with a spare run for later.
- **"Who pulls the electrical permit?"** A good answer names the electrical contractor under its own permit, or you under a homeowner permit, before any trench is dug.
- **"What color temperature, lumens and shielding does each fixture have?"** A good answer is a cut sheet that meets the town's code and the HOA's rule.
- **"What turns the lights off?"** A good answer is a switch for the green and a timer set to the town's or the HOA's cutoff.
