**Checked September 27, 2026.** A basement or garage slab can look dry and still give off moisture. The ASTM calcium chloride test starts from that point: every concrete floor emits some water vapor, and any floor covering can fail from too much of it ([ASTM F1869](https://store.astm.org/f1869-23.html)). Turf laid indoors is a floor covering, and some cushioned gym turf has no drainage holes in its pad. So the first step is a moisture test. In Northern Colorado the second is finding out whether the slab was built to move.

This page covers the floor under a home gym or golf simulator. The putting green itself, with its break, size and speed, is in [an indoor putting green for a basement or garage](/guides/indoor-putting-green-for-home/). Patios and outdoor slabs are in [artificial turf over concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-over-concrete/).

## How do I know if the slab is dry enough for turf?

Test it before anything covers it. The relative humidity standard, ASTM F2170, says moisture left in a slab under a floor covering can cause debonding, deterioration and microbial growth, and that flooring makers generally require a test first ([ASTM F2170](https://store.astm.org/f2170-19a.html)). Three tests are in common use:

| Test | How it is done | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Plastic sheet (ASTM D4263) | An 18-inch square of plastic taped to the slab for at least 24 hours, then the concrete is checked for darkening | Yes or no: moisture near the surface |
| Calcium chloride (ASTM F1869) | A calcium chloride disk sealed under plastic on the bare slab collects vapor and is weighed | Pounds of vapor per 1,000 square feet in 24 hours |
| Relative humidity probe (ASTM F2170) | A probe set in a hole drilled to 40 percent of the slab's depth | Humidity inside the slab |

- **The plastic sheet** is the quick look. ASTM designs it for use before coatings go on ([ASTM D4263](https://store.astm.org/d4263-24.html)), and Tnemec, a coatings maker, points out that it doesn't measure how much moisture there is and only sees the top of the slab ([Tnemec](https://tnemec.com/remedy-index-page/3-ways-test-concrete-moisture/)).
- **The calcium chloride test** gives a number to hold against a turf maker's limit. One indoor turf installation overview treats 3 lbs or less as acceptable for most turf, says the risk of moisture problems rises between 3 and 5 lbs, and warns that the test takes 3 days to run ([GrassTex overview](https://www.greatmats.com/specs/grasstex/install-grasstex-artificial-turf.pdf)). Ask the maker of your product for its own figure.
- **The probe** reads the middle of the slab, where the surface tests can't reach.

Tnemec recommends testing once the heating and cooling have run at least 48 hours. A result holds only for the day and spots tested, so test more than one place, including the wall nearest the downspouts and sprinklers; the Colorado Geological Survey counts irrigation water soaking in beside the foundation among the landscaping problems on expansive soils.

## What if the basement slab moves on clay?

Plan for it. The Colorado Geological Survey calls cracked and heaved garage and basement floor slabs very common signs of expansive clay across the state, and says building without allowing for that movement is a very common source of damage ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)).

Its homeowner publications describe the design meant for it. A "floating" slab is kept separate from bearing walls, columns, stairs and utilities so it can rise and fall, and some cracking is expected ([CGS SP-11](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-11.pdf)). A basement over one should stay unfinished or have partitions designed for the slab to move ([CGS SP-14](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-14.pdf)). In unincorporated Larimer County, the building code amendments in force since January 1, 2026 require non-bearing basement walls on such a slab to be floated, with at least a 1.5-inch gap in a double bottom plate ([Larimer County amendments](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/1-2024-building-code-amendments-final0.pdf)). Towns such as [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) and [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) adopt their own building codes, so ask your town's building department what applies to your house. Not legal advice.

What that means for turf:

- **Keep the floor and the walls apart.** A platform, framed subfloor or trim fastened to both the slab and the walls works against the gap the house was built with. Keep them freestanding.
- **Choose a method that lifts.** Loose-laid or taped turf can come up if a crack needs repair; a full glue-down bonds across every crack.
- **Watch the cracks first.** SP-14 suggests measuring certain cracks every month to see whether they hold, widen, or open and close with the seasons. Once turf covers them, nobody can.
- **Leave the service points open:** the floor drain, sump pit, cleanouts and where any radon pipe meets the floor.

If the house was built new, Colorado requires the builder to give the buyer a summary of the soils analysis and site recommendations at least 14 days before closing ([C.R.S. 6-6.5-101](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_6-6.5-101)). If it is in your closing papers, it may say how the basement floor was designed. Not legal advice.

## What changes in an unheated garage?

**Cold.** Adhesive needs warmth. The GrassTex overview asks for a slab no colder than 65°F and a room between 65 and 95°F, held for 48 hours before, during and after the install. At the nearest NOAA station, in [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/), the low reaches 32°F or colder on 150 nights of an average year ([NOAA normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json)). Unless the garage is heated, plan a glue-down for warm weather, and ask whoever supplies the tape what floor temperature it needs.

**Snowmelt.** The same station averages 51.4 inches of snow a year, and whatever a car carries in melts on the garage floor. The International Residential Code requires the parking area to slope so liquids move to a drain or toward the main vehicle door ([IRC R309.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)). Turf in that path soaks it up, and a pad may hold it: Flooring Inc's gym turf guide recommends one padded turf for indoor use only because its pad isn't built to drain ([Flooring Inc](https://www.flooringinc.com/blog/sports-turf-buyers-guide)). Lay turf in a bay the car doesn't use, at the high end of the floor, and leave any floor drain uncovered.

## Should gym turf be glued, taped or laid loose?

| Method | What holds it | Suits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loose-lay | Its own weight, with tape or adhesive at edges and seams | A room that may change; a slab still being watched | Edges creeping under a sled |
| Double-sided tape | Tape strips between turf and slab | Sled lanes; turf you may lift later | Tape laid across the sled's path |
| Full glue-down | Adhesive spread over the slab | A permanent room on a slab that passed its tests | Slab temperature, filled cracks, hard removal |

**For a sled lane,** Flooring Inc advises a low pile so a sled can be pushed without wrecking the floor, and lists visible lane lines as a drawback of one longer-fiber turf. Heroturfs, which makes sled tracks, says to let a new track rest 24 hours before fitting, tape its full underside rather than just the edges, and run every strip of tape in the sled's direction, because tape laid across the drag leaves a ridge that brakes the sled and wears the fibers ([Heroturfs](https://heroturfs.com/turf-installation-guide)). How pile length changes with use is in [artificial turf pile height](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/).

**For a glue-down,** the GrassTex overview calls for filling cracks 1/8 inch wide or more and letting the turf relax 24 hours before it is glued. Plan the spot where barbells drop separately, and ask whether the turf you are buying is sold for dropped weights.

If someone else lays it, ask:

- Which moisture test they ran, where, and the reading against the product maker's limit. A good answer is a written result.
- Whether the turf's backing or pad is perforated.
- How it is held down, and how it comes up again.
- Whether anything will be fastened to the walls.
- In a garage, what the slab temperature will be on install day.

## What goes under turf for a golf simulator?

Two surfaces, not one. Carl's Place, a simulator retailer, says most setups use a hitting mat or strip for full swings and turf as the floor of the room, because putting turf and high-pile turf aren't built for repeated full swings ([Carl's Place](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/golf-simulator-flooring)). Under the turf it describes:

- **Foam tiles,** commonly about 1/2 to 1 inch thick. Quick, and easy to cut around a mat.
- **A wood subfloor,** typically 2x4 framing under 3/4-inch plywood. More work, and the surest way to set the mat flush.

Plan the heights so the mat sits flush, and tape the turf's edges and seams so they don't creep. The same guide calls a moisture barrier a smart precaution on a basement or garage slab, because concrete can pass humidity with no moisture showing, and suggests one under a wood subfloor. On a floating slab, keep that subfloor clear of the walls. A platform also takes height off the room; ceiling heights and a green that shares the space are in the indoor putting green guide.

## How do I keep turf clean indoors?

Treat it much like carpet. Flooring Inc says to vacuum indoor gym turf, disinfect and deodorize it with a carpet cleaning solution, and lift stains with warm water and household detergent, or white vinegar mixed half and half with water. Outdoor care, and which cleaners are safe on turf, are in [how to clean artificial turf](/guides/how-to-clean-artificial-turf/).

Skip the hose: on a slab, water has nowhere to go. EPA says mold can hide on the underside of carpets and pads, and that damp materials dried within 24 to 48 hours of a spill usually don't grow it ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home)). Blot spills, run a fan, and keep the room below 60 percent relative humidity, ideally 30 to 50, as EPA advises for homes. Loose sand infill travels through a house, so ask whether the product needs any; see [artificial turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).
