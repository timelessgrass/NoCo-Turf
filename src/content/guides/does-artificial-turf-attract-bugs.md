  question: "Does artificial turf attract mosquitoes, ants, fleas or other bugs?"
  answer: "Not by itself. Turf has no roots for bugs to eat and no tall grass to hide in. Mosquitoes breed only in water standing about a week, so a ponding low spot, a clogged backing or a toy full of rain is the risk. Ants nest in the soil beneath; fleas and ticks arrive on animals."
faq:
  - q: "Are the swarms of little bugs over my turf mosquitoes?"
    a: "Usually not. CSU Extension says non-biting midges are the insects most often mistaken for mosquitoes; they look alike but don't feed on blood. Crane flies, which resemble very large mosquitoes, develop in damp soil rich in organic matter, and the adults don't bite either."
  - q: "Can my dog get West Nile virus or heartworm from mosquitoes in the yard?"
    a: "Both spread through mosquito bites. Larimer County says dogs and cats can get West Nile virus, though most infected pets have mild or no symptoms and recover, and there is no evidence pets pass it to people. CSU Extension lists heartworm in dogs among Colorado's mosquito-borne diseases, so ask your vet about prevention. More in [is artificial turf safe for pets](/guides/is-artificial-turf-safe-for-pets/)."
  - q: "Will turf stop skunks digging for grubs?"
    a: "It removes what the grubs eat. CSU Extension says white grubs and billbugs damage lawns by feeding on grass roots, that white grubs have long been common in towns along the South Platte River valley, and that raccoons and skunks dig up lawns to find them. Turf has no roots. Animals that burrow under turf are a separate problem, covered in [gopher and vole wire under turf](/guides/gopher-wire-under-artificial-turf/)."
  - q: "Is there Lyme disease in Northern Colorado?"
    a: "Not from local ticks. CSU Extension says the blacklegged tick that carries Lyme disease doesn't occur in Colorado, and there has never been a confirmed case from a tick bite here. The tick that most often bites people and dogs in Colorado is the Rocky Mountain wood tick."
  - q: "Should I spray my whole turf area for mosquitoes?"
    a: "There is little on dry turf to spray. Mosquitoes don't breed there, and CDC says adults rest in dark, humid places such as under patio furniture, and in grass and shrubs. For water you can't drain, the state health department suggests Bti, a larvicide. Neighborhood spraying is up to your town; in Larimer County, an HOA that asks for spraying pays for it."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** The turf itself gives bugs little: its plastic blades never grow tall enough to hide in. Bugs come to a turf yard for the water left on it and the soil under it. No study we found counts bugs in turf yards against lawns, so this page goes by what each bug needs.

| Bug | What it needs | Where to look in a turf yard |
|---|---|---|
| Mosquitoes | Water that stays long enough to raise them: 7 to 10 days from egg to adult for Culex, the West Nile carriers ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/about/culex-mosquitoes.html)) | Low spots that pond, putting greens, saucers, toys, wading pools |
| Ants | Soil for the nest and cover over it | Seams, edges, the strip beside a slab or foundation |
| Fleas | An animal to arrive on, and humid air | Where pets sleep; dens under decks and sheds |
| Ticks | Plants to climb and wait on | Native grass or brush along the back fence |
| Flies | Pet waste and garbage | The dog's corner, the trash cans |
| White grubs, billbugs | Grass roots | Nowhere: turf has none |

Food brings them too. FieldTurf's field-care manual says to rinse spilled sports drinks off promptly "to avoid bugs being attracted to the surface" ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/workspace/uploads/files/fieldturf_brochure_maintenance-guidelines_apr2017_006.pdf)).

## Can mosquitoes breed in artificial turf?

Only in water that stays on it. Culex mosquitoes lay floating rafts of 100 to 300 eggs on standing water, including small containers such as buckets, toys, birdbaths and flowerpots, and an egg needs 7 to 10 days to become a flying adult. Water that drains the day after a storm can't raise a generation; a puddle that lasts a week can. That is why Larimer County asks residents to drain standing water in their yards at least once a week ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/health/communicable-disease/west-nile-virus)).

Where water lingers in a turf yard:

- **A dip over slow clay.** [Longmont](/areas/longmont-co/) warns that its "clay soils absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)), and in [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) the water department finds hard clay "sometimes just inches below the surface" ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/waterwise-landscapes)). A low spot with nowhere to send its water holds a puddle for days. See [why water puddles on turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/) and [turf that's sinking or uneven](/guides/artificial-turf-sinking-low-spots/).
- **A backing that no longer drains.** Leaves left over winter can rot into "a layer of organic matter that can impede drainage," FieldTurf's landscape guide warns ([FieldTurf Landscape](https://fieldturflandscape.com/maintenance/artificial-turf-maintenance-guide/)). See [clearing leaves off turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/).
- **A putting green.** Putting green turf commonly has a backing that lets no water through, Synthetic Grass Warehouse says ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/how-does-backing-quality-affect-your-artificial-turfs-drainage/)), so rain leaves only by running off the contours. See [putting green maintenance](/guides/artificial-putting-green-maintenance/).
- **A leaking cap.** Sprinkler heads capped under turf "may leak over time," SGW's installer guidance warns ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/blog/irrigation/)), and Larimer County's mosquito list asks homeowners to repair broken sprinkler heads ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/health/communicable-disease/west-nile-virus/mosquito-control-information)). More in [what happens to the sprinkler system](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).
- **Things left on the turf.** Once a week, CDC says, empty and scrub, turn over, cover or throw out toys, pools, birdbaths and flowerpot saucers ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/mosquito-control/mosquito-control-at-home.html)). Longmont adds clogged gutters and kiddie pools to the usual breeding sites ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/parks-and-natural-resources/wildlife/mosquito-control/)).

Resting adults find little shelter on turf. CDC says Culex rest "in grass, shrubs, and other thick vegetation where it is cool, dark, and humid," and Larimer County asks that tall grasses be cut. Turf stays short; the shrub bed beside it and the shade under the patio chairs don't. Nor does a dry yard stop mosquitoes flying in: CDC says Culex have been known to fly up to 2 miles, and CSU Extension says adults typically fly "a mile or two to feed" ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/west-nile-virus-and-mosquito-management/)). Draining your yard cuts what hatches there, not what the neighborhood raises.

## How much West Nile virus is in Northern Colorado this year?

Some every summer. Larimer County says the virus "is likely already circulating in local mosquito populations, as it does every summer," and has found infected mosquitoes every year since 2003, when Colorado's largest outbreak brought over 2,800 reported cases and 63 deaths.

| | Larimer County | Weld County |
|---|---|---|
| First human case of 2026 | An adult in [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/), announced June 25, who may have been infected while traveling ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/spotlights/2026/06/25/larimer-county-reports-first-human-case-west-nile-virus-2026-launches-new-data-dashboard)) | Announced Aug. 31, a month later than in 2025 ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/Newsroom/2026-News/First-positive-case-of-West-Nile-Virus-in-Weld-County)) |
| The 2026 season so far | 5 confirmed cases and 3 hospitalizations as of Sept. 23 ([dashboard](https://apps.larimer.org/api/health/west-nile/)) | Culex numbers below the 5-year average, but infected mosquitoes in all three trapping zones |
| Recent seasons | Infected mosquitoes every year since 2003 | 34 human cases in 2025, 15 in 2024, 52 in 2023 ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Health-and-Environment/Environmental-Health-Services/Animal-Related-Diseases/Zoonotic-Diseases-Seasonal-Updates/West-Nile-Virus)) |

Weld's trapping zones take in Greeley and [Evans](/areas/evans-co/), [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/) and [Milliken](/areas/milliken-co/), and [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) and [Dacono](/areas/dacono-co/). Culex bite most from dusk to dawn, and CSU says the West Nile carriers peak in July and August: midsummer evenings on the patio are when Larimer County's advice to wear an EPA-registered repellent counts. Its dashboard draws a 1-mile circle around every trap that tests positive, so you can see whether your address falls inside one.

Spraying is up to towns. Larimer County has no mosquito program of its own; towns such as [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), [Timnath](/areas/timnath-co/), Loveland and [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) pay the contractor VDCI, and the county asks residents to report standing water to their town. Longmont contracts VDCI too.

## Why are ants nesting under my turf?

Because they were there first. "Tremendous numbers of ants normally reside in a typical house lot," CSU Extension says, mostly underground where nobody notices ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/ants-in-the-home/)). Pavement ants nest under pavement or rocks and pile small mounds of soil at the entrances; field ants nest in loose soil. Turf over a compacted rock base covers a colony much as a paver does, and the ants still need a way up. On turf that shows as a little pile of sand or fine soil at a seam, an edge or a drain hole, and the holes stay open because water and air pass through them.

CSU calls ants "quite beneficial" overall; many eat other insects. When a colony is a nuisance, such as mounds on the putting line, CSU's advice works on turf too:

- **Bait, don't spray.** Insecticide on a nest often kills only the ants on the surface, and a disturbed colony opens new entrances elsewhere. Baits, which workers carry inside, "will usually provide the most satisfactory control," with results in a week or two.
- **Keep baits fresh.** They "often become unattractive when they dry out, a common problem in the arid climate of Colorado," so check them often in a sunny yard.
- **Leave the kettle inside.** CSU says boiling water carries more hazard than ant insecticides, with less chance of working.
- **Read the label** for when kids and pets can go back on the turf; CSU reminds readers that following it is the applicator's job.
- **Take away food and cover.** Rinse spills the same day, and keep heavy mulch and leaf piles off the foundation, where CSU says they can shelter a colony.

## Can fleas or ticks live in artificial turf?

They can cross it, but neither makes a home in it.

**Fleas** come in on animals. CSU says fleas "are rarely problematic" given Colorado's dry climate, because cat flea eggs need more than 50% relative humidity to hatch ([CSU](https://agsci.colostate.edu/agbio/ipm-pests/fleas/)), and the Colorado Climate Center calls the state's humidity "generally quite low" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). Larvae stay near the host's bedding or nest, away from light. If the dog has fleas, check its bed, the doghouse and any den under a deck or shed; CSU links the human flea most often with skunks, foxes and coyotes. Near a prairie dog colony, Colorado Parks and Wildlife asks pet owners to use flea preventatives, because prairie dog fleas can carry plague ([CPW](https://cpw.state.co.us/living-prairie-dogs)).

**Ticks** wait on plants. CSU Extension says hard ticks climb onto vegetation and hold still until a passing animal brushes them, gathering along "brushy areas along the edges of fields and woodlands or commonly traveled paths through grassy areas and shrublands" ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/colorado-ticks-and-tick-borne-diseases/)). They are most common at higher elevations and most active in spring and early summer. Short turf gives a tick nothing to climb; a strip of native grass or brush where a lot backs onto open space does. The exception is the brown dog tick, which develops only on dogs and turns up where dogs are kept together, such as kennels; see [artificial turf for dog daycares](/guides/dog-daycare-artificial-turf/).

## What should I check each week to keep bugs down?

From June through early September, when Weld County says its mosquitoes are most active ([Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Health-and-Environment/Environmental-Health-Services/Animal-Related-Diseases/Zoonotic-Diseases-Seasonal-Updates/West-Nile-Virus)):

1. **Walk the turf the day after a storm.** Water still standing is a drainage problem; see [why water puddles on turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/).
2. **Empty anything that holds water:** saucers, toys, wading pools, pet bowls. Larimer County says to change birdbath water at least weekly, twice a week in hot weather.
3. **Keep leaves and cottonwood fluff off** so the backing keeps draining.
4. **Look for a wet spot in a dry week.** It can mean a leaking line or cap under the turf.
5. **Pick up after the dog and rinse the spot.** CSU says blow flies commonly breed in pet waste ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/flies-in-the-home/)); more in [dog poop on artificial turf](/guides/dog-poop-on-artificial-turf/).
6. **Scan seams and edges** for little piles of sand, the sign of an ant entrance.

Two questions for whoever builds or repairs the turf: where does water leave the base on this lot, and was the old lawn zone shut off at the valve? A good answer names a place lower than the turf, such as an edge, a bed or a drain, and says the zone was turned off at the valve instead of capped under the turf. More in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/), and for dog yards on the [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/) page.
