Turf can be heated, and some sports fields are. A share of the soccer fields in Scandinavia and Finland run warm fluid through pipes under the turf all winter, and one snowmelt maker has published a heated turf dog spot. Here is how those systems work, what they draw, what they do to the ground under the turf, and what works in a Northern Colorado yard instead.

## How is artificial turf heated?

There are two ways to do it:

- **Warm fluid in pipes (hydronic).** This is how the heated fields of Scandinavia and Finland work. A 2024 research paper published through Sweden's Chalmers University of Technology describes the usual layout: plastic PEX pipe 25 mm across (about an inch), laid about 8 inches apart, either below the top layers of the field or, in newer fields, in grooves in the shock pad, which heats the turf more effectively ([Chalmers paper](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/544313/file/544313_Fulltext.pdf)). Pipelife, which makes these pipe systems, says they work under artificial pitches as well as grass ([Pipelife](https://www.pipelife.com/buildings/heating-and-cooling/undersoil-heating-systems.html)).
- **Electric.** SIS Pitches sells a system that runs current through a stainless-steel grid under the surface and says it is fully compatible with synthetic grass pitches ([SIS Pitches](https://www.sispitches.com/system/sis-heat-dynamic/)). For a home, the electric version is snowmelt cable set in concrete, which changes the whole base (more below).

Even a heated field gets plowed in a heavy snow. The paper's authors report that the heat is sized to keep the turf free of frost and to melt light snow on milder days, while heavier snow is still removed by plowing and brushing. Pipelife describes its heat melting "the surface layer of ice left behind after snow removal." A heated field is a frost-free field, not a snow-free one.

## Where is heated turf actually used?

Mostly on Nordic soccer fields, and even there it is the exception. About 300 of the roughly 3,000 artificial turf soccer fields in Scandinavia are heated, mostly by district heating, to stretch the playing season over the winter: about one field in ten ([Chalmers paper](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/544313/file/544313_Fulltext.pdf)). Two published examples:

- **Naantali, Finland.** Heating pipes went under the Karvetti artificial grass field in 2007 to keep snow off it, because plowing breaks up the turf's rubber granule surface and can be costly to maintain. The heat has run from January to the end of March, and the researchers note that melting snow takes a lot of energy ([Turku University of Applied Sciences](https://www.turkuamk.fi/en/article/computational-model-for-snow-melting-research-team-optimises-heating-of-naantali-artificial-grass-field/)).
- **Uppsala, Sweden.** Measured in 2014 and 2015, one heated field used about 120 kWh of heat per square meter a year. A survey of Swedish heated fields works out to about 150, by the paper's estimate.

For homes, the one published example we found is a 32-square-foot dog relief spot in Ohio, shown by the snowmelt maker WarmlyYours, with its cable in a 1.5-inch concrete layer under the turf ([WarmlyYours](https://www.warmlyyours.com/en-US/showcases/turf-backyard-in-avon-ohio)).

## How much power does heated turf use?

It depends on the job: keeping frost off, or melting snow as it falls.

| What the heat does | Power per square foot while running | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Keeps a Nordic field frost-free and melts light snow | About 4 to 5 watts with pipes in the shock pad; up to about 9 with pipes in the sand below it | Chalmers paper: 40 to 50 and up to 100 watts per square meter (our conversion) |
| Melts snow as it falls, on a driveway or a heated turf pad (designed for 1 to 3 inches an hour) | About 50 watts | WarmlyYours |

Over a winter, the Swedish fields above come to roughly 11 to 14 kWh per square foot a year (our conversion), and that is mostly keeping frost off.

Melting falling snow takes five to ten times the power or more. WarmlyYours gives the arithmetic: at 50 watts a square foot, every 100 square feet draws 5 kilowatts while the system is on ([WarmlyYours](https://www.warmlyyours.com/en-US/posts/How-to-Calculate-the-Cost-of-a-Heated-Driveway-1181)). For a 500-square-foot dog yard, that is 25 kilowatts, and a six-hour storm comes to 150 kWh (our arithmetic). The U.S. Energy Information Administration puts the average home's electricity at about 899 kWh a month in 2022 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=97&t=3)), so one storm on that yard adds about a sixth of an average home's month. The Ohio dog spot's showcase also recommends preheating the slab 2 to 4 hours before a storm, which adds more.

The home example is a 32-square-foot pad, not a lawn, and even that draws 1,500 watts and 13 amps at 120 volts. An electrician can tell you whether your panel and a circuit can carry even that.

## Can artificial turf take the heat?

Yes, at the temperatures these systems run. TenCate Grass lists the yarn melting point of its TigerTurf Power sports turf at 128°C (260°F) ([TigerTurf spec sheet](https://tigerturf.com/us/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/TigerTurf-Power-1.75-in-Spec-Sheet.pdf)). Nordic fields usually send their heating fluid at about 35°C (95°F) on the coldest days, with one reported example of 50°C (122°F) or more. WarmlyYours says its heated turf runs well below the melting point of polyethylene and polypropylene, typically keeping the surface just above freezing.

The danger is concentrated heat against the turf. SYNLawn's care manual warns that equipment set down on turf, as landscape crews do when trimming trees or blowing leaves, can melt it ([SYNLawn](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/SYNLawn-Care-and-Maintenance-ENG.pdf)). And WarmlyYours says snowmelt cable cannot go directly under turf at all: it must be embedded in concrete or mortar, which it says the National Electrical Code requires. So never lay a heat mat, cable or heat tape loose under turf.

The backing is the open question. The Synthetic Turf Council lists polyurethane, hot melt polyolefin and latex as the coatings on the back of synthetic grass ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf)), and none of the turf spec sheets we read gives a temperature limit for any of them. Before heating under turf, ask its maker in writing whether a heated slab is allowed and whether it changes the written terms. Turf melted by sunlight reflected off a window is a separate problem with its own guide: [why turf melts near windows](/guides/artificial-turf-melting-from-windows/).

## What does heating do to the base and drainage?

On a field, the heat sits inside a base built to drain. The shock pad is slotted so rain and melting snow pass through, a sand or gravel layer lies below, drain pipes run along the bottom, and the surface slopes about 1% from the center line to the sides ([Chalmers paper](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/544313/file/544313_Fulltext.pdf)). At Naantali the heating system sits about 20 centimeters (about 8 inches) deep, and the researchers calculated that a system half as deep could give up to ten more days of play for the same energy ([Turku University of Applied Sciences](https://www.turkuamk.fi/en/article/computational-model-for-snow-melting-research-team-optimises-heating-of-naantali-artificial-grass-field/)). Depth costs energy.

At home, the electric version changes the base itself. Because the cable has to be set in concrete, a heated turf spot sits on a slab instead of a stone base that lets water soak down. ForeverLawn, a turf maker, builds its base from layers of stone so drainage isn't held back, and says turf laid over concrete gives "lackluster drainage performance" ([ForeverLawn](https://www.foreverlawn.com/learn-more/faqs/)). Melt water has to run across the top of the slab to its low edge and away, toward ground that may still be frozen; [what winter does to turf](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/) covers water that has nowhere to go. What turf over concrete needs in order to drain is in [artificial turf over concrete](/guides/artificial-turf-over-concrete/).

WarmlyYours's turf advice for a heated slab:

- **Short pile,** 1 to 1.5 inches; a pile of 2 inches or more insulates. More in [pile height](/guides/artificial-turf-pile-height/).
- **Silica sand infill,** not crumb rubber or cork, which conduct heat poorly. Compare infills in [artificial turf infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).
- **A perforated backing,** so melt water drains through.
- **Flat, tight contact with the slab,** with no thick foam pad above the cables.

A slab is permanent, so it has to be decided before the base goes in, not added under a finished lawn.

## What works instead in a Northern Colorado yard?

Usually the sun. The Colorado Climate Center says Colorado's thin atmosphere lets more sunlight through, giving pleasant days even in winter, and that chinook winds greatly moderate winter temperatures near the mountains ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). How quickly snow tends to leave turf here, and when it is worth clearing, is in [how to clear snow off artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).

For the part of the yard you use every day:

- **Put the dog's spot and the daily path on the sunny side.** CSU Extension notes that southern exposures tend to be hot and dry and northern ones cool and moist ([CSU Extension](https://extension.colostate.edu/gilpin/resource/mountain-microclimates/)). A spot on the south side of the house is the one the winter sun works on; the shaded side has its own guide, [turf in a shady, north-facing yard](/guides/artificial-turf-shady-north-facing-yard/).
- **Clear a strip by hand.** Which tools are safe on turf, and why rock salt stays off it, is in the [snow removal guide](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/).
- **Heat the walk, not the lawn.** Snowmelt is made for hard surfaces: WarmlyYours says its cables and mats are embedded under asphalt, concrete or pavers. A heated concrete walk to the dog's spot clears the route without changing the turf or its base.
- **If you still want heated turf,** keep it to a small pad like the 32-square-foot example, plan it before the base is built, and get the turf maker's answer in writing first. For a dog yard, start with [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/).
