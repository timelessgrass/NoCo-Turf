  - q: "Should a steep bank be turf or plants?"
    a: "Often plants. CSU Extension suggests water-wise ground covers for steep slopes that are impractical as lawn or a mowing hazard, and says ground covers help reduce erosion. One layout that suits a hillside is turf on the flat tiers and planted banks between them. Our guides to mixing turf, rock and plant beds and to turf versus xeriscape cover the planting side."
  - q: "Can turf run right up to the top of a retaining wall?"
    a: "Yes, but the last foot matters. Allan Block's manual finishes the top course with soil behind the blocks instead of drainage rock, partly to keep water from running behind them. A turf base of open stone laid to the back of the cap does the opposite, so ask how that edge is built and where its water goes."
  - q: "Does freeze-thaw move turf on a slope?"
    a: "It works on the fasteners. Fort Collins averages 150 nights a year at or below freezing, and every thaw on a slope sends water downhill through the base. Check the toe, the seams and the top edge each spring, and brush infill back up the slope after snowmelt. Our nails and staples guide covers what frost does to each fastener."
  - q: "Can a putting green go on the slope too?"
    a: "Yes, but a green needs its cups on nearly flat ground, far flatter than a lawn can tolerate. How much slope a green can carry at the speed you want, and when the ground under it gets cut and filled, is in our guide to putting greens on sloped yards."
  - q: "Does a sloped yard cost more to turf?"
    a: "It usually takes more work. FusionTurf, a turf maker, says sloped installs take more base work, anchoring and detailing than flat ones, and cost more when geogrid, drainage or terraces are added. This page carries no prices; our cost guide lists what moves a Northern Colorado quote."

**Checked September 25, 2026.** A slope doesn't rule out artificial turf. It changes how the base and turf are held on the grade and where storm water goes, and on steep ground it raises the question of a wall.

## How steep is too steep for artificial turf?

Slope is rise over run: ground that drops 1 foot over 5 feet of run is a 5:1 slope in code language, or 20 percent. Iowa State University Extension's classes, written for planting, give a yardstick ([ISU Extension](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/gardening-slopes-and-hillsides)):

| Slope | Ratio (run to rise) | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| Under 5% | flatter than 20:1 | Gentle (ISU) |
| About 8 to 16% | about 12:1 to 6:1 | Hard to walk; water tends to run off before it soaks in (ISU) |
| 20% | 5:1 | Steep (ISU). Firestone bars turf on anything steeper |
| About 30% | about 3:1 | Too steep to mow (ISU). Heron Lakes in Berthoud caps landscape slopes and berms at 3 to 1 |
| 50% | 2:1 | Often the steepest slope that can carry plants (ISU) |

**Firestone** is explicit: artificial turf "shall not be installed on slopes greater than a five to one (5:1) slope," a town permit comes first, and at least 3 feet of living landscape must separate turf from any property line ([Firestone Development Code 16.6.4](https://library.municode.com/co/firestone/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=TIT16FIDECO_CH6DEDEST_16.6.4LASCFE)). How the town reads 5:1 on a lot of mixed grades depends on the plan: ask [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/) Planning before it is final.

Measure yours with the board-and-level method in [putting greens on sloped yards](/guides/putting-green-on-a-slope/).

## What holds turf and its base in place on a grade?

Gravity pulls on base, turf and infill all the time. What makers call for:

- **Seams that run downhill.** FusionTurf, a turf maker, runs seams with the fall to limit creep and warping, and calls seams across the slope a common mistake ([FusionTurf](https://find.fusionturf.com/answers/artificial-grass-on-a-slope/)).
- **A base locked in at the bottom:** a shallow key at the toe where erosion is a risk, a thicker base on steeper runs, and geogrid within it or terraces on steeper grades. Materials and depths: [what base goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).
- **More fasteners where the pull is:** close spacing at the perimeter, staggered anchors across the field, extra at seams and the toe. PolyTurf adds 6-inch nails through the field on slopes; see [nails or staples](/guides/artificial-turf-nails-vs-staples/). Firestone requires turf anchored at all edges and seams, to the maker's specifications.
- **Some wrinkling.** Synthetic Grass Warehouse's installer column expects wrinkles on slopes and mounds, sometimes needing relief cuts ([SGW](https://syntheticgrasswarehouse.com/company/ask-an-expert/ask-jw/preventing-wrinkles-in-turf-part-2-2/)); see [wrinkles and ripples](/guides/artificial-turf-wrinkles-ripples/).

**Infill is where makers disagree.** PolyTurf calls infill less desirable on sloping surfaces and doesn't recommend its acrylic-coated sand there ([PolyTurf manual](https://polyturf.com/uploads/files/polyturf_installation_manual_landscape_only.pdf)). FusionTurf brushes coated silica sand in light lifts from the bottom up, watching the lower third. The Synthetic Turf Council says to check infill after heavy rain in case it has migrated with the slope ([STC](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf)). Ask what your turf's maker specifies, and expect to top up the low side. Options: [infill types](/guides/artificial-turf-infill-types/).

## Where does the water go when a storm hits a turf slope?

Downhill, on top and underneath. Some sheets across the surface with loose infill; the rest drops into the base and meets soil that, on clay, takes it slowly: Longmont says its "clay soils absorb water slowly" ([City of Longmont](https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/water-conservation/conserving-water-outdoors/)). On a clay slope, water in the base tends to follow the clay to the turf's bottom edge and come out there.

Storms here arrive fast. NOAA Atlas 14 puts the peak 5 minutes of a 100-year storm in central Fort Collins at 12.5 inches an hour, and a one-hour storm expected about every two years at 0.879 inches ([NOAA Atlas 14](https://hdsc.nws.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/hdsc/new/fe_text_mean.csv?lat=40.5853&lon=-105.0844&data=intensity&units=english&series=pds)). What handles it:

- **Catch water above the turf.** ISU Extension advises redirecting driveway and downspout water at the top of a slope with a small trench, drain tile or a surface drain.
- **Give the bottom an outlet.** FusionTurf uses a swale, channel drain or French drain at the toe, to a safe outlet, with subdrains and separation fabric on clay.
- **Break long runs** with terraces or landings.

Firestone's code says turf may not impede or alter a lot's drainage pattern. [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/)'s says landscaping, turf included, may not change the drainage pattern or stormwater runoff set by the lot's approved grading plan ([Frederick Land Use Code](https://www.frederickco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/33088/2026-Land-Use-Code)). Cut and fill can change both, so it depends on the plan on file: ask Frederick or Firestone Planning first. Not legal advice. Puddles and drain rates: [does artificial turf drain](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/).

## What if the slope falls toward the house?

Then the turf must hand its water off before the foundation. The 2021 International Residential Code wants ground to fall at least 6 inches in the first 10 feet from a foundation, or drains or swales where a slope prevents it ([IRC R401.3, UpCodes](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/4/foundations)); ask your building department which edition applies. The Colorado Geological Survey lists poor surface drainage among landscaping problems on expansive soils ([CGS](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/)), and its SP-11 guide says flattened slopes by a foundation pond rain, snowmelt and irrigation water ([CGS SP-11](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-11.pdf)). Around central [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/), soil surveys map Nunn clay loam on 1 to 3 percent slopes, high shrink-swell at a linear extensibility of 6.6 (USDA-NRCS soil survey data).

So the swale or drain goes between the turf and the house. Clay prep: [artificial turf on clay soil](/guides/artificial-turf-on-clay-soil/).

## When does a slope need a retaining wall, and when does the wall need a permit?

A wall turns part of a hill into flat ground: ISU describes terraces built with retaining walls, field stones or timbers to slow runoff, and each tier can take turf like a level yard. A tier 10 feet deep cut into a 5:1 slope holds 2 feet of grade.

| Where | Retaining wall rule | Height measured |
|---|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Business/Permits-and-Licensing/Building-Permits) | No permit up to 4 feet unless it supports a surcharge; a second wall uphill at least the lower wall's height away | Low-side grade to top |
| [Unincorporated Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/1-2024-building-code-amendments-final0.pdf) (2024 code, since January 1, 2026) | No permit up to 4 feet unless it supports a surcharge, if the next wall uphill is at least twice the upper wall's height away | From a point 8 feet downslope to the grade behind |
| [Unincorporated Weld County](https://www.weld.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/9/departments/building/documents/bldg-permit-needed-handout.pdf) | No permit up to 4 feet unless it supports a surcharge | Bottom of footings to top |
| [Greeley](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT24DECO_CH5REDEST_S24-502REDEST) | Permit for any retaining wall over 4 feet | Not stated |
| [Milliken](https://library.municode.com/co/milliken/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH16LAUSCO_ARTIIIGESTAPALDI_DIV3LABUFEWAST_S16-3-390FEWA) | Over 48 inches: engineered and approved first by the Town Engineer and Building Official; walls approved with a final drainage plan | Not stated |
| [2021 IRC](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/1/scope-and-administration), the model towns amend | No permit up to 4 feet unless it supports a surcharge | Bottom of footing to top |

- **The same 4 feet measures differently.** A wall showing 4 feet can be exempt where height starts at grade and need a permit where the buried footing counts.
- **The hill can be the surcharge.** Allan Block's manual speaks of "slopes or other surcharges above the wall" ([Allan Block](https://allanblock.com/installation/residential-installation/water-management)); ask whether yours counts.
- **Exempt is not rule-free,** Fort Collins says, and its permitted walls need a structural engineer's design and stamp.

Elsewhere it depends on the town: ask its building department first. Not legal advice.

Allan Block says most walls are designed around keeping the ground behind them relatively dry, and calls for a toe drain on walls over 4 feet or with a slope above. Turf passes rain into its base, so a turf tier just above a wall feeds water toward it unless that base drains elsewhere.

## How do HOA review, the town and the trades fit together on a hillside?

On a slope, drainage sets the order of work.

- **Start from the approved grade.** Heron Lakes in Berthoud won't let landscapers begin without the builder's certificate of drainage and topographical accuracy ([Heron Lakes rules](https://www.berthoudheritagemd.live/s/Heron-Lakes-Design-Rules-and-Regulations-June-1-2026.pdf)). The [Severance](/areas/severance-co/) Shores metro districts want a grading and drainage plan with the landscape plan, generally don't permit retaining walls, and approve any change affecting drainage ([Severance Shores guidelines](https://ssmd.specialdistrict.org/files/3cc9774d3/SSMD+-+Amended+RIGSR+09.05.24.pdf)).
- **Then the wall and its drains,** engineered and permitted where required.
- **Then sprinkler changes, base and turf.** See [sprinkler systems after turf](/guides/sprinkler-system-after-artificial-turf/).

Colorado law bars an HOA from prohibiting artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home, though design guidelines still apply ([SB23-178, C.R.S. 38-33.3-106.5](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92307/download)); walls and regrading are still reviewed. Not legal advice. More in [HOA approval for artificial turf](/guides/hoa-turf-approval/), [turf on a new-build lot](/guides/new-build-backyard-turf/) and [turf on acreage lots](/guides/artificial-turf-on-acreage-lots/).

## What should I ask an installer about a sloped yard?

- **What is the slope, and how was it measured?** A percent or ratio from a level or survey.
- **Which way do the seams run?** With the fall.
- **What holds the base at the bottom?** A key at the toe, and a thicker base or geogrid where it steepens.
- **How does fastener spacing change on the slope?** Closer at the perimeter, seams and toe.
- **Which infill, per the turf maker's slope guidance, and who tops it up after storms?**
- **Where does water from above go, and where does water leaving the bottom go?** A swale, drain or outlet on the plan.
- **If there's a wall: who designs it, does it need a permit, and how does the turf base above it drain?**

More in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/), [checking the base was installed](/guides/check-turf-base-was-installed/) and our [artificial turf installation](/services/artificial-turf-installation/) page.
