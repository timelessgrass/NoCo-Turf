faq:
  - q: "Can you vacuum artificial turf?"
    a: "Only where you plan to put infill back, or to pick up an absorbent you spread on a spill. A vacuum lifts the sand or granules between the blades along with the dirt. The Synthetic Turf Council's guidelines vacuum out infill after an oil spill, then install new infill. For topping it up, see [how often to add infill](/guides/how-often-to-add-infill-to-artificial-turf/)."
  - q: "Can I rinse turf in winter?"
    a: "Pick a day above freezing and rinse early, so the water drains before night. NOAA's Fort Collins normals count 150 days a year with a low at or below 32°F, but the Colorado Climate Center says chinook winds can raise temperatures 25 to 35°F within a short time, so mild afternoons do come. What frost does to turf is in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/)."
  - q: "Are store-bought artificial turf cleaners worth it?"
    a: "Read what the label claims. Some are odor products for dog areas, which is a different job, covered in [dog urine smell](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/). A product that claims to kill germs should carry an EPA registration number, because EPA registers products that sanitize or disinfect. Whatever the bottle says, its rinse water stays out of the gutter."
  - q: "What should I ask an installer about cleaning?"
    a: "Ask for the maker's care sheet for the exact turf and infill going in, which cleaners it rules out, and whether using one of them affects the maker's coverage. Ask which way the yard will drain once the turf is down, so you know where rinse water goes. A good answer hands you the care guide and points to the low spot on the plan. More in [questions to ask a turf installer](/guides/questions-to-ask-turf-installer/)."

Most turf cleaning is a brush and a hose. The Northern Colorado part is where the rinse water goes next: in Fort Collins, driveways drain to creeks, and each town's stormwater code says what may reach a storm drain. Pet messes, [leaves](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/) and [mold](/guides/does-artificial-turf-get-moldy/) have their own pages.

## How do I clean artificial turf day to day?

Brush it and rinse it. SYNLawn's care manual says turf "will accumulate dust, pollen, dirt etc." and, although rain helps, "will benefit from routine washing" ([SYNLawn care manual](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/SYNLawn-Care-and-Maintenance-ENG.pdf)). Rain won't do much of it here: the Colorado Climate Center describes the plains as having "infrequent rains and snow" and "moderate to high wind movement," with winters of "dry air and strong winds" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). Dust arrives on the wind and stays until someone rinses it off.

1. **Clear debris first;** [getting leaves off turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/) has the method.
2. **Brush against the grain** with a stiff synthetic-bristle brush or turf rake to loosen dust and stand the blades up. Flattened lanes are covered in [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/).
3. **Rinse with a garden hose** on a shower setting. SYNLawn warns that spray above 300 psi "can severely damage the turf and displace any infill material," and MSI's care sheet says "Never use a power washer" ([MSI care sheet](https://cdn.msisurfaces.com/files/flyers/turf-care-and-maintenance.pdf)).
4. **Rinse toward the middle of the yard,** beginning at the edges by the driveway and walks, so water soaks down through the turf instead of sheeting across concrete to the street.

Do it when the turf feels gritty or after a dusty windstorm.

## When can I run the hose under this year's watering rules?

Watering rules were written for sprinklers and rarely mention turf, so the hour and the wording decide.

- **Fort Collins.** The voluntary Water Shortage Watch, effective May 1, 2026, asked residents to water lawns no more than 2 days a week and not between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. ([City of Fort Collins](https://www.fortcollins.gov/News-Articles/News/2026-Q2/Voluntary-Water-Shortage-Watch)). Rinsing before 10 or after 6 sidesteps the question.
- **Johnstown.** Level 3 keeps watering out of the 10-to-6 window, wants hand watering on the property's scheduled day through a self-closing nozzle, and says hardscapes "should not be spray-washed," suggesting "sweeping or spot cleaning" ([Johnstown water FAQs](https://www.johnstownco.gov/m/faq?cat=37)). Turf isn't named; brushing and spot-rinsing fits. The season ends October 15 ([2026 schedule](https://www.johnstownco.gov/665/2026-Watering-Schedule)); for a whole-yard rinse after that or on an off day, it depends on how the town reads its rules, so ask Johnstown's water conservation staff.

Every town's rule is in [do you have to water artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/).

## What can run off my yard into the gutter?

Clean water, and little else. Fort Collins Utilities says runoff "flows directly from storm sewers into the creeks and Poudre River without treatment," and that "driveways drain to creeks and other surface water" ([Fort Collins Utilities](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Other-Utilities-Pages/Water/Stormwater)). None of these four codes names artificial turf. Not legal advice.

| Town | Code | What it bars, and what it lets through |
|---|---|---|
| [Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/) | [Sec. 26-498](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH26UT_ARTVIISTUT_DIV1GE_S26-498WAQUCO) and [26-491](https://library.municode.com/co/fort_collins/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=CH26UT_ARTVIISTUT_DIV1GE_S26-491DE) | No "pollutant or contaminated water" into a street or storm drain, or onto property where it could migrate to one. Pollutants include dirt, sand, chemical waste, cooking grease and household waste. Lawn watering is exempt. |
| [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/) | [Sec. 13-5-50](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH13MUUT_ARTVSTQU_S13-5-50ILDIPREX) | Names solvents, oil and grease, pesticides, pet wastes, "cleaning products" and sediment. Lawn watering is exempt; pool water only when dechlorinated to under 0.05 ppm chlorine. |
| [Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/) | [13.20.130](https://library.municode.com/co/loveland/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIILOCOMUCO_TIT13UT_CH13.20STQU_13.20.130ILDI) | Bars "any substance other than naturally occurring stormwater runoff." Lawn watering and non-commercial car washing are exempt. |
| [Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/) | [Sec. 20-563](https://library.municode.com/co/greeley/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=PTIICOOR_TIT20PUWOUT_CH3WASASESE_ARTVIISTDRSY_DIV1INGE_S20-563ILDIALDINOTA) | Bars discharges "not composed entirely of stormwater" unless listed. Landscape irrigation and residential car washing are listed; turf cleaning isn't. |

What that means for a turf clean:

- **Plain water that soaks into the yard** is the safe case. Water sheeting across a driveway carries infill and dust, and Fort Collins counts sand and dirt as pollutants.
- **Anything with a cleaner in it stays on the turf.** EPA says "Disinfectants are pesticides" ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/selected-epa-registered-disinfectants)), and Windsor names pesticides and cleaning products. A turf cleaner isn't the residential car wash these codes exempt.
- **Your own yard counts.** Fort Collins' rule reaches private property wherever there is "a significant potential for migration" to a storm drain, so never rinse a cleaner toward the street.
- **A spill into a storm drain** is an emergency in Fort Collins: the city asks for spills "directly into a storm drain, creek or river" to be reported by calling 911.

Elsewhere in Northern Colorado, it depends on the local code; ask your town's public works or stormwater office. How water leaves the turf is in [does artificial turf drain](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/).

## How do I get grease, sunscreen, gum and sap off artificial turf?

Fast, and dry first. SYNLawn's manual calls promptness "the first rule": lift solids with a spatula or table knife, blot liquids with paper towels or a dry absorbent such as kitty litter, then sweep the absorbent up. Some cleaners "safe for the face fibers can be harmful to other components of the turf system," it warns, so solvents go on from a cloth, never poured.

| Spill | What the care guides say to use | Rinse afterward? |
|---|---|---|
| Food, drinks, most water-based stains | About one teaspoon of mild detergent per pint of warm water (SYNLawn), or hot but not boiling water with detergent and a stiff brush ([Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf)) | Yes, gently, keeping the water on the turf |
| Sunscreen, suntan oil, cooking oil, grill grease | Mineral spirits, sparingly, from a soaked cloth (SYNLawn); flammable, so away from the grill | No; blot |
| Chewing gum, tree sap | Freeze with ice, aerosol refrigerant or dry ice, then chip or scrape off. Peanut butter softens smeared gum (STC) | No |
| Motor oil, hydraulic fluid | Soak it up, vacuum out that spot's infill, wash the blades with dish soap and water, replace the infill (STC) | The soapy water stays in the turf |
| Oil paint, stains that won't lift | Ask the turf maker; SYNLawn says these may need a commercial carpet cleaner | Per the maker |

The Synthetic Turf Council adds two exclusions: no cleaning chemicals with "alcohol or acetone solvents," which rules out rubbing alcohol and acetone nail polish remover, and no chemicals without checking with whoever built the turf.

Sunscreen earns its row: the Colorado Climate Center blames "the intense high-elevation sunlight" for sunburn in Colorado, so sunscreen lands wherever people sit. See [turf around a pool](/guides/artificial-turf-around-pool/) and [a grill or fire pit on turf](/guides/fire-pit-on-artificial-turf/).

## When does turf need disinfecting, and what does "disinfect" mean on a label?

Less often than ads suggest. The CDC says "cleaning alone with soap and water can remove most germs" in most situations, and disinfecting at home "is likely not needed unless someone in your home is sick or if someone sick has recently visited" ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/cleaning-and-disinfecting-with-bleach.html)). The label words differ ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/coronavirus/whats-difference-between-products-disinfect-sanitize-and-clean-surfaces)):

| Label says | What it does, per EPA | Registered with EPA? |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaner | Removes dirt and organic matter with soap or detergent | Only if it also claims to sanitize or disinfect |
| Sanitizer | Kills bacteria; "not intended to kill viruses" | Yes |
| Disinfectant | Kills viruses and bacteria | Yes |

Before a disinfectant goes on turf, run [EPA's checks](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-04/documents/disinfectants-onepager.pdf):

1. **Find the EPA registration number,** "the only way to accurately identify a product."
2. **Read the use sites and surface types.** EPA says to check whether the product "is approved for the intended-use site." Countertop directions don't cover turf; look for a label that names artificial turf, or ask the turf maker.
3. **Clean first** with soap and water if the surface is visibly dirty or the label says to.
4. **Keep it wet for the contact time.** The surface "should be visibly wet for the entire contact time," which "may mean the product needs to be reapplied." On a dry, breezy afternoon here, plan on it.

**After vomit or diarrhea,** the CDC's norovirus steps are to wipe up with paper towels and bag them, then disinfect with a chlorine bleach solution of 1,000 to 5,000 ppm "or use an EPA-registered disinfecting product against norovirus," leaving bleach on at least 5 minutes ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/norovirus/prevention/index.html)). One turf maker rules out bleach (below), so on turf use a product from EPA's norovirus list (List G) whose label allows the surface.

**Play areas** get the same routine, with the dog's spot kept well away ([dog poop on turf](/guides/dog-poop-on-artificial-turf/)). See also [is artificial turf toxic](/guides/is-artificial-turf-toxic/) and [playground turf](/services/playground-turf/).

## Do vinegar and bleach work on artificial turf?

- **Bleach: no.** SYNLawn's manual says "DO NOT USE cleaners that contain chlorine bleaches." It clashes with the gutter rules too: Windsor lets pool water reach storm drains only below 0.05 ppm chlorine, and the CDC's norovirus bleach solution starts at 1,000 ppm. Never mix it: the CDC says bleach "or any disinfectants" combined with other cleaners "can release vapors that may be very dangerous to breathe in."
- **Vinegar: one job.** SYNLawn brushes in equal parts white distilled vinegar and water for hard-water mineral deposits, then flushes it; the same manual warns off "highly acidic cleaners," so dilute as written. For killing germs, use an EPA-registered disinfectant instead. In a dog area vinegar has another drawback; see [dog urine smell](/guides/dog-urine-smell-artificial-turf/).
- **Ammonia: stubborn stains only.** SYNLawn allows a 3 percent household ammonia solution, rinsed out with clean cold water. Never with bleach, and not where a dog goes: Humane World for Animals says strong smells such as ammonia "may encourage your pet to reinforce the urine scent mark" ([Humane World for Animals](https://www.humaneworld.org/en/resources/how-remove-pet-stains-and-odors)).
