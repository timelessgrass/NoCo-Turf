faq:
  - q: "Can I flood the turf instead of using a liner?"
    a: "Not usefully. Turf is built to let water through: CCGrass describes a free-draining base under a perforated backing. Iron Sleek says that even on a lawn, an unlined rink's water drains away in a thaw, and most winter days on the plains here climb above freezing (see the table above). The liner is what holds the water."
  - q: "When should the liner go down on turf, and when should it come up?"
    a: "Rink makers time liners around grass. EZ ICE says to lay the liner after the first frost and lift it early in spring, and Iron Sleek warns that a liner over grass that has started to grow can suffocate the lawn. Turf doesn't grow, so on turf the timing is about the ice: fill when nights are forecast to stay below freezing, and lift the liner as soon as the water is off in spring."
  - q: "Is a backyard rink on turf safe for kids?"
    a: "Only with an adult checking the ice first, every time. EZ ICE warns that a person can drown in a small amount of standing water, that a rink must be confirmed frozen before each skate, and that children shouldn't be on or around a rink unsupervised. On the plains more than a third of winter days reach 50°F, so ice that held in the morning can be soft by afternoon."
  - q: "What about synthetic ice panels on turf?"
    a: "EZ ICE says its synthetic ice panels have to go on a firm, flat surface. Ask the panel maker whether turf counts as firm before you buy, and don't screw or stake anything through the turf to hold panels down. Other games on turf are in our guide to a [backyard sports area on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-for-backyard-sports/)."
  - q: "Will a rink change what my turf's written terms cover?"
    a: "Ask before you build. None of the rink or turf makers we read address a rink on artificial turf, so ask your installer and the turf maker what their written terms say about stakes, a winter-long liner and weeks of weight, and keep the answer in writing."

A rink can spend a winter on artificial turf, and one worry that comes with a rink on a lawn doesn't apply: there is no grass under the liner to smother. What decides whether it works here is the weather, how the rink is held together, and where the water goes when it melts. Here is each one, with the numbers and who said them.

## Does a Northern Colorado winter stay cold enough for backyard ice?

At night, nearly always. All day, not often. EZ ICE, which makes backyard rink kits, says to fill a rink when temperatures, especially overnight, are forecast to stay below freezing, and that the water normally takes 2 to 3 days to freeze, longer in a deeper rink ([EZ ICE assembly guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0021/5623/2816/files/PDF_Classic_Square.pdf)). NOAA's normals show how often each part of Northern Colorado manages that across the 90 days of December, January and February:

| Nearest NOAA station (towns closest to it) | Nights at or below 32°F | Days whose high stays at or below 32°F | Days that reach 50°F or warmer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fort Collins ([Fort Collins](/areas/fort-collins-co/), [Wellington](/areas/wellington-co/)) | 85.2 | 12.1 | 33.8 |
| Loveland 2N ([Loveland](/areas/loveland-co/), [Berthoud](/areas/berthoud-co/)) | 88.0 | 15.5 | 33.3 |
| Greeley UNC ([Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/), [Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/), [Johnstown](/areas/johnstown-co/), Severance, Evans, Eaton, Milliken) | 86.0 | 15.1 | 33.0 |
| Longmont 2 ESE (Longmont, [Mead](/areas/mead-co/), [Firestone](/areas/firestone-co/), [Frederick](/areas/frederick-co/), [Dacono](/areas/dacono-co/)) | 88.7 | 12.7 | 38.4 |
| Estes Park 3 SSE (Estes Park) | 81.2 | 31.3 | 5.5 |

*NOAA U.S. Climate Normals, 1991 to 2020, winter counts for each station: [Fort Collins](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053005&format=json), [Loveland 2N](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00055236&format=json), [Greeley UNC](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00053553&format=json), [Longmont 2 ESE](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00055116&format=json), [Estes Park 3 SSE](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00052761&format=json). What each count means is in NOAA's [normals documentation](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/normals-annualseasonal/1991-2020/doc/Normals_ANN_Documentation_1991-2020.pdf).*

On the plains, then, almost every winter night freezes, but only about 12 to 16 days stay at or below freezing from morning to night, and more than a third of winter days reach 50°F. A rink there freezes overnight and softens on a lot of afternoons. Estes Park, at the bottom of the table, is the one NoCo town where ice has a much better chance: about 31 winter days that never thaw, and only 5.5 that reach 50°F.

Two Colorado habits make the plains harder on ice. Chinook winds, warmed as they descend, bring sudden warm-ups, and the Colorado Climate Center says rises of 25 to 35°F "within a short time are not uncommon." The same source says the thin atmosphere lets more of the sun's energy through, which is why winter afternoons here feel pleasant ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/climate_long.html)). What those swings do to the ground under turf is in [does artificial turf freeze](/guides/does-artificial-turf-freeze/).

A liner is what makes an on-and-off winter workable. Iron Sleek, which makes rink brackets and liners, says a lined rink rides out a thaw: the water levels itself, and when the cold comes back it can take as little as one evening to skate again. Without a liner, it says, the water drains away in a thaw and you start over ([Iron Sleek](https://ironsleek.com/use-a-liner)).

Check the ice every time anyone goes out. EZ ICE warns that a person can drown in a small amount of standing water, and that skating on a rink that hasn't frozen, or froze and then melted, risks serious injury ([EZ ICE](https://ezicerinks.com/pages/ez-ice-pro-home-arena-system-assembly)). After a 50-degree afternoon, ice that held at breakfast may not hold at dinner.

## Will rink brackets or stakes damage artificial turf?

Anything driven into the ground goes through the turf first, and many rink kits anchor that way. Iron Sleek's brackets are pushed in by hand, then hammered down until they are firm in "relatively solid ground," with a one-foot ground stake added in soft soil or where the water is deeper than 12 inches ([Iron Sleek instructions](https://ironsleek.com/pdf/SleekInstructionsv6_71912.pdf)). In spring each bracket is rocked loose, pulled straight up, and its divot filled ([Iron Sleek](https://blog.ironsleek.com/2016/02/backyard-rink-time-to-tear-it-down-for.html)).

On turf, each of those is a hole through the backing. CCGrass, which makes artificial turf, lists stakes among the things that cut blades and puncture turf backing ([CCGrass](https://www.ccgrass.com/what-can-damage-artificial-grass/)). Below the backing is the base, compacted gravel layers, and CCGrass ties a poorly compacted base to uneven surfaces and poor drainage, so a stake through it disturbs the layer that keeps the turf flat. Patching a hole is covered in [how to repair artificial turf](/guides/how-to-repair-artificial-turf/), and the base in [what goes under artificial turf](/guides/what-base-goes-under-artificial-turf/).

A rink that stands on top avoids all of that. EZ ICE says its rinks assemble on any flat surface without tools, held together by straps that clip into the brackets on either side of the rink, and sells them as needing "no stakes" ([EZ ICE assembly guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0021/5623/2816/files/PDF_Classic_Square.pdf), [EZ ICE](https://ezicerinks.com/)). Those brackets still sit on the turf and reach about 15 inches past the boards, so EZ ICE says to allow an extra 2.5 feet of length and width. It also caps the ground at nine inches of slope across the rink, so measure the slope of the turf before buying.

- **None of the rink makers we read mention artificial turf by name.** Ask the kit maker before you buy.
- **Ask your installer** what the turf's written terms say about stakes, a liner and weeks of weight.
- **Lace up at the boards.** CCGrass names cleats along with stakes as things that cut blades. Skate blades are sharper still, so walking across turf in skates invites the same damage (our reading, not CCGrass's).

## How heavy is a backyard rink, and what does the weight do to turf?

Heavy, and for weeks. USGS lists water at 32°F at 62.416 pounds per cubic foot ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density)), so every inch of rink water puts about 5.2 pounds on each square foot of turf. EZ ICE's rules are no less than 3 inches of water and no more than 12, with an average of 3 inches to minimize cracking ([EZ ICE FAQ](https://ezicerinks.com/pages/faqs)):

| Water depth | Weight on each square foot (our arithmetic) |
|---|---|
| 3 inches, EZ ICE's minimum and recommended average | about 15.6 pounds |
| 12 inches, EZ ICE's maximum: the deep end of a yard with 9 inches of fall | about 62.4 pounds |

For a whole rink, a 20 by 40 foot rink at an average 3 inches holds about 1,500 gallons and about 12,500 pounds of water (our arithmetic, with the [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/document/2026-nist-handbook-44-appendix-c) figure of 7.481 gallons to the cubic foot). Iron Sleek says a 20 by 46 foot rink can hold 35,000 pounds of ice, and warns against trying to chip out a full sheet.

The turf makers we read give no load limit for something like this. What CCGrass does say is that weight left in one spot compresses turf fibers and may leave lasting dents, and to avoid long-term heavy pressure. A rink is long-term heavy pressure by design. Plan to bring the pile back up in spring: CCGrass's fix for flattened turf is to top up infill if it's low and brush against the grain with a stiff synthetic-bristle brush or a power broom. More in [matted artificial turf](/guides/matted-artificial-turf/).

## Where does the meltwater go in spring?

Off the turf, by pump or siphon, and not all at once. While the liner is whole, the water stays in it. Iron Sleek says not to punch holes in the liner, and that opening one corner to let everything run out floods the ground, and that repeated flooding can lower the grade ([Iron Sleek](https://blog.ironsleek.com/2016/02/backyard-rink-time-to-tear-it-down-for.html)). Its preferred method is an electric or gas pump with a discharge hose, which home improvement stores usually rent: a 30-gallon-a-minute pump empties a 20 by 46 foot rink in 2 to 3 hours, and the hose lets you send the water where it can drain without flooding your house or a neighbor's. A siphon is slower, and Iron Sleek points out that the slow flow gives the ground a better chance to soak it up.

Why not let it run into the turf? Turf is built to drain: CCGrass describes a free-draining base under a perforated backing. The catch is timing. SIS Pitches, which builds synthetic sports surfaces, says heavy rain or a quick thaw on frozen synthetic turf can flood it, because frozen sand infill stops water draining until it thaws ([SIS Pitches](https://www.sispitches.com/news/winter-pitch-maintenance/)). Hundreds or thousands of gallons arriving at once on infill that is still frozen is that problem at full size. Wait until the ground has thawed, aim the hose at a spot that can take the water, and see [does artificial turf drain](/guides/does-artificial-turf-drain/) for what normal drainage looks like.

Then lift the liner promptly. FieldTurf, which makes synthetic sports turf, says a tarp over a turf field in winter is permitted but has caused more condensation and freezing, with "unfavorable results" ([FieldTurf](https://fieldturf.com/en/articles/detail/everything-you-need-snow-removal/)). FieldTurf wrote that about sports fields, not rinks, but a liner is a sheet over the turf all winter too. Get it off once the water is out, let the turf dry, and brush it. If damp spots stay damp, read [does artificial turf get moldy](/guides/does-artificial-turf-get-moldy/).

## Does a clear liner, or sun through the plastic, hurt the turf?

Use white, for the ice's sake first. EZ ICE's liners are all 6 mil bright white poly ([EZ ICE FAQ](https://ezicerinks.com/pages/faqs)), and Iron Sleek says white liners help keep ice from melting by reflecting radiant heat away on sunny winter days ([Iron Sleek](https://ironsleek.com/use-a-liner)). Clear is the one to avoid: Iron Sleek says see-through liners absorb heat from what lies underneath and break the ice down more quickly ([Iron Sleek blog](https://blog.ironsleek.com/2015/08/top-ways-iron-sleeks-ice-rink-liners.html)). It wrote that about grass. Under a clear liner on turf, the sun lands on green plastic instead; what sun does to turf temperature is in [how hot artificial turf gets](/guides/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/). With Northern Colorado's strong winter sun, a clear liner works against the ice from below.

As for the turf's own sun protection, we found nothing from a turf maker on what a winter under clear plastic does to it.

Keep the ice clean, too. EZ ICE says leaves frozen into the ice attract sunlight and melt holes in it, and that snow left on a rink insulates the ice and makes it melt, so shovel the rink after each snow ([EZ ICE assembly guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0021/5623/2816/files/PDF_Classic_Square.pdf)). Clearing snow from the turf around the rink is a different job, covered in [snow removal on artificial turf](/guides/artificial-turf-snow-removal/), and fall leaves in [leaves on artificial turf](/guides/leaves-on-artificial-turf/).

## Where can the water come from in winter?

A hose or a truck, with the faucet looked after. EZ ICE says a garden hose is usually enough, over several hours or longer, and that a local water company can fill a rink faster by truck. Work out the volume first: about 1,500 gallons for a 20 by 40 foot rink at an average 3 inches, more on a sloped yard. Iron Sleek says a small residential rink takes as little as 5,000 gallons ([Iron Sleek](https://ironsleek.com/use-a-liner)).

Protect the outdoor faucet. Greeley's water department tells residents to disconnect and drain outdoor hoses for winter, because a single hard overnight freeze can burst the faucet or the pipe ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/water-and-sewer/about/news/2024/time-to-winterize-4-things-to-add-to-your-to-do-list/)). When the rink is full, take the hose off and drain it the same day.

The town rules we read:

- **[Windsor](/areas/windsor-co/):** the code says water may not be used through hoses without a nozzle or sprinkler attached, a rule that applies to all users of the town's water service ([Sec. 13-2-180](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH13MUUT_ARTIIWA_S13-2-180REWAUS)). So fill with a nozzle on the hose. Windsor's lawn watering hours, no lawn watering between 10:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m., apply only from May 1 through September 30 ([Sec. 13-2-420](https://library.municode.com/co/windsor/codes/charter_and_municipal_code?nodeId=WI_CH13MUUT_ARTIIWA_S13-2-420LAWARE)).
- **[Greeley](/areas/greeley-co/):** the 2026 rules allow no lawn watering without a variance from November 1 to April 14, and no watering between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. ([City of Greeley](https://greeleyco.gov/government/city-administration/city-departments/water-and-sewer/save-water/restrictions-violations-variances)). They don't mention filling a rink, so ask Greeley's water department before you fill one.
- **Other towns:** we haven't read every provider's winter rules with a rink in mind. The hose rules we did read are in [do you have to water artificial turf](/guides/do-you-have-to-water-artificial-turf/), and your water provider can say whether filling a rink counts as outdoor watering.
