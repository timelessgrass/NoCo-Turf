## What decides whether play turf is safe to fall on?

The pad, not the turf. Turf over a compacted base is built to stay flat, and that makes it firm. Where children climb, swing and jump, a shock pad goes between the base and the turf to take the impact.

Three things decide what that pad has to be:

- **Fall height.** The highest point a child can fall from: a deck, the top of a climbing wall, the arc of a swing. The pad is rated for a fall height at least that tall.
- **Use zone.** The ground around the equipment where a child can land. It runs past the edge of the set on every side, and further in front of and behind swings; the equipment maker's instructions give it for each set.
- **Test data.** A pad's fall-height rating comes from the manufacturer's impact testing. Ask for the document, not the adjective.

Wood chips and rubber mulch are the other common play surfaces. Turf over a pad stays level, and it doesn't get kicked out from under the swings.

## How does play turf go in under an existing playset?

1. **Measure the set.** The highest platform, the swing beam, and the use zone around both.
2. **Protect or move the set.** Some sets can be worked around; others come out and go back once the base is done.
3. **Excavate and grade.** The old lawn or mulch comes out, and the ground is shaped to drain away from the house. The [Colorado Geological Survey](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) lists poor surface drainage among the landscaping problems on expansive soils.
4. **Build the base.** Crushed rock, compacted flat, the same as under a lawn.
5. **Pad, then turf.** The shock pad goes down across the use zone, the turf goes over it, and both are cut neatly around every post.
6. **Edges.** The play area is secured where it meets lawn, rock or a curb, like the poured curb in the photo at the top of this page.

## How hot does play turf get, and what helps?

Hotter than grass in direct sun. NOAA's 1991–2020 normals put the Greeley UNC station at 58.3 days a year at or above 90°F, and the Fort Collins station at 29.5.

- **Shade.** Put the set where it gets afternoon shade, or add a sail over the play area.
- **Water.** A quick hose-down cools the surface before kids go out.
- **Check it.** Put your hand flat on the turf for a few seconds. If it's uncomfortable for your hand, it's too hot for bare legs.
- **Glare.** Sunlight reflected off some windows can concentrate on turf and damage it. Look at the afternoon glare before choosing a spot.

## Can daycares, schools and HOAs still put in play turf?

Under state law, yes. Since January 1, 2026, Colorado cities, towns and special and metro districts may not install or allow nonfunctional artificial turf as part of new development or redevelopment on the property types the state law covers: commercial, institutional and industrial property, HOA common property, and street rights-of-way, parking lots, medians and transportation corridors. Playgrounds aren't nonfunctional. Colorado's 2025 amendment defines functional artificial turf to include playgrounds, sports fields, picnic grounds and parts of parks.

Some town codes go further:

| Town | What the code says |
|---|---|
| Evans | Artificial turf is not acceptable for public or common areas. |
| Windsor | Artificial turf is barred from any landscape plan for new development or major redevelopment, with functional artificial turf allowed only by the Director's exception. |
| Fort Collins | Artificial turf is barred from development landscape plans; development on existing single- and two-unit home lots is exempt from that section. |
| Dacono | Artificial plants and synthetic turf can't be used to meet the code's landscaping requirements. |

**At home.** Colorado law says an HOA's landscaping rules may not prohibit artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home, although the HOA can still adopt design and aesthetic guidelines.
