## What makes a backyard green roll well?

A green is the least forgiving turf job in the yard. Putting turf is short and dense, so it hides nothing: a dip in the base that would vanish under a lawn becomes a putt that wobbles.

What decides the roll:

- **The base.** Crushed rock, compacted and finished smooth enough to putt on before any turf touches it.
- **The ground under the base.** The [Colorado Geological Survey](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) says the state's expansive clays can swell up to 20% by volume when wet. Water that sits under a green can lift part of it, so the base is shaped to drain.
- **The surface.** Putting turf is made to roll; lawn turf isn't. The two usually meet at the fringe.
- **The sand.** Sand brushed into the surface steadies it and changes the speed.

## How are break, speed, cups and fringe set?

**Break** is built into the grade. Slopes, ridges and tiers are shaped in the base before the turf goes down, and they can't be added afterward. A little break goes a long way on a small green.

**Speed** comes from the surface and the sand. A heavily filled, firmly brushed green runs differently from a lightly filled one, so it's worth saying who will be putting: someone grinding on lag putts and kids playing a round after dinner want different things.

**Cups** are set into the base so the lip sits flush with the surface. Where they go is a design decision. Spread them so one spot gives you different lengths and breaks, and keep them far enough apart that every putt isn't the same putt.

**Fringe** is the collar of longer turf around the putting surface. It frames the green, catches balls that run long and gives you a lie to chip from. It's also the transition to rock, lawn or concrete, like the fringe meeting the driveway in the photos below.

## What does a Front Range winter do to a green?

Artificial turf doesn't go dormant, so a green that's clear of snow is a green you can putt on in January. Brush snow off with a broom or a plastic shovel, or leave it to melt; a metal edge can snag the turf.

The bigger winter test is underneath. NOAA's Fort Collins station averages 150 days a year with a low at or below 32°F (1991–2020 normals). Water freezing and thawing in the clay under a green is how a smooth surface grows a ridge, and the answer is the same as everywhere else in the yard: a grade and a base that drain.

Spring is a good time to brush the surface up and top off the sand where the most putts are hit.

## Can a putting green go in an HOA common area or a golf community?

**Behind your house.** Colorado law says an HOA's landscaping rules may not prohibit artificial turf in the backyard of a detached single-family home, although the HOA can still adopt design and aesthetic guidelines.

**On common ground.** Since January 1, 2026, Colorado cities, towns and special and metro districts may not install or allow nonfunctional artificial turf as part of new development or redevelopment on the property types the state law covers. Putting greens aren't nonfunctional: the 2025 amendment counts golf playing areas such as putting and chipping greens as functional artificial turf.

**Town codes can be stricter.** Windsor's landscape code bars artificial turf from any landscape plan for new development or major redevelopment, allowing only functional artificial turf by the Director's exception. Fort Collins' Land Use Code bars artificial turf from development landscape plans, though development on existing single- and two-unit home lots is exempt from that section. In Firestone, a town permit comes before any artificial turf is installed.
